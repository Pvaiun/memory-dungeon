# Authored gaps: the director's intent

Recorded by the agent from the conversation of Nov 16–17, for the director to correct. Their latest word wins.

## What it's for

- **The value is the building of the builds.** Each pick should be a thought: "trying to build towards something powerful, that's where the fun of the game comes from." Played hundreds of times, the game should "always be surprised by what gets made".
- **A run shows about a quarter of the loot.** The set's size (450 pieces of gear, 50 formations) comes from that: "trying to hit a 25% of all loot" for a finished run. Slay the Spire shows 40 to 60%, with characters that each play completely differently, so a game like this one needs "a much lower percent".
- **As cross-build as possible:** "so that you can find unique effects every single time you play the game. It should feel surprising every time."
- **The nine builds stand.** They were context for making content that works together, and they do their job: they feel, play and look different.
- **What went wrong twice.** First, pattern matching: a player took "everything that said the word shield", and it always worked. Then, asked for exotic effects to break that, the result was "super random and esoteric". The agent makes sets with many connections; it doesn't author interesting things to explore inside them.
- **The director's side.** Good at shaping, taste and unique ideas; not for data entry, mass content or brainstorming from nothing. "I can pretty easily create unique things, but I can't guarantee that there's any consistency or coherence between them."

## The method

- **The agent builds the web,** as generic skeletons, from the kept effects, the classes and the roads. "You are building a web and you're using me to fill gaps in the web or as the origin for sections of the web that you don't have at all."
- **The earlier loot is no part of it:** "I would not be basing any of your decisions off the last loot... the loot we had before is not relevant to this at all."
- **Gaps instead of guesses.** "Leave empty gaps in the web whenever you're even a little bit unsure, writing what you were trying to do but didn't know what to choose."
- **Then it asks.** Each gap comes to the director as an objective to author against: a link between two mechanics, a capstone for a build, a function to fill, a piece two classes would both want, or a whole build's direction ("I don't know where to start with the shock build").
- **Creative decisions on the structure or a build's goals are asked, never made by the agent.**

## The shape of the web (Nov 20)

- **The picture:** a star with the builds at its points and "a gradient of color" between them, filled in "so that we know that there's little clusters of related things that players can find and use and build their own unique builds."
- **The builds guide the makers, not the player:** "the player shouldn't really understand the concept of these builds... It's just a guiding principle for us so that we're not placing things randomly." Rules for how many effects must answer each build led players to pick by colour, and with so few builds that was easy to work out.
- **What the authored effects are for:** enough great elements in the web that the simple effects can then be filled in by AI.
- **What they aren't sure of:** that the breadth and spread of the hundred-odd effects is being curated, managed or understood, and that the web is still the idea the work is run by.
- **What they asked of the agent,** as the data scientist: what the web is as a data model, whether it has to be represented and how ("I don't believe that just lines of text is going to be able to do it"), and how that finds the gaps.
- **Far-apart builds may share little.** A player explores, refining a build as they go, so two builds on opposite sides of the star having few overlaps makes sense to them. They "could go either way".
- **The classes are solid as identities and weak in their details.** Each class's "general vibe and their general things that they do" stands. Their upgrades, versions and each ability's exact effect were made in the same mass generation as the loot and are "super underdeveloped": "about 50% of them are just bad". The classes are to be read as broad pictures, a few threads each, and they change in reaction to the web: if the web puts Bonfire nearest another build, the Pyromancer can be given more of that build's effects.
- **Open, and theirs to rule:** whether the builds are "helpful signposts, or... poor design" (Slay the Spire's defined builds on several characters, against Hades's one character and a unique thing every time); and whether the web is built piece by piece or has to be authored in its shape first, and if so whether the way it's authored today is right.

## A turn the director is weighing: the classes as the anchor (Nov 20)

Said late and tired, as a ramble to react to: "it's likely this is like worthless, but give me your thoughts." Not a decision. Nothing is to be built from it until they say so.

- **The classes are this game's gods.** "Aren't classes the single biggest choice that the player has, and the single biggest chunk of gameplay that they select? And shouldn't they be the signposting the different mechanics in the game?"
- **Today the work runs backwards.** It goes from builds and rhythms to gear, and then to class upgrades that fit the gear, with the classes "after the fact kind of being appended onto these different mechanics with loose theming". The builds were made as an anchor because the classes weren't one: the game was first conceived with five party slots, where a class filled a general role.
- **A party isn't that diverse underneath.** It needs ways to deal damage and ways to sustain, by mitigation or healing: tank, support and damage, with the usual permutations (burst that kills before damage comes in, supports stacked on one damage dealer, bruisers).
- **The idea:** far fewer classes (five to seven), each much deeper (six or seven abilities, with versions given by "some other sort of mechanic" than a free pick), each fitting all three roles to a degree in its own flavour. A party is then a matching of classes and their flavours, and builds are "the way that you capitalize on those choices".
- **Gear may be per class,** every piece for a single class, with formations as "the sort of monkey wrench that connects all three". The web is then designed "around combinations of known classes, rather than trying to connect everything to everything else".
- **On the status builds being one group in the web:** that was on purpose. They made each piece cross-build as they wrote it.

## What the director is chasing (Nov 20)

- **Discovery inside a single run:** "a player is faced with choices, and they make the choice they think is best in the moment, but they do not know where it will end up, and they are surprised every time. And over hundreds of runs, they learn to make the right choices to lead to good outcomes most of the time, but they still don't know what those outcomes are going to be. That's what I'm chasing."
- **It's the looting, not the fights:** "what we're chasing is an exploration and the feeling of looting. And not so much combat. Individual fights don't have to be, and never will be, interesting because of the auto-battling nature of the game."
- **Two ways the game could go, and they see both.** Keep adding classes, to thirty, forty, fifty or sixty, with a class as "a piece of the arsenal". Or four to six classes, "all deep as hell", mixed together. The second is "basically a full redesign of the game", and they are "not convinced yet" it's the right way.
- **Their own objection to few classes:** with six classes, a rule for who wears what (two in light, two in medium, two in heavy) is "a stupid A or B choice every single time".
- **The classes' identities come first.** With sixteen classes kept and the classes a core part of the web: "before we continue... we should decide what each class is supposed to represent in that web."
- **The classes themselves may have to change:** "is the pyromancer really a valid class?"

## The classes as the guiding light (Nov 21)

The director, stepping back: "I feel like my rambling yesterday derailed this." What stands from it, in their words and still a sketch:

- **The web as the agent drew it stays:** "really powerful visually, and I think systemically."
- **The classes take the builds' place as the guide:** "instead of 8 builds, we use classes as our guiding light." They don't sit on the outside of a star. Each stands for a conception of what it does "that has yet been designed, but it isn't based on what their current kit does".
- **A guess at the form:** one to three stars on each of a few roles, to be defined, perhaps dealing damage, mitigating damage, and enabling or disabling. Then tags on each class "based on the way that they do those roles and the different words that they use. And then those become the scaffolding for the rest of the web."
- **What they can't yet see:** how the words for a class are decided beyond its theme, and how classes come to overlap now that the builds don't make them ("we gave the Bard shock specifically because it wanted to be a member of the shock build"). "Are we just going back to the drawing board for the sake of the drawing board?"
- **They asked to step back from actions** and untangle it first.

## Builds as ways to live and ways to kill (Nov 21)

A conversation the director opened by asking whether the builds, and their number, are right. Nothing in it is ruled.

- **How they're working:** "taking those builds and trying to put them at the edges of a map and trying to build out things that connect to each other and go in the direction of those builds."
- **An analysis of the builds can't rest on the effects:** it "build[s] the conceptualization off the items that already exist, aka the ones designed with those builds in mind". They want the game's mechanics analysed.
- **Surviving:** "doesn't that push us towards thinking that actually surviving needs to be in almost every build, so actually the builds need to be looked at differently?"
- **Ways to live aren't one to a party:** "You could totally have a shield and a dodge in the same party. And they can also be kind of matched with any way to kill."
- **How the builds came about:** combining ways to live with ways to kill, then pulling toward "an identity that feels like it has a cool fantasy". "We were looking for the most mechanically deep way to express the system as we have now, and we thought that rhythm would be one of the best ways to do it, in addition to status effect, unfortunately."
- **What they're asking for:** "a way to categorize these ways to live and ways to kill and their overlap cleanly enough to make a grid that lets players be creative and find unique combinations, but not build anything they want... There's a fine line. The builds prevent us from having pyro chill parties." The same distinction the builds' poles gave, "but with not just how you live, but how you live and how you damage as separate elements".
- **Set aside by them for now:** whether Weaken's and Shock's cuts should multiply each other.
- **On the agent's grid of four lines** (`report.md`, The builds question): "an interesting way of looking at it", and not decided: "let's still wait on deciding if it's the grid for now." They asked for a couple of example builds a player would use, placed on it.
- **Fire and frost:** "Frost and fire can be partners. I think that's fine."
- **Their read of the agent's four lines:** the ends are "non-optimal combinations", not opposites. Thorns against Evasion "is a very specific antagonism", not seen "between most other things in your model, like shield and evasion, or from max HP and power when low, or crits and attack riders".

### The director's own axes (Nov 21)

Offered "for the sake of argument", and "this might be a weaker conceptualization". Not a decision.

- **More macro:** "it's more about the party makeup than the mechanics a party uses, for example shield and dodge are just seen as two flavours of the same thing here."
- **Not a list of keywords:** "it's more of a subjective per-effect thing, for example shield are both a way to have a focused tank survive but also a way to have a spread team survive, it depends how it's applied. It seems like many words find themselves across a few axises." Certain axes are meant to work better with others.
- **Time is left out:** "most fights have the same duration according to simulations."
- **The party, defensive strategy.** Absorb: taunt, defense, shield, overheal, hidden, evasion, big narrow heals. Mitigate: stun, slow, weaken, guard, shock, silence, chill, small wide heals, blur.
- **The party, offensive strategy.** Carry: crit, high MP abilities, cooldown reduction, might, burn and shock detonators, shatter, ATK, marked, usually one carry. Attrition: burn, bleed, shock, haste, slow, on-hit effects, thorns, retaliation hits.
- **A unit, survival.** Take hits: shield, defense, thorns, on being hit effects. Avoid hits: evasion, blur, hidden, on dodge.
- **A unit, resources.** Giver: supports team resources, tanky, fixed cost actions, resupplies others. Taker: spends team resources, vulnerable, action cost (HP or MP) multiplier.
- **A unit, focus.** Singular: maximizing one stat, maximizing one status effect, multipliers, value from direct action. Spread: versatile moveset, both offensive and defensive, value from presence and status effects.

## The triage (`triage.md`)

Every effect that has been on gear so far, in three piles:
- **Great:** "genuinely compelling".
- **Fine:** "take it or leave it": they work, and not all of them need using.
- **Cut:** not listed, and not to be built from.
