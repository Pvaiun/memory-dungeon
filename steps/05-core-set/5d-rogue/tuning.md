# Step 5d tuning trail

One line a round. The simulated player is `src/rogue-ai.ts` (rules of thumb, no hidden information); `npm run rogue` plays whole runs with it (`src/rogue-sim.ts`), `npm run rogue:calibrate` sets each fight's own knob (`src/rogue-calibrate.ts`). Win rates are the simulated player's at heat 0, leaders Knight, Cleric and Ranger in turn. With 600 runs a rate is good to about ±2 points; 800, ±1.7.

**Targets, set before tuning:** the simulated player wins about a third of its runs (a careful human should do better, and heat is the way up); bosses end more runs than anything else; a regular fight wears the party down rather than ends the run; elites pay for their risk; every decision kind (route, draft, spoils, campfire, shop) is worth points of win rate when made well rather than at random.

1. **First build** (120 runs, first-guess knobs): 37% won. The Fire Mine loses 82% of the runs that enter it (the Crypt 13%).
2. **The trace** (300 runs): 28%. Attrition is weak: 77% of fights start with the party above 80% HP, a won fight costs 10 points of party HP, and deaths cluster in a few fights (the Fire Giant's three fights kill 58% to 86% of parties that meet them, the Powder Room 48%).
3. **Every fight its own knob** (`npm run rogue:calibrate`): snapshots of real parties before each fight, then each fight's knob bisected until it costs its tier's share of HP against those parties made whole (easy 12/14/16, regular 18/20/22, elite 32/34/36 by act) and a boss wins its share (12/20/28%). 100 fights in 50 s. Bomb Bugs can't be eased enough (three explode for a wipe, as their document says): no fight has more than two, and packs that explode never grow. Result: 13%, half the runs dead in act I. Measuring at full MP overrated the casters, who reach a boss short of mana.
4. **Regular fights measured with MP as it is, bosses against parties as they arrive:** 15%; regular fights and elites kill as many as bosses.
5. **Cheaper fights** (easy 10/11/12, regular 15/16/17, elite 28/30/32): 19%. Elites in acts II and III kill 9% to 15% of the parties that walk in above 80% HP.
6. **Classes, unbiased** (1,200 runs, the draft at random; then each class as leader with random companions, 240 runs each): as leader, the Vampire wins 37%, the Monk 20%, the Knight 13%, the Bramble knight and Valkyrie 12%, the Paladin 11%, the Pyromancer and Stormcaller 10%, the Geomancer 9%, the Ranger 8%, the Cleric, Hoplite and Assassin 7%, the Bard and Hexer 5%, the Frostweaver 4%. The weak ones die in act I (the Bard reaches act II in 32% of runs, the Vampire 84%).
7. **The starting ability** (each alternative, 240 runs): only the Vampire moves (Wither 19%, Frenzy 38%, Blood drain 37%). No alternative lifts a weak class by more than 2 points: their trouble is the class, not the start. **The Vampire starts with Wither.**
8. **Measured class values in the simulated player's draft, elites 25/26/27:** 32%. Bosses cause 56% of deaths, elites 19%, regular fights 26%.
9. **Each decision, priced** (600 runs each, that decision at random): route 19% (−13), spoils 11% (−21), campfire 10% (−22), shop 28% (−4), events 27% (−5), draft 15% (−17), which ability to learn 32% (0); no gear beyond the plain weapon, 0%. Avoiding elites 34% (+2), seeking them all 28%.
10. **What a reward is worth** (gifts at the start, 600 runs each): three legendaries +4, three rares +12, 150 gold +0. The legendaries are sidegrades by the core set's rule, and the player hoarded gold (spent 160 of 450). **A legendary piece carries a rare's two affixes; the player buys anything worth a gear step, best value for the gold first.** 38%; three legendaries +15, three rares +13, 150 gold +6; avoiding elites still +3.
11. **Elites 22/23/24:** 30% (re-calibration measures fights against the now stronger parties, so the targets, not party power, set the difficulty). Elites break even.
12. **An elite's trophy:** its legendary piece is won outright, on top of the pick (Slay the Spire's relic), and calibration on 300 runs with 60 parties a fight. 800 runs each: taking elites when healthy 36%, avoiding them 34%, seeking them all 35%.
13. **Mana, legendaries, swaps** (800 runs, 36%): the party starts a fight with 88% to 92% of its MP on average, below 40% in 1% of fights or fewer. MP doesn't bind because the simulated player avoids the casters (they're the weakest classes, round 6). Legendary pieces worn at the end, with their runs' win rates, spread from the Herald's horn (62%) and the Cleaving charm (63%) to the Glass heart and the Thirsting jewel (14%). About one swap a run.
14. **The calibration checked in whole runs:** act II bosses beat 4% to 17% of the parties that reach them (target 20%), act III bosses 20% to 47% (target 25%). Measuring a boss against 60 parties from every region of its act isn't precise enough.
15. **The run-level tuner** (`npm run rogue:tune`): each round plays 1,500 to 2,000 runs and moves each boss's knob toward its act's target share of arrivals beaten (12/20/25%) and each region's knob for its regular fights and elites toward its act's share of runs ended on the road (6/9/12%). At two knob points per point of loss it overshot (the Fire Giant's fights went 7%, 35%, 8%: Inferno grows with the knob); at one point, clamped to 15 a round, three rounds of 2,000 runs put every boss within 5 points of its target and every region within 3. **40%** won.
16. **The first two AI playtests** (fresh agents, the command line, player-visible information only; seeds 101–103 and 201–203): a thoughtful first-timer won 2 of 3, an experienced roguelite player 0 of 3. Both found the same holes: foes hidden until the party is locked (gear is a blind guess), deaths the summary doesn't explain, recruits who never reach the party (no campfire before floor 9 on many paths), level-ups with one option, legendaries that don't say what they do, and Monk or Vampire or "Knight wall plus Assassin carry" winning. Bugs: taking off a +MP piece and putting it back refilled MP (missing MP was clamped); the Old Master's HP cost never applied (it came after the choice); a Strange Chest's legendary arrived silently.
17. **The fixes** (all of them, `report.md`, section 2): foes shown before a fight with the party still open to change, nothing clamped, costs before choices, killing blows and damage by kind in the summary, a campfire mid-act on every path, two shops an act, no fight twice in an act, ordinary offers always wearable, legendaries' rules shown, level-ups that offer learning or upgrading, upgrades that say what they change, the Gambler at +10 gold expected, the Empty Camp searchable. Runs got easier (48% before correction); three tuner rounds: **43%**.
18. **Final measurements, first pass** (2,000 runs, then 1,000 a variant): 40% won. A random pick costs: the route 7 points, spoils and boons 22, the campfire 26, the shop 4, the draft 17; events and which ability to learn, nothing. No gear beyond the plain weapon, 0%. Elites neutral: 40% avoiding them, taking them when healthy, or seeking them all. Three legendaries at the start +15, three rares +11, 150 gold +6. Heat at 15 a level: 22%, 10%, 3% (heat 1, 2, 3): too steep. **All three abilities from the start: 63%** (+23): learning abilities is the run's biggest power curve. As leader with random companions: the Vampire 47%, the Monk 38%, the Knight 31%, the Valkyrie 28%, the Paladin 24%, the Bramble knight 23%, the Stormcaller 21%, the Ranger 18%, the Cleric and Frostweaver 17%, the Pyromancer and Geomancer 16%, the Assassin 15%, the Hoplite 14%, the Hexer 12%, the Bard 8%.
19. **Heat and elites:** heat at 5 a level gives 35%, 29% and 17% (heat 1, 2, 4), about 6 points a level. Elites paying 50 to 65 gold and a pick of rares (with the trophy): avoiding them 42%, taking them when healthy 46%, seeking every one 45%. **Both adopted.**
20. **Re-tuned** (two rounds of 2,000): 41%.

A random pick costs: the route 9 points, spoils and boons 25, the campfire 29, the shop 6, the draft 19; events and which ability to learn, nothing. No gear, 0%. Avoiding elites 41%, taking them when healthy 43%, seeking every one 44%. Three legendaries at the start +13, three rares +12, 150 gold +9. Heat 1, 2, 3, 5: 37%, 30%, 23%, 13%. All three abilities from the start: 64%. As leader with random companions: the Vampire 48%, the Monk 37%, the Knight 31%, the Valkyrie 28%, the Bramble knight 25%, the Ranger 23%, the Stormcaller 21%, the Paladin 20%, the Cleric 19%, the Assassin and Geomancer 17%, the Pyromancer and Frostweaver 15%, the Hoplite 14%, the Hexer 10%, the Bard 8%. 72% of regular fights and elites start with the party above 80% HP; below 40% they're lost 68% of the time.
22. **The weakest classes joining with two abilities** (a rogue-only lever, each as leader, 300 runs): the Bard 9% (8%), the Hexer 10% (10%), the Hoplite 18% (14%), the Frostweaver 17% (15%), the Pyromancer 18% (15%), the Assassin 19% (17%), the Geomancer 16% (17%), the Cleric 18% (19%). Within noise: their trouble is the class, not the start. **Not adopted**; the class pass is the lever.
23. **Pace** (the first playtests' "fights are over before the build shows"): regular fights lasted 21 to 25 ticks, and an ability in slot 1, 2 or 3 was cast 0.97, 0.67 and 0.44 times a fight in act I. A shape for regular fights, applied before their knob (foes +80% HP, −30% damage a hit), makes them longer and softer at the same cost: 37 ticks, 1.40, 1.23 and 0.73 casts. Easy and regular fights recalibrated, then two tuner rounds of 2,000 runs: **42%**. Bosses (70 to 120 ticks) and elites kept their shape.
24. **The second two AI playtests** (seeds 301–303 and 401–403, on the build before round 23): each won 1 of 3. Both named the same least-favourite moment: a run lost with healthy members at camp who couldn't step in (a knocked-out member, a fight on the only way up, no phoenix; or a wipe after a gear change). Both found gold piling up (576 and 378 unspent at the end of a won run: training was once each a visit) and numbers hidden (how big a Protect or a Backstab is, what an upgrade changes, the HP a piece takes with it when it comes off). Also: the boss's moves are only seen at the boss, after the last chance to swap; a status's killing blow named its holder ("doom from Valkyrie"); a win said nothing about each foe; the Pilgrims' text promised the fallen rising and gave a feather when nobody was down; no way to leave an unwanted ability out (Burning fist against a fire-absorbing giant). Stuns and Silence still lock bosses out of their big moves (the Storm King fell in 71 ticks without casting Rally).
25. **The second playtests' fixes**, each measured (2,000 runs): a fallen party member can trade places with one standing at camp any time between rooms (42% with it, 42% without: the simulated player rarely meets the case, and it's a fairness rule, not a balance one); a shop's training can be bought again in the same visit, 30 gold dearer each time (41% to 42%, 27 more gold spent a run); a recruit offer is three times as likely to be a class that could wear a legendary piece nobody in the roster can; an ability can be left out of fights. Shown now: each ability's size for the unit that has it, what an upgrade changes in its numbers, a foe ability's size before Armor (from its stats and the fight's knob), the boss and its moves from the map and at every campfire, how each foe fell, who laid a status that dealt a killing blow, the HP a piece takes with it. The Pilgrims' text says what they do. **The boss lock-out, measured** (`src/rogue-bosses.ts`, 300 runs): every boss casts its big move 1.1 to 1.8 times a fight; Silence costs it a cast 0.05 times a fight at most and stuns hold a wind-up 0.1 to 0.6 times. The lock-out the playtesters saw needs the Knight's Shield bash with the Assassin's Garrote, a pair the simulated player rarely drafts. No boss rule added; flagged in the report. **42%** won; no re-tune needed.
26. **The third two AI playtests** (seeds 501–503 and 601–603, on round 25's build): each won 1 of 3. Better: the boss shown from the start, the fork, the bench and the new `order` all made plans ("read 'absorbs fire, weak to ice / Inferno: very long cast', benched my best damage dealer, put Hush first: Inferno is lost"; "benching Burning fist against the fire-absorbing giant felt like real play"). Found: % heals counted from the max with a +HP piece worn just for the heal (REST and a tonic, then the piece back off: about 1,500 HP free); the same legendary twice in a run and the same tome twice in an offer; an elite's trophy for nobody in the party (Archmage's robes with no caster; a dagger for a bow Ranger); the fork's boss shown as "one of" three, so its resistances came after the pick; foe moves without who they aim at (Crush past a Taunt: it aims when its wind-up starts); command-line slips (space-separated `order` silently left abilities out, `seat` crashed bare, empty upgrade numbers, negative MP shown). Both called most legendaries "stat sticks with a small rider" again, and one called act I the most dangerous act and act III a stroll (a regular floor-7 fight of 5 snakes and 3 bandits ended a run). **Fixed:** heals by share count from the max at the last fight's end; no legendary twice in a run while others are left, no tome twice in an offer; trophies from pieces aimed at the party; each legendary names who it's for (new content: `aims`; a recruit leans toward a class a bagged piece is for); the fork shows each way's real boss and its moves; foe moves say who they aim at; a stat glossary; the command line's syntax, reasons for "not now", clamps.
27. **The legendary pass** (`npm run rogue:legendaries`: each piece handed over at the start without affixes and kept on the party member it suits best all run, 1,000 runs a piece on the same seeds as a run without it, counted on the seeds where it was worn). Handed over and left to the simulated player instead, most pieces were never worn, which measures nothing. **The zero point** is an ordinary piece locked the same way: a +HP amulet +5.2, a sword +7.2, medium armor +4.4, plate +2.6 (any piece from the start is worth something; robes, worn by whoever got them, −5.3). **Before:** of the 29 pieces this mode adds, only the Herald's horn (+16) beat an ordinary piece of its slot; the rest sat 1 to 25 points under it (the Gravecaller −18, the Shadow cowl −12, the Hunter's bow −11, the Static coil −10). The core set's 14 powerful pieces: the Twin-soul charm +5 and the Cornerstone axe +3; the rest −1 to −25 (the Mist claws −25, the Needle and Glass heart −18). **Two passes on the rogue mode's own rules** (bigger stacks and amounts, Haste for 40 ticks, a Shield for the Stone idol, a Bleed from the Hunter's bow and a Burn from the Cinder rod, Silence and Stun immunity for the Silver bell, HP for the Soul jar, Might for the Siren shell, a stat step on pieces with none): two-thirds of them now match an ordinary piece of their slot or beat it, and six beat it clearly (the Herald's horn +19, the War banner +8, the Grave lantern +7, the Frostfang +6, the Briar coat +6, the Soul jar +5, against a zero that fell to +0.4 to +3.6 as the pieces got stronger in the baseline's own runs). Six niche pieces are still 3 to 8 points under (the Siren shell, the Frost shard, the Shadow cowl, the Wound sigil, the Silver bell, the Earthshaker; the first four got an HP step after their last measure). The core set's pieces are left as they are (shared with the journey and free play): `report.md`, decision B. The simulated player's values for the rules rose to match. With the pass, runs got easier (the same seeds: 41% to 47% before re-tuning).

29. **The fourth two AI playtests** (seeds 701–703 and 801–803, on round 28's build): 2 of 3 and 1 of 3 won; both would play another run right away at 7 out of 10. They liked the boss forecast and the fork ("Inferno means bring Sanctuary"), the legendary hunting (the Hourglass "made the Stormcaller a monster", the Shadow cowl "made the Assassin untouchable", the Grave lantern and the Soul jar), and the readable deaths. Found: the bench's free REST and FOCUS as "a free heal battery" (the third player to say so), raising a member at camp stacking with it; the campfire's swap window closing with the pick (a run lost to it); a wipe with healthy members at camp (the fourth player to lose a run that way); act I hardest (the snake fights again), acts II and III easy once a carry is online; aggro at the floor (100, against a tank's 6,000 or more) as near-immunity, and the Paladin's Judgement against Taunting foes (core-set questions, `report.md`). **Fixed:** swaps open until a campfire's LEAVE; half a REST and half a FOCUS for the bench, none on top of a RAISE; the reserves (a lost fight with someone standing at camp passes the road to them: a regular fight or elite is passed with no spoils, a boss faced again); gambles and event gold noted; training too dear listed; a shield's cap stated. Each priced over 2,000 runs: none moves the simulated player's 41% (it rarely meets the cases). **The snakes, measured** (act I fights from 2,000 runs' traces): the Viper Den and the Snake Pit ended 8.6% and 6.7% of the act I runs meeting them and cost 26 and 14 points of HP when won; every other act I fight ended 2% or fewer. Their knobs were at the floor already: Bleed doesn't shrink with the knob, and the regular fights' shape gave snakes more HP and so more hits. With three snakes and no growth they still cost 17% and 22%; with two, 14% and 15% at knobs −42 and −57. Snake packs never grow now, anywhere.
