# Authored gaps: rulings and handoff

The reasoning behind this step is in `brief.md`; read that first. This page holds the director's rulings, how the request page works, and what the next session does. Nothing in the game has changed.

## Ruled by the director

1. **The same nine builds, with a better web.** The web is rebuilt from what the director marked Great, and new Greats come from their answers to the requests.
2. **Status effects don't stay plain as a rule.** A road may have Greats on its own status.
3. **No filling from the Fines.** They were the bare minimum acceptable: the pool isn't started with mediocre pieces. Some basic pieces will exist, later. A Fine comes in only where something needs it.
4. **A clean slate.** Today's pieces aren't the skeleton: "What's in the build now is not better than what is in the list."
5. **Requests come in batches of about fifteen, on a page the director types into,** never in the chat. Requests and conversation don't share a message.
6. **Formations are in the pass,** triaged the same way (`triage-formations.md`: 7 Great, 19 Fine, with the seats the director liked starred). Upgrades and mythics weren't triaged; the agent's recommendation (upgrades stay as part of the kits, mythics last and from scratch) hasn't been ruled on.

7. **No size on a request unless it needs one.** The director writes whatever they find most interesting, with no numbers; where a request needs a particular size, its ask says so. The agent sizes afterwards, and says plainly when an answer is too small or too big.
8. **Answers won't be colour-coded.** The director's answer to a road's request may not name that road's status, and may serve many roads. It's placed by what it does, not by the card that asked.

9. **How the agent asks,** after batch 1 (six of fifteen marked as the wrong question): a request names a job in the game's own terms and says what kind of thing is wanted back (an applier, a payoff, a seat, a link between two named things). No request for coverage: before asking, check that nothing the director kept already does the job. No abstractions in the ask.
10. **The agent's read of each answer goes on the page,** on its card, with a reply box where it asks something. The chat carries only the summary and the decisions.

11. **No Fines on a card** (after batch 2 went out with them). Most Fines won't be in the game, and the director's answer outranks any it overlaps, so there's nothing to steer around. "Near it" shows only Greats, starred seats, Great formations and the director's own placed answers. A card doesn't mention Fines at all: a job only a Fine does today is a job nothing in the web does, and the card says that. The director is never asked to replace one.

12. **The agent judges whether a mechanic is overused, and tells the director.** The director writes the pieces and doesn't manage the web, so they can use one trigger or mechanic too often without seeing it, which is how a player comes to take everything that shares a word. The judgement is the agent's, and the director is told, not asked.
    - **A count is kept in `web.md`** (The count): for every effect in the web, what sets it off, what it reads, and what it changes or moves, updated each time an answer is placed. The test is in `brief.md`, The count.
    - **An answer on an overused mechanic** gets a read that says so plainly: the idea is good, the web has too much on that mechanic, give me something else for the same job. It's marked as needing the director. The agent never moves their idea onto another mechanic: what replaces it is theirs. If they answer that they want it as written, that's the ruling and it's placed.
    - **A card may ask for its job to be done from a named underused mechanic,** in one plain sentence in the ask.
    - **How this fits ruling 9.** Ruling 9 is about whether to ask: a card needs a job the web lacks, and "nothing names this mechanic" is never that job. This ruling is about how to ask once there's a job: the card may name the underused mechanic it wants the job done from. An underused mechanic with no job waits in `web.md`, Seen, not yet asked, until one turns up.

13. **The agent builds the web; the director fills its gaps, or originates the sections it doesn't have.** The classes and the roads are fair things to ask from. The earlier loot is no part of it and no decision rests on it ("not relevant to this at all").
14. **The web grows by batches, from what it holds, and is what it ends up being.** The director: "this batch thing of building based on what we have is working really well". No skeleton of each road's and class's needs is drawn up first: the agent proposed one, the director asked whether it was a mistake, and the agent agreed it was (`brief.md`, Tried and dropped).
15. **The set's size is the target: "I'd like to reach 450 pieces of gear and 50 formations."** All loot, commons and plain pieces included. A plain number, not a share and not a gap count (the director: "a number quota and letting the web be what it ends up being"). It stops the agent calling the web done early, as it did at 81.
    - **Where the number comes from:** a finished run should have shown its player about a quarter of all the loot (`intent.md`, What it's for). The agent's rough count from the rogue mode's settings, not yet simulated: a run shows about 90 pieces, about 65 of them with an effect. That's about a fifth of 450, a little under the director's quarter, on the side they lean ("perhaps that number is low"). Measure it with whole runs at build.
    - **What it asks of every piece:** with a fifth of the loot shown, two named pieces meet in about one run in 25. A piece that needs one particular partner almost never works; a piece with many partners works every run, with a different one each time. That's the director's "as cross-build as possible", and the agent checks each answer for it (`brief.md`, Reading the answers).
    - **How many of the 450 are authored isn't set.** By the loot's rarity rules a common is stats and an uncommon a simple effect, so the authored pieces are mostly rares and legendaries.
    - **A batch carries three or four formation cards** (the director: "We can put 3 or 4 formations per batch if we want"). Each still needs a stated job; a batch with fewer real ones carries fewer.

16. **A card may name two builds' keywords.** The director, on Bleed on a Marked foe: "it should probably be allowed because that feels pretty limiting to making builds cross-compatible". The line in `docs/gear-design/builds.md` (The director's rulings) is rewritten, and the word "affliction" keeps its use for a card that reaches every status road. The agent's check stays: a card that needs both words already in the party has few partners, and is told so.
17. **An answer may change a class,** and the director says what goes. The Mark's answer changed two versions (`web.md`, Kit changes, for build). The kits stay as they are until build.
18. **Nothing in the loot touches the bench:** "I don't think players should meta-game the bench in terms of affecting combat."
19. **A card keeps its whole conversation.** The agent's later responses are added under the director's replies, never written over the first read.
20. **Cards in plain words.** The director found the cards' language cryptic, and had to ask what two of batch 4's meant. A card says what's wanted in its first sentence, and names an effect by what it does, never by the agent's nickname for it.
21. **The shape of the web comes first:** "the shape of the web is the paramount thing you need to be managing." The work is a catalog of effects that grows to the set's size, with good coverage across the builds and good links between them. Sizes, slots and other details of how a piece will be built are minor: a note beside the effect at most. The folder holds no history of the workflow.
22. **The agent doesn't predict how the director will answer.** No notes on their manner, and no reading of old answers to learn it. Their rulings are what an agent needs from them.

23. **The web is tracked by threads.** Every effect is written as what it wants and what it gives, in one fixed list of threads. Which effects link, which groups they fall into, what's thin and what's a group on one word are worked out from that. No effect is tagged with a build, and the map is a reading of what's placed: no number to reach for any build, class or pair (ruling 14 stands). The director, on the first pass: "exactly the type of thing I was hoping for", "largely this looks mostly correct".
24. **The classes are the guide, in place of the builds:** "instead of 8 builds, we use classes as our guiding light." They're the web's biggest pieces and the biggest choice a player makes, so they go into it first and gear is made around them, where until now the kits were fitted to the gear last. A class's theme stands. What it does is to be designed, and "it isn't based on what their current kit does": its upgrades, versions and each ability's exact effect are open, and the classes change in reaction to the web (ruling 17).
25. **A class is described by stars and words.** The director's sketch: one to three stars on each of a few roles, and tags for "the way that they do those roles and the different words that they use". The roles aren't defined yet; their guess is dealing damage, mitigating damage, and enabling or disabling. The words are threads from the one list the gear uses, each given or needed. "Those become the scaffolding for the rest of the web."
26. **Sixteen classes, for now:** "I do think the number of classes we have is correct for now." No turn to a few deep classes: it would be a full redesign, and a small set gets solved by its players. More classes may come later, each authored the way the gear is, with a small kit and a card that reads at a glance.
27. **What the director is chasing is discovery inside a run, in the looting** (`intent.md`, What the director is chasing): a choice made on what's in front of the player, an end they can't foresee, and skill that's judgement, never a recipe. A piece's worth should depend on the run it turns up in. Fights don't have to be interesting, "and never will be".
28. **Not every trio has to live.** The player's job is to build a party that lives, and the game's is that every run offers a way to one. A class's stars say what it brings, so the offers can see to it. Agreed in principle; designing it is the rogue mode's, in a later step (`docs/open.md`).
29. **A card is short, and never a list to proofread.** While how the work is done is being talked through, it's talked through in the chat and nothing is seeded.
30. **Nothing is built early to test the way of working.** "I think we should take a gamble on a successful strategy and see if it pays off. If it doesn't, that's fine. We do it again." The whole web, classes and effects, is carried through on paper and built once, at the end. The check on the way is on paper too (`brief.md`, The classes in the web).
31. **The classes are described in the web at the same time as the effects:** in the same catalog, in the same words, and after the classes are designed in the same batches.

**Leaning, not ruled:**
- **Far-apart classes or builds may share little.** It made sense to the director, who "could go either way". The agent flags a group on one word with nothing leading out of it, and a class with one way in; an empty pair is left alone unless a job turns up.
- **What becomes of the nine builds.** They're no longer the guide. The agent proposed keeping them as names for places where several classes overlap, to check the web can still produce them. One reason it gave, a rhythm to watch, is weaker now that fights don't have to be interesting. The director hasn't said.
- **When the versions and upgrades are reworked:** inside this pass, or after the gear.

## The builds question: measured, and proposed

The director asked whether the builds are right (`intent.md`, Builds as ways to live and ways to kill). What follows is the agent's, from the rules alone, on engine 0.30. Nothing here is ruled, and the next steps above wait on it.

**How it was measured.** Three plain bodies at the average class line: a plain sword, no abilities, no passive, no gear, Neutral. The rogue's own fights (the build test's set), each sized so the plain trio loses 40% of its HP. Then one thing switched on at a time, at the size the rules give it and held all fight, so every figure is a ceiling. The probes were scratch scripts and aren't kept.

**What each way of living buys** (the 40% fight then costs):

| Way to live | In the rules | Costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A second pool | one full Shield | 3% |
| Mending | Regen always / Lifesteal 20% | 2% / 26% |
| Their hits miss | Evasion 35 / 60 | 25% / 15% |
| Their hits are smaller | Weaken / Guard | 18% / 31% |
| They act less | Slow / Chill held at 6 / one foe always Stunned | 28% / 32% / 29% |
| They die sooner | Attack +50% / the Mark always on the lowest foe | 26% / 30% |

- Layers multiply: two halve the cost again, three leave about 8%.
- Taunt alone does nothing (42%). It makes one unit's layer cover the party: Regen on one unit 27%, on one that Taunts 10%.
- Weaken's and Shock's cuts add to each other: both held, with Shock at its cap, 2%. Set aside by the director for now.

**Which ways to live starve which ways to kill.** Each way to kill fed to every foe at a fixed rate; regular fights and elites only. 100 is the share of the killing it did with no way to live.

| | Shield | Regen | Weaken | Evasion 60 | Slow | Stun | The Mark | Hits +50% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Burn | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 98 | 100 | 95 | 76 |
| Shock | 100 | 101 | 100 | 99 | 89 | 75 | 90 | 73 |
| Bleed | 100 | 102 | 100 | 100 | 96 | 100 | 104 | 42 |
| Chill (its shatters) | 100 | 106 | 101 | 97 | 90 | 91 | 74 | 20 |
| Thorns on one who Taunts | 102 | 101 | 103 | 54 | 86 | 76 | 88 | 91 |

- Bigger hits take a bigger share by themselves; allowing for that, the last column costs Burn and Shock about a tenth, Bleed nearly half and Chill three quarters.
- Shield, Regen and Weaken starve nothing, so nothing argues against taking them.
- Fire and frost don't oppose each other: both want the fight long.

**The same five ways to live, by how long the fight runs** (points of the party's HP saved a fight; the plain trio's hits made half again as big, or a third smaller):

| | Fast: 18 ticks, the fight costs 23% | Slow: 48 ticks, the fight costs 78% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Shield of 25% of max HP at the start | 16 | 20 |
| Regen kept up on everyone | 14 | 49 |
| Evasion 35 | 8 | 24 |
| Lifesteal 20% | 5 | 6 |

- A Shield and Lifesteal are fixed amounts a fight; Evasion and kept-up Regen grow with its length. So the order of the same options changes with the party.
- The same damage in swings half the size took 45% longer, by the Defense rule, and Burn carried on swings did 55% of the killing against 26% on plain swings. The heavy end couldn't be sized: in fights turned down this far a heavy swing kills a foe outright.

**The agent's view, not ruled.** It first proposed four lines of opposed ends (time, the foes' attacks taken or slipped, HP kept or spent, a unit's actions). The director's read was right: only time and Thorns against Evasion bite, and Shield against dodge is two flavours of one thing. Their own axes (`intent.md`) brought the part that was missing: who is hit. What the rules hold up is three facts about a party, each a quantity it has one value of, and each changing what a piece is worth:
- **How long its fights run.** It sorts a fixed amount a fight from a rate or a share (the table above).
- **Where the foes' hits land,** on one unit or on all three. It sorts what sits on one unit from what sits on everyone (Regen on one unit, 27%; on one that Taunts, 10%).
- **How big its swings are.** It sorts what pays by the hit from what pays by the blow. Measured in direction only.

Nothing here makes two things work against each other; layers still multiply. What it gives is the order of a party's options changing as its picks move those three, which is a thought at each pick only while slots are short. Read off an effect's own definition, with no keyword: when it pays, who it lands on, and whether it's a fixed amount or a share.

**The director, on it:**
- **Ruled: fire and frost can be partners.**
- **Whether this is the grid waits.** They asked for example parties placed on it first.
- **Say it plainly.** The overlap table's scale lost them, and so did "what does each use up that something else wants". The table's number is how much of its job a way of killing still does beside a way of living. The question about Shield, mending and Weaken is whether to give a player a reason not to always take them; it isn't about making them exclude each other.

## The page

`https://claude.ai/artifact/EBRC2rs3Tw6JW2AWMgxZDf`. **The page is built: use it as it stands, and never make it again.** A session's whole flow goes through the page's store, with the ArtifactData tool on that address:

- **Seed a batch:** write its cards to `requests/<id>` (`b5-01` and on, `b5-open`) with the next batch number, in one batch write. Fields: batch, order (the open card is 99), kind, title, ask, why, near (a list of `{t, tag}`). The page shows them by itself, and redraws a card whose request changes.
- **Read the answers:** query `answers` for the batch, once the director says they're done. Fields: text, status, reply, more.
- **Write a read:** set `replies/<id>` to `{kind, text}`. Kind is placed, question, overused, withdrawn or again; question and overused show under Needs you.
- **Answer a reply:** append to `replies/<id>.turns` (a list of strings) and set `kind` to where the card now stands. Send the whole list back: an update replaces the field. Never rewrite `text`. The director's first reply is `answers/<id>.reply`; their reply to turn *n* is `answers/<id>.more[n-1]`. A reply is unanswered when `reply` is set and there are no turns, or `more` is as long as `turns`.
- **A request's kind** is road, link, capstone, supply, function, pair, formation or open. **A near line's tag** is great, seat (a seat the director starred), gform (a Great formation) or yours (an effect they wrote in an earlier batch). No Fines.

None of this needs the page's source read or anything republished. Where the director asks for a change to the page, edit `requests-page.html` in place and republish it to the same address; don't write a new page.
