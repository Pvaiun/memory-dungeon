# Batch 3: the director's answers and the agent's read

Copied from the page's store (Nov 19). Each card: what was asked, the director's answer, their reply to the agent's read where they gave one, and the agent's read as it stands after that reply. Where a reply changed the agent's read, the first read is in git. Two reads still ask the director something (the stacks piece, and the formation of wide and narrow). The open card wasn't answered.

## The Hunt: where to start with the Mark

**Asked:** I don't know where to start with the Mark, and I'd like you to. Tell me what the Mark should be in the loot: where a party gets one, and what a Marked foe gives the party besides its aim. Write as much or as little as you like. I'll turn it into pieces and bring back what I'm unsure of.

**The director (answered):**

> Abilities:
> Assassin's Vanish version A (Smoke Screen), replaced with: Ambush - Goes Hidden, mark the enemy with the lowest HP (current total, not % or Max HP)
>
> Bard's War Ballad version A (Lament), replaced with: March - Might on every ally,  mark a random enemy
>
> Gear:
> Hits add bleed to marked enemies (scaling from attack)
> Hits on a marked enemy also spread to another enemy
> When an enemy is stunned, and no other enemy is marked, mark it

**The director's reply to the read:**

> So I'm not sure I remember writing that rule about multiple build's words on one card. I think now that I'm designing them it should probably be allowed because that feels pretty limiting to making builds cross-compatible but challenge me if I misunderstanding something.
>
> And yes March should only apply if there's no marked unit
>
> I think if there's any one class that isn't a support it's the Assassin and so I'd like to preserve their ability to deal big damage from hidden but then also allow them to start chaining kills from ambush

**The agent's read (Placed):** Settled, all of it.

Two builds' words on one card: allowed, and I won't challenge it. The line was in your builds rulings ("No card names two builds' keywords"), and I've rewritten it. It was doing a second job, which I'll keep doing by count: a card that needs both words already in the party meets in few runs, and I'll say so when one comes up. Bleed on a Marked foe isn't that. It needs only a Mark, and brings the Bleed.

The three gear effects stand as placed: Bleed on a Marked foe, hits on a Marked foe spreading to another, and a Stunned foe being Marked.

March: Marks a random foe only while no foe is Marked, so the Bard's Mark stays until its foe falls.

Ambush: in Smoke screen's place, as you wrote it. The Assassin keeps Shadow step's big hit from hiding, and Ambush starts the chain: it hides, Marks the foe with the least HP, and the party follows.

What that costs, for when the kits change at build: nothing in the kits will hide another unit. Your Hidden from Guard becomes the way to hide someone else.

## The Hunt: a fight with one foe

**Asked:** I need something for a party built on kills in a fight with one foe. A payoff.

**The director (answered):**

> Explicit payoff for a single foe (they spend more time at lower HP):
>
> Damage done to an enemy increased by X% for each % of HP they are missing.
>
> We could also find a way to trigger foe falls effects without a foe falling.

**The director's reply to the read:**

> When a foe is silenced trigger the party's on foe fallen effects

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed, both.

More damage for each share of HP the target is missing: as I read it before.

A Silenced foe counts as a foe falling: when a foe is Silenced, the party's "when a foe falls" effects happen. It's the opening's second way to repeat, and it works in a fight with one foe.

It limits itself. A Silence can't land on a foe that's already Silenced, and a boss shrugs Silence off after the first few, so on a boss it fires a handful of times a fight. On a real kill only the kill counts, so nothing happens twice.

What I've assumed: it sets off effects worded "when a foe falls", not "on a kill", since nobody landed a killing blow. And an effect that moves something off the fallen foe, like the Torch passing on its Burn, does nothing, since the foe still stands.

Two things to know. It works only beside an Assassin or a Hexer, the two classes that Silence, so most runs that find it can't use it. Silence from gear would widen it, and I'll bring that as a card. And it adds to the fight's start, the most crowded word in the web: ten effects now. I've placed it because we left room for it and it's bounded.

## A way to hide

**Asked:** I need a way for a unit that isn't an Assassin to become Hidden during a fight. A piece or a seat.

**The director (answered):**

> This unit becomes hidden any time it receives Guard

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed. A unit that gains Guard becomes Hidden. It gives Guard a job, which nothing in the web did.

What it does beside the rest: a Knight's Fortify lands on the ally with the lowest HP, so the hurt unit vanishes, and with your mana while Hidden a hurt caster comes back refilled. With Twins, one Guard hides two, and the rule that someone stays in the light keeps the third out.

On a wall it works against its wearer: a Hidden wall draws nothing, and a Taunt ends Hidden.

It has few partners today: in the kits, the Knight's Fortify and two versions. It needs Guard from gear to work in most runs. Guard is a staple, so the plain parts will carry it, and this piece is the reason some of them will.

## The wind-up

**Asked:** I need a second thing that happens while a unit is in a wind-up: something for the caster or its allies, not a status on the foes.

**The director (answered):**

> Every tick of a wind-up regens other allies' MP by 5% - caster support, general team sustain
>
> or
>
> Every tick of a wind-up gives this unit 1 might - nukers nuke more with their big spells

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed: Might on every tick of a wind-up. Left out: the mana one.

Why Might: it has more partners. Your Great that doesn't spend Might on swings keeps what the cast built. Twins and the Great that shares a status with the lowest ally pass each tick's Might on. And with your Chill from statuses laid on its own side, every tick of a long cast now lays Shock on every foe, Might on the caster and Chill on the coldest foe.

Why not mana: MP is already the most-named thing in the web, with ten effects. This one would also make the unit with the longest cast the party's battery, which is Barrage turned round. Say if you want it anyway.

Might is spent one stack an action, so the spell that wound up uses one and the rest carry to the next action. I'll size the Might a tick so one long cast doesn't fill the cap.

## A count that grows

**Asked:** I need a third thing that counts up over a fight and gets stronger as it goes. Not casts and not dodges: Crescendo and Heat already count those.

**The director (answered):**

> Gain 3 heat on every crit. - Tell me if the crit trigger is overused here, but crit is something that fits thematically with the speed/dodge theme of heat.
>
> Also, let's change heat so that it loses 50% of its HP on losing health rather than being hit, so shields and aegis are a way to preserve heat.

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed: 3 Heat on a crit. And Heat's rule is changed as you say: it loses 50% of its Heat when its holder loses HP, not when it's hit.

You asked about crits. Not overused: with this and the crit formation, five things in the web name a crit, and the line is about eight. All five add up on one unit that crits, so crits go on watch and I won't ask from them next batch.

What the new rule does: a Shield or an Aegis keeps Heat. So does your Great that takes damage on MP in place of HP, while its mana holds. A status's damage now costs Heat, where before only hits did. I'm reading a price paid in HP as not costing Heat.

One thing to know: it's one more reason to hold a Shield, which is the most crowded thing in the web. I've placed it because it changes a rule and adds no Shield piece.

I'm reading "on a crit" as once a crit, not 4 times with your crit piece. Otherwise one crit fills Heat to its cap.

## The Monk and the Hoplite

**Asked:** I need a piece a Monk and a Hoplite would both want, each for its own reason. They share the spear and the cloak. I'd like it to be a Sunder piece: nothing in the web names Sunder.

**The director (answered):**

> Gain 5% double strike per stack of sunder on enemies. - the goal is to turn the monk into a double strike engine and the hoplite's basic attacks into a devastating force, but it might be too lock and key to name sunder directly like this

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed. Double strike for each stack of Sunder on the foes: the first thing in the web that names Sunder.

It isn't lock and key. Five classes can lay Sunder: the Monk, the Hoplite and the Ranger from their base kits, the Hexer and the Stormcaller from a version. So it's a set-up a party makes, not one class's piece.

What it does: the Monk feeds itself, since its second strikes add Sunder too. The Hoplite's shove Sunders every foe at once, so in a pack its slow double swings start landing twice. It counts every foe's stacks, so it's biggest in a pack, and on a lone boss it stops at Sunder's own cap.

With your two extra-strike pieces it chains: more second strikes, each rolling again and each carrying what the swing carries. I'll size a stack so a fully Sundered pack is near a sure second strike and no more.

**The director (answered):**

> Convert 50% of your Defense into Attack - probably better as a horn
>

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed as a piece. The pair is withdrawn: the problem was my ask, not your answer. With Shields and dodges set aside the two share too little. I'll bring them back when the web is fuller, as you offer.

Half its Defense becomes Attack. It doesn't join these two: the Ranger has little Defense to turn, and the Knight's trunk doesn't use Attack. The ones who want it are the walls that swing, the Hoplite most, and it holds horns too.

It sits well in the web. Your amulet that gives Defense for wearing no armor now gives Attack as well. A Bearer under the Bannerman turns the Banner's Defense into more Attack. A wall that wears it can become the top-Attack ally that two of your Greats read. It pulls against abilities from Defense.

Card 10's answer pays the same price, half its Defense. Worn together, a unit keeps a quarter of its Defense and hits harder and faster. I think that's a build someone finds, not a problem.

The narrowing is yours to choose. Tell me that one, or another.

## A formation about who acts often

**Asked:** I need a formation about time: how often each seat acts is what the seats trade. It should change how the party fights, not add to it.

**The director (answered):**

> Formation: If holding a 2h weapon, double attack and halve attack speed, if holding a 1h weapon, halve attack and double attack speed
> Seat 1,2,3: just stats
>
> Tell me it you want something less catch-all, or if you want me to make two different ones.

**The director's reply to the read:**

> What's your suggestion?

**The agent's read (Placed):** Placed, the way I'd suggest, since you asked: the trade touches basic attacks only. With a two-handed weapon, basic attacks deal ×2 and come half as often. With a one-handed weapon, they deal half and come twice as often. Attack itself doesn't change, so abilities are untouched.

Why: nobody gets something for nothing, and it's the trade of time the card asked for. Damage comes out even, so what changes is everything counted by the swing. One hand: twice as many statuses from swings, on-hit triggers and crits. Two hands: each blow gets through Defense better and gets more out of each stack of Might.

So a Landslide unit picks two hands and a Thousand Cuts unit picks one, in the same formation. The two-hand side is the Hoplite's own passive, so a Hoplite here swings a quarter as often for four times the blow.

What it gives up: casters get nothing from it. It's a formation for a party that swings. Tell me if you'd rather keep it on Attack.

## A formation about wide and narrow

**Asked:** I need a formation about how many units an action lands on: abilities that reach every foe or every ally, against those that land on one. It should change how the party fights, not add to it.

**The director (answered):**

> Seat 1: all single-target actions target every member (party or enemy) instead, at 75% power (healing, damage, stacks)
> Seat 2: all multi-target actions target a single target instead, at 2x power (healing, damage, stacks)
> Seat 3: none

**The director's reply to the read:**

> Seat 1: all self-targeted actions target the whole party
> Seat 2: multi-target actions multiply their effectiveness and target a single unit (the first random target)
> Seat 3: none
>
> Is this better? Don't say yes if it isn't

**The agent's read (Open, with a question):** No, I don't think it's better, and that's my fault: my warning was overstated.

What the new version fixes: the first seat no longer turns on how many foes there are, and the outsized basic attack is gone.

What it loses: the first seat is now Twins for three. "What it gives itself, the party gains" is the rule of Twins and of your Great that shares a status with the lowest ally, so this would be the third. Few abilities target only their caster: of the twelve kits I've read, four have one. So the seat does nothing for most units. And everything that placing the first version found was on the foe side: the pile gathering a widened status, Shock from every ability, a Monk Sundering the whole pack.

Why my warning was overstated: in your first version the two seats pull opposite ways in the same fight. In a pack the widened unit is ahead and the narrowed one behind. On a lone foe it's the reverse. So the formation as a whole has no good fight and no bad one. What it does is turn a single-target unit into a wide one and a wide unit into a single-target one, and that's about what the party is, not the next foe.

My suggestion: keep the first version, with the second seat's target as you now give it (the first random target, so a Mark takes it), and the first seat's share sized by me well under 75%. Which do you want?
