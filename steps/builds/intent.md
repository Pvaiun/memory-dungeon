# Intent: the builds, redesigned

The director's read of the twelve builds after about fifty full rogue runs, recorded Nov 9, 2026. The method that made them stands; what it produced mostly didn't.

**Keep the method.** "The philosophy was excellent. Do not change the way that we ideated builds. It's just the reality of what they actually produced that didn't work." The pairs, the rhythm space, threads, routes, rungs and the web stay the way builds are ideated (`docs/gear-design/philosophy.md`, How the loot is made).

## Their read, build by build

1. **Landslide: worked.** "Big shields, big hits, big crits, it worked." They weren't sure they ever knew they were playing a named build; the idea made sense on its own.
2. **Thousand Cuts: seen, never came together.** The elements were there and they knew what they were meant to see. "Even at 75 or 85% evasion, my tank just died," and the build went with it.
3. **Bonfire: the fire showed occasionally;** the Pyromancer was too squishy for the build to happen. They assume the class step fixed that.
4. **Cold Snap: the same,** with the Frostweaver. "I could never have it happen."
5. **The Hunt: Marking is valuable,** and mattered maybe once in fifty runs. Two reasons: Marking was rare, and it rarely marked a foe that mattered.
6. **Harvest: didn't exist.** "This was 100% not a real build." Not seen from the gear, not perceived, not experienced.
7. **Ambush: hinted at by the gear,** but they wouldn't have made the connection without having read the build. "The enemies are so tanky that the idea of nuking someone instantly just doesn't really work in this game. I don't think it's a good design."
8. **Overflow: the fantasy is viable; the implementation was "completely underwhelming and useless."** The formations were the only thing in the game that spoke to it (the well, seats where "the next person has this"), and taking one "was kind of like agreeing to try some stupid gimmick that you knew wouldn't work." Esoteric, gimmicky, irrelevant.
9. **Lockdown: works, but "it isn't an identity."** One unit's thing against certain enemies, not a build, and never a full lock on a whole enemy party.
10. **The Toll: the same.** Clusters are very susceptible, some units are very strong at it, "but it wasn't a party build. It was just a single tank that was basically having thorns."
11. **Blood Pact: seen in the gear, non-viable in play.** "Every single time I tried to use it, I just died. It never once worked."
12. **Phoenix: non-viable.** Once in fifty runs a unit fell with the benefit, and it was "absolutely not worth it. Death is devastating, the metagame of our build. Simply a non-viable playstyle."

## Open with the director

Questions the design can't start without; the findings behind them are in the session's report to the director.

- Whether the count stays at twelve in six pairs when Ambush and Phoenix go as concepts, and whether the set-aside builds (wither, radiance, gambler) are the pool to refill from.
- Whether Lockdown and the Toll are redesigned as party builds or demoted to threads in other builds.
- Whether Blood Pact keeps its fantasy with the implementation redone, as Overflow does, or goes with Phoenix.
- Whether an Evasion tank is a thing the game should have, given the rules (status damage never meets Evasion) and the Evasion cap.
- Whether the Pyromancer's body stays glass (its stat line didn't change in the class step) and the build gives it a keeper, or the line changes.
