## 1. Rules

### Rarity: what each tier is

| Rarity | What it is | Stats | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common** | Fills a slot and carries a build's first stats. | Two steps, leaning by kind. A special stat at medium is a step. | None. |
| **Uncommon** | A build's first rung: a small effect most runs find. | One step. | One small, general effect on a thread (a rider, a start-of-fight status, a small reaction). No condition on a class. Each one helps two to four builds a little. |
| **Rare** | A build's second rung: specific, often conditional. | None, or one step where the effect alone is thin. | One designed effect, worth a lot to one or two builds and little elsewhere. |
| **Legendary** | A build's far end, or an exotic: it bends a rule. | Only where the shape needs them. | A rule change, a new trigger or a payoff that grows. Strong where it fits; where it's strong in many places, it gives something up. |
| **Mythic** | Rewrites one class, past any one ability. | As its shape needs. | One per class (section 8). |

- **Rarity is specificity, not size.** A rare can be bigger than a legendary for the build it serves. Power by act comes from the offers (later acts roll more rares and legendaries, as today), not from per-act versions of a piece.
- **No random affixes,** at any rarity. Every piece is fixed. Tempering (the Master Smith) turns a piece into a piece of the next rarity in the same kind, which fits the same wearer.
- **No piece says what it's for.** The "for" column of this spine is the designer's, and stays inside (the build step can use it to aim drops, as `aims` does today).

### The stock

| Slot | A kind holds | Common | Uncommon | Rare | Legendary | In all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon (11 kinds) | 8 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 88 |
| Armor (4 types) | 12 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 48 |
| Offhand (10 kinds) | 8 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 80 |
| Amulet | 24 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 24 |
| **All** | | **60** | **60** | **60** | **60** | **240** |

Plus the 11 plain weapons, which stay the starters and the cheapest drops outside the count, and the 16 mythics.

- **Why even quarters.** Offers already weight rarity (a regular fight's pick rolls 35% common, 32% uncommon, 27% rare, 6% legendary). The stock's split decides how often the same piece comes back. Sixty legendaries means a run sees about 24 of them, under half, and two runs toward one build get there through different pieces: the philosophy's "chaos". Sixty commons are enough, since a common is only stats.
- **Two-handers** (bow, great weapon, staff) are worth a weapon and an offhand: their commons carry three steps; their rares and legendaries pair two effects or carry a bigger one.

### Numbers: the ranges for the fill

*At gate 2 (Oct 30) the director ruled out balancing rules in this step: these are the first values to write, not limits. Statuses are universal: no piece applies one for less, or more, than its own length.*

Today's ranges, so balance mostly holds.
- **Stat steps** as gear has them: +1 or +2 of a stat, half a point of HP, ±10 Aggro, a special stat medium (high only on a legendary).
- **Stacks** in the classes' words: a few, several, many, read off the applier's stat the way a kit's are. A rider on every hit is "a few" or less.
- **Damage boosts:** a small one 10 to 20%, a narrow one 30 to 50%, "double" only on a condition a fight meets a few times.
- **Statuses keep their own numbers.** A piece decides who gets a status and when, never what it does: the director's rule (a status scales by its stacks, never its applier) holds everywhere.
- **Cooldown changes:** a few ticks (2 to 6), or "ready", only on a trigger a fight meets a few times.
- **Counters that grow over a fight** (hits landed, Heat, Spite) are capped or grow slowly enough that a 40-tick fight and an 80-tick fight differ by about half.

An uncommon never carries a rare's effect at a smaller size in the same kind: the best choices are between builds, not between a small and a big version of one thing.

### New statuses and words

- **Marked** (a debuff on a foe): the party's basic attacks pick a Marked foe while it lives. Its abilities still follow their own targeting. A short duration, Refresh. It's the Hunt's focus, and the only new word a player learns in this pass.
- **Counters,** shown as an icon with a number on the holder, kept for the fight: **Cuts** (hits landed), **Heat** (power that builds), **Spite** (Thorns that grow), **Tempo** (speed that builds), **Surge** (many-foe hits), **Fervor** (falls and returns), **Charge** (hits saved for one blow, then spent). Each is one status of its own with one fixed meaning, so they don't stack into each other.
- Everything else uses today's statuses and stats.

### Engine additions

`builds.md` listed seven; the pieces need these, each with its first users:
1. **Changing a cooldown or an attack timer:** refund some ticks, or make ready (Hunting spear, Still hand, Bell of the hour, Pack leader's coat; the Hunt, Lockdown, Overflow, Ambush).
2. **Marked:** a debuff that makes a foe the pick of the party's basic attacks (Marking knife, Huntsman's bow; the Hunt).
3. **Changing an MP cost** (a stat: costs × (1 + stat/100); the Weaver's robes, Annotated grimoire, Archmage's mantle, Brimming rod; Overflow, Phoenix).
4. **HP as a cost:** an ability paid in HP, in full or in part (Blood vial, Blood covenant; Pact).
5. **Reading the other unit of an event,** such as a dying foe's Burn, to pass it on (Wick, Ember heart; Bonfire).
6. **The count of foes an action hit** (Stormweave, Supercell; Harvest).
7. **A heal given,** the healer's side (Saint's knucklebone; Pact).
8. **New: overhealing,** the part of a heal past full (Phylactery; Pact).
9. **New: a reaction that strikes,** an extra strike fired by an event rather than a basic attack (Duelist's leathers, Pike wall; the Toll).
10. **New: extra strikes that chain,** a bend of "extra strikes don't chain", on one legendary only (Flurry wraps).
11. **New: taking a hit for an ally** (Oathbound shield; the Toll, Bonfire).
12. **New: after a won fight,** a moment pieces can react to before statuses end, for returns between fights (Lamp of vigil, Last bastion; Phoenix).
13. **New: a seat that reads who sits in it** (a class, a weapon kind, an armor type, a kind of ability), for formations.
14. **New: gear that changes its wearer's own abilities** by what they do, never by name: their cost (Weaver's robes), their stacks (Banked staff), their cooldowns (Bell of the hour). Shares its parts with 1 and 3.
15. **Small: "has any of"** as a condition (a foe Stunned or Silenced or Weakened).
16. **Small: Shields received,** a stat beside healing received (Earthmother totem).

17. **Small, a rule: Taunt ends Hidden.** A unit that gains Taunt stops being Hidden, and Hidden doesn't land on a Taunting unit. Today no unit can be both by content alone; with Taunt and Hidden on gear, versions and seats, a rule is simpler than checking every pair.

**Aegis stays the Hoplite's signature:** no piece or version gives it to anyone else.

## 7. Upgrades

Every ability and passive, the 64 of them. Each line: the **power step** (plain, more of the same), then version **A** and version **B**, each with the builds it pulls toward. The two versions are a choice; the power step is separate.

**How it's trained (my assumption, to confirm):** the power step and the version are two separate trainings, in either order, so an ability goes from its base to a version, to its power step, or to both. A version stands on its own against the base, and the power step lands on whichever version is taken. A fully trained class is eight trainings; no run trains everyone fully, which is the choice.

Written after sections 2 to 6 and against them: no version copies a piece. Where a version and a piece share a thread, they do it differently.

## 11. What I decided, and what I'm least sure of

**Decisions you might not expect** (each is mine to change at your word):
1. **The stock is even quarters** (60 of each rarity), with the plain weapons outside the 240. Offers keep weighting rarity, so a run sees under half the legendaries.
2. **Commons carry no effect,** only stats. Uncommons are where the first rungs live. Your "only legendaries are interesting" is answered by uncommons and rares, not commons.
3. **An upgrade is two trainings:** the power step and the version, in either order. A fully trained class is eight trainings, about twice what a run gives today; the build step sizes the training a run offers.
4. **One new word, Marked,** and seven counters (Cuts, Heat, Spite, Tempo, Surge, Fervor, and the Storm pike's Charge), each its own status. Fewer are possible: Heat and Surge could be one, and Charge could be Cuts spent.
5. **32 formations and Neutral** (today 10): 11 common, 12 rare, 9 legendary.
6. **Three rules bent, each by one or two pieces:** extra strikes that chain (Flurry wraps), a hit taken for an ally (Oathbound shield), effects after a won fight (Lamp of vigil, Last bastion, The undying). **One rule added:** Taunt ends Hidden (engine 17).
7. **No piece grants an ability.** Effects are reactions, pulses, riders and rule changes, so "more abilities per class" stays out of this step.

**Least sure:**
1. **Overflow** is still the most engine-dependent build (cost changes, an ally's cast as a trigger), and has no amulet of its own. If a link reads as bookkeeping in the fight log, it fails your "relay is too complicated".
2. **Ambush's tail.** Its second rung has four pieces, and what keeps a spent volley from being all or nothing is mostly the Rear guard seat; Ready stance and The ambuscade make the tail weaker on purpose. It may still be all or nothing.
3. **The dodge that pays lives only on Leather** (Duelist's leathers, Windrunner's garb). The Toll's dancer is a Leather class (the Monk), so it works, but no Mail or Plate unit can dance.
4. **Two-handers** are written to be worth a weapon and an offhand (bigger commons, paired effects). Whether the Volley bow or Cataclysm staff is worth giving up a lantern or tome is a test, not a design.
5. **Phoenix's far end is crowded** (ten pieces) and its second rung thin (four). Several returns stacked on one unit (Phoenix ember, Vial of the last breath, Shield of the chosen) could make a party that can't lose its first member.
6. **Harvest gets the most versions** (26, from 12 classes), because "spread it" is the natural other side of many single-target abilities.
7. **Ambush and Pact have no mythic of their own.** The Paladin's went to Lockdown after the fresh read (its first draft repeated a mace).
8. **Exotics are five,** half the one in ten I'd aim for. The fill can add one per offhand kind if you want more chaos.
