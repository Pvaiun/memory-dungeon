# Design: the builds

The design stage of the builds pass, at the director's gate for the third time. The intent is `intent.md`; the evidence is `state-of-play.md`. It's written the way `docs/gear-design/philosophy.md` asks for a builds stage: each build as a way to play, with its rhythm, threads, routes, rungs and needs, and the web between them. No pieces, and no numbers but the measurements under *Who stands in front*. Each build also says what the intent asks of it: how a player finds it at each phase of a run, how it stays alive, and what it asks of the kits.

Nothing here is built. Approved, it replaces `docs/gear-design/builds.md`, and the loot's spine is written from it.

## For the director

### Your questions

**Is an engine a crossing, or a stopping point on a road?** Mostly a stopping point, and you caught a real fault. Your shield party never touched the Toll: you were on one road, stacking Shields for safety, and one rule twisted it. I'd written that every engine must cross two mechanics and be reached from two builds. That test was mine, not your shield party's, and I built the nineteen from a grid of build pairs to pass it. That's why they read as "what if we combined these two".

**So what is one?** Every mechanic has moments that do nothing: a Shield breaking, a fire draining away, a wound closing, mana poured into a full pool, a blow that kills with damage to spare. An engine gives one of those moments a verb. That's what happened to your Shields, and it's why it felt found: it had been happening all along. This set is built from those moments, road by road: 24 sit on a road, 5 join two.

**Do engines replace the journey between builds?** No. They add to it, and I was wrong to drop the doors. A run still drifts from road to road through the common things both want. Most engines are where one road turns into something specific; a few are bridges. The doors are back, with the bridges marked on them.

**It's still a lens,** and a lens makes things look alike. The test of an engine is whether you'd be glad to find it, which is yours to judge. I've marked the eleven I'd fight for.

### Shock and Bleed: my call

**All four damaging statuses have a road.** A status is the sign a player can read on floor one, play showed that, and having two of the four without one read as an omission from your chair and would from a player's. What stops them being one build in four colours is that each is set off by a different clock:

| Road | Its status | The clock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bonfire | Burn | the party's feeding: it dies unless it's kept up |
| Cold Snap | Chill | its own count: it builds until it breaks |
| Thousand Cuts | Bleed | the party's hits: opened once, cashed by every hit after |
| The Storm | Shock | the foes' actions: everything they do costs them |

- **Thousand Cuts is Bleed's road now,** said plainly. It was already built on the wound and the flurry.
- **The Storm is back with the hook it lacked.** It runs on the enemy's clock, so it taxes the actions that never touch the party: a healer pays for every heal, a caster for every cast. It's how a party gets at a back row it can't reach, where the Hunt goes and kills it. It's still the road I'm least sure of.

### The wall: who stands in front

You're right, and I leaned into the problem. Going back for why a wall was needed every time, the cause is structural, not only tuning:

- **In Neutral, the only front line is a class's own Aggro, and only two classes have it.** Beside two casters a Knight takes 60% of the foes' picks. A Hoplite, Paladin, Vampire or Geomancer takes 47%; a Valkyrie, Monk or Frostweaver 40%. So every sturdy class but the two walls is nearly as exposed as the caster behind it, and 71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral.
- **A front seat fixes that on floor one.** In a seat that draws far more, with the casters in seats that draw less, the Hoplite takes 67% and the Valkyrie 60%: what a Knight takes in Neutral. Then nine classes can stand in front from the first fight, and they take the blows nine ways (*Who stands in front*).

So, to your three options: **mostly "quicker", through the seat.** Some "by default": the classes meant to stand in front have to work there from stat line and passive, not a rolled ability, and I've flagged two that don't. And some "too hard": act I should ask whether a party has a front, not whether it has a Knight. The seat belongs to your separate change, formations at a run's start. I'm flagging that the builds lean on it harder than I'd said: Neutral itself may want a front seat.

### Rulings applied

- **The Drought is cut:** nothing is built on a number the player can't see.
- **Bonfire on upkeep** stands. Burn's drain is the balance pass's number.
- **Stranglehold stays down,** as one bridge (the Row).
- **Two more engines I've cut myself:** Twice read (it was a route through two kits, not an engine) and Tinderbox (Embers does its job, and pays against a boss too).

### Decisions

1. **The engines as redone:** stopping points on roads, with a few bridges. Which would you be glad to find?
2. **Nine roads,** four of them a status's. Is the Storm's hook enough?
3. **The front seat.** The builds assume a party can put someone in front from the first fight. If your formations change won't give that, the builds need another answer and I'd want to find it before the loot.

### Least sure

- **The Storm.** Its hook is real against packs and busy back rows. Against a lone slow boss it pays on the boss's schedule, and leans on one engine.
- **Thousand Cuts,** centred on Bleed, no longer holds "speed for its own sake". Speed is a thread every status road wants now.
- **The lens.** "A moment that does nothing" made a better set than the grid did. It may also be hiding the engines that don't fit it.
- **Blood Pact** still asks the player to spend what the game drains.
- **Four engines:** the Row (a ramp, not a loop), Deep frost (small), Radiance and the Dance (each is mostly a kit doing what it already does).

## Roads and engines

**A build** is a road: a broad way to play, with a shape, a rhythm, several routes and two rungs. Its sign shows in act I and it's built in act II.

**An engine** is a gimmick: a specific rule that takes something a party is already making and gives it a new verb. It has no routes or rungs. It's where a run arrives, and what the run is remembered for. The director's shield party is the model: Shields laid for safety, and a rule that made their breaking the party's damage.

**What makes an engine:**
- **It starts from something a road already makes in volume:** Shields, a fire, wounds, kills, casts.
- **It gives a verb to a moment that does nothing today.**
- **Something re-arms it,** so it happens many times a fight.
- **It changes what the run is.** After it, the party's damage or its safety comes from the gimmick.

**Where engines sit:**
- **Most are stopping points on one road:** where that road turns into something specific. A road has two or three.
- **A few are bridges:** the verb makes what another road runs on.
- **They add to the doors.** A run still drifts between roads through the common things both want.

**What that asks of the loot:**
- **A road's own steps are the common things:** commons, uncommons and the common formations. **Engines are made of the specific ones:** rares, legendaries, versions, seats and mythics, each worth wearing alone on its road. The spine is written backwards from both.
- **Every part comes from more than one place** (a piece in more than one kind, a version, a seat), since a run sees under half the legendaries.
- **No part names its partner.** A card says what the part does.
- **Parts overlap on purpose,** so one engine's part is half of something nobody wrote down. Exotics stay.

## The frame

**What a build is doesn't change** (`docs/gear-design/philosophy.md`): a way to play you can watch, with a rhythm, woven from several threads, reached by more than one route, in rungs.

**What a run gives a build:**
- **Act I:** three units knowing an ability or two each, a weapon each and a piece or two more by the boss. A build can show its direction here. It can't be assembled.
- **Act II:** most slots filled, a version or two a unit, formations, a mythic. This is where a build is built, and where an engine's first part turns up.
- **Act III:** whole kits and legendaries. Where an engine closes.

**Left unjudged, as the intent asks:** kits arriving one ability at a time, and cooldowns carrying. The set is written to hold either way.

**What every build answers:**
- **Through a run:** the sign and the build, each saying what the player sees, what the step is and what it pays if the run goes no further.
- **Its engines:** where the road can stop. Each names the moment it's built on.
- **Stays alive:** who stands in front, and how they take it.
- **Packs and bosses:** it has a game for both, and says which is its worse fight. Three of the twelve were written to fear a lone boss, and bosses and pairs of elites are where most of the sample's runs ended.
- **Asks of the game:** kit or rule changes.

**What every build asks of the loot,** so it isn't repeated nine times:
- **A first step that's seen the first time it fires.** In play a few Might, a tenth off a cost and a cooldown a few ticks closer were never seen to happen.
- **A first step that doesn't cost the front.** In act I a careful party takes defence first, and it's right to.
- **A formation about one thing:** three seats that all serve what the build does.

## The rhythm space

| Build | Shape | Damage over a fight | Danger | Turns | What it spends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Landslide** | two load, one strikes | long quiet, then one huge number | steady, soaked by stone | few, big | MP on stone, to save HP |
| **Thousand Cuts** | one opens, all pour | spikes when a wound opens, in a dense stream of small hits | even, and paid back hit by hit | each wound opened | HP: little casting |
| **Bonfire** | three feed one fire | as high as the party is feeding it right now; it leaps when casts land together | early, and whenever the fire sags | each flare, and the blow that reads it | MP, on the feeding |
| **Cold Snap** | two lay frost, one breaks | bursts on a beat you can count | falls as the field slows | many, medium: each shatter | MP |
| **The Storm** | three charge the field | follows how much the foes act | spread, and highest while a pack still stands | whenever a foe moves | MP, on the spread |
| **The Hunt** | three on one foe | a volley at the first Mark, then a staircase of kills | early, falling with each kill | regular: each kill | both: HP in the first moments, MP on reach |
| **The Toll** | one is struck, two profit | follows how much the foes attack | all on one unit | few | HP: the anchor's |
| **Barrage** | two serve one caster | in salvos from one unit | a cliff when the mana or the caster stops | each salvo | MP, more than any other |
| **Blood Pact** | one pays, one drinks, one gives | high and steady | swings and rights itself | many, medium | HP, in place of MP |

Pairs on opposite sides of the space:
- **Landslide and Thousand Cuts:** one huge blow, or many tiny ones.
- **Bonfire and Cold Snap:** a fire that dies unless it's fed, or a frost that builds until it breaks.
- **The Hunt and the Storm:** go and kill the back row, or make its own work kill it.
- **Barrage and Blood Pact:** mana poured out, or life.
- **The Toll** stands alone: it's the only one that waits to be hit.

## The builds

### Landslide

**Identity.** A party that loads one blow. Long stretches of small numbers go by behind stone bars. A wind-up bar fills on the hammer while Might piles onto it and its target is opened up. Then one number lands far bigger than anything else in the fight, and an elite loses a large share of its HP. The stone is rebuilt, and it happens again. It's waiting for the blow to come round.

- **Threads:**
  - the heavy action: slow swings, long wind-ups, long cooldowns;
  - loading it: Might on the slowest hitter (it's spent an action at a time, so it lasts where actions are rare), crits and Crit damage, Sunder laid on the target before the blow lands;
  - Defense, max HP and the Shield as damage stats, so the body is the weapon;
  - stone on the whole party, as the time the blow needs;
  - aim: at the healthiest foe, the most armored, the stunned.
- **Routes:** Geomancer, Knight, Hoplite · Geomancer, Paladin, Bard · no earth: Knight, Hoplite, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** a hammer whose actions come less often and land bigger; an anvil that wears the stone and draws the blows; a loader that gives the hammer what the party has to give (stone, Might).
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One unit whose heavy thing already out-sizes everything round it: rocks that crit from behind a Shield, a double swing, a bash from max HP. The sign is the number, and that Defense or HP on a piece makes it bigger: here the careful pick and the build's pick are the same piece. The step is the first piece that says so on its card, heavier and rarer.
  2. *The build.* A loading crew: Might aimed at the hammer, the target opened first, stone on everyone, the blow aimed at something worth it. Elites lose a large share at a time, and a boss takes a handful of blows.
- **Its engines:**
  - **Rubble.** *A Shield breaking.* It deals damage by the size it was laid at, and comes back smaller, to break again. Stone laid for safety becomes most of the party's damage. The director's shield party.
  - **The Slide.** *A blow that kills with damage to spare.* What's left rolls on into the next foe, and the next. The one huge number becomes a row of them, and a pack stops being a blow wasted.
  - **Overburden.** *A Shield laid on a unit whose stone is already full.* The surplus goes into the hammer's next blow. Every mason in the party is loading it.
- **Stays alive:** stone on everyone, across heavy bodies, and any of the three can stand in front. Fights run long, so the stone has to be rebuilt, and that's where its MP goes.
- **Packs and bosses:** bosses and armored foes are its best fights: a huge hit loses little to Defense. A pack is its worst: a blow spent on a wolf is a long wait wasted. Its pack game is the shove that hits every foe, the aim that saves the blow for the biggest, and the Slide. It fears dodgers, and anything that strips Shields.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** heavier and rarer, for swings and for abilities; Might for one unit, not three; damage from Defense and max HP. Every Landslide piece promises a blow on its card: the ones that promised stone alone were worn as plain defence, by everyone.
  - **Formations:** hammer, anvil, loader; formations that trade the party's speed for size.
  - **Upgrades:** slower and heavier versions; versions that aim the blow.
- **Asks of the game:** none. Its miss was the loot's signposting, not the kits.

### Thousand Cuts

**Identity.** A wound opens on a foe: a crit, a barb, a thorn. Then the party pours hits into it, tiny numbers, several a tick, and every one pays again in red beside its own white. The wound closes a little with each hit, until someone opens it wider. It's waiting for the next wound, and it never stops hitting.

- **Threads:**
  - Bleed: opened wide in one stroke, cashed by every hit after, closing as it pays;
  - the opener and the pourers: a few hits that open (crits, barbs, thorns) and many that cash;
  - speed: fast weapons, Haste, Attack speed;
  - extra strikes: Double strike, multi-hit abilities;
  - staying alive by hitting: life drunk, a Shield built, an ally mended, hit by hit;
  - Evasion, and the dodge as one more trigger;
  - Sunder under it all, its answer to Defense.
- **Routes:** Vampire, Assassin, Monk · behind briars: Bramble knight, Assassin, Ranger · Valkyrie, Ranger, Vampire.
- **Seats it likes:** an opener whose crits come more often; two blades that act more often and hit smaller.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit that opens wounds (four base kits do): the party's hits on that foe print twice. Early units mostly swing, so the step is anything that makes more hits: the faster of a class's two weapon kinds, a second strike. It's worth taking on any unit.
  2. *The build.* An opener and two who pour, a pacer's Haste, and a front kept standing by its own hits. Armored foes stop being walls: Bleed goes past Defense, and the hits only have to land.
- **Its engines:**
  - **Salt.** *A wound closing as it's cashed.* Extra strikes cash a wound and don't close it. A second strike, a three-hit ability, a volley: each lands on the wound at its widest.
  - **The Feast.** *A wound paying out.* Whoever's hit cashed it is healed by what it paid. The party lives on the wounds it opens, and its front is whoever hits most. It bends a rule: a status's damage heals nobody today.
- **Stays alive:** by hitting. Its front is a Vampire who drinks or a Valkyrie whose hits build her Shield, or it stands behind briars, where foes arrive already bleeding. The dodge alone didn't hold in play.
- **Packs and bosses:** a lone boss suits it: every hit on one foe, the wound never closing. In a pack its swings scatter where Bleed wants them together; its pack game is the Mark, multi-hits that fan out, and wounds opened on every foe at once. It fears foes that harden under many hits (the Ice Golem's crust), dodgers, and the Wisps' Shock, which fast units pay for most.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Bleed from more than crits; hits, however they come; a drink or a Shield per hit on Mail and Leather; something for the opener that isn't one more wound.
  - **Formations:** opener and blades; formations that make the whole party faster at a cost.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that add hits, or open a wound on every foe.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### Bonfire

**Identity.** A fire that dies if it isn't fed. Burn falls away fast, so the number on the hottest foe is the party's feeding, live: it leaps when two casts land together, and sags when a feeder is stunned or between spells. Everyone feeds it: casts, swings that carry fire, the keeper's own blows. While it's high the foe melts, and the blow that reads the fire lands for the fight's biggest number. It's waiting for the fire to roar, and working to keep it there.

- **Threads:**
  - Burn as upkeep: it drains fast, so its height is how hard the party is feeding it right now;
  - two kinds of feeder: swings that carry fire hold it steady, casts make it leap;
  - the pile: every feeder aims at the hottest foe;
  - a blow that reads the fire and leaves it, and that waits for a fire worth reading;
  - tempo for the feeders: Haste, Cooldown, Cast speed, MP;
  - a keeper who holds the early fight, keeps the feeders casting and burns what it holds.
- **Routes:** Knight, Pyromancer, Monk · Paladin, Hexer, Pyromancer · no mage: Paladin, Monk, Hexer.
- **Seats it likes:** a keeper that holds the early danger; a stoker that acts more often; a torch whose fire reaches every foe that's already burning.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One unit that leaves Burn: three base kits do, and a version or a piece puts it on a swing. The fire stands as high as it's fed, and it gets through Defense where hits don't. The step is a second feeder, and because the fire dies so fast the step shows at once: the number stands twice as high. It sits on a weapon or a version, so it takes nothing from the front.
  2. *The build.* Three feeders on one pile, some steady and some in bursts, behind a keeper who feeds too, and a reader that waits for the height. Elites and bosses burn for more than the party hits for.
- **Its engines:**
  - **Embers.** *A fire draining.* What a fire sheds each pulse lands on another foe. A high pile lights the whole field just by burning. With no other foe to catch them the embers fall back on the fire, and it drains slower.
  - **Ash.** *A fire going out.* It bursts for the height it reached. The other way to play fire: one enormous cast, left to burn down, and the blast when it dies.
  - **Bellows.** *A fire standing high.* While it does, the party swings faster, and swings that carry fire feed it higher. A feeder stopped drops the fire under the line, and the speed goes with it.
- **Stays alive:** a keeper in front, who feeds the fire, so the seat isn't lost to the build. A feeder stopped is the fire sagging, so the keeper guards the tempo as well as the HP.
- **Packs and bosses:** a lone boss is its best fight: one pile, all fight. A pack is its worst: every kill throws a fire away. Its pack game is fire laid thin on the whole field, and Embers. It fears fights too short to get a fire up, and anything that stops its casters (a Silence, a Stun on the wrong unit).
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** fire on swings, for many kinds; a way for a front to feed (fire on its slow swing, or on what strikes it); a choice between a fire that lasts and one that flares; tempo where casters wear it.
  - **Formations:** keeper, stoker and torch, all three serving the fire.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that move fire from casts to swings, or make one cast a flare; a reader that waits for the fire to be high.
- **Asks of the game:** nothing of Burn's behaviour. Its rhythm is Burn's drain, a number (`STATUS.burn`): fast enough that an unfed fire is gone in a few ticks. That's the balance pass's, and it holds for every use of Burn.

### Cold Snap

**Identity.** Frost creeps up the foes and they visibly slow: each swing comes later than the last. Then a SHATTER: a burst, a Stun, and the party's heaviest hit lands on the frozen foe. Then the next one. It's waiting for the next foe to reach the cap, and the colder the field, the less the party is hit.

- **Threads:**
  - Chill to its cap and the shatter; anyone's Chill counts;
  - the slow on the way up: a colder field hits less often;
  - many small applications: swings that carry Chill, multi-hits;
  - the pile or the spread: one foe shattered again and again, or a pack reaching the cap together;
  - the opening a shatter makes: a stunned, sundered foe, and the heavy blow saved for it;
  - cold that attackers carry away from a front.
- **Routes:** Frostweaver, Hexer, Ranger · a front in frost: Knight, Frostweaver, Monk · no Frostweaver: Hexer, Ranger, Geomancer.
- **Seats it likes:** two frost seats that act often and carry Chill; a breaker that hits a frozen foe hardest.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One Chill source (three base kits have one): the foes slow as the counter climbs, and one shatters. The step is a second source. Every stack also slows a foe, so it's never a pick against the party's safety.
  2. *The build.* Sources enough that the same foe shatters again in the same fight, and a breaker for each one. The second shatter is the line between the sign and the build. It comes from the Chill counts, which *Core set: classes* already makes the balance pass's first job, and from the engines below.
- **Its engines:**
  - **Snowball.** *The shatter itself.* It brings round the spell that caused it. A frost spell on the whole field re-arms itself for as long as something keeps reaching the cap.
  - **Splinters.** *The shatter's burst.* Every other foe takes a share of it, and a little Chill. One shatter in a cold pack sets off the string. The Frostweaver's kit holds half of this as a version.
  - **Deep frost.** *Chill laid past the cap.* What's left over when a foe shatters is on it when it wakes. The second shatter is nearer than the first.
- **Stays alive:** the Frostweaver in Mail can stand in front, and so can anyone in frost, so that what strikes the front slows. The cold is the second layer: a chilled field acts less, and every shatter is a Stun.
- **Packs and bosses:** spread over a pack, the cold slows everything and the shatters come together, like a string of firecrackers. Piled on a boss, it's a slow all fight and a burst on a beat; a boss shrugs off the Stuns after the first few, not the slow or the burst. It fears fights too short to reach the cap.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Chill on swings, for the fast kinds; Chill from being struck, for whoever stands in front; payoffs on a frozen foe.
  - **Formations:** the frost seats and the breaker; formations where one seat's hits set up another's.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that aim at the foe nearest its shatter, or spread the cold wide.
- **Asks of the game:** nothing of Chill's behaviour. The Chill counts, as above.

### The Storm

**Identity.** Everything the foes do costs them. The party's strikes spread a charge across the whole field: an arc to the foe beside, a chain down the line. Then the foes move, and each one that swings, casts, heals or calls for help is hurt for it. A wolf pack fries in its own frenzy. A healer behind a shield wall pays for every heal. A heavy blow comes down on the most charged. It's waiting for them to move.

- **Threads:**
  - Shock: paid whenever its holder acts, whatever the action, so the busiest foes pay most;
  - spread: strikes that arc, chain or land on three, so the whole field is charged, back row included, with no need to reach it;
  - quick, light strikes that keep the charge topped up, since half of it goes each time it pays;
  - a storm on the whole party's swings for a while;
  - a blow that reads the charge on its target and leaves it;
  - someone in front while a pack still stands.
- **Routes:** Hoplite, Stormcaller, Valkyrie · Knight, Stormcaller, Monk · no Stormcaller: Valkyrie, Hexer, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** two squalls whose strikes spread; a rod that holds the field's attention.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit whose strikes spread and shock: fast foes take damage every time they swing, and the foe behind the front line is charged without being aimed at. The step is charge from a second unit.
  2. *The build.* The whole field charged all fight, the storm on everyone's swings, and a blow that reads the charge. Packs burn themselves down, and the back row pays for its own work.
- **Its engines:**
  - **Live wire.** *A charged foe acting.* Each time one does, an ally's next swing comes at once. The foes' tempo becomes the party's: a fast pack makes it frantic, and every extra swing lays more charge.
  - **Gathering storm.** *A foe winding up.* A charged foe gathers more charge for as long as its wind-up lasts, and pays it all when the cast lands. The longer the cast, the bigger the bolt. The Storm's game against a boss.
- **Stays alive:** someone in front. Shock punishes a foe for acting; it doesn't stop it, and in a pack every foe is alive and acting until late. What helps is that it lands hardest on whatever is acting most, which is what's hurting the party most.
- **Packs and bosses:** fast packs are its best fights, and groups with a busy back row (healers, cursers, summoners) its next best. A lone, slow boss is its worst: the charge pays on the boss's schedule. Its boss game is the blow that reads the charge, and Gathering storm. It fears Bomb Bugs (every blast at once) and Lava Slimes (an even field splits every slime).
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Shock on swings, for the fast kinds; a blow that reads the charge, for more classes than the Valkyrie; spread that comes with something else on the piece, since a slot whose whole promise was one more foe was never taken.
  - **Formations:** squalls and a rod; formations that pay for how many foes one action touches.
  - **Upgrades:** one foe or every foe; a longer storm or a harder one.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### The Hunt

**Identity.** The pack takes them one at a time. A foe is Marked, and everything the party has lands on it: swings, arrows, the bolt that was going to go anywhere. It falls, the Mark moves, and it happens again. Each kill pays for the next: a blow ready again, a volley. The foes' numbers drop like a staircase. It's waiting for the next kill.

- **Threads:**
  - focus: the Mark, which takes every pick the party makes at random, and aim at the lowest HP;
  - the first strike: the first Mark and its volley at the first tick, and each class's own opener (a first swing that lands bigger, a first hit that always crits, a strike from hiding);
  - reach: Seek past a Taunting front;
  - the kill as a trigger: a reset, a volley, the Mark moving;
  - executes: damage that grows as the foe's HP falls, so a boss is one long staircase;
  - crits and Crit damage on a few chosen hits;
  - Silence on the back row's casters.
- **Routes:** a judge who calls the target and stands in front: Paladin, Assassin, Stormcaller · no Assassin: Hoplite, Valkyrie, Ranger · Assassin, Ranger, Valkyrie.
- **Seats it likes:** a marker whose pick sets the target; a chaser with reach; a warden that holds the front.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit that picks its prey (the lowest, the back row, past a guard): the dangerous foe dies first, or never acts. The step is a kill or a first strike that pays something seen on the very next hit.
  2. *The build.* A Mark the whole party follows, hunters whose abilities aim where the Mark sits, and a kill that pays. Mixed groups fall in a drumbeat that speeds up.
- **Its engines:**
  - **The Drumbeat.** *A Marked foe falling.* The Mark moves to the next foe, and every new Mark calls a volley: each ally's next swing at once. A slow, heavy swing gets a free one every kill.
  - **First blood.** *A kill.* The killer's start-of-fight effects happen again. Everything a run has gathered that fires when a fight begins (a Shield, Might, Haste, a first hit that crits) fires on every kill.
- **Stays alive:** a warden in front, and kill order: the most dangerous foe dies first and the dead don't hit. Against a lone boss kill order buys nothing, and the warden is the plan.
- **Packs and bosses:** mixed groups with a dangerous back row are its best fights. A lone boss is its worst until the executes come: one step, no staircase. It fears Ghouls raised behind the hunt, and a Taunting front if it has no reach.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** the Mark from more hands than the Paladin's; kill rewards sized to be seen (a whole action, not a few ticks of one); executes; openers in each class's own terms.
  - **Formations:** marker, chaser and warden, all three serving the kill.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that change what an ability aims at, or make it ready again on a kill: the kind that carried a run.
- **Asks of the game:** Marked takes the party's random picks (approved).

### The Toll

**Identity.** They hurt themselves. Foes crowd onto one unit, and every blow they land comes back: Thorns flash, the attacker leaves bleeding, charged or slowed, and it pays again when it next moves. The swarm that rushes the wall dies on it. It's waiting to be hit. The danger is all on the anchor, and the rest of the party is almost untouched.

- **Threads:**
  - the draw: Taunt and Aggro;
  - Thorns and retaliation: being struck strikes back;
  - what an attacker carries away (Bleed, Shock, Chill, Weaken): each ally adds its own toll to the blows on the anchor;
  - the anchor's keeper: Guard, Regen, heals, Weaken on what hits it;
  - the dodge as the other anchor: a dancer who slips the blow and answers it.
- **Routes:** Bramble knight, Cleric, Stormcaller · Knight, Hexer, Hoplite · the dancer: Monk, Valkyrie, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** an anchor that draws and returns; a warden that keeps it standing; a free seat that finishes what the anchor started.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* On floor one, from the kit: a Bramble knight, or a Knight that Taunts in thorns. Packs die on it. There's nothing to find, which is why it was in most parties.
  2. *The build.* The other two join the toll: blows on the anchor set off what they've laid (the attacker bleeds, sparks, slows, weakens), and the anchor has a keeper. Swarms die on the wall while the third seat does something else.
- **Its engines:**
  - **The Mirror.** *A blow stopped.* What Defense, a Shield or an Aegis stops goes back to the attacker. The one great blow of a boss that barely swings pays as a whole swarm does.
  - **The briar patch.** *Thorns answering.* The return counts as a hit, so it cashes the Bleed the last return left. Each blow on the anchor opens the attacker's wound and pays the one before. The Bramble knight's passive is half of it.
  - **The Dance.** *A blow that misses.* The dancer strikes back, and the strike-back counts as a swing: it drinks, shields and carries what the swings carry. The other anchor.
- **Stays alive:** it is the front. Its risk is the one play showed: when the anchor falls, the rest fall behind it. So the keeper is part of the build, not an extra.
- **Packs and bosses:** swarms and fast foes are its best fights. A boss or a pair of elites that barely swing is where it died in play, and the Mirror is written for that. It gets nothing from foes that don't strike (Bone Priests, Witches), which is the Storm's ground, or from Area, which ignores the draw.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Taunt on gear, for more anchors; what an attacker carries away, for the allies to give; Thorns that grow with the beating.
  - **Formations:** the anchor seat, with two seats that add to its toll.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that change who the anchor is and what it returns.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### Barrage

**Identity.** One caster, and the same great spell again and again. Its name scrolls up the log, twice running. Its MP bar is refilled by the other two, and the spell is ready before its cooldown should be done. The party's basic attacks are an afterthought. It's waiting for the spell to come back round. When the mana or the caster stops, so does the party.

- **Threads:**
  - one ability worth repeating: the soloist's;
  - cooldowns: the Cooldown stat, Haste, an ability made ready at once;
  - the cast twice;
  - MP that flows to the soloist (Clarity, Refrain, Siphon, life for mana);
  - Cast speed, and a wind-up that can be lost;
  - costs that change.
- **Routes:** Knight, Bard, Pyromancer · Frostweaver, Bard, Stormcaller · no Bard: Cleric, Vampire, Geomancer.
- **Seats it likes:** a soloist whose abilities come faster; a conductor who gives it tempo; a well that gives it mana.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A caster with one spell worth repeating, and anyone who gives mana. The sign is the caster not swinging its staff. Every caster party feels the shortage, so the step (mana in fights, however it comes) is one it wants anyway.
  2. *The build.* A conductor: the spell twice, or ready again; Cooldown and Cast speed on the soloist. The spell lands several times in a fight where it used to land once.
- **Its engines:**
  - **Crescendo.** *The same spell cast again.* Each time it's cast in a fight it lands bigger, and costs more. The reason to repeat one spell and not rotate three.
  - **Overflow.** *Mana given to a full pool.* It goes into the next cast. A well that gives more than the soloist can hold is loading the spell.
  - **The Dynamo.** *A spell touching many foes.* Mana and time come back for each one. Against a pack the spell pays for itself; against one foe it doesn't.
- **Stays alive:** the well stands in front where it can: the Knight's Taunt and Clarity are one ability, the Frostweaver is a sturdy well, and a Geomancer can be soloist and front at once. The soloist draws little. Its danger is a cliff, not a slope.
- **Packs and bosses:** whatever the soloist's spell is; choosing the soloist is choosing that. It fears Silence (the Wraiths, the Silent One), Leeches, and a fight longer than the mana.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** links sized to be seen: a whole cast's mana, a whole cooldown, not a tenth of either; Cooldown and Cast speed where casters wear them; an answer to Silence; something of their own for the two who serve, so they're more than batteries.
  - **Formations:** soloist, conductor, well.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that make an ability ready again on a cast or a kill, or pass mana on.
- **Asks of the game:** none in the kits. Clarity's and Refrain's mana have to be big enough to change what the caster does: the balance pass's.

### Blood Pact

**Identity.** Life spent and bought back. The party's HP bars swing. A unit pays a chunk of its life for a blow, or rides low on purpose, and drinks it back off the foes; the healers work twice as fast while someone is at the brink; a Shield or an Aegis sits over whoever is lowest. The party fights at half and ends full. It's waiting to be low enough.

- **Threads:**
  - Lifesteal, and drinking more the lower it is;
  - HP as a price, in place of MP;
  - below half as power: for the one who's low, and for the allies who answer it;
  - nets: a Shield or an Aegis over a low unit (a price paid isn't damage, so it doesn't spend the Shield);
  - life passed on: a heal given as a trigger;
  - max HP as a damage stat;
  - the last drink: ending the fight full.
- **Routes:** Vampire, Valkyrie, Cleric · Vampire, Bard, Hoplite · no Vampire: Assassin, Paladin, Monk.
- **Seats it likes:** a bleeder that pays HP for power; a drinker that feeds on hits; a donor that passes life on.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A drinker: the Vampire, or Lifesteal on anything that hits hard. A unit that heals itself by hitting, and potions saved. It's taken as sustain, which is right: sustain is the build's floor.
  2. *The build.* The price: abilities and pieces paid in HP that buy what mana can't, power below half for the payer and from its allies, and a net over whoever is low. Fights where the mana never runs out.
- **Its engines:**
  - **The blood hammer.** *A price paid in HP.* The next blow lands harder by what was paid, and Lifesteal on one huge hit drinks it back in a gulp.
  - **The brimming cup.** *Healing on a full unit.* What's drunk over the top becomes a Shield, and the Shield stands over the next price.
  - **Radiance.** *A heal given.* The healer's next swing lands as hard as the heal, and at the brink it heals twice as fast. The Cleric's kit holds both halves, one as a version.
- **Stays alive:** the drinker stands in front, and the lower it is the more it drinks; the healers quicken at the brink; a Shield or an Aegis takes the blow that would finish a low unit. It's the riskiest plan in the set, on purpose.
- **Packs and bosses:** long fights against things it can drink from. It fears Bats (a dodged hit drinks nothing), the Witch's curse (a weakened hit drinks less), Rot, and burst that catches it low.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** prices in HP that buy more than their mana would, repaid inside the same fight; power below half that doesn't leave a unit low when the fight ends; nets; a last drink.
  - **Formations:** bleeder, drinker, donor; formations where life moves between seats.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that pay life or pass it.
- **Asks of the game:** none in the kits. Only the Vampire's base kit pays HP; the build widens through Lifesteal's kinds and versions other kits already hold, and the Berserker, when it's added, is its natural second home.

## The bridges

Five engines whose verb makes what another road runs on.

- **The Cull.** *The Hunt and Thousand Cuts.* A crit opens the wound, the execute finishes the wounded, and the kill makes the execute ready. One unit's loop. It carried a run to act II, and it's how the player found those two builds as one.
- **The Capacitor.** *The Storm and Cold Snap.* A foe that can't act can't pay, so charge piles on a frozen or stunned foe. A blow on a foe as its Stun ends reads the charge and leaves it, and then the foe acts and pays.
- **The frozen gate.** *The Toll and Cold Snap.* Foes that strike the anchor carry Chill away, and each one that shatters lays a Shield on the anchor.
- **The Quake.** *Landslide and Cold Snap.* Crits that Stun, and a blow that lands far bigger on the stunned; a shatter's Stun starts it. The Geomancer's versions hold both.
- **The Row.** *The four statuses' roads.* Every different affliction on a foe makes each hit on it bigger. A party that mixes its colours. Malison, as approved, is its first part.

**The eleven I'd fight for:** Rubble, the Slide, Embers, Ash, Live wire, Gathering storm, the Mirror, First blood, Crescendo, the blood hammer, the Capacitor.

## The doors

Where a run drifts from one road to its neighbour, through the common things both want. A bridge is named where one stands on the door.
- **Any status road into another, through a second colour on a swing.** A fire party picks up frost. The Row is the bridge.
- **Thousand Cuts into the Hunt, through the opener's crits.** The crit that opens a wound finishes the weakest. The Cull.
- **Thousand Cuts into Blood Pact, through the drink.** A party that lives on Lifesteal starts paying with it.
- **The Toll into Thousand Cuts, through briars.** Foes leave the anchor already bleeding.
- **The Toll into Cold Snap, through frost on the anchor.** The frozen gate.
- **The Toll into Landslide, through Defense and stone.**
- **The Storm into Cold Snap, through a held foe.** The Capacitor.
- **The Storm into the Hunt, through the back row:** taxed, or reached.
- **Landslide into Cold Snap, through the heavy blow on a frozen foe.** The Quake.
- **Landslide into Barrage, through the conductor:** the blow twice.
- **Landslide into the Hunt, through the first strike:** Might at the start on the slowest hitter.
- **Barrage into Bonfire, the Storm or Cold Snap, through the soloist:** the spell worth repeating is the one that feeds the fire, spreads the charge or lays the frost.
- **Blood Pact into Barrage, through life for mana.**
- **Blood Pact into Landslide, through max HP in the blow.**

## Who stands in front

The intent's question was whether every build needs a tank. The director's ruling at this gate: a wall in every build would make every run start the same, which is what the pass is meant to correct. **The design's answer: every party has someone in front, and the game should offer many kinds of front from the first fight, not one.**

**Why it was always a wall.** Foes pick by draw, and in Neutral draw is a class's own Aggro (`RULES.partyDraw`). Beside two casters:

| In front | Share of the foes' picks, in Neutral | In a seat that draws far more, casters in seats that draw less |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knight, Bramble knight | 60% | 78% |
| Hoplite, Paladin, Vampire, Geomancer | 47% | 67% |
| Valkyrie, Monk, Frostweaver | 40% | 60% |

Without a seat, only the two walls are a front at all, and 71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral. With a seat, seven more classes take the share a Knight takes today.

**Nine fronts, and what each leads to:**

| Front | How it takes the blows | Roads it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knight | soaks, behind its own Shield | the Toll, Landslide, Barrage, Bonfire |
| Bramble knight | returns them | the Toll, Thousand Cuts |
| Hoplite | soaks; the seat gives it the draw its kit lacks | Landslide, the Storm, the Hunt |
| Paladin | soaks and mends | the Hunt, Bonfire, Blood Pact |
| Geomancer | stands in stone, and hits hardest while it holds | Landslide, Cold Snap, Barrage |
| Vampire | drinks them back | Blood Pact, Thousand Cuts |
| Valkyrie | builds her Shield with her own hits | the Storm, the Hunt, Thousand Cuts |
| Monk | slips them | the Toll, Thousand Cuts |
| Frostweaver | slows what strikes | Cold Snap, Barrage |

**The director's three options:**
- **Quicker: yes, through the seat.** A front seat from the first fight is what makes the table above true on floor one. It belongs to the director's separate change (formations at a run's start), and the builds lean on it: the first formation a run holds, Neutral included, wants a seat that draws.
- **By default: for the fronts.** A class meant to stand in front has to work there from its stat line and passive, since its first ability is rolled. Seven of the nine do. Two flags: the Geomancer's passive needs a Shield that only one of its abilities gives, and the Monk's dodge alone didn't hold in play. The glass classes stay glass.
- **Too hard at the start: yes, in one respect.** Act I should ask whether a party has a front, not whether it has a Knight. That's the balance pass's: act I's regular fights set so a party with a front of any kind reaches the boss on its supplies. Eleven of the sample's 23 runs ended before act I's boss.
- **One more lever, outside this pass:** the draft already offers a healer while nobody heals. It could offer someone who can stand in front while nobody can.

**No front at all** (three light bodies living on kill order, or on a field too slowed to hurt them) is somewhere a run can end up, not a way to start. At today's sizes Weaken and Slow on every foe take about three fifths off what the foes deal (`STATUS.weaken`, `STATUS.slow`), reached late and paid for in MP.

**What I can measure.** Today's hand-built parties for the twelve (`npx tsx tools/build-test.ts --quick --no-ablate`): whole kits, each party in its build's formation, every fight from full HP, two seeds, today's knobs. Nearly every fight is won, so the read is HP lost per won fight, on act II's fights at rung 2. A pointer, not a verdict.

| Party | Who takes the blows | HP lost per won fight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knight, Hexer, Hoplite | a wall | 6% |
| Bramble knight, Cleric, Stormcaller | a wall | 8% |
| Valkyrie, Ranger, Vampire | a drinker or a shieldmaiden | 6% |
| Vampire, Monk, Cleric | a drinker | 9% |
| Valkyrie, Ranger, Assassin | a shieldmaiden | 11% |
| Frostweaver, Hexer, Ranger | nobody built for it | 28% |
| Stormcaller, Hexer, Pyromancer | nobody | 30% |
| Bard, Frostweaver, Hexer | nobody | 37% |

Once a party is built, a front that isn't a wall costs no more HP than one that is. What costs three to five times as much is having nobody in front.

**For the balance pass:**
- **Test each front,** with hand-built parties, from act I: a party fronted by a Vampire, a Valkyrie, a Hoplite, a Monk.
- **The wall is cheap by rule.** A unit built to draw takes 85–90% of the foes' picks (`docs/rules.md`, Aggro by doubling), and under one kind of damage the prototype's report found walls stronger still. If a wall still beats every other front at every stage, the draw rule is the thing to look at.
- **Fronts fail suddenly.** In play the anchor fell first in most fights where anyone fell, and the others, near full, fell after it. Whatever stands in front needs someone keeping it there.

## Elements, not builds

Things the twelve treated as builds, or as a build's whole identity, that are better as things any party can have.

- **Falls and returns: the net.** Nobody chooses who falls, so nothing is built on it. A revive, and whatever softens a fall, serves every build. What the net has to do: the returned stay up (each of the five revives inside a fight in the sample was followed by a second fall), the survivors get cover in the moment after, and a knockout needn't be carried into the next fight. It's sized as protection. No piece wants a fall.
- **The first move.** The fight's start is the one trigger nothing gates. It's the Hunt's first Mark, the thing First blood repeats, and a thread several others use: stone at the start, cold at the start, a Taunt at the start, a first cast free.
- **Spread.** Reaching more foes is each build's pack game, and four engines' work (the Slide, Embers, Splinters, the Dynamo). The Storm is the one road whose nature is spread.
- **Stopping a cast.** A Stun or a Silence timed to a wind-up is an answer to a boss's great cast, as *Core set: enemies* asks, and the act's boss is shown from the start. The versions that wait for a wind-up are that answer. A wind-up was caught five times in 150 fights, so no build waits for one.
- **Foes that do less.** Weaken, Slow and Silence make a party's fights cheaper. At their sizes today they don't keep one alive.

**What a formation has to be** for a party to pivot to it, from what was and wasn't fought in:
- **About one thing.** All three seats serve what the build does, and no seat is a tax.
- **A promise in plain words,** big enough to see: a stat, a status by name, who it lands on.
- **Seats many classes can fill.** A seat that reads who sits in it says so on the seat.
- **A front seat in the common ones,** since a run's first formation is what gives it a front.
- **Builds and engines both.** Every build has a common formation a run could start in and a rare that reads who sits where. A legendary formation is an engine's part, or a whole engine in three seats.

A build still plays in Neutral, and no build needs one formation. How formations arrive in a run is outside this pass.

## Checks on the whole set

- **The rhythms and shapes spread across the space:** rare and huge (Landslide), spikes in a dense stream (Thousand Cuts), a fire that leaps and sags (Bonfire), bursts on a beat (Cold Snap), following the foes' actions (the Storm), a volley and then a staircase (the Hunt), following the foes' attacks (the Toll), in salvos from one unit (Barrage), see-sawing (Blood Pact).
- **Four roads are a status's, each on its own clock,** and five aren't a status's at all. A status the party stacks read as a build in play; it isn't what a build has to be.
- **Every build's sign is in a base kit.** At least one class shows each build's direction with a passive or one ability, before any piece, and most builds have two or three. It's a check on this set, not a rule for what a build is.
- **Every build has a route with a starter** (the Knight, Cleric or Ranger).
- **Every build has more than one kind of front** among its routes. Bonfire has the fewest: its keeper is a Knight or a Paladin.
- **Every build has a pack game and a boss game,** and says which is worse.
- **Every road has two or three engines of its own,** each built on a moment that does nothing today, and seven of the nine stand on a bridge.

**Set aside:**
- **Stranglehold:** a build on debuffs. Its denial can't keep a party alive at today's sizes. It comes back if the balance pass makes debuffs big enough to stand on.
- **Phoenix:** a build on falls. Nobody chooses who falls; revives are the net.
- **Ambush alone:** an opening with no tail. On the test parties it has the most distinct rhythm of the twelve, which is why the first strike is kept in the Hunt, and First blood repeats it.
- **Harvest alone:** spread as a build. It's each build's pack game, and the Storm's nature.
- **Lockdown:** waiting for the foe's wind-up. Stopping a cast is an answer, not a build.
- **Overflow's relay:** mana passed round three casters. Its links can't be made big enough to see without being a build's whole power. Its name is an engine now.
- **The chorus:** the party's own buffs as the thing stacked. Today a buff helps every build about equally, which is what a piece shouldn't do.
- **The gambler:** crits on everything. The Hunt's and Landslide's crits cover it, and it may come back as exotics.
- **Engines cut:** the Drought (the foes' MP is hidden), Twice read (a route through two kits), Tinderbox (Embers does it better).
