# The wheel, version B: the brief

Five colours for the game, found in the director's answers to problems the agent poses. The frame they live in is `sources/colours.md`: read it first, and its rulings bind the work. The director's intent is `intent.md`.

## What it's for

Five colours, each on one card (what it never does, the mechanics it prefers, its profile and style, what it can't answer), that pass the checks in `sources/colours.md`, What checks a wheel. The director picks or merges one wheel, and names it.

## The idea

The director invents best against a constraint: a specific problem, solved creatively, one at a time. The patterns come after, from material that's already alive. So the agent's job is to pose problems that are worth solving and that cover the game between them; the director's is to solve them; the colours are then sorted out of the answers by attitude. Sorting concrete, living answers is safe ground for an agent, because the meaning is already in the material. Abstracting a shape from the bare rules is not: it strips the game to numbers.

**A colour is held together by attitude, not synergy.** Answers grouped because they work together make a build, which is a deck a party ends as, not a colour. A colour is answers from several ways to win that share one temperament, whether or not they combo.

## How to work

Batches of problems, each ending with the director; then the triage; then the pick. Each session runs one batch or one stage and stops. Every problem posed and its answer are kept in `problems.md`; the page's link and each session's handoff are in `report.md`.

### 1. A batch of problems

**What a problem is.** One specific thing about how fights work under these rules that's worth solving: something that goes wrong, a tension, a job nothing does, two things that could meet and don't, a challenge a party has no good way to answer. A good problem:
- says what's wanted in its first sentence, in plain words, and is short;
- is tight enough to answer in a few lines, and open to many answers;
- is grounded in this game's own rules (the sources), and in what a player would notice, want or hate;
- never hints at its answer, names a theme, or asks for a colour, an attitude or a temperament: those show in how the director answers, unasked;
- leaves the form of the answer free: an effect, a way a unit behaves, a seat, a rule, whatever solves it.

**The first batch is posed wide.** Five agents, run together and each working alone, draft candidate problems: one for each way to win, and one for where mechanics meet across the ways. Each reads `sources/colours.md`, `sources/vocabulary.md`, `sources/combat.md`, `sources/primitives.md`, `sources/gear-rules.md` and `sources/challenges.md` (each challenge is a problem a party faces, and good ground for more). The session chooses and sharpens about twenty, spread across the four ways and the places they meet, and checks the spread against `sources/threads.md`: a word no problem touches is either a gap or a word with nothing in it, said which.

**Later batches are aimed** by the session alone, from the answers so far, as in any loop of questions: where the answers are thin, a way to win few answers serve, a tension between answers that pull apart, an attitude that's showing in only one way to win. About twenty a batch.

**The page:** each problem with a box for the answer, saved where the agent can read it back (the artifact's database). The director can skip a problem, or say it's the wrong question; both are information. Then stop until the director says the batch is answered.

**The agents' prompts give intent and ranges, never examples.** An example problem in a prompt becomes twenty problems like it.

### 2. The triage

When the director says there's enough, which is likely to be after about a hundred answers:
- **The answers:** every answer in `problems.md`, and the director's earlier answers in `sources/earlier-answers.md`, which count the same.
- **The director may sort them first,** in their own way, before seeing any agent's sort. If they do, theirs is the wheel the others are read against.
- **Three agents, each working alone,** each given `sources/colours.md`, `sources/challenges.md` and the answers, and none another's work. Each proposes a wheel of five unnamed colours (by letter), and for each colour:
  - its attitude, in a sentence or two;
  - the answers it holds, from several ways to win, and why they share the attitude;
  - what it never does, traced to its opposites, with the answers it couldn't hold;
  - its profile and its style in each way it leans to;
  - what it can't answer, named as challenges from `sources/challenges.md`;
  - the checks run, the answers left with no colour (the exotics) or in two colours, done two ways, and the problems a colour still needs answered.
- **The session compares them** and brings the director the wheels side by side, the colours they share and where they part. A colour that comes out of every sort is robust; one only one sort found is a question for the director. Where a colour is thin, a short batch of problems aimed at it can follow before the pick.

### 3. The audit, the pick, the question and the names

- **The audit:** the session checks the leading wheel against everything in the sources, for what's missing: a way to win, a mechanic or a challenge with no home and no deliberate absence; and for the failures below.
- **The director picks or merges a wheel,** and the session writes each colour's card into `wheel.md`.
- **The question the five answer** is named from the wheel, now that it can be seen.
- **Names last,** with the director. A name chosen early pulls a colour toward everything else that shares it.
- **Then the work goes back to the main line** with the director, where it's compared with version A's and joins the design.

## What fails a colour

- It's held together by synergy, not attitude: a build in disguise.
- It holds answers from one way to win only.
- It could sit unchanged in another fantasy game, or matches one of Magic's colours one to one.
- It's an element, or a role.
- Its profile mirrors another colour's, or the wheel gives every colour the same shape.

## Rules for the work

- **No examples** in anything written for an agent, and none of the director's illustrations written down as design.
- **Plain words.** A problem or a card says what it means in its first sentence.
- **Symmetry is a pull to resist** (`sources/colours.md`, A colour).
