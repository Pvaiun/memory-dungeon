Three ways to read it

The loot web

The 108 effects the web holds, drawn three ways: between the nine builds, between seven clocks, and with no frame at all. The dots and the lines are the same each time. Only where the dots sit changes.

  • A dot is a word: something an effect can give or want, like a Shield, a dodge or Burn. A bigger dot holds more effects. A dashed ring holds none.
  • A line is an effect that wants one word and gives another. A dashed line is what a status does by itself: Chill ends in a Stun.
  • Left off: swings, casts and Attack, which every unit has, and the six effects that name every affliction or every buff at once. They would join everything.

Zoom in to read the names, then drag to move.

The same 108, under the builds and under the clocks

A place is a build or a clock. An effect has a place under the builds when it names a word that matters most to one, and under the clocks when the agent could read a clock off its own words.

    Between each pair

    Every clock against every word

    How many effects run a word on a clock. A dot in the corner marks a build's own pairing: its clock and a word that matters most to it. Everything without a dot is a pairing no build describes.

      Near and far

      A road runs from one word to another when an effect wants the first and gives the second. This counts how far apart the words are by road, and which words everything leads to.

          What's solid and what's a reading

          • Solid: what each effect gives and wants. It's read off the effect's own words and was checked row by row.
          • From the builds page: which words matter to which build. Move a word there and it moves here.
          • A reading: each effect's clock. The agent gave every effect one clock, two, or none in a single sitting. Where a word sits under the clocks follows from that.
          • Not a list of needs. An empty place is somewhere to look for a job. It isn't a card until there's a job.
