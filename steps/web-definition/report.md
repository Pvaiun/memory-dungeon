# Defining the web: the report

The director's intent is in `intent.md`, and what they've ruled of the frame is in the brief, The director's rulings. This is what's left to settle, and the way of working that follows. Nothing in the game has changed.

## Decisions needed

1. **The ways to win, before the wheel.** Each colour's card names the ways to win it's good at, so the list comes first. It's drawn from what a fight is (a race between two sides' HP, with time capped by the enrage), not from the builds' rhythm table. A first cut, to argue with: **outpace** (kill before the damage piles up), **outlast** (still standing when they aren't), **grow** (stronger every moment the fight goes on), **deny** (they don't get to act), **turn** (their own doing kills them), **strike** (everything spent on one moment). Some look like old builds because both come from the same rules; a way to win is something each colour aims at its own way, not a deck.
2. **Who seeds the colours.** The director's phrases for some or all of them, or the agent's candidates built from temperaments (what a colour values and can't stand) and the game's mechanics. Not from the classes' fantasies or the builds: either would bring the old scaffold back in.

## The read

The four lines, the clocks and the threads describe how a party wins and what it's made of. Neither can be a pole: identity comes from what a thing refuses, and an abstract line refuses nothing (29 of the 40 pairs measured changed nothing). A colour refuses for a reason a player can feel. Colours are the missing layer between the mechanics and the decks.

## The frame

| Layer | Magic's | Ours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanics | keywords, card types | the threads, the statuses, the stats, the gear kinds |
| Poles | the five colours | the colours, new |
| Commanders | the commander, a signpost | the classes: a trunk in a home colour, versions that may lean to other colours |
| Ways to win | aggro, control, ramp… | a short list of its own, the designer's |
| Decks | archetypes, and all the rest | what a party ends as; none is designed in advance as a target |

## What checks the wheel, in place of the builds

On paper, before anything is built:
- **Coverage:** every colour can sustain and kill, and can field a party alone.
- **Spread:** each way to win is strong in about two colours, and each colour in about two or three ways, so no colour holds a way to win alone and no way to win is left out. This catches four decks landing in one colour.
- **Pairs:** every pair of colours has at least one way to win both are good at, and something one gives that the other wants.
- **Identity:** each colour reads as one thing, by the director, on its card.

Whether the decks that come out are fun is judged later: the director on samples, then simulation and the playtest.

## The way of working

1. **This step: the frame.** The two decisions above. Words only.
2. **The wheel.** Two or three candidate wheels, each colour on one card: what it values and can't stand, the ways to win it's good at, what it can't do (named as the challenges it loses to), its exclusives, and how it reads the shared mechanics. The checks above. The director picks or merges one.
3. **The pie.** The threads against the colours: primary, secondary, never, and how each colour reads a shared thread. Then measure: tag the web's kept effects and the core set's pieces by colour, for how much survives, which colours are thin, and what has no colour.
4. **The classes,** read through the pie: which colours each trunk and version carries. A kit or version changes only where a class has no home, or a colour has too few commanders.
5. **Authored gaps resumes,** making loot into the pie's holes.
6. **The run and the numbers:** the mana question, whether colours are shown, recruit offers, and simulation that the pairs are viable and a one-colour party isn't the default.

Nothing in the game changes before step 4.
