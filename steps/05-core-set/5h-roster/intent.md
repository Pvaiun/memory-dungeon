# Step 5h intent: the roster

The director's words from the session of Oct 28, 2026, in four messages: a brainstorm on agency in fights (a reviewer's read of the fights with no control, then a designer's reply), and their reactions to it. Recorded verbatim; nothing here is the designer's. Only the parts about this step are kept. Gear, formations and upgrades are step 5i (`steps/05-core-set/5i-synergy/`), whose `intent.md` keeps those parts. The read-back is the design gate.

## No control in fights (context for every step)

> I'm kind of convinced that the ability to affect outcomes during a fight is a mistake. I think it's creating a different game, and I think because humans are intelligent, they're going to figure out when the right moment to do something is, and the whole game is going to rely around pressing one button. Like, you're going to win or lose the same team, the same comp, because you pressed the potion button at the right time. That's fundamentally against the concept that this game wants to be.

## The roster

> So the first one, and I think this is the biggest one, is I would increase the bench size to like 5 or 6 units, not just 2. I would make recruits more common. Like maybe you get them at campfires or on treasure chest slots, you get a recruit that comes with a piece of gear. I don't know, something like that. I'll figure it out that or later. And we make it so that you can swap party members at any moment when you're not in a fight, not just at a camp. And we make it so that party members that are in reserves, like on the bench, get a, you know, MP heal whenever a fight happens. And then we increase the MP cost of everything, as well as the capacity of everything, by quite a bit, so that MP is a resource that drains over fights and doesn't really matter within a fight. The idea being, now we have the ability to basically have the player make, you know, parties that react to what they're seeing in the fight preview screen, and that they're forced to rotate their parties in order to keep their resources up, unless they focus a lot on support. So that's the first idea, and I think if the enemies were tuned in reaction to this new flexibility from the player, it might make for a dramatically more compelling game.

Their answers to the designer's reply (which proposed: MP back on the bench and never HP; pools sized for about three fights of casting, costs raised in step, MP regen in fights near zero, the spend-to-recovery ratio matched to the bench size; and the reserves rule removed):

> 3. Having played a lot, I think the roster and bench thing is super important. I think it's one of the pillars of how it's going to change the way this game is played. And I think it's something we should do sooner rather than later. I agree with what you're saying about measuring it in pools. And I also agree with the MP back only in the bench rather than HP as well. And I also agree with getting rid of the reserves rule. Like, you should just wipe if you wipe now that you have the chance to build your party with fresh people. We're also going to need to think about, like, more instances to train abilities if we have all these units, and probably a cleaner UI for it because it's going to be overwhelming. It's going to be a big balancing thing just because of how much mana tuning is going to matter here. But I actually think it's going to be easier to balance because we are looking for an average over many fights rather than something that feels right in every fight.

## Recruits and formations: more of them, without a choice after every fight

> The issue right now is that they're really rare to find within the rogue. Like, it's rare to get more than like one or two in a whole run. So we would need to make them more available, which we'll have to do with new recruits as well. I think being inspired by the pick three of classic roguelites would be a great way to infuse recruit and formation choices. The roguelite structure is like a first pass right now, so I wouldn't hesitate to mess with it a bit. Not at the same place, but we need to make them more available.

What "not at the same place" meant, in the next message:

> And by not at the same place, what I meant is I don't want this game to just be, you know, a bunch of choices that you're making every single time you beat an enemy or something like that. We already have, you know, a couple of random events. I think if you inspire yourself by the roguelites of the, you know, the classic ones with the maps like this, you can come up with a structure where formations and recruits are another lever of run, design, and reward. I also think it's compelling to have potentially rarities for formations. At least, you know, like 2 or 3 rarities. And I would even say that maybe when you unlock a recruit, it's kind of similar, where the quote unquote rarity would just be like which abilities they have unlocked (non-upgraded). There's no reason it's always the first one they start with unlocked in the list. And there's no reason they always only start with one, especially if you're gonna get a lot of units throughout a run. Like, roguelites want a lot of mystery, so I think having more events than less and having there be variants is cool, and these are cool rewards that we can give.

## The director's notes on the first design (verbatim)

Given the same day, after reading the first `design.md` (a bench of five, a leader plus three picks, MP regen as the bench's recovery). The second design answers them.

> Couple of notes, respond to them before you start building.
>
> 1. Bench of 5, roster of 8 with 4 to 6 chances to pick a unit with 16 classes means that the player is going to have the exact same build every single time. Whichever starter they pick, they can always gun for the same party. The only thing that would stop them right now, the only sunk resource, is upgrading abilities, which I guess you can't do as easily for later units. But I think that's a problem. Couple of things I would do to address that. First off, I wouldn't make the opportunities pick 1 of 3 for classes. I would make them pick 1 of 2, just like the starting pick, especially if you have this many opportunities. And we're expecting classes to be able to cross build between each other So we should try to force that creativity a bit more. I almost want to say that we start with 2 units and then get more later, but for the sake of balancing and stuff, we can stick with what we have for now. I would start with a roster of 2 flat. And if you think that's a bad idea from a design perspective, then I would do a roster of 2 and then a roster of 3 for Act 2 and a roster of 4 for Act 3. The reason I say that is because needing to swap out a unit that you've invested in for one that is more high synergy, or maybe even for the same unit again, but that you want to pick a different upgrade for. That's another option too, though I don't think right now you can see units you already have, so let's drop that for now.
>
> 2. Be careful about what you're suggesting with manner regen. I get the identity, and I think it is fine to start clean with no manner regen in fights. It might be a problem. Like, you might need to have such absurdly huge mana pools. It's also a lot more intuitive for units to recover flat amounts or flat percentages at the start of rounds went on the bench. Rather than it be proportional, wouldn't your mana regen stat just always be proportional to your mana stat, and therefore it's kind of just a nothing stat? Also, since you can swap gear anywhere, the way you're describing mana regen gear is very problematic, because there's going to be the cheese to equip the mana regen gear on your units that are on the bench and then change their equipment when they're getting off the bench. I think that's not a good place. I would use something different than the mana regen stat to give them the regen on the bench. In theory, I think it should be a percentage base, just because that's how the tonics work. But I'll leave that to you, to how to determine it. If you want to use the mana regen stat, you can. However, I would not use the gear giving that stat. I would just remove that stat from the gear, or give some of it siphon, or the alternate proposal is just keep mana regen in combat as a thing. It's just, you know, defaulting to zero, and so you're gaining it from gear, and it's like a choice. Those are the things that I would suggest, but not what you're proposing here. Give me your revised pitch before starting.
>
> 3. I would not suggest starting with the leader and 3 picks. I would suggest starting the leader with 2 picks, and then, like I said, a roster of 2, potentially, that grows. I think the game isn't a roster swapping game. It's a strategy. It's an additional strategy to making cool builds happen and upkeep your units. I could even see a world where you start with 2 roster and getting a 3rd or a 4th is like something you have to choose as a reward above something else. I leave that to your discretion.
>
> 4. Additionally, for the starter pick, I would add the same rule as you have added for the picks where you get the random ability on the heroes. Like, I would roll a random ability on the starter unit that you see, always only one. And I would not include that special exception for the Ray's abilities. Their ability is just like anything else, and give the players a choice not to select it if they don't want to. I think bad choices are sometimes part of what makes a roguelike compelling.
>
> 5. I agree with your pushback on raising MP costs and mana pools. I trust you more than myself for that. And I agree that benched units should preserve their cooldown. However, I have a suggestion, which would be that newly recruited units, the units you start the run with, and abilities that you just unlocked, not upgraded, but unlocked, start with their cooldowns half full or something like that, because I've had many runs in the build where I've had a dead unit, so I've unlocked Raise on my Valkyrie or Cleric, whichever one has that ability, and then died in the next boss because the cooldown is like 45 ticks, and that's like a whole fight's worth of cooldown. Giving one free cast to start, or like half free or something like that, does not seem egregious to me.
>
> 6. I think I challenge your target about the caster versus the wall in terms of their relative number of fights to run dry. Having a wall run dry after 5 or 6 and a caster after 2 is going to mean that if you have any sort of mana regen, you basically only ever need to swap out your casters and never your walls. And you're going to swap out your casters like constantly in a way that I think is going to make it hard to make a build. It's hard to tell what the right number is going to feel like because I don't know how the rest of it's going to play out, especially because tonics are still a thing in the game. So maybe your run out after two for the casters is fine. But in that case, I would make the gap way smaller and do two for the caster, three for the wall, or two or three for both, or something like that. If a wall can sustain an entire act without leaving the entering the bench, then they're not engaging with the mechanic at all. and every party is going to be built around the wall. Gun to my head, I would choose to go more scarce than not for this first version, since we're going to be adding a bunch of gear and formation and upgrades for abilities that are going to affect this and probably give the player more choice.
>
> 7. I would do a quick math pass on gear economy. Right now, gear is given in stores (where you can often buy 3-4 pieces in act 2, in chests, in random events, every fight you get a pick of a piece of gear, and every elite gives you a legendary gear guaranteed, and then pick of gear. In my experience, because one, the gear given is always something that mandatorily your class is able to use, according to the drop rules. So you're not like fishing for something. You're not fishing for a class because you got a cool piece of gear for them. And two, because there's only three gear slots and amulets can be equipped by anyone and armor is only limiting heavier equips, not lighter ones, you really quickly end up with your whole party, including your two reserves, wearing relevant armor, which then puts a big burden on the quality of that armor to be really different enough for you to want to switch or feel the need to switch. You can run the numbers, but I believe we're either dropping gear too freely, we're giving rarities too freely, or we're not restricting slots enough, or there isn't enough like common loot and too much rare and legendary, or we shouldn't restrict drops to only usable gear. There's something going on there that I think you should check as part of the reward plan, it dulls rewards, makes builds feel complete early and clogs the UI. And this is going to need to feed into the plan for the kickoff of the next AI for the gear, formation, and upgrades, since potentially part of the fix is more specific loot that combos together so you'll want to switch more. I won't start design on the next phase until we've decided on this plan.
>
> 8. Don't hesitate to do the pattern also of picking type A versus type B when it comes to choices. Like having to pick between a piece of gear or a formation, stuff like that. I know that a lot of games like, you know, Slay the Spire keeps cards really separate from trinkets, for example, but I don't think our game yet has matured in its design to know which things really are what matters for build making at what moment. And so right now I feel like in order to add variety, if you think it fits, the pattern of choices not always being between the same type is something to look at.
>
> 9. Finally, in terms of testing, the idea of a dumb picker, given the level of complexity we're adding now with all of the formations, the loot, the gear, I think it's going to be pretty much incapable of even coming close to the power of a human. Like, I was experiencing, even just in the current version, a dramatically better outcomes than the AI just because of using potions in a good way. And these choices we are presenting here are dramatically more complex. Maybe in a final balancing pass, there's value in, once all of the systems are in, including the gear and everything, in doing some sort of, like you said, like simulate all these different versions where they choose different things and then run through the whole run and balance it all. If you think that doesn't take too much compute power and tokens, I think there's value to that. But I feel like for the first pass, the best bet would be to construct yourself, you know, dozens of parties that you believe are like representative of the power level of the type of enemy that they're at, you're at. And then run fights against enemies with those levels and see what happens and use that for tuning, at least as a first pass, because we're also going to probably eventually randomize enemies to a certain degree. So the simulation thing is going to be like, the whole run simulation thing is going to become increasingly expensive, though my understanding is it doesn't cost a lot of compute time, so it's very likely worth doing after this step and the gear formation and upgrades step. It's just I've seen us spin for hours at a time running thousands of whole run simulations within one iteration, only to realize that like we got something super obvious wrong right at the start and it was a waste of time.
>
> I'm waiting on your response with regards to gear that has mana and how you want to handle mana regen. For the rest, mostly it's up to you, though obviously ask me a few questions if there's things you're not sure which way I'll lean. And then based on everything that you've decided, I want you to update the kick off with any relevant changes, especially if you're doing things to gear. And then I'll start the development on this and the design of the other one.
