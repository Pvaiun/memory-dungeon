# Step 5i, step 2: the slots

Oct 29, 2026, for the director to pick from. Each structure is judged against `philosophy.md` (Slots): pieces that can be kept apart, rewards that read at a glance, and choices that aren't "picking which of your favourite toys you wanted to throw in the garbage". The builds are step 3 and the pieces step 4, so no piece is named here.

## Outcome (Oct 29): step 2 closed

The director picked A, then gave the designer two ways to do its offhand and asked for the better one for build making. The structure follows the content: where pieces come from gets reshaped to fit what the builds need, after step 3. Their words are in `intent.md`.

**The structure:**
- **Weapon:** by kind, as today.
- **Offhand (new):** shared kinds of two or three classes, mostly three. Each class wears one kind, and a few wear two. The kinds group the classes along different lines from the weapons and the armor.
- **Armor:** locked by type. Three or four types, and a second type for certain classes, as the builds say.
- **Amulet:** anyone.
- **Mythic, a rarity above legendary, in any slot:** made for one class, about five or six a class. Like rare and legendary pieces, a mythic has no random affixes.

**Why the designer picked shared offhands with mythics** over a class-only offhand:
- **The web lives in shared pieces.** With a class-only offhand, a quarter of the loot would matter only through one class.
- **A mythic sits wherever its effect belongs,** in any slot, not only the offhand.
- **Mythics are the far end of what a build asks:** specific, late, and far stronger for the builds that want them. Everything below them is shared, so a build gets something from what most runs find before its mythic arrives.
- **The looting works.** Shared slots carry the base (the count below). Mythics are few enough to reach through a targeted room or event, such as the director's pick of one from three for a class, without making runs alike.
- **The bespoke work is bounded and goes depth first:** the dozen builds' classes get their mythics first.

**Open, for after the builds (step 3) and with 5h's numbers:**
- **Where mythics come from.**
- **Whether a unit can carry more than one.** Five or six a class is few enough to check every pair by hand, so no rule yet.
- **Mythics and branches** are both made for one class. A branch is chosen and kept; a mythic is found. Step 3 checks that they don't read alike.

## After your read (Oct 29)

**You picked A.** Armor can have four types, and certain classes can wear a second; the builds will tell us which (step 3). Your words are in `intent.md`.

**The offhand: you asked whether one class each makes for bad looting. It does, so I now recommend sharing it.** A crude count, assuming:
- about 27 offhands offered a run (a quarter of A's 110);
- 5h's two thirds of offers aimed at the roster;
- a roster of six;
- 48 offhands written in all, every class in one kind.

| Offhand kinds | Offhands a fighter can wear, a run | Acts where a fighter sees none | A given piece seen in a run | Pieces a class can reach | Offers no fighter can wear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One class each (A as written) | 3 | 33% | 65% | 3 | 66% |
| Pairs | 4.7 | 19% | 54% | 6 | 53% |
| **About three classes each** | **6.7** | **9%** | 49% | 10 | 39% |
| Threes, a quarter of classes in two | 8.1 | 6% | 49% | 12 | 31% |
| Fours | 7.9 | 5% | 48% | 12 | 32% |

With one class each, a fighter sees about one offhand an act, and a third of acts none. Each class reaches only its own three pieces, so runs repeat. Kinds of about three, for the same 48 pieces, double what a fighter sees, make an empty act rare, and triple each class's reach.

Recommendation:
- **Kinds of two to four classes, mostly three, each class in one kind and a few in two.**
- **They cut the classes along different lines from the weapons and the armor,** so each slot reaches a different set of neighbours.
- **The card shows the kind's faces, with the roster's lit,** as fits does today.

What it costs:
- **A piece is tuned for three kits, not one.** It's still 3 of 16, so pieces can still be kept apart. An offhand can't name one class's ability, so that depth stays with the branches. That also settles my worry about offhands crowding the branches.
- **Fewer drops are for nobody** (10%, was 33%), so offhands pull less toward a missing class. Weapons keep that pull, and 5h's third of offers for classes off the roster can aim at kinds nobody in the roster wears.
- **Five or six kind names.** The faces carry the reading, so nobody has to learn them.
- **New classes join a kind** and get its list, instead of needing their own from scratch.

I also considered the other fix: offering offhands to the fighting party much more often. That raises the count, but each class still reaches only its own few pieces, so runs repeat. It also means writing a full list for every class.

## A. Four slots, each reaching a different circle (recommended)

| Slot | Who wears it | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon | its kind's classes (2 to 7) | the basic attack, as today |
| **Offhand** (new) | **one class.** Each class has its own, an object from its theme. | bending that class's kit |
| Armor | one type: 5 or 6 classes, by role | how a unit in that role holds up |
| Amulet | anyone | what any class can carry into a build |

- **Keeping pieces apart:** the narrower a slot's circle, the better the designer knows what a piece will sit beside. An offhand only ever sits beside its own class's kit, so it can bend that class further than any shared piece. Only the amulet meets every class, so an amulet has to stay narrow through what it does.
- **Reading a reward:** an offhand shows one face, armor its type's faces, a weapon its kind's. Only an amulet has to be read for its effect. An offhand for a class the run doesn't have says so, which makes it a reason to recruit (5h offers a third of its gear for classes off the roster).
- **Choices:** each of a unit's four seats asks a different question. Across the party, the amulet is the "who gets it" choice. Armor is sometimes one too, when two members share a type. The offhand never is.
- **Your amulets-and-rings point, answered another way:** the effects that crowded the amulet move to the offhand and armor. The amulet becomes one pool of four instead of every rule in the game, and there's no rare ring to weigh against a rare amulet.
- **Load:** one more row on the unit page, and no new word to learn, since an offhand is named by its class.
- **Today's classes:** each gets an offhand, empty at the start. Armor is locked as above. Nothing else moves.

## B. Five slots: an offhand by kind, an amulet and a ring

| Slot | Who wears it | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon | its kind's classes | as today |
| Offhand (new) | its kind's classes. About six to eight kinds: each class wears one or two, and a kind is worn by one to four classes. Your example fits: a shield for several walls, a holy sigil for the Paladin alone. | as A, but shared |
| Armor | one type | as A |
| Amulet | anyone | what its wearer does: a trigger or a rule |
| Ring (new) | anyone | what its wearer is: stats, special stats, and any new dial a build needs |

- **The rare ring:** an amulet and a ring differ in job, not size. They never compete for a seat, so they're never compared, and a rarity means the same thing in both. A rule and the number it scales from can meet on one unit; two open rules can't. Your two-ring version (smaller rings, two seats, as in Diablo) also works, but it makes six seats, and I wouldn't on a phone.
- **Keeping pieces apart:** weaker than A. Two seats meet every class, and a shared offhand has to be tuned for several kits.
- **Reading a reward:** the offhand kinds are a second weapon table to learn. Amulets and rings are read for their effect.
- **Choices:** the most of the three: two open seats and a shared offhand. It also has the most toys to throw away, since two seats any class can fill is halfway back to today.
- **Load:** two more rows, and six to eight new words.

## C. Three slots, nothing for everyone

| Slot | Who wears it | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon | its kind's classes | as today |
| Offhand | one class | as A |
| Armor | one type | as A |

The amulet goes, and the offhand takes its seat.

- **Keeping pieces apart:** the strongest of the three. Every piece has a short list of possible neighbours.
- **Reading a reward:** every card reads by its faces.
- **Choices:** "who gets it" is rare: it comes up only when two members share an armor type or a weapon kind. A thread reaches every class only by putting it on all three armor types.
- **Load and cost:** as today. 5h's reward numbers hold as they are.
- **What it loses:** the open seat. No piece fits every run, so a build has fewer routes in, there's less of the chaos the philosophy asks for, and an exotic has to pick a circle.

## Why A

- **It adds the one circle the game doesn't have: a single class.** Weapons already reach small groups, armor will reach roles, and the amulet reaches everyone. Putting an effect in a slot decides who it can reach, so four circles give the web four reaches to work with.
- **It keeps one open seat.** Any run can use a found amulet, a build has more routes in, and an exotic gets seen.
- **It meets the goal of amulets and rings without a second open seat.**
- **It costs** one row and about a third more loot.

Not B: two open seats are halfway back to the problem you named, and five rows plus a table of kinds is the most to carry. Not C: it's the cheapest and keeps 5h's numbers, but it gives up the open seat and most of the party-level choice.

## What every structure costs

- **5h's numbers:** the seats and gear volume above. The gear counts follow 5h's own ratios: a run takes about one and a half times what it ends up wearing, and sees about three times what it takes. Its legendary count is set by how many legendaries fit a build, not by seats, so it moves less. Its rooms and its split between kinds of reward stand. You've accepted that 5h's numbers move once both steps land.
- **Interface:** the unit page's gear list (a scrolling list, so a row is cheap), the gear picker, a slot icon from the packs, and the cards' fits line.
- **Schema and code:**
  - new slot names;
  - each class names its offhand (A and C) or its offhand kinds (B);
  - `canEquip`: armor by exact type, offhands by class or kind, rings for anyone;
  - the rogue's slot list, and the sim tools' slot lists (`balance.ts`, `sim/kits.ts`, `sim/parties.ts`);
  - the rules document's Gear section, when it's built.
- **Engine:** nothing. It already adds up every slot the schema lists, so the structure alone needs no engine version bump.
- **Content:** all of it is new anyway. A one-class list needs fewer pieces, but every class needs a few or its seat sits empty. Depth first: the dozen builds' classes get full lists, and the rest get a handful of plain pieces until the set widens.

## What I'm least sure of

- **Offhands and branches (A and C):** both are made for one class. The line between them: a branch is chosen in training and kept, while an offhand is found, so it's where chance shapes a class instead of the player's pick. The risks are twice the bespoke work per class, and pieces that read like branches. Step 3 will show whether they crowd each other.
- **Three armor types may be too coarse a circle.** Each holds five or six classes, and the Leather six are a mixed bunch (archers, fighters, the Bard). Splitting a type later is an option.
- **The Geomancer and the Paladin cast in Plate.** Locked, they never reach Robes. That's fine if their builds are Plate builds; if not, they get a second type.
