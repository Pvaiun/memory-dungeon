# Method: how each design step is done

This project builds a game, and it also tests a way of making one. A human director sets the vision. AI does most of the designing, writes most of the content and tunes it, using simulation in place of most of the build-test-react rounds a studio runs by hand. The director's attention is the scarcest resource, so it goes on direction and judgement, never on volume.

## Roles

| Role | Who | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Director | The human | Gives intent, workshops designs, signs off. Never has to write a format or read a data file. |
| Interviewer | AI: the step's own session, or a separate one | Draws out the director's intent and records it. |
| Designer | AI | Turns intent into a design, and pushes back on direction that doesn't fit the game. |
| Tuner | AI | Builds the approved design, then simulates and adjusts it until it works, or shows where it can't. |

One session usually plays both designer and tuner. The roles are separate because each has its own rules.

## Direction is guidance

Nothing the director gives is a law. A designer who believes a direction doesn't fit the game says so, with reasons, before anything is built. A tuner who finds that a direction doesn't work, or works worse than an alternative, says so as soon as the evidence is clear. Two things never happen: changing a direction silently, and grinding around a direction that is the real problem.

How firmly the director holds an item sets how strong the case for bending it must be:

- **Firm:** bend it only with evidence and the director's agreement.
- **Lean:** the designer may bend it, and says so in the intent map.

The concept spec is the game-wide intent: its principles are firm, and the rest is lean. `DECISIONS.md` records what has been decided since. Both can be challenged like any other direction; until a decision is changed there, it stands.

When unsure whether something needs the director, ask: would this surprise them? If so, it's a challenge.

## The loop

Every design step runs three stages, and each ends with the director.

### 1. Intent: what the director wants, before the AI proposes anything

1. The step's session writes `brief.md` (see Files), starting from the step's **Ask the director** line in the design plan.
2. The director answers it in one of three ways: in the step's session; in a fresh session on this repo with `/intent N`; or by pasting the brief into any other chat and bringing back the result. When the input is a list (names, phrases, gimmicks, reference monsters), offer a small input page the director can fill in on their phone instead of a conversation.
3. The interviewer writes `intent.md`, reads it back as a short list, and fixes whatever the director corrects. That read-back is the gate.

Rules for the interviewer:

- **Ask, don't design.** Where an open question is hard to answer cold, offer options to react to, but hold back the AI's own design until stage 2.
- **Keep it short.** One sitting and a handful of questions. Follow up only where an answer is ambiguous.
- **Their words, not yours.** Record what the director said, lightly cleaned. Mark the items that were picks from the AI's options, so the AI doesn't end up quietly writing the vision.
- **Tag every item** as firm, lean, reference (with what to take from it) or idea (take it or leave it).
- **"I don't mind" is an answer.** Record it as the designer's call.
- **Don't re-ask what's settled** in `DECISIONS.md` or earlier steps, unless the director reopens it.

### 2. Design: a design that serves the intent, agreed with the director

The designer reads `intent.md`, the concept spec, `DECISIONS.md` and earlier step reports, then writes `design.md`:

- **The design,** in plain language. For content, the rules the content will be generated by, plus a sample, never the full set.
- **Intent map:** each intent item marked used, bent or set aside, with one line on why.
- **Challenges:** direction the designer thinks doesn't fit the game, whether from this step's intent, the concept spec or an earlier decision. Give the reasoning and the alternative. Raise these now, before anything is built.
- **Review sample:** typical cases, the extremes, and the items the designer is least sure about. Never only its best work.
- **Variants,** where the intent leaves a real fork. Forks about feel go to the director; forks about numbers go to simulation in stage 3.
- **Predictions:** what simulation should show if the design is right. Stage 3 checks them.
- **Open questions.**

The director reacts and the designer revises, for as many rounds as it takes, but each round asks only what changes the design. The director can ask for a random sample at any time, which keeps the curation honest.

**Gate:** the director approves. The approved design is what stage 3 builds.

### 3. Build and tune: the approved design working, or clear evidence of where it doesn't hold

The tuner implements the design as data, plus engine changes where needed. Then it loops: pick the biggest problem, form a hypothesis, change something, simulate, read the result. Each round is one line in `tuning.md`. Set a round budget at the start; when it runs out, stop and report what's left rather than spiralling.

What the tuner may change:

| Level | What | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Numbers, and anything the design left open | Just do it. |
| Report | Changes that keep what the design is for, such as swapping an ability that broke balance for one with the same fantasy | Do it, and list it in the report. |
| Challenge | Changes to a firm direction, to what the approved design is for, or to a recorded decision | Don't make the change in place. Test the alternative alongside the approved version and bring both, with numbers. Keep tuning the approved version unless it's blocked; if it is, stop and ask. |

Challenge as soon as one of these happens, not after the budget is spent:

- The same problem comes back after two rounds of tuning around it, and it traces to a direction.
- A version that breaks a direction clearly beats the approved one, even if the approved one passes. Suboptimal counts, not just broken.
- A prediction from `design.md` fails in a way tuning can't explain.

**Price the tightest direction.** At least once per step, also simulate a variant without the direction the tuner is working hardest around, so the report can say what that direction costs. Directions that cost little stay; ones that cost a lot get raised.

Rules for simulation:

- **Don't overfit.** Hold some builds and encounters out of tuning, and check the final result on them.
- **Check it holds** across seeds, and across simulated player skill levels where those matter.
- **Say how sure.** Every conclusion says how solid it is: "holds across all 20 seeds", "only for skilled players".
- **Balance isn't fun.** Pair the numbers with feel checks: an AI reads fight logs with enemy stats hidden, and says what it can tell about the enemies and what felt flat. Pick 2 or 3 samples for the director, in plain words: a fight retold in a few lines, never a raw log. Fight logs are dense; they're for the AI and for debugging.

**Gate:** the director signs off on `report.md`.

## The report

`report.md` is what the director reads. It's short and plain, and leads with what needs them:

1. **Decisions needed:** challenges (with both versions' numbers) and open questions.
2. **What was built,** and what changed from the approved design.
3. **What the numbers say,** in words, with how sure.
4. **Feel:** what read as flat or unclear, and the samples worth reading.
5. **Method notes:** a line or two on what worked or didn't in this method. The method is part of what's being tested, so update this doc from these notes.

After sign-off, add the step's decisions to `DECISIONS.md`, update the Status section in `CLAUDE.md`, commit and stop.

## Files

Each step gets a folder, `steps/NN-slug/` (for example `steps/04-combat-rules/`). The files hand the work from stage to stage, so any stage can start in a fresh session by reading this doc, `DECISIONS.md` and the step's folder.

| File | Stage | Written by | The director |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `brief.md` | 1 | The step's session | Answers it, here or elsewhere |
| `intent.md` | 1 | The interviewer | Confirms the read-back |
| `design.md` | 2 | The designer | Workshops it and approves |
| `tuning.md` | 3 | The tuner | Doesn't read it; it's the trail for later sessions |
| `report.md` | 3 | The tuner | Signs off |

Code and content data go where they live (`src/`, `fixtures/` or later content folders), not in the step folder.

**`brief.md`** has to work when pasted into any chat, so it's self-contained:

- A paragraph on the game and on this step, for a reader with no context.
- What's already decided, so it isn't asked again.
- 4 to 6 questions, with options to react to where they help.
- The interviewer rules above, and the `intent.md` format below, so the other chat's output can be saved as it is.

**`intent.md`:**

```
# Step N intent

How it was gathered (this session, /intent or another chat) and the date.

- [firm] ...
- [lean] ...
- [lean, picked from options] ...
- [reference] Game or work: what to take from it.
- [idea] ...

## Left to the designer

- ...
```

## How steps vary

Every step runs the loop, but not at the same weight. Each step in the design plan says what to ask and how it's tested.

| Steps | Intent | Tested by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2, engine | Almost none: confirm the engineering is the designer's call, and take any thoughts. Don't write a brief of questions for the sake of it. | Working. With no tuning, the design's predictions are the acceptance checklist, the round budget is a time budget, and `tuning.md` is a build log. |
| 3, harness | Light: what reports should show, and which players to simulate | Producing a readable report |
| 4–6, combat concepts, verbs and mechanics | References, and which concepts and roles they want | Functionality tests; there's too little content to measure balance |
| 7–12, traits, units, formations, gear, runs, encounters | References, feel, how it should play | Simulation and feel checks |
| 13–16, content | Heavy: names, phrases and gimmicks, often by input page | Simulation at volume; the director reviews samples and rules |
| 17, AI playtests | What a playtester should report on | It is the test |
| 18–20, quest board | How quests should feel, and edge cases | Sample boards and walk-throughs, not combat simulation |
| 21–23, interface | Reference screens, and how it's used on a phone | Mockups and AI playtests |
| 24, principles review | None | A checklist |

Research tasks, like the tech-stack brief, aren't design steps and skip the loop.
