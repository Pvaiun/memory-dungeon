# Step 5f intent: the combat economy, from the fights up

The director's words, Oct 27, 2026, after reading the 5e report and before playing its build. Recorded verbatim; nothing here is the designer's. Their framing, from the same conversation: "I'm really looking at this on a per-fight level rather than a macro winrate level, which I believe gives me a different and valuable perspective." They asked for challenges where warranted and for questions where the designer is unsure, "since a lot of this is very subjective. I let you gauge if you need to do that or not."

> Ok here's my short (long) list of thoughts. This is a huge rebalance and a rework of a bunch of stuff. It's filled with a bunch of subjective interpretations of the game. But I'm really looking at this on a per-fight level rather than a macro winrate level, which I believe gives me a different and valuable perspective. This is going to take a lot of time to get right, and you have as many hours as you need. You can challenge me where you think it's right to, and I think it's better to pause and ask me questions when you're unsure instead of just making a decision since a lot of this is very subjective. I let you gauge if you need to do that or not.
>
> In terms of methodology, right now the game functions at a macro level in terms of winrates and class picks, but it breaks apart in terms of the balancing of combat economy, relative value of abilities, and effectiveness of different strategies in battle. Right now we need to get that side of the equation working better before the winrate is something that really matters. Especially because it's quick to tune afterwards. I want you to start with the more intimate changes and thoroughly address them before moving to winrate and difficulty. Additionally, consider Rogue the main mode if you every need to tiebreak anywhere.
>
> **REACTIONS TO YOUR BALANCING**
>
> 1. Let's not raise HP on classes, it goes against their identity, we need to fix them in other ways.
> 2. I'm weary about the other stat changes, appended to the philosophy I'm presenting here. It's worth restating my observations and suggestions are based on the build previous to the one you just produced
>
> **COMBAT PACING**
>
> 1. Cooldowns are a bit too long across the board. (you seemed to have addressed this). Fights shouldn't feel like pure ability spam, but right now most units don't get to use most of their abilities before a fight ends. The rhythm is too constant, rarely do abilities feel meaningfully faster than others since they're all mostly one-offs in a fight
> 2. Similarly, bosses almost never perform their special abilities, even when fights go long. Their abilities should need to be played against, and occur multiple times per fight, not be rare occurrences
> 3. Basic attacks going from 32% to 66% of actions was a positive, and it feels better to watch but I think we went a little too far. This is related to the action economy and feeling like abilities for players and enemies are rare to trigger.
> 4. Continuing on the last point, almost every tick is occupied by someone attacking or doing something, which leaves over-time effects like status effects, channels, cooldowns, all of these things have no room to breathe in the action economy. Fights have no room to breathe, they're very spiky. They need less density of action. It's something to try
> 5. General action economy is flat across all classes, there are no higher cooldown and lower cooldown classes, very few faster or slower weapons. Every effect is paced relatively the same, asking things like debuffs and damage to compete at the same rhythm is a balancing nightmare. Few characters are spammy, none are slow and clunky (except Hoplite), and everything in between, there's little variety.
> 6. Cast times are extremely short across the board, with the exception of a few boss abilities (that rarely fire). This contributes to the spiky nature of combats, removes a balancing lever vs cooldown, removes distinction between different similar abilities of other classes, and I believe hurts overall fight pacing. Not saying every cast time needs to be 10T long, but right now the cast times feel largely irrelevant.
>
> **GENERAL BALANCING**
>
> 1. Basic attacks seem like a large portion of the damage for every class. This feels thematically inappropriate. On average casters seem to do more damage with basic attacks than non-casters, also true with staffs equipped on units that can also wield a weapon. I'm assuming this is because magic resist is usually zero where armor isn't. We need to tune down this balance. From a fantasy standpoint mages and healers should be doing more damage with abilities and statuses than they should with their basic attacks. At the very least each unit should have an identity of basic attack vs ability vs status damage and right now it feels flat across classes
> 2. Many single target randomly targeted abilities (especially egregious on those that give debuffs) are just too random and slow to ever really matter
>
> **MANA ECONOMY**
> Mana economy is completely off.
>
> 1. Mana essentially doesn't exist for non-mages. Their mana costs are so lower (sometimes 10x less than a mage!) that they have functionally infinite mana. It also just reads as a bug to see barbed arrow cost 10 and a heal cost 128, 13x more! It means mana regen on gear is a totally crapshoot stat since it's supposed to operate on two different scales. Mana needs to be normalized to values that make sense across the roster
> 2. Mana should matter for every single class just in different amounts based on their class identity, you should be able to gauge this from their class fantasy, but as a general rule the more a unit is a caster (magical or physical like the rogue) the more mana matters. However, all units should care about mana. If you need to gut mana regen as a base stat on most units to make this work, you can and should
>
> **ENEMY BALANCING**
> NOTE: I mention these because of some synergistic possibilities between some other changes. But if it adds complexity, I would prioritize changing classes before enemies. With the exception of resistance which seems important.
>
> 1. Enemies very rarely buff themselves (only 6 in the roster are capable of it) if more had simple buffs some abilities would gain new targets and utility. Only do if it's relatively risk free, it might just be better to rework those abilities.
> 2. Enemies almost never taunt, hurting the identity of certain classes, could be risky to change, doesn't need to be done now if now easy to envision working
> 3. Very few enemies have multi-hits
> 4. Enemies only have magic resist when they're weak to something or strong against something as a premade answer. They never have any blanket flat amount of mag resist relative to their strength the way units have armor. I think this is breaking the balance between magic damage and status effect damage applied by magic.
>
> **STATUS EFFECTS and STATS**
> Status effects are poorly balanced overall. Many are nearly useless and others very strong. Here are my takes, though they're not exhaustive and an analysis could take place to find the rest of the issues:
>
> 1. Chill is weak, bordering on useless: it's barely better than Slow at max stacks, and it ticks down by a third every time and rapidly so in practice it's strictly worse than slow. The fantasy was to slowly deep freeze a target with some upkeep, it feels like a dead status effect right now. It's also present on two of the weakest classes, which is telling. This likely needs a redesign, or an additional effect appended onto it, but for now we need to at least try executing on the current design. The desire was that the unit very slowly loses stacks, so chill is powerful if you can apply it multiple times to keep a unit. This is why the Hexer applies it on basic attacks and the Frostweaver applies it with 2 abilities.
> 2. Clarity is useless: on any target, it gives back less MP than it costs. It's also doubling the unit's MP Regen whereas you'd expect it work like HP regen where it provides a flat (most likely) or proportional amount. Lowering its cost does increase the value, but not the esoteric double mp regen stat effect. Players should notice clarity is a meaningful top-up for a price
> 3. Haste is maybe too weak. The smoking gun for this is that the bard is considered useless despite being one of the main applicators (more on the bard later). This is probably the point I'm least sure about since other appliers are strong. It might be a bard problem instead.
> 4. Burn seems to have an identity crisis. It ticks down too slowly, meaning it doesn't really fulfill its stacking fantasy, additionally it doesn't do very much damage so it plays more like slight bonus attrition damage
> 5. Shock seems to do too few damage, even at max stacks a trigger of it can do around the same amount as a basic attack
> 6. Bleed is by far the strongest damaging effect. A single hit on an enemy given bleed by an ungeared ranger does more damage than I've ever seen a single burn tick do. I don't know if this means bleed is too strong, but for sure the others are too weak
> 7. Damaging status effects in general are way too small of a proportion of the damage of the units that apply them, they feel like an afterthought in most fights, compounded by the fact that abilities rarely trigger during combat.
> 8. Abilities that apply status effects are dramatically skewed towards their upfront damage as their main reason for existing. An ungeared Ranger deals 5000 damage with fire arrow to apply only 18 burn stacks (540 of damage on a tick), It's also wildly inconsistent. The Pyromancer deals 2150 damage to every foe with fireball only to apply 18 burn on each one, The Valkyrie deals 3000 damage to apply 16ish shock. This is a problem across the board, unless it's a boss most status applying abilities just nuke the enemy leaving the status to do barely anything. This is compounded by the no-downtime action economy that means over time effects are quite useless
> 9. Status effects damage vs non-status effect damage on applying units needs to be looked at. The share of damage from status effects must be dramatically larger, it's hard to give it an exact number, but I'd start by aiming for somewhere in the 40-60% range depending on the unit's identity. A unit built around it like the Pyromancer might want that even higher. This number might be way off, you should use AI agents watching fights to find the right ones, but right now it's dramatically off.
> 10. Capless Sunder seems extremely potent. Is it eventually boosting damage past 0 armor? It shouldn't
> 11. Might is very finicky. It likely shouldn't tick down with time but with action. Right now its power level is so variable based on what actions the mighted unit has available to them. It's very hard to tell if it's doing anything at all or if it's too powerful
> 12. Weaken doesn't have stacks so it doesn't have the problem with duration might has
> 13. I have no clue if guard or ward are powerful, worth doing some math, but in gameplay I can't perceive them doing anything (maybe ok, math needs to carry these)
> 14. Taunt is powerful but only ever present on a single unit in the vast majority of cases (the knight's rarely triggers)
> 15. Silence last a tiny amount of time leading it to be essentially useless. Especially with how rarely enemies cast abilities (if they even have any!) I bet you could 5x the duration of this and it would still be underpowered
> 16. Stun is super situational, we should either look at stun targeting rules or more probably just increase the duration so it's an actually meaningful effect
> 17. Does Rot do anything? Do allies even heal? Rot seems like a total wash of an ability on the vampire
> 18. Regen seems to be a weak healing source, to the point where I sometimes don't unlock it on my cleric so I don't waste turns using it instead of the regular heal
> 19. Unsure on if thorns is too strong or too weak, math in simulations will tell us that. But remember, if a class is built around thorns that should be a significant portion of their damage just like the other damaging status effects
>
> **HEALING**
>
> 1. Healing is too important right now and found only on 3 classes, which forces them to be in almost every party. This is explicitly forced in the rogue mode. Eventually the solution will lie in giving people more ability choices so healing will be more widespread by choice, but right now we need more sources of healing in the game. I'm adding it to a few classes below to help with this
>
> **UNDERPERFORMING MAGES**
>
> 1. A lot of the squishier characters are underperforming now, partly because they just get nuked the instant combat starts. Lowering cooldowns across the board and giving fights more time to breathe will help
> 2. I think the other big problem is likely the aggro curve and units' aggro stats. The curve seems to make aggro not matter much beyond extreme gear/formation combinations but also the variance in aggro is very slight between units with 13 of 16 units having an aggro stat of either 90 or 100. This means squishy characters have no protection as the system was intended to provide them. Raising the variance of aggro stats this should help squishier classes, I would do this before modifying health.
>
> **CLASS BALANCING**
>
> **BARD**
> The bard is totally useless, here are my rapid fire suggestions and thoughts. A lot of this I think is because this is an economy and status effect class in a game with broken economy and status effect balancing.
>
> 1. The Bard is supposed to be a team buffer but their abilities have massive cooldowns, so even when put in the first slot their buffs tend to trigger only at the end of the fight, causing them to be functionally useless. Since they have status effect only abilities, I have a hunch their cooldowns should be dramatically shorter to fill the bard identity of the rapid caster supporting in small ways from the backline
> 2. Their passive should be more useful when mana actually matters on all classes, we need to confirm this, it should be a big part of their identity. I could even see it scaling mana given off attack to give them a weapon itemization choice
> 3. I would try combining Allegro and War Ballad by letting it give might as well as haste. Additionally, the rework of how might ticks down should help this ability find usefulness. It also needs a shorter cooldown to actually be usable in fights before they end. I would give it a random target but make it very powerful.
> 4. I would fill their open party slot with a team-wide heal, a small amount but with a small uptime, giving them the classic fantasy of constantly helping their team in little ways
> 5. I wouldn't raise their HP, it goes against their identity. I would start by lowering their aggro and their cooldowns first.
> 6. Thunder chord should be made better when shock is buffed
> 7. They can only use physical weapons despite nothing in their kit scaling from physical. I would consider adding attack scaling somewhere and giving them access to staves for a true mixed caster
>
> **HEXER**
>
> 1. The chill buff should help with its passive, right now it's not doing much. This as well as the frostweaver passive indicate that perhaps speed economy should play into staffs and ice staffs should be faster attacking lower damage weapons. This would help with both passives
> 2. Hush has a huge cooldown, as well as just applying what is probably the single worst status effect in the game (silence) to a single target. I ran a simulation that showed that it silences an ability only 2% of the time. Unravel also has a huge cooldown and simply removes a buff from a single target, something that foes rarely possess. I think HEXER needs a rework.
> 3. REWORK: I would turn the HEXER into the general debuff bot, the counterpart of the bard. They should apply the elemental debuffs liberally, though they might still be underpowered after this, we will need to see. They already apply chill so it fits the identity.
> 4. I would change Hush into a new short cooldown ability that applies shock and burn to all enemies. Meant to help carries stack their status effects
> 5. I would change Unravel to be a new medium cooldown ability that silences and dispels a single target, but it waits until either a foe is casting or a foe is buffed before firing. Situational, but it's consistent when needed
> 6. Slow and chill overlap. I would remove slow from enfeeble and replace it with scaling sunder stacks
>
> **PYROMANCER**
>
> 1. I think pyromancer mostly gets fixed from action economy, damage source spread on a class rebalancing, and burn buffs
> 2. Passive is really piling on with the Hexer and Frostweaver but let's leave it for now
>
> **FROSTWEAVER**
>
> 1. Chill rework should fix this hopefully. If chill stacks linger then hopefully the passive does some serious damage
> 2. Clarity is useless now, buffing it will help
>
> **PALADIN**
>
> 1. In the spirit of adding more healers to the game, I would add healing to Absolve and make it trigger even if there's nothing to cleanse
> 2. I think the passive is very situational and probably too powerful when it's relevant, might need a nerf
>
> **KNIGHT**
>
> 1. Passive is extremely situational, rarely triggers, and when it does it usually spells death anyways. It's one of only 2 taunt sources in the game. I would change it to say that the knight starts the fight with a shield (scaling off health)
> 2. Sanctuary is very powerful and overlaps the hoplite, I would change it to a new ability that gives Taunt to the Knight and clarity to an ally. I suspect with these mana changes we'll want more mana sustain sources as well
> 3. I would scale shield bash on health instead or armor, to give knight the health tank personality to distinguish it from hoplite
> 4. Instead of I would give the knight 5000 health and 4000 armor, once again to differentiate it from the hoplite
>
> **HOPLITE**
>
> 1. I would raise the Hoplite's attack to 4000
> 2. I would raise it's Aggro stat relative to 110 or 120
>
> **ASSASSIN**
>
> 1. The cooldown for vanish is particularly egregious considering how squishy the assassin is
> 2. Garrote has 50% more cooldown than backstab despite doing less damage at base. I haven't played a lot with Assassin but perhaps the silence buff will help this, either that or a targeting rule that's more intelligent. Open to other takes on this one since I haven't played them much
>
> **RANGER**
>
> 1. Ranger needs to pick between being a physical nuke or a status effect applier, right now they do both and they're amazing at it. I expect the damage sources rebalance will fix this. It's all about identity, maybe rending arrow wants to lean nuke but the others not for example
>
> **VAMPIRE**
>
> 1. I'm pretty sure Wither does literally nothing in 99.9% of scenarios since only a single enemy has heal. Unless we add heal all over the game which is a big change I suggest we just drop Rot for now. It isn't even needed against the bone priests which are the only enemy that have it.
> 2. I want to try something crazy here, rather than nerf the lifesteal of the vampire, I wonder if we can try it out as an exotic class. I want to replace Wither with a medium cooldown ability that costs the Vampire HP to cast that heals and gives clarity to an ally. It should be a substantial cost.

## How this step reads the intent

Everything above is **firm** direction unless it says "maybe", "unsure", "open to other takes" or "your call", which the design treats as **lean**. The rules of 5e stand (a status scales by its stacks; MP costs set by hand, now on one scale; Aggro by draw). The order is the director's: the per-fight economy first (pacing, mana, statuses, enemies' resistances, then classes), win rates and difficulty last. Rogue breaks ties.
