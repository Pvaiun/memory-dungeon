# The builds: state of play

Where the twelve builds today's loot was made for stood after real play: the evidence the nine of `docs/gear-design/builds.md` were designed from, and that the loot pass reads. It says what happened, how it looked from the player's chair, and where the two differ, why. It's the state of things, not proposals: the director's intent for the pass is recorded separately, and the questions it has to answer are at the end.

**How to read it.** The sample measures the builds and the game beneath them, and the two have to be kept apart. Some builds were fielded enough to show their own mechanism failing; some were barely fielded, so the case against them is reasoning, not observation; and the tuning that ended most runs in act I, where a party that took a build's pieces over defence tended to die, runs under all of it. The table under *Each build: what the sample can say* keeps the three apart. What read on screen in these runs is not a rule for what a build must be. The director's reading is in `docs/gear-design/philosophy.md` (*The director's intent*).

**Where it comes from.** The director's 23 saved runs (150 fights, Oct 31 to Nov 4, engine 0.17 and 0.18, heat 0), each replayed fight by fight at the commit it was played on, with the log on; all 150 replayed to the same outcome and HP. Every offer and pick, tagged with the designer's build data; what each party wore and sat in at each fight; where every fight's damage came from. And the director's own account of about 30 sessions, which is how those fights were seen, and which also covers sessions that weren't saved. Where the account and the fights differ, this page says what happened and where the impression came from.

## In brief

- **Two builds happened and carried runs.** Bonfire, built on purpose from Hearth and three Burn-on-swing riders, reached act III. A crit-and-Bleed build on one Assassin, with the Hunt's kill reset and execute, reached act II floor 11 with Bleed as most of the party's damage. Burn and Bleed were the two biggest damage sources in the whole sample.
- **The Toll happens from the kit alone, on floor one, in any party with a Bramble knight or a Taunting Knight.** It is the fastest build online and the default shape of a surviving party, and it ends at the bosses and two-foe elites the document says it fears, which is also where every other run ended.
- **The other nine did not happen.** Phoenix and Landslide were taken heavily and never worked as builds: Phoenix because a fall is nearly always the start of the loss, Landslide because its blow never came and what was taken was defence. Blood Pact's pieces were used as sustain. Cold Snap, Lockdown, Harvest, Overflow and Ambush never formed at all.
- **The game beneath the builds decides most of this.** Units fought with one ability known in 264 of 450 unit-fights and entered 194 of them with every ability still on cooldown; act I parties fought with under two of four slots filled and 17 of 23 runs ended in act I; 71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral; defence is what a party needs first and what the player takes first.
- **What reads as a build is a visible status the whole party stacks.** Burn, Bleed and Thorns were each understood, chosen piece by piece and watched winning. The rhythms the builds were designed around (the pass, the beat, the volley, the window) sat on top of that and never showed.

## The game the builds live in

### Survivability is the scarce resource
A tanky element is mandatory and a healer is not. Every run that reached act II had a Knight, Paladin or Bramble knight seated; two of the five deepest had no healer at all, and where a Cleric sat it cast one to four Cures a fight and dealt 98% of its damage with its mace. Someone fell in 49 of 150 fights and 23 of those were lost. The first to fall was the highest-Aggro unit in 31 of the 49 (the Knight 17 times); otherwise a squishy, against archers, Liches and Ghosts, or in the gap after a Taunt lapsed. The Knight ended 19 of its 65 fights knocked out, the Bramble knight 8 of 29, the Cleric 11 of 44. When the tank falls the others are near full and then collapse.

From the chair this is the whole game: squishies die in three or four hits, so anything a build asks of a squishy unit over a long fight (an engine, a stack, a blur, a tail) fails because the unit is dead first, and the Toll and the stone front feel like the only parties. The feeling that a healer was mandatory came with the tank's necessity; the fights separate the two.

The runs were all heat 0, at the knobs recorded fight by fight: act I regulars from −57 to +150, elites −7 to +60, bosses +47 to +50; act II regulars +31 to +280, elites +91 to +241. Four of the six runs that left act I died at those act II elites. That's the balance pass's business, but it's the wall the builds were tested against.

### Kits arrive one ability at a time, cooldowns carry, and nothing chains
At fight time, 264 of 450 unit-fights were fought by a unit knowing one ability, 152 two, 34 three. Cooldowns carry between fights and don't reset: 194 of 450 unit-fights began with every known ability on ten or more ticks of cooldown, 402 with something on cooldown, and in 44 the unit used no ability at all. The Haunted Hall loss (Oct 31 23:45) began with the Knight's three abilities at 29, 33 and 37 ticks; it fell at tick 14 having only swung. Cooldown words are 14, 22, 30, 40 and 56 ticks against regular fights of about 20 to 60 ticks and bosses of 50 to 90, so a medium ability fires once or twice and a long one often not at all. Training went to learning (63 picks) and versions (74) over power steps (35), which is the right instinct, but a one-ability unit is a basic attacker: the Cleric 98% of its damage from its weapon, the Hexer 86%, the Bard 85%, the Valkyrie 79%, the Paladin 76%.

This is why anything that chains, a hit begetting a hit, a kill feeding the next, a cast feeding the next, stalls: the second link needs an ability that isn't ready. Two chains did close, and they're the two builds that worked: the Hearth's Torch seat passing Burn on every swing (no cooldown), and Coup de grâce resetting on each kill. Both ride basic attacks or a reset, not a cooldown.

The builds are written as kits plus loot. The parties as played were sticks plus loot. A Frostweaver that starts with Stillness, a Cleric with Raise, a Valkyrie with Valhalla, a Paladin with Sanctuary each fought three or four fights contributing nothing of its identity.

### Act I gives one piece a unit, and runs end in act I
In act I the weapon slot was filled 99% of the time, armor 28%, offhand 24%, amulet 16%. By act II it's three slots of four. Seventeen of 23 runs ended in act I, eleven before the boss and six at it; five in act II, one in act III. Rung 2 of any build asks for two or three pieces on one unit plus a seat or a version, which the game supplies in act II. The builds that happened early did it from the kit (Thicket plus Briar) or from one basic-attack rider plus a formation (Hearth plus Kindling). Everything that needs pieces to meet was still waiting for them when the run ended.

### Statuses are the damage the player watches; small triggers vanish
In 26 of 150 fights more than half the party's damage came from statuses laid on foes; in the Bonfire and Bleed runs it was half to three quarters, fight after fight. Burn (437k), Bleed (397k) and Thorns (157k) were the three biggest damage sources in the sample; Shock (80k) and Chill (25k) far behind. A status is a counter on the foe and a coloured number when it ticks, so it reads as a thing you collect: Burn was understood as "the burn build", Bleed as "the crit bleed build", Thorns as what the tank does. The Hunt and Harvest, whose threads are targeting and spread, were never recognized as builds; Lockdown's pieces read as "what is this design".

Triggers sized below the statuses don't register. A few Might (6% a stack, spent one per action, so about three actions at +18%), a first cast free, a tenth off a cost (about 30 MP of a 1,000 to 3,800 pool), Clarity once a fight, a cooldown a few ticks closer: next to Burn and Bleed dealing hundreds a tick, these were never seen to fire, and some weren't. Bounty charm's kill reward was invisible; Coup de grâce's reset and Pack leader's coat's volley, which are also kill rewards, were most of a run's damage.

On screen a status's damage belongs to no unit. That is what makes those builds watchable, and it may be why the Hexer, whose Malison deepens everyone else's statuses, had the lowest damage share of any class (12%) and was benched or fell in 7 of 19 fights.

### What piles and what spreads
Burn and Chill pile: Firebolt, Pyre and Stoke aim at the foe with the most Burn, Frost lance and Frost arrow at the one with the most Chill. Peak Burn reached 43 to 59 stacks on bosses, and Chill reached its cap in most Frostweaver fights. Bleed, Shock and Marks spread: Barbed arrow and Lacerate land where the hit lands, and basic attacks pick at random. From the chair the board looked like it was all burning and nothing was concentrating, which is also true: Fireball and the Torch seat put fire on everyone while the hottest foe climbed. Cold Snap's failure is not spread; the cap is reached and shatters once, and the second shatter never comes (below).

### One unit's slots synergize; three units' rarely do
The strongest single thing seen was one Assassin in a full set: Thousandth cut, Venom vial, Gambler's coin, Night's edge, Opening wound, Coup de grâce. A player can see and assemble a synergy within four slots and three upgrades on one unit. Across three units, with random basic attacks and a tank that falls first, the twelve's web didn't form, with one exception: Hearth, whose three seats all serve the same fire, holding three units whose swings all add Burn. That took a formation about one thing and three riders that each pay alone.

The roster worked against it. Long runs swapped members 12 to 19 times, eleven of thirteen captives and strangers were taken, and legendaries followed the churn: of 54 legendary and mythic pieces taken, 11 were never worn in a fight and about a dozen were worn once or twice, because the wearer was benched (Still hand, Volley bow twice, Vestments of return twice) or the trigger never came.

### Formations
71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral; every act II fight in a formation. Ten formations owned at a run's end were never fought in (First light four times; Thorn hedge, Reaping line, Hearth, Watchtower, Heavy line, Arrowhead, Blood oath, The wheel once each). The ones fought in most are the plain ones about the front line: Pyre of heroes (11 fights), Shieldwall (9), Vigil (9), Wellspring (7), Ring of steel (6), The undying (6), Heavy line (6). The build-defining rares and legendaries were fought in once each: Hunting party, The pack, The vise, Relay; Hoarfrost line and Hammer and anvil three times; Blood oath twice. Taken against offered, Overflow's formations were at 16%, Thousand Cuts' 29%, Blood Pact's 31%, Cold Snap's 33%; Harvest's 73%, Phoenix's 65%, the Hunt's 64%, the Toll's 59%.

Formations read when they were about Aggro, Defense and protecting the squishies, and they were used that way: fitted to the party at hand by the seats' stat words, after the party was set. Seats were filled by whoever was there: Hammer and anvil with a Cleric in the Hammer seat and an Assassin, then a Pyromancer, as the Mason; Hoarfrost line with a Knight and a Bramble knight as the two Frost seats; The pack with the Cleric as the Lead; Spur with a Bramble knight as the pacer; The vise on a party with no Stun or Silence. Two runs cycled through every owned formation before each fight, 26 changes before six fights in one. Engine formations didn't read: Wellspring felt like a debuff (and was the one engine formation fought in at all, seven fights, for its plain promise of mana), The choir's no-regen a risk, Relay never started, Gaol added nothing visible, The ambuscade plainly worse; "lighter kit" and several cards were cryptic. The intent that a new formation makes you rethink the party didn't happen: the party was rethought for other reasons and the formation followed. The mountain and Thorn hedge, which read well from the chair, are from sessions that weren't saved; in the saved runs The mountain was never offered and Thorn hedge never fought in.

### What the player takes
Build-tag weight taken against offered: Cold Snap 52% (a small sample, mostly Frostwalker's furs and Icebreaker), Thousand Cuts 49%, the Toll 46%, Phoenix 46%, Overflow 43%, the Hunt 42%, Lockdown 42%, Harvest 39%, Bonfire 39%, Blood Pact 38%, Landslide 38%, Ambush 31%. Among far-end legendaries, Phoenix's were offered 26 times and taken 9; Blood Pact's 17 and 5; Bonfire's 3 and 1. 86 tonics were drunk against 62 potions and one phoenix feather in 150 fights. Blood vial was worn one fight, Blood covenant two.

The hand is consistent: defence first, because the tank falls first; spread and openers last, because a piece that reaches one more foe loses to one that does more to the foe already hit, unless the slot is empty. Death and HP aren't spent: a knockout carries into the next fight and can't be potioned, so Phoenix's risk dwarfs its payoff, and HP is what the game drains, so paying it was never considered and Blood Pact's pieces were taken as healing. Preconditions the fights rarely supply are worth nothing: Silence comes from two enemies (the Wraith and the Silent One); Stun from four (the Shieldman's and Mummy's Shield slam, the Ogre's and Troll's Flatten), two of them common in act I, still too few to build around; a wind-up was caught by the party five times in 150 fights.

### Classes
The Frostweaver is weak and was played that way: dismissed from one roster, benched in two others, 61% of its damage from its weapon. The Pyromancer is not: it ended 4 of 28 fights knocked out, fewer than any other caster, and carried two of the five deepest runs; the impression that it dies in most fights isn't in the saved runs. The Stormcaller had the highest damage share of any class (39%) in its 11 fights and was knocked out in 3. The Bard was seated in five fights across two runs and traded or benched otherwise, so nothing can be said of it from the fights; from the chair it was a dead unit. The Hexer, as above, is the status amplifier the player can't see. Evasion can't be judged from the fights: no evasion party lived long enough. From the chair it is not a defence as a probability, only near its cap, where one or two misses still kill; Skirmish line was fought once and lost, Duelist's leathers worn twice by units that drew nothing, Windrunner's garb once (Tempo climbed to 11, then the Monk fell).

## The builds, one by one

### Bonfire: happened, rung 3, the deepest run
Nov 3 16:46 reached act III on Paladin, Hexer and Pyromancer in Hearth (Keeper, Stoker, Torch), chosen one piece at a time: Hex of flames, Zeal, Kindling, Slow burn, Flashfire, Pyre of judgement, Hexweave mail and, at the end, Crown of embers. Burn was over half the party's damage in every elite and boss fight but Bomb Nest (37%, with Shock the rest); peak Burn climbed from 27 to 59 stacks across the run; the Torch seat did the passing-on. A second run (Oct 31 23:45, Knight, Pyromancer, Ranger in Shieldwall) reached act II floor 11 on Kindling, Fireball and Pyre with Burn at 28% to 58% of its damage, without chasing it.

It read as "the burn build", a status to collect across gear, versions and formation, good in a tank party because tanks add Burn too. The rhythm didn't show: kills in the Bonfire fights were evenly spaced, not each sooner than the last, and the fire passing from a dying foe never appeared. Its pieces were there and passed over: Wick offered once and skipped, Ember heart once in a shop and not bought, Kindled robes twice and not bought. Burn did hold on one foe (the casts aim at the hottest), and the whole board burned around it.

### Thousand Cuts and the Hunt: happened as one crit-and-Bleed build on one unit, rung 3
Nov 3 02:20: an Assassin with Thousandth cut (Cuts climbing to 17), Venom vial, Gambler's coin, Opening wound, Coup de grâce and Night's edge, a Ranger with Deep barbs, Pack leader's coat and a Briar totem, and a Knight in Heavy line's Holder seat. Bleed was 46% to 64% of the party's damage in act I's elites and boss and 28% to 48% in act II; kills came as a staircase (the Packmaster fell at ticks 24, 28, 31, 39, 54); Coup de grâce was up to 83% of the Assassin's damage in act II. The run died when the Assassin was benched for a bare Hexer against seven hellhounds, with the Knight carried in knocked out for a tick-one Valhalla.

By the document this is two builds, and the player found them as one thing and knew it as a crit-Bleed build (the Hunt's name was confused with Ambush's). The Hunt's kill threads worked: the reset and the execute were most of the damage. The Hunt's defining thread didn't: Marked was on the field in about twenty fights across six runs (Marking knife in three runs, Gravemark in two, Huntmaster's quiver, Pack leader's coat, Bounty charm three times, Hunting party and The pack once each) and never changed anything the player could see. It steers basic attacks only; abilities keep their own targets; so the party's picks never agreed and no one noticed a marker. It was one unit's strength. Thousand Cuts as a party never happened: the Bard was absent, the casters frail, the Ranger wanted elsewhere; Flurry wraps was worn ten fights across two runs on a Monk and a Vampire, Hummingbird charm seven, each as a stat stick.

### The Toll: happens from the kit, rung 1 everywhere, rung 2 once
Thorns was the Bramble knight's biggest damage source (44% of its own over 29 fights; 67% to 89% against wolf packs). The Knight's Hold the line made a Knight an anchor with no gear (77% of its damage from Thorns against Night Wolves). Spinebark pole, Briar totem, Portcullis, Urchin shell and Ring of steel's Beacon were taken and used. Rung 2 happened once: Nov 2 17:54 (Bramble knight with Briar totem beside a Vampire) ran Bleed at 29% to 53% of its damage from its third fight to its eleventh.

It is the default because being tanky and Taunting is already necessary, so damage from being hit comes free; it comes online on floor one and works sooner than any other build. It ended where every run ended: at the Ogre Chief, the Packmaster, and twice at the Lich Lair, bosses and two-foe elites that barely swing, which is the document's list of fears. Whether its pieces add anything past rung 1 is untested: Thorn hedge was taken twice and never fought in, Thornheart plate and Heartwood mail never offered, the dodge that pays worn twice by units that drew nothing.

### Phoenix: taken most, functioned as insurance, never as a turn
Pyre of heroes, Vigil and The undying were fought in 26 fights across seven runs; Lamp of vigil, Martyr's locket, Last bastion, Bones of the returned, Thrall's chalice, Mantle of the fallen, Challenger's plate and Phoenix ember were all worn. Someone fell in 49 fights and 23 were lost. Eleven revives happened, six of them the after-the-fight stands of Lamp of vigil and The undying; each of the five in-fight revives was followed by the revived unit falling again before the fight ended. The martyr's fall fired Pyre of heroes once (the Chief's Return, won) and The undying three times (the others healed to full, the Paladin back after the fight): the one place the build worked as written. Last bastion on the Martyr seat held the Knight at one HP in two of its three fights there, so the seat's flare never came while it could have mattered. The return with everything ready never happened in a fight. One phoenix feather was used in 23 runs.

A fall is nearly always the start of the loss: the tank goes, the survivors are near full, and fall payoffs sized at a heal and a few Might do nothing against what follows. A knockout carries into the run and can't be potioned, so the risk dwarfs the payoff and there is no agency over who falls or when. The document's own warning, that the second rung is thin, holds.

### Blood Pact: functioned on one unit, never as a party; its pieces used as sustain
Blood oath with a Vampire Bleeder, Blood covenant and Bloodsworn mail (Oct 31 13:28): the Vampire took 8,459 and healed 8,449 in the fight it lost, alone, after the Cleric fell at tick 11 and the Assassin at 21; Shared feast had nobody to feed. The Wellspring Vampire of Nov 2 17:54 with Savage drain was the best Vampire seen (31% to 44% of its party's damage), and that run's Cleric wore Phylactery and Censer mace: the donor pieces work as healer pieces. HP as a cost showed up in one fight, because HP is what the game drains and paying it was never considered.

### Landslide: did not happen; what was taken was defence
Its pieces were the most worn of any build that didn't happen. Earthmother totem was worn nine fights by a Ranger in a party with one Shield source; Worldbreaker six fights by a Knight knocked out in three of them; Cornerstone maul one, Heartsplitter three, Icebreaker three, Rubble plate five, Spiked bulwark two, War horn one, Stone totem never; Millstone offered four times and never taken. Hammer and anvil was fought three times with a Cleric as the Hammer. The Geomancer fought 20 fights and never sat in a Hammer seat or swung a Millstone or a Cornerstone maul; its blows were Rockfall (13% to 58% of its own damage) and the crits that came while its Shield held and stopped when it broke (60% of its party's damage in one Snake Pit, five to eight crits a fight). No fight showed a few huge blows; the biggest single hits in the sample were Coup de grâce, Backstab and Skewer. The runs that carried the stone kits ended at the Ogre Chief twice, the Packmaster, and the Lich Lair; none passed act II.

From the chair it felt like the best build, effective and interesting, with every piece good alone and many routes. What was valued was Shield, crit and Taunt each on its own, and the keyword arriving often read as a build being assembled; the build was never done. Its popularity is a measure of how overwhelmingly defence matters right now, not of the build. One thing it is written toward did happen, on the wrong class and unplanned: Rubble plate on a Knight with Stonewall and Earthmother turned the Shield breaking into half the party's damage in one act II fight, the stone becoming the blow.

### Cold Snap: attempted four times, never functioned
Twelve shatters in 150 fights, at most three in one, nine fights with any. The Frostweaver fought 16 fights in three runs: Chill reached its cap in most of them, shattered about once, and thawed. Hoarfrost line's two Frost seats went to a Knight and a Bramble knight; Chill peaked at 2 to 8 and never shattered in those three fights. Frostwalker's furs (seven fights) and Winter's chain (one) need shatters to do anything. Rime knuckles, Heart of winter, Frostbite bow and Primer of frost were each offered once and skipped.

The build is clearly signposted and the player chased it. The stacks do pile, since the lances aim at the coldest foe, and the cap (12 stacks, thawing one per 14 ticks, 250 damage a stack and a Stun at the cap) is reached; the shatter spends them, and the next application is a cooldown away, so the second shatter doesn't come. The rhythm, bursts on a beat you can count, was never on screen. No tank or healer adds Chill, so the Frost seats fall to slow Plate units or the party has no front.

### Lockdown: the worst; pieces taken, never a build
Stuns on foes came one or two a fight from Shield bash, Tremor and Pressure point (8 ticks each); Silence once a fight from Unmake or Garrote (16 ticks); a wind-up was caught five times in 150 fights. Chains of light was worn four fights by a Paladin whose Smite rarely fired. The vise was fought once, by a party with no Stun or Silence. Idol of binding was offered five times and never taken; Opportunist's charm four times, taken once, never worn; Still hand taken, never worn; Gaol never offered. Measured blow and Frozen moment, the wait-for-the-wind-up versions, were each trained once; the unit was then benched or the run ended.

The window is rare and short, the player can't make it happen, and nothing coordinates the pile-in; the cards read as a design without a plan. Enemy Stuns exist in the version played (four enemies) but are too few to build around, and nothing in the loot does.

### Harvest: rung 1 by accident, the rest never
The Stormcaller dealt 49% to 61% of its party's damage against packs (Nov 1 17:35), most of it through Thunderstorm. Oathkeeper on a Knight turned a Taunt into a sweep that was 27% to 79% of the Knight's damage, but the Knight's share of the party's was 3% to 10%. Sweeping blade on a Bramble knight, Chain wand on a Bard, Cataclysm staff on a Cleric whose third ability was Raise: the spread pieces landed on units with nothing to spread. Stormweave was offered four times, Last page twice, Storm pike once; none taken. Reaping line taken once, never fought in.

Area was valued as raw power, never recognized as a build: a piece that reaches one more foe lost on its slot's opportunity cost to one that did more to the foe already hit. The field never fell together.

### Overflow: never, even a little
Wellspring was fought seven times, with the two cheapest casters in the game as its Spenders, for its plain promise of mana. Relay was fought once; The choir never offered; Horn of the keep skipped for a recruit; Annotated grimoire skipped; Bell of the hour taken and never worn; Archmage's mantle worn three fights on a Bard. Nobody cast all fight, because nobody had the abilities to cast; the mana problem the build is for was felt in every run (86 tonics; the Monk under 150 MP at most fights' ends, the Frostweaver and Stormcaller at zero). Its links are sized below perception, its engine seats are the squishy units that fall first, and its cooldowns are too long to see a link twice.

### Ambush: taken least, never a build
First light was owned five times and fought in once. The ambuscade was offered once and First light taken over it. Knives drawn, Quietus (twice) and Drawn steel (twice) skipped; Horn of the charge never offered; Volley bow taken twice and never worn; Ready stance worn two fights on a Bramble knight; Shadow cloak worn once. The Assassin's Vanish fired one to three times a fight in the early runs; its Hidden opener is part of the crit-Bleed set above.

Nothing in the fight's text marks the opening as a moment; first-X effects run out in fights that run long; the party that functions while an Ambush is assembled is the tank party, and picking for Ambush loses the run. An opening with no tail isn't viable on the way.

## What there is to build on

Facts that point somewhere, without saying where.
- **A status the whole party stacks reads as a build at once and carries runs.** Three of five have done it (Burn, Bleed, Thorns). Chill reaches its cap and stops; Shock was a byproduct. The pieces that made these builds are basic-attack riders and a formation seat that spreads them: no cooldown in the chain.
- **Upgrades that change what an ability aims at or when it resets carried.** Coup de grâce (up to 83% of a unit's damage), Gutting arrow, Deep barbs, Hex of flames, Zeal, Hold the line. The versions taken most were the ones that put a status on a swing or made a unit tankier; the wait-for-the-wind-up versions were taken once each and never got a fight.
- **One formation made a three-unit synergy, and it was the one whose three seats serve one thing.** Formations whose seats are about the front were used; formations whose seats are an engine were refused.
- **A single unit's four slots are where the player builds.** The deepest synergy seen was on one Assassin.
- **Marked fires and is invisible.** The focus thread exists in the engine and on the loot and never reached the player.
- **The Toll is first online.** Anything that wants to be a build in act I has the Toll's head start to match, from the kit, by floor three or four.
- **Half the loot never surfaced.** Ten mythics were never offered; the far ends of Bonfire, Cold Snap, Lockdown and the Toll were offered once or never; eleven legendaries were taken and never worn. Whether those pieces work is unknown, not refuted.

## Questions for the intent

The director's answers are in `docs/gear-design/philosophy.md` (*The director's intent*) and `docs/gear-design/builds.md`.
- **Should a build be reachable from one unit's kit plus one piece, in act I?** The two that happened early did it that way. The ones that didn't all need two or three pieces to meet on one unit, which act I doesn't supply.
- **Should the kit carry the build, with loot steering it, and should kits arrive whole sooner?** Units fought with one ability known in 59% of unit-fights and with everything on cooldown in 43%. Either abilities arrive sooner and cooldowns don't carry so far, or the builds are written for basic attacks and one ability.
- **Is a fall allowed to be anything but the end?** Phoenix needs falls that aren't losses; in play they were. Either the martyr's fall comes early and cheaply, or the build is about standing back up between fights.
- **Can HP be a currency while it's the scarce resource?** Blood Pact as written depends on yes; play says no.
- **Formations: chosen for the party, or the party for the formation?** They were fitted to the party afterwards by stat words, or left at Neutral. If a formation is to make you rethink your party, its promise has to be one the party can't refuse, and the party has to hold still long enough to build toward it.
- **Which rhythm, if any, has to be on screen?** The statuses were the watchable damage, and the builds built on them happened. The pass, the beat, the volley and the window didn't show. Is the twelve's rhythm space still the frame, or is "a status the whole party stacks, plus one turn" the frame the game can show?
