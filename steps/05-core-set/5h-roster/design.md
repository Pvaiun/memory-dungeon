# Step 5h design: the roster, MP across fights, and the reward plan

The director's words are in `intent.md`. This is the third version, after two rounds of their notes (session of Oct 28, 2026). They gave the go to build it. Gear, formations and upgrades are step 5i (`../5i-synergy/kickoff.md`). This step builds the structure they're found in, the reward economy they drop into, and the training screen they'll fill.

## The idea in one line

Every fight's preview is a chance to adjust the party from a small roster. Fighting costs MP and sitting on the bench restores it, so a party is a build to keep running, not a list to rotate. Loot comes in enough volume, legendaries above all, that a run can find the pieces its build wants and swap toward them.

## The roster

- **Party of three, bench of two at the start (a roster of five, as today), up to four on the bench.** A third and fourth seat are rewards you choose over something else: one of the boss's boon options, and an occasional event. A small roster with one-of-two recruit offers forces creativity (the director: otherwise every run guns for the same party), and a seat costs a reward.
- **The start draft is unchanged:** the leader, then two picks of one from three. While the party has no healer, one of the three heals. It's its own system and keeps its screen.
- **Every starting unit, the leader included, knows one ability, rolled at random** and shown on its card. That includes Raise and Valhalla. A bad roll is a choice the player can decline.
- **Recruits are offered one of two.** A recruit's rarity is what it knows: one, two or all three of its abilities, at random, leaning higher by act. Recruit offers never show a class already in the roster.
- **Swaps any time outside a fight.** The campfire's SWAP tab goes. A full roster sends someone home to take a recruit.
- **The reserves rule is gone.** A wipe ends the run.
- **Cooldowns:** benched units keep their cooldowns as they left them. **Fresh abilities start half charged:** every ability of the starting party, of a new recruit, and an ability just learned (not upgraded) begins with half its cooldown already run.

## MP: drained by fighting, restored on the bench

- **No MP regen in fights by default.** Every class's MP regen stat goes to 0.
- **MP regen stays as a stat on gear** (and on Caravan's Quartermaster seat): a fighter wearing it regains MP during fights. That makes it a build choice. It only works in fights, on whoever wears it, so there's no bench cheese. The share it gives is raised so a piece is felt.
- **The bench recovers a flat share of max MP after each fight it sat out.** Every class is the same and no gear touches it, like a tonic. It counts from the max the member last fought with (the rule REST and tonics follow), so +MP gear worn on the bench adds nothing. It starts at 50%, then is tuned.
- **The target: from full, every class runs dry in about two to three fights** (casters two, walls three), erring scarce. **Tonics are in the check:** every won fight offers a potion or a tonic, and a tonic gives one member half its pool. So a player who always takes the tonic covers a big part of one member's drain. The targets have to hold with that player in mind, not only without tonics. If they can't hold with the tonic at half a pool, I bring the numbers before changing it (it's your 5g pick).
- Costs and pools are tuned class by class. There's no global scaling, which you agreed changes nothing alone.
- **Fighters' other MP:** Clarity, Refrain, Siphon, the Soul jar, tonics and FOCUS. That's the support path.
- **Unchanged:** the act's start restores everyone. Free play loses in-fight regen too, since it's one rule in the pack.

## The reward plan

### How much gear a run needs, worked out from the roster

- **Seats.** A roster of five to seven has 15 to 21 gear seats. The three fighting carry 9 of them, and the build lives there.
- **What a good run looks like.** By act I's boss the party wears six or seven real pieces, the first build taking shape. By act II's boss the party is full and the bench half geared. By act III the roster is nearly full, with a legendary or two on each fighter.
- **Churn.** Builds change as recruits and legendaries arrive, so a run takes about one and a half times what it ends up wearing. It sees about three times what it takes, because most offers are a pick of three.
- **So a full run takes about 28 pieces (about 9 an act) and sees about 80.**
- **Legendaries are the build-makers.** Each names two to four classes. If about one in three legendaries a player sees fits what their run is doing, then two fitting legendaries an act means **seeing about eight legendaries an act (about 25 a run) and taking about four (about 12 a run)**.

Today, from 300 runs of the simulated player, a full run takes about 17 pieces, 4.7 of them legendary. That undercounts what a human gets (a human buys more at the shop), but the offers are the system's, and they come out at about a third of the legendaries this plan wants. You were right that the problem isn't too much loot. The earlier version of this design cut it, and that was wrong. What the numbers do show is that nothing is offered for classes off the roster (0.05 pieces a run), so nobody fishes for a class.

The rogue has 43 legendary pieces. A run that sees about 25 sees most of them, so 5i's new pieces matter for variety.

### The plan

Most choices stay between things of one kind (a pick of three pieces of gear). Some, in the more exotic places, are between kinds. That's about 60/40, as you suggested.

| Where | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular fight | Gold, then a pick of 3, mostly gear (common 55%, uncommon 33%, rare 12%), sometimes a tome, a recruit or a supply | Gold, then a pick of 3. About 60% of fights offer three pieces of gear; the other 40% mix gear with a tome or a formation. Gear rolls common 35%, uncommon 30%, rare 22%, **legendary 13%**. **No supplies**, since every won fight already drops a potion or a tonic. No recruits: they have their own rooms. |
| Elite | Gold, a legendary outright, then a pick of 3 rares | Gold, a legendary outright (kept), then a pick of 3 rolling **rare 60%, legendary 40%**; sometimes the pick is mixed (a rare piece, a formation, a recruit). |
| Chest | A rare (70%) or legendary (30%) piece | A legendary (45%), a rare (35%), or a cage: a recruit with a piece suited to it (20%). |
| Shop | 6 pieces a visit (2 common, 2 uncommon, 1 rare, 1 legendary), 1 recruit, 1 formation | 6 pieces, **2 of them legendary** from act II, 1 recruit, 1 formation. |
| Boss's boon | A pick of 3: legendary, formation, recruit | A pick of 3 from: a legendary, a formation (rare or better), a recruit (two or three known), **a seat at camp** (up to four). |
| **The Muster** (new room) | | A pick of **1 of 2** recruits, or none. |
| **The Drill ground** (new room) | | A pick of **1 of 2** formations, or TRAIN two members instead. |

- **A third of offered gear** (outside the shop) is **for a class not in the roster**, so a piece can make you go looking for a class. Recruit offers lean toward classes a bagged legendary is for, as today.
- **A piece can be dropped from the bag any time**, so more loot doesn't clog the screens. Selling stays at the shop.
- **The new rooms:** about two Musters and two Drill grounds an act, on different paths, so routing toward a recruit or a formation is a choice. Also a stranger at the fire at some campfires (one recruit, take it or not).
- **Events with variants.** Each event rolls one of two or three versions. About six new ones around the roster: a Deserter, Mercenaries, a Prisoner Cart, a Rival Company, an Old Banner, a Training Hall. The Drill Yard and the Fallen Standard fold into the Drill ground and the Old Banner.
- **Formation rarities:** common, rare, legendary, rolled by act. The nine formations get provisional rarities; 5i sets the final ones and adds more.

All these rates are first values. The prediction below says what they should produce, and tuning moves them.

### Training

- **More chances to train, in one nested screen** (member → its abilities and passive → that one's upgrades), built with today's single upgrade. 5i fills in two branches for every ability and passive.
- **Where it comes from:**
  - the Drill ground and the campfire each train two members;
  - the act's level-up;
  - the shop (repeatable);
  - tomes;
  - the Old Master and the Training Hall;
  - recruits arriving with more known.

## Testing: hand-built parties, not a simulated player

A rules-of-thumb player can't come near a human who uses potions and builds well, and whole-run simulation has burned hours on something obviously wrong from the start. So this step tunes without it:

1. **Representative parties, built by hand:** about three dozen, by act and tier (regular, elite, boss). They span the classes and the build shapes, with the gear, known abilities and formation a run would have there under this reward plan. They're written down in the step folder so you can read them.
2. **Each fight's knob** from those parties: the calibrator, fed by them instead of by the simulated player's snapshots.
3. **The MP economy:** each act's representative roster plays its act's fights in sequence with a plain, stated rule:
   - bench whoever is under a third of its MP, field the best of the rest;
   - after each fight take a tonic if a fighter is under 40% MP, else a potion, and drink it on the emptiest fighter.

   This measures fights-to-dry by class, with and without tonics, and how often the bench is used.
4. **A feel read** of a few fights, then the report.

The simulated player is kept working so runs can be played end to end (tests, the interface), but nothing is tuned on it. Whole-run simulation comes back after 5i, when every system is in, if it stays cheap.

## Predictions (checked in the report)

- Every class runs dry in two to three fights from full, tonics included; none goes an act without the bench.
- A full run is offered about 80 pieces and 25 legendaries, and can take about 28 and 12. The party's seats fill by act II's boss.
- Four to six formation picks a run (were one or two).
- Fights the rogue softened for a fixed party come back without a spike: Bomb Bugs three to a fight, snake packs that grow.
