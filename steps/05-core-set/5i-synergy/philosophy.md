# Step 5i philosophy

Oct 29, 2026. Step 1 of the restart agreed with the director (their notes of Oct 28 and 29 are in `intent.md`). One page on what we're making and what makes it good. It's a direction, not a rulebook: each build and each piece is designed as a thing, and this page is what they're judged against. Steps 2 to 4 follow: the slots, a dozen builds and their web, then the pieces, formations and upgrades built backwards from them.

## What we're making

All of the rogue's gear, formations and ability upgrades, from scratch. Nothing is kept because it already exists; today's pieces are raw material at most.

## A build is a way to play

- **You can watch it.** It has an identity and a rhythm: what the fight looks like while it runs, and the moment it turns. *The duel:* one unit locks the boss in a long exchange of blows while the two behind it, never touched, build something slow, and the fight ends when that lands.
- **It's woven from two or three things,** not one keyword turned up to the maximum. Elements are themes, not only their statuses. Earth can be weight and patience, and Lightning can be a hail of small hits that ignore Armor with no Shock at all. Physical subtypes, timing, targeting, mana, Weaken in a stall: anything in the game can be a thread.
- **The payoff can be one big moment or many small ones.** Casts that set off more casts are enough on their own, with no grand finale needed.
- **Two builds on one element should look different on screen.** If they only differ by the colour of the numbers, they're one build.

## How builds come together

- **Several routes in.** A build can be started with what most runs find, and be modest. Or it can be completed with specific pieces that come late, and be potent. That difficulty is a feature: the hard version is worth the hunt.
- **A web, not bundles.** Pieces serve more than one build, sometimes in ways that aren't obvious, like a quiet single effect that one build happens to love. The Volley bow from the first sample is the model: it pays off whatever rides on a swing, whichever build put it there.
- **Exotics.** Some pieces are one-offs that bend something because it's fun to watch. Not everything belongs to a named build.
- **Chaos.** A run is finding a build in what it's given, not assembling the same five pieces every time. Two runs toward the same build should get there differently.

## What makes a piece good

- **It's worth a lot somewhere and little elsewhere.** The gap matters more than the average.
- **Something that wants to be strong everywhere pays for it,** in whatever way suits that piece: a cost, a drawback, a seat it competes for, a unit it shuts out.
- **The best choices are between two things that are good for different builds,** not between a small and a big version of one thing.

## Rarity

Rarity is mostly how far a piece bends the game and how specific it is. It also means more power as the acts go on, as long as that power is narrow: a legendary can be far stronger than a rare, but only for the builds that want it. There are no random affixes on rare and legendary pieces for now. Beyond that, each piece is whatever it needs to be.

## Slots (step 2)

What the structure has to give:
- gear that can be kept apart, so pieces that would be too strong on one unit together can still exist;
- rewards you can read at a glance;
- choices that aren't "which of my favourite toys goes in the garbage".

Step 2 brings two or three structures to pick from: an offhand, armor locked to one or two types, and amulets and rings apart.

## Formations

A formation is a shape: three seats, each with a role, that give a squad an identity and a fantasy.
- A seat can carry its own effect.
- A formation can be built around one unit, and be very strong with some units and weak with others.
- It's part of a build. So it shouldn't be a counter-pick you'd swap in for one kind of foe, as Siege was.

## Upgrades

- **Designed for each ability and passive, with no pattern across them.** A branch can be a different move, timing, target, trigger or effect: whatever that ability's best other versions are. It keeps the kit's identity or extends it in a way a player would understand. In an auto battler, targeting and triggers are where a lot of this lives.
- **Not strictly better.** A branch is better for the builds it suits and worse for others. The original can stay a fair third choice; for some abilities it's fine if it doesn't.
- **Open question 1 asks** whether a plain power upgrade also exists.

## How it reads

- **Every effect is phrased so you know what will happen,** at whatever length that takes.
- **A card says what a piece does and who can wear it** (today's "fits" list).
- **It never says what the piece is "for".**

## What still holds

- No control during fights.
- Numbers stay in today's ranges, so balance mostly holds. Enemies get harder at the end, and a full balance pass comes later.
- The engine grows as builds need it: new stats, triggers, conditions, target rules.
- Testing uses hand-built parties, a few for each build, once 5h is in. Then the gear, formations and upgrades go in, and the rest is readjusted around them.

## Open for you

1. **Upgrades.** You offered two shapes: bespoke pairs, or build-your-ability with at least two A/B levers. I recommend bespoke pairs, with the original as a fair third:
   - abilities are locked to their classes, so bespoke designs carry little risk;
   - each version reads as its own move on screen;
   - it's 128 designed versions to judge, against 192 or more combinations, with no lever every ability must have.

   And should a plain power step (today's "+") exist separately? I'd keep it. Training then has two kinds of picks, "make it stronger" or "make it different". The run keeps a power curve from training, and branches are free to be true sidegrades. Without it, a run's power rests on gear and learning abilities alone.
2. **Anything on this page** you'd change before step 2.
