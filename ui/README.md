# The interface

A browser interface on the headless engine, so the director can set up a party and watch the core set play, on a phone. It's a design tool, not Memory's game tab: nothing here runs in Memory, and the code stays small on purpose.

**Fourth version (Oct 24, 2026).** The director's review of the third asked for two things: an aesthetics pass with the art they supplied (tilesets, faces, weapons that show in the hand), and, more important, a fight that can be understood: statuses big enough to read and shown on the field, and the fight told one message at a time the way Dragon Quest and Pokémon tell it, with the log a tap away rather than the thing you read. This version keeps the third's bones (the double-bordered windows, the pixel font, the beats) and changes the field, the message window, the sheets and the art.

## Running it

- **On a phone:** every push to `main` builds it and publishes it to GitHub Pages at **https://pvaiun.github.io/memory-dungeon/** (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`). One-time setup in the repo: Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source: "GitHub Actions". On Android, Chrome's menu → "Add to Home screen" installs it as a full-screen app (`public/manifest.webmanifest`).
- **On the same Wi-Fi as a laptop:** `npm run ui` serves it and prints a Network URL; open that on the phone.
- **Locally:** `npm run ui` at http://localhost:5173. `npm run ui:build` writes `ui/dist` (ignored by git).

It's Vite and plain TypeScript, no framework. The game logic is the repo's engine, imported as is (`src/engine/`, `src/sim/`); the interface only reads the engine's state and its log. `npm run typecheck` checks it with `tsc -p ui`.

## The look

`src/pixel.ts` is the pixel grid, `src/style.css` the theme. The frame is 270 logical pixels wide; each logical pixel is a whole number of device pixels (`--u`), picked from the screen size, so nothing blurs: on a 1080-pixel-wide phone one logical pixel is four device pixels, and a row of the 8px font is 32. `1rem` is 8 logical pixels and the stylesheet sizes everything from it, so 33 characters fit a line.

Windows are the Dragon Quest kind: a dark fill, a light double border, light text. Colour carries meaning and nothing else: HP bars go green, yellow, red; MP is blue; a Shield is a pale segment on the HP bar; buffs are cool, debuffs warm; each element has its own colour (fire orange, ice cyan, lightning yellow, wind green, earth tan, light cream, dark violet) on its icon, its damage number and its ability's name; a gear code reads green when it raises a stat and red when it lowers one; powerful gear is gold. Settings offers a **day** theme (cream windows, dark text) with the same meanings.

The font is Press Start 2P (SIL OFL, `public/fonts/`); the field draws its numbers and words from the same font, rasterized once into 8×8 bitmaps, in any colour, with a dark outline.

## Screens

Every screen is its own layout: a header with ◀, windows, and lists that scroll inside their own window, never the whole screen.

- **Home:** the title, the party standing in its formation with their weapons in a strip of the road scene, then the menu, chunky and in the middle of the screen: FIGHT, PARTY, CLASSES, BESTIARY, SETTINGS; the party, seed and formation under it.
- **Fight:** the seven runs with a line each, then a run's fights with their foes ("Shield Knight, Centaur Archer ×2", ★ for the boss) and RUN ALL; the seed with a NEW SEED button.
- **Party:** party, formation and gear in one place. The formation at the top with ◀ ▶ (tap its name for the list of ten with a line each). Three slot cards, each with its slot's name and stat changes (on the stat sheet's scale: a +2 step reads +20), the member and its name. One thing is selected at a time, a slot or a class in the roster below, and every move goes back to nothing selected: a slot then a class puts the class in that slot; a class then a slot does the same; a slot then another slot swaps them; the same slot twice deselects it; the same class twice opens its sheet, whose back returns here. A class already in the party swaps places with whoever it's put on. SHEET opens the selection's sheet, REMOVE takes it out of the party. Nothing stays selected when the screen is left.
- **Unit sheet** (from a slot, or CLASSES), laid out to be read: a head window with the face, the name, the role and the figure holding its weapon; a stats window as a grid of label-over-number cells (HP MP REG ATK / MAG ARM CRT AGG) with the change gear and the formation make in green or red beside each, and the special stats under it; then one scrolling window with the class's sentence, PASSIVE (icon, name, text), GEAR (each piece as two lines: its own sprite or icon and name, then its kind, speed, hit and codes; tap for the picker), what the class can use, and ABILITIES as cards: the number to tap in pairs to reorder, the icon, the name in its element's colour, a cost line (MP, how often, the wind-up), the documents' sentence; the basic attack last. ADD TO PARTY or REMOVE in the header.
- **Gear picker:** everything that fits, with the weapons' own sprites: a weapon's kind and speed ("SLASH/10"), its hit for this unit, and its stat codes on the stat sheet's scale; NOTHING for armor and amulets. The equipped piece is boxed in gold and says EQUIPPED. Tap to equip and return.
- **Classes:** the 16 with their faces and a role line, to the sheet. **Bestiary:** every enemy with a likeness and its rank, to a sheet: sprite, description, elements it resists or is weak to, passives, actions with their sentences, and where it's found. Designer view adds hidden stats and worked-out numbers.
- **Settings:** theme, default speed, sound (effects), music, auto-next, **party HP on field** (thin HP bars under the party in a fight, like the foes', with the statuses moved below them; off by default), designer view, number size, **portraits** (what stands for a class in the menus: FACES everywhere, SPRITES everywhere with their weapons, or MIXED, the default: sprites in the party's slot cards, faces in the roster, the class list and the battle rows), reset.

## The battle screen

One field, 270×228, on a backdrop composed from the director's tilesets, one per dungeon (the road through the pines, the crypt's bricks and candles, the fire mine's lava brick and lava stream, the ice pass, the storm tower's blue stone, the deep crypt in violet, the tower top's battlements under rain and stars). **The party stands on the left at the packs' own size, with its weapons in hand; the foes stand on the right at theirs, which is bigger**: the director asked for the earlier scale (heroes at twice their size, monsters shrunk when crowded) to be inverted. A foe's sprite is never shrunk unless the fight has seven or more; small creatures (wisps, drones, mini slimes) are drawn small. Up to three foes stand in a loose triangle; four to six in a staggered front and back row; a boss stands in front with its followers to its left.

Every unit stands on a soft pixel shadow, which goes forward with a lunge and back with a wind-up's step. Over the field: a foe's thin HP bar under its feet, in the bar's colour with a pale Shield segment; **under everyone, their statuses as the pack's icons**, the 14×14 drawing of each without the tile's empty border, (recoloured per status: a green flask for Poison, a flame for Burn, a snowflake for Chill, a shield for Guard, a chevron for Haste…) with the stack count in the corner, up to four per unit (a status that lands drops in from one and a half size; one that ends fades up and out); a thin wind-up bar beside the figure, upright and filling from the bottom (left of a party member, measured from the pose it's in so it clears the step back a wind-up takes; right of a foe), so it never covers the figure or the statuses of the unit above; damage numbers at double size that rise and fade (yellow with CRITICAL under them for crits, the element's colour for spells, a WEAK! or RESIST or SHIELD label), heals in green, a status's name with its icon when it lands; the VFX strips in colour. A hit flashes and shakes the sprite; a knocked-out foe fades when its defeat is told, not before: the engine settles a whole tick at once, so the HP bars, the party rows and the knockouts follow the messages (each damage or heal event carries the HP after it), and two hits on one tick play out in order instead of the second seeming to strike a foe that's already gone; party members use their sheets' poses (chanting while winding up, the swing or thrust or bow-draw of their weapon kind, damage, dead, victory), and the weapon follows the pose.

Below the field: the **message window**, the party's rows, the foes by kind with how many stand and the tick, and the controls. A party row has three columns: the portrait; the name with short HP and MP bars and their numbers; and **the actions' cooldowns**, big. Statuses aren't repeated here, since they show on the field under each unit and in the inspect window. The basic attack comes first, small and centred (it comes round fast), then a gap, then each ability at twice its icon's size, always in the same place. Each is shaded from the top by the share of its cooldown still to run, with the ticks left in the corner; it's outlined in gold when ready, and blinks while it winds up.

**The fight is told in beats** (`src/battle/narrate.ts`, `src/battle/battle.ts`), one at a time. A beat is one thing to read as a whole: an action and what it did, a status ticking, a knockout, at most four lines. **The window shows only the current beat, typed out a character at a time, and the engine's clock stops while it's read**; when the beat is done a ▼ blinks until the hold is over and the next beat replaces it, the way a Dragon Quest window does. A beat's lines come straight from the engine's log in plain English: "KNIGHT ATTACKS!" "BANDIT B TAKES 10." "PYRO CASTS FIREBOLT!" "BANDIT A TAKES 18 AND BURNS (7)." "CRITICAL! BANDIT C TAKES 15!" "KNIGHT DODGES!" "CLERIC USES PURIFY ON RANGER!" "BANDIT C TAKES 1 FROM POISON." "SNAKE A IS DEFEATED!" "6 WOLVES DRAW NEAR!" An action line carries the icon of its element or kind. **Tap the window** to finish the typing, or to move on to the next beat (on a paused fight, a tap plays one beat, so a fight can be tapped through like a Pokémon battle). **LOG** opens the whole fight so far with tick marks, over the screen, and pauses while it's read.

How long a beat holds comes from its weight: the typing at about 26 ms a character, then half a second plus a third of a second per line, all at 1×; halved at 2×; at 4× the text appears at once and the hold is quartered. Between beats the engine runs at half a second per tick at 1×. The controls: ◀ menu, II pause, **STEP** (a mode, remembered across fights like the speed: the fight waits after every beat for a tap on STEP, the message window or Enter; it skips the dead time before the next beat, but while anyone is winding up it runs at the speed set, so the wind-up bar can be watched filling; ▶ leaves the mode, II only pauses), 1× 2× 4×, >> (plays the rest at once; the log still gets every line), LOG. Keys: space, Enter or n (advance), 1, 2, 4, s, l, Escape. **The phone's back button** (it works through one history entry the app adds only on a tap and steps back onto after each back press; Chrome skips entries added without a tap, which once made a second back leave the app) closes whatever overlay is open (a unit's state, the log, a sheet) anywhere; in the menus it goes back a screen like the header's ◀; in a fight with nothing open it asks LEAVE or STAY, so a fight is never left by accident.

**Inspect:** tap a unit on the field, a party row or a foe's name. The fight pauses and a window opens over it: HP (and MP for the party), every status with its icon, name, stacks, how long it has left and what it does, its actions with READY, the ticks to go or CASTING… (a foe's abilities, and its basic attack's kind and speed), and FULL SHEET, which opens the unit's whole sheet. Closing it resumes the fight.

◀ during a fight asks first (LEAVE or STAY), since leaving loses the fight and the run. A fight ends on VICTORY or DEFEAT: the tick, and per member the damage dealt, taken and HP left; then NEXT, AGAIN, NEW SEED, MENU or LOG. In a run HP and MP carry over; auto-next (Settings) goes straight on.

## Numbers

The data stores every amount at 1,000 per point of the documents' 1–5 scale (`docs/core-set/README.md`). The interface shows **10 per point**: a Knight has 50 HP, a plain sword hits for about 11, a Poison tick for 1, a boss has 900 HP. Percent stats (crit, evasion, resistances, Lifesteal) show as they are. Settings → NUMBERS: BIG switches to 100 per point (`DISPLAY_DIV` in `src/content.ts`) for reading small ticks. Shield shows its points.

## Art

The packs are pixel art drawn at three image pixels per art pixel (the iconset at two). `ui/tools/assets.py` (Python with Pillow) builds `ui/public/assets/` from the packs, which is committed so the interface runs without them. It finds each pack by name anywhere under the folder it's given, so they can be unzipped as they come, and skips the ones that aren't there, keeping their committed output:

```
python3 -I ui/tools/assets.py <folder with the packs extracted> ui/public/assets
```

- **Classes** use the "Simple 8-bit Sideview Battlers" Style 2 sheets (RPG Maker MV layout: 18 poses × 3 frames), one per class, shrunk to their art size by the tool (a 144px frame becomes 48px), flipped to face right at draw time. The tool fixes two things in the pack: its **"chant" pose is drawn turned around on every sheet** (measured: each class's chant frame matches its wait frame only when mirrored), which made every class look backwards while winding up, so it's mirrored back, keeping the step back the pack gives it, and the weapon in hand moves back with the figure; and its **"item" pose is a placeholder of another sprite**, so it's replaced by "spell".
- **Weapons** come from the same pack's weapon sheets: three 48×32 frames per weapon, drawn in the hand at the pack's anchor (the frame's bottom centre 12 pixels in front of the feet). `WEAPON_SPRITES` in the tool maps every piece of weapon gear to a sheet: daggers, swords, bows, spears and axes to a drawn one each, the staffs to a staff whose gem and glow are recoloured by the staff's element (the wooden shaft keeps its colour). Fist weapons show nothing. The attack poses run through the three frames; at rest the weapon is held (a bow close to the body).
- **Faces** are the humanoid face pack's 36×36 portraits, one picked per class (`FACES` in the tool: a face column and a palette row), used in the party rows, the party screen, the classes list and the sheets. The monster face pack is not used: the bestiary keeps the sprites' own likenesses.
- **Enemies** use one recolour each of the sideview enemy sprites, as drawn. At run time `downscale` in `src/pixel.ts` takes one art pixel per block (the commonest colour, aligned to the art's own grid), so they keep their three or four colours; the few drawn small (`scale` ≤ 0.6 in the manifest) and crowds of seven or more shrink the same way with an outline; bosses never. Seven are renamed on screen to match the sprite that fits them, and only on screen: the content's ids, names and behaviour are unchanged, and the documents still use their names. Bandit (a hooded hobgoblin thug, name kept), Archer → Centaur Archer, Shieldman → Shield Knight, Bat → Night Wyrm, Witch → Cursed Tome, Hellhound → Hellcat, Mummy → Crypt Guard. The bestiary shows both names.
- **Backdrops** are composed by the tool from the overworld, dungeon and castle tilesets (1x): `backdrop_*` functions tile a wall or a horizon and a floor and stamp pillars, candles, mountains and pines, one 270×228 scene per dungeon theme, plus the home strip. Indoors, the band where units stand is a plain tile dimmed by `darken`, so sprites, bars and status icons sit on a calm floor; the walls keep their pattern.
- **The iconset** (16 columns of 32px cells, 16×16 art pixels each) gives the armor and amulets their icons (`gearIcon` in `src/art.ts`) and the abilities their tags (an element, a sword, a spear, a hammer, a heart, up or down arrows, a shield).
- **Statuses** are the pack's 16px icons, recoloured per status by the tool (`STATUSES`: an icon number in the 400-icon set and a colour ramp), drawn on the field, in the party rows, in the inspect window and on the sheets, with the stack count in 3×5 digits in the corner. The hand-drawn 8×8 glyphs of the third version are gone.
- **VFX** are the pixel effects pack's frames, packed into strips: an element's effect on magical hits, slashes, stabs, impacts, arrows and bites on physical ones, sparkles for buffs and debuffs, a heal, a revive, smoke for summons and splits.
- **Sound and music** are the director's: a retro effects pack and two tracks, converted by hand with ffmpeg to small mono mp3s in `public/audio/` (`src/battle/audio.ts` plays them). The menu track plays on every menu screen and the battle theme in fights, both looping. The effects: `tap` (a screen change) is "menu select"; `hit`, `crit` and `block` are the characters' medium, strong and fast attacks; `miss` is "zip"; `magic` (an element's spell lands) is "laser gun"; `cast` (a wind-up starts) is "question"; `heal` is the third coin pickup; `status` is "select sound"; `down` is "fall"; `win` is "cool thing"; `lose` is "embarrassed". SOUND and MUSIC in Settings switch each off. The director sourced them under a free licence. Browsers start audio only after a tap, so the menu music begins on the first touch.

`window.__gallery()` in the browser console shows every sprite with its weapon, every face and every status icon as drawn, for checking the art.

## Decisions taken here, for the director to react to

- **The scale is inverted, as asked:** heroes at their art size (about 30 pixels tall), monsters at theirs (40 to 70). The field is tight for six wolves, so a crowd stands in two staggered rows and the back row's bars and icons draw over the front row's heads; the alternative, shrinking them, is what the director rejected.
- **One beat at a time, typed.** The window never scrolls; what it shows is the one thing happening now, and the log is a button. The engine still waits for the reader (the third version's model), and a tap advances. If 1× reads too slow or the typing too fast, the three numbers at the top of `src/battle/battle.ts` are the knobs.
- **Inspect pauses the fight.** Tapping a unit opens a window over the screen and stops the clock, because reading what's happening to a foe shouldn't race the fight; closing resumes it.
- **Statuses are the pack's icons everywhere**, at 16 pixels (64 on the phone), on the field under each unit and in the party rows: the director preferred them to the hand-drawn glyphs once bigger was the point.
- **Weapons show in the hand**, in every pose, from the gear equipped; swapping a sword for an axe changes the figure on the home strip, the sheet and the field.
- **Faces for the party, sprites for the monsters.** The monster face pack would fit the bestiary, but mixing portraits and sprites in one list looked inconsistent; it's extracted-ready if the director wants it.

## Limits, on purpose

- No gear or class ownership: everything is available, as the core set says.
- Single fights start fresh; a run carries HP, MP and knockouts as the rules say.
- The interface reads the engine's log for what happened and its state for bars; it never changes a fight, so what it shows is exactly what `npm run smoke` plays.
- `window.__scene` and `window.__driver` are a running fight's field and engine side, for scripted checks. `window.__battle("old_road~ogre_bridge", seed)` starts a fight and `window.__go("party")` opens a screen from the console or a script, for screenshots.
