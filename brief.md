# The wheel, version C: the brief

Five colours for the game, found in the distinctions the director draws between choosable things, three at a time. The frame they live in is `sources/colours.md`: read it first, and its rulings bind the work. The director's intent is `intent.md`.

## What it's for

Five colours, each on one card (what it never does, the mechanics it prefers, its profile and style, what it can't answer), that pass the checks in `sources/colours.md`, What checks a wheel. The director picks, merges or writes one set of five, and names them. (A **wheel**, in this brief and the frame, means just that: the five colours together, with which are each other's opposites. It's the result of this work, not a later step.)

## The idea

The director can't design colours on a blank page, and can't judge a list of moments in a fight: the fight is where the player has no say. What they can do, fast and well, is look at a few concrete things and say which belong together and why. That's a known way of drawing out the categories an expert works with but can't lay out from nothing (triads, from Kelly's repertory grid). So:
- **The cards** are things a player could choose, spanning everything the rules allow. They're stimuli, not answers: what matters is what they draw out of the director.
- **The triads:** three cards at a time. The director names the odd one out and says, in a few words, what connects the two and what the third is instead. The words are the point.
- **Add one:** the director may write a card connected to the two in the same way, ideally from another way to win. It's where their own invention comes in, a card at a time, and it tests whether a distinction spans the ways to win, which a colour must.
- **The distinctions** that keep coming back, in the director's own vocabulary, each with its two sides, are temperaments in the making.

**This draws out the director's taste; it doesn't add any.** The creative output has to come from the director: in their words, and in the cards they add. Everything here serves getting it out of them.

**A colour's temperament is the connective tissue between its mechanics:** a pattern, not something born from any one mechanic. Most mechanics belong to more than one colour, read through each one's temperament and set among different company, so colours aren't sorted by mechanics. A temperament still has to be carried by gameplay, or it's a theme painted on. The director's words carry both, often in one line, and both are read. The language of builds (a payoff, a setup, an enabler, a multiplier, a build-around, upkeep, a moment) is how a colour's gameplay shows: when its value comes, who supplies it, what it multiplies, what it asks of the party.

## Who knows what

- **The session knows the goal:** it aims the triads and gathers the director's words, and needs the frame to do it. It never suggests words to the director, groups cards in front of them, or aims a triad to lead toward an answer.
- **The agents who write the cards don't.** Whoever writes the stimuli knowing what's being looked for plants it. Their prompts say nothing of colours, temperaments or what the cards are for beyond being cards a player could choose, and they don't read `sources/colours.md`: they read `sources/ways-to-win.md` in its place.

## What a card is

One thing a player could choose in a run (a piece, an ability or a version of one, a seat), in one or two plain sentences saying what it does, as a card in the game would. Scale words, not numbers. No flavour, theme, name or attitude, and no slot or class.

- **One specific idea a player could pick, said plainly.**
- **Bounds every card keeps:** the player gives no orders in a fight; no positional targeting; a status does the same thing whoever applies it; elements are themes, never mechanics; MP is fuel, and no card is about lasting more fights. A card the rules don't allow yet, but a small new building block would, is kept and marked as needing one.
- **The card-writers' prompts give intent and ranges, never examples.** An example card in a prompt becomes a hundred cards like it.

## The page

One triad at a time, the three cards side by side, the same question every time: **which two feel connected?** (the director's wording) Nothing else frames them: the page never says which way to win a triad is drawn from, what it's testing, or how the cards were chosen. The director reacts to the cards alone. For each triad, the director:
- taps the odd one out, or **no two belong**, or **splits two ways** (and gives both), or **none of these grabs me**;
- writes a few words: what connects the two, and, if it comes, what the third is instead;
- may **add one:** a card connected to the two in the same way, ideally from another way to win.

**The director isn't shown which way to win a triad comes from,** so "another way to win" is their guess, and an added card may land in the same way. Work out each added card's way to win from what it does, never from the instruction; one in the same way is data, not a missed instruction, and only one that really is in another way counts as a distinction crossing the ways.

The director looks for a split in every triad, even a loose one: the split they reach for is the point, and the obvious ones have been taken away. **No two belong** is for cards that don't make sense together at all.

Answers are saved where the agent can read them back (the artifact's database). The director checks that one answer survives a reload before going on. Everything the director writes is kept in their words.

## How to work

Each session runs one stage or one batch and stops. **A report leads with what the director's answers show:** the distinctions coming back, in their words, with the triads they came from, and which of them their added cards carried into another way to win. Questions for the director come after, and only those whose answer changes what happens next. Every card is kept in `cards.md` (the director's own marked as theirs), every triad and its answer in `triads.md`, the page's link and each session's handoff in `report.md`.

### 0. The pilot

To tune the cards on the director's reactions before a deck is made: how bold, how long, how specific.
- **One agent writes thirty to forty cards,** about a quarter for each way to win, on as many different mechanics as it can. It reads `sources/ways-to-win.md`, `sources/vocabulary.md`, `sources/combat.md`, `sources/primitives.md` and `sources/gear-rules.md`.
- **Ten triads, each inside one way to win,** the three cards on different mechanics, so the obvious split (what kills against what protects) isn't there to reach for. The ten spread across the four ways.
- **Put up the page,** and stop.
- **The next session reads the answers with the director:** which cards were dead and why, and what made a card easy to have a feeling about. Anything the director wants changed about the cards goes into `report.md`, and the deck's writers are given it.
- **Nothing restarts.** The pilot's cards join the deck and its triads count as the first. Going through the answers and stage 1 can be one session: once the director has said what, if anything, should change about the cards, run stage 1.

### 1. The deck, and the first batch

- **Five agents, run together,** each working alone, write the deck, with any change the director asked for after the pilot: one for each way to win, and one for where mechanics meet across the ways. Each reads the same sources as the pilot's agent.
- **The session merges them** into `cards.md` with the pilot's cards and any the director added: one card per distinct choice, the doubles taken out, checked for gaps against `sources/threads.md`. Somewhere between one and two hundred and fifty cards; the space decides.
- **The first batch:** about twenty-five triads, each inside one way to win, on different mechanics, spread across the deck. Then the page, and stop.

### 2. More batches, aimed

After the first, each batch is as large as what it's aiming at needs, aimed by the session from the answers so far:
- **Across the ways to win,** once distinctions are showing: cards from different ways that might be connected.
- **At the distinctions showing,** to test them: two cards the director put together before, beside a third that might split them a new way; cards that sit on either side of a distinction, in new company.
- **At cards and corners not yet seen,** and at the director's own added cards.
- **At cards that share a mechanic,** late: how the director reads one mechanic in different company.
- **Cards the director finds dead** are rewritten or dropped.

**When to stop:** when batches stop bringing new distinctions. Say in `report.md` after each batch how many new ones it brought; the director decides.

### 3. The distinctions

- **The director's words, gathered** into `distinctions.md`: each distinction with its two sides, in their words, how often it came up, which ways to win it was seen across, and the triads and added cards it came from.
- **The data stays raw.** Nothing the director answers is scored or flagged. A distinction is weighed by how often it came back, in different company; what defines a colour won't be a one-off.
- **Nothing is merged without the director:** where two of their phrasings look like one distinction, the session proposes the merge and the director confirms or refuses it.
- **Every card placed** on the main distinctions, as the session's guess, shown to the director to correct, a sample or all of it, as they choose.

### 4. The five colours, drafted from the distinctions

- **The director may draft the five first,** if they want to: from their own distinctions, which sides seem to belong to one colour. It's optional, and it's done before seeing any agent's draft. If they do, theirs is the set the others are read against, and the agents' job becomes challenging it.
- **Three agents, each working alone,** each given `sources/colours.md`, `sources/challenges.md`, `distinctions.md` and the placed cards, and none another's work. Each proposes five unnamed colours (by letter), and for each colour:
  - its temperament, from the director's distinctions and in their words, and the gameplay that carries it;
  - the cards it holds, from several ways to win;
  - what it never does, traced to its opposites;
  - its profile and its style in each way it leans to;
  - what it can't answer, named as challenges from `sources/challenges.md`;
  - the checks run, and the cards left with no colour (the exotics) or in two colours, done two ways.
- **The session compares them,** with the director's if there is one: the colours they share and where they part. A colour every draft finds is robust; one only one draft found is a question for the director.

### 5. The audit, the pick, the question and the names

- **The audit:** the leading set checked against every card, for what has no home and no deliberate absence, and for the failures below.
- **The director picks, merges or writes the five,** and the session writes each colour's card into `wheel.md`.
- **The question the five answer** is named from them, now that they can be seen.
- **Names last,** with the director, from their own words where they can be.
- **Then the work goes back to the main line** with the director, where it joins the design.

## What fails a colour

- It's held together only by cards that combo with each other, or by a shared mechanic.
- It's a temperament with no gameplay behind it: a theme in disguise.
- It holds cards from one way to win only.
- It could sit unchanged in another fantasy game, or matches one of Magic's colours one to one.
- It's an element, or a role.
- Its profile mirrors another colour's, or the five share one shape.

## Rules for the work

- **No examples** in anything written for an agent, and none of the director's illustrations written down as design.
- **The director's words are kept as they wrote them.** Summarise around them; never paraphrase them into the agent's own vocabulary.
- **Plain words.** A card says what it does in its first sentence.
- **Symmetry is a pull to resist** (`sources/colours.md`, A colour).
