# Step 5i philosophy

Oct 29, 2026, second version. This is step 1 of the restart agreed with the director; their notes are in `intent.md`. On purpose, it states intent and ranges, not examples, because anything named here as an example would be copied. Each build and each piece is designed as a thing of its own, and this page is what they're judged against. Steps 2 to 4 follow: the slots, then a dozen builds and their web, then the pieces, formations and upgrades built backwards from those builds.

## What we're making

All of the rogue's gear, formations and ability upgrades, from scratch. A piece from today's game doesn't earn a place by existing.

## A build is a way to play

- **It can be watched.** Someone watching a fight should be able to tell which build is playing and what it's waiting for. That's its identity.
- **It has a rhythm:** how a fight unfolds over time for it. That covers where the damage and the danger fall, how steady or uneven the fight is, how often it turns, and how big each turn is.
  - Every point in that space is open.
  - The dozen builds should spread across it, so that no two share a rhythm.
- **It's woven from several threads,** not one mechanic turned up. Anything in `gap-map.md` can be a thread: any mechanic, stat, damage kind, element, timing, targeting rule or resource. That includes the ones that look minor today.
- **An element is a theme, not only its status.** What an element is like can shape a build's rhythm and identity far beyond the status that bears its name. An element's builds may leave that status out, and builds sharing an element should feel nothing alike.
- **Two builds that differ only in which damage type shows on the numbers are one build.**

## How builds come together

- **What a build asks of a run is a range.**
  - At one end, it starts from what most runs find and gives back a little. At the other, it needs specific pieces that may come late, and gives back far more.
  - Most builds can be played at several points along that range, and what a build gives back rises with what it asks.
- **More than one route.** The same build can be reached through different classes, pieces and formations.
- **A web, not bundles.** Most pieces matter to more than one build, by different amounts and for different reasons, some of them not obvious. Builds connect through the pieces they share.
- **Exotics.** Some pieces exist for their own sake: a strange effect worth seeing that belongs to no build in particular.
- **Chaos.** A run finds its build in what it's given. Two runs toward the same build should get there differently, and a run should be able to land on a build nobody planned.

## What makes a piece good

- **It's worth a lot somewhere and little elsewhere.** The gap matters more than the average.
- **A piece that's strong everywhere gives something up for it,** and what it gives up is designed for that piece.
- **The best choices are between things that are good for different builds,** not between a smaller and a bigger version of one thing.

## Rarity

Rarity is mostly how far a piece bends the game and how specific it is.
- It also means more power as the acts go on, as long as that power is narrow: a legendary can be far stronger than a rare for the builds that want it, and not for the rest.
- There are no random affixes on rare and legendary pieces for now.
- Beyond that, each piece is whatever it needs to be.

## Slots (step 2)

What the structure has to give:
- gear that can be kept apart, so pieces that would be too strong together on one unit can still exist;
- rewards a player can read at a glance;
- choices that aren't, in the director's words, "picking which of your favourite toys you wanted to throw in the garbage".

Step 2 brings two or three structures to pick from, starting from the director's ideas in `intent.md`.

## Formations

- **A formation is a shape:** three seats with roles that together give a squad an identity and a fantasy.
- **Seats can differ from each other as much as the shape needs,** and a formation can depend on who sits where as much as it wants.
- **It belongs to a build:** it's chosen for what the party is, not for the next foe.

## Upgrades

- **Each ability and passive gets one plain power step and one choice between two versions** (decided, Oct 29).
- **The two versions are designed for that ability,** with no pattern across abilities. They can change anything about it, as long as the kit still reads as the class's own.
- **What matters is how the two differ from each other.**
  - Either, both or neither can be better than the original.
  - Each should pull toward different builds, and often toward several.
- **How a unit behaves matters as much as what it does.** What an ability aims at, what it waits for and when it fires are as open to change as its numbers. In an auto battler they're among the most readable things a player can see change.

## How it reads

- **Every effect is phrased so the player knows what will happen,** at whatever length that takes.
- **A card says what a piece does and who can wear it** (today's "fits" list).
- **It never says what the piece is "for".**

## What still holds

- No control during fights.
- Numbers stay in today's ranges, so balance mostly holds. Enemies get harder at the end, and a full balance pass comes later.
- The engine grows as builds need it.
- Testing uses hand-built parties for each build, once 5h is in. Then this step's content goes in, and the rest is readjusted around it.
