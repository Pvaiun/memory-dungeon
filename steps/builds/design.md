# Design: the builds

The design stage of the builds pass. The director's intent is `intent.md`; the evidence from play is `state-of-play.md`. It follows the method `docs/gear-design/philosophy.md` gives for a builds stage: each build as a way to play, with its rhythm, threads, routes, rungs and needs, and the web between them. No pieces, and no numbers but the measurements under *Open problem*. Each build also says what the intent asks of it: how a player finds it at each phase of a run, how it stays alive, and what it asks of the kits.

**This is a working design, not an approved one, and nothing in it is built.** The builds, the doors and the engines are the base to continue from. Two things wait on the director before the loot's spine can be written from it: the engines, to be judged on a sample, and a ruling on how a build stays alive without a tank, which is measured here with the ways to solve it and a pick.

## Where it stands

**Ruled by the director:**
- **The web is roads, doors and engines.** A build is a road, a door is where a run drifts from one road to the next, and an engine is a found synergy designed in advance (*Engines*).
- **Marked takes the party's random picks:** abilities aimed at a random foe follow the Mark as basic attacks do, inside the Taunt rule, and an ability with a target phrase of its own keeps it. Approved, not built.
- **The Hexer's Malison pays for breadth:** it does more for each different affliction a foe holds, as its own damage, in place of adding one more stack of each. Approved, not built.
- **Burn and Chill keep their base behaviour.** What a build wants from a status beyond its definition comes from a class, a version, a piece or a formation (`docs/rules.md`).
- **Bonfire runs on upkeep:** a fire that dies if it isn't fed. Burn has to drain fast enough for that, which is a number for the balance pass (`STATUS.burn`).
- **Debuffs don't carry a build.** Stranglehold, a build on afflictions, is set aside.
- **A wall is not every build's start,** and neither is a front seat. Staying alive without a tank is an open problem (*Open problem*).
- **No engine rests on a number the player can't see,** such as a foe's MP.

**The design's own choices, for the director to confirm:**
- **The engines** (*Engines*): thirty-two designed, beside the one found in play. A sample to judge them by, one or two of each kind: the avalanche, the fuse, the capacitor, the lead's colour, mana as armour, the favourite, the goad, the carrier.
- **The open problem's pick** (*Open problem*): act I sized for a party without a wall, the fallen standing up after a won fight, and each road's own way to live left to its threads and its engines.
- **Nine builds.** The director left it to the design whether Shock and Bleed have builds. All four damaging statuses have one, each set off by a different clock (*The rhythm space*).
- **The Storm's hook:** it runs on the foes' own actions, so it taxes the ones that never touch the party.
- **The Hunt is led by the Mark,** with hiding as the Assassin's opener and no more.
- **Each build's far end,** which the director hasn't read in this form.

**Reopened by the engines, and left as written for the director to rule:**
- **Two far ends are now engines.** Landslide's stone as a weapon is the detonation, and Cold Snap's shatter that seeds the next is the firecracker string. An engine is found early from few parts; a far end should be what only a whole build reaches.
- **Hiding comes back to the Hunt as something found.** The vanishing blade hides the killer after each kill, and the borrowed gift shares a Vanish. The road is still led by the Mark.

**Open, and next:**
1. **The director's read of the engines,** on the sample.
2. **The director's ruling on staying alive without a tank** (*Open problem*): the pick, or another of the ways.

**Least sure:**
- **Among the engines:**
  - **The goad** speeds the foes up, which a player may never choose. It also sets Shock off on the party's clock, by moving the foe where the rules forbid moving the status.
  - **The carrier** may read as clever and pay too little in fights this short.
  - **The capacitor** needs no piece, so it may be a door and not an engine. Shock's cap bounds it.
  - **The one swing** may be Landslide's road and not an engine, and it's the lever the loot holds back as "one swing shape per unit".
  - **The warmth** sits close to healing from a status's damage, which the rules rule out. It reads the fire's height and leaves it.
  - **The favourite,** with an Aegis that lands on it every time, may be a unit that can't fall.
- **The open problem's measurement** is this branch's game on parties built by rule, in Neutral. It ranks the ways, and its sizes are pointers.
- **The Storm.** Its hook is real against packs and busy back rows. Against a lone, slow boss it pays on the boss's schedule.
- **Bonfire.** Upkeep is a rhythm only if the fire visibly leaps and sags. If it settles to a level, it reads as a status again.
- **Four of the nine are a status's road.** It's the cheap way in. Whether it's the right one is open.
- **Thousand Cuts,** centred on Bleed, no longer holds speed for its own sake. Speed is a thread every status road wants.
- **The Hunt** without hiding at its centre may be thin for a class whose kit is hiding.
- **Blood Pact** asks the player to spend what the game drains.

## The frame

**What a build is doesn't change** (`docs/gear-design/philosophy.md`): a way to play you can watch, with a rhythm, woven from several threads, reached by more than one route, in rungs.

**The web has three things:**
- **A road** is a build: broad, easy to see, with a shape and a rhythm.
- **A door** is where a run drifts from one road to its neighbour, through something both want.
- **An engine** is a found synergy, designed in advance for a player to find, on one road or more (*Engines*).

**What a run gives a build.** The rungs are written against the three phases of a run:
- **Act I:** three units knowing an ability or two each, a weapon each and a piece or two more by the boss, mostly in Neutral. A build can show its direction here. It can't be assembled.
- **Act II:** most slots filled, a version or two a unit, formations, a mythic. This is where a build is built.
- **Act III:** whole kits and legendaries. The far end.

**Left unjudged, as the intent asks:** kits arriving one ability at a time, and cooldowns carrying. The set is written to hold either way.

**What every build answers:**
- **Through a run:** its three rungs as the sign, the build and the far end. Each says what the player sees, what the step is and what it pays if the run goes no further.
- **Stays alive:** what the build itself brings. Where that's a tank or nothing, it says so (*Open problem*).
- **Packs and bosses:** it has a game for both, and says which is its worse fight. Three of the twelve builds this set replaces were written to fear a lone boss, and bosses and pairs of elites are where most of the sample's runs ended.
- **Asks of the game:** kit or rule changes.

**What every build asks of the loot,** so it isn't repeated nine times:
- **A first step that's seen the first time it fires.** In play a few Might, a tenth off a cost and a cooldown a few ticks closer were never seen to happen.
- **A first step that doesn't cost the party its safety.** In act I a careful party takes defence first, and it's right to.
- **A formation about one thing:** three seats that all serve what the build does. The one formation that made three units one build in play was that.

## The rhythm space

| Build | Shape | Damage over a fight | Danger | Turns | What it spends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Landslide** | two load, one strikes | long quiet, then one huge number | steady, soaked by stone | few, big | MP on stone, to save HP |
| **Thousand Cuts** | one opens, all pour | spikes when a wound opens, in a dense stream of small hits | even, and paid back hit by hit | each wound opened | HP: little casting |
| **Bonfire** | three feed one fire | as high as the party is feeding it right now; it leaps when casts land together | early, and whenever the fire sags | each flare, and the blow that reads it | MP, on the feeding |
| **Cold Snap** | two lay frost, one breaks | bursts on a beat you can count | falls as the field slows | many, medium: each shatter | MP |
| **The Storm** | three charge the field | follows how much the foes act | spread, and highest while a pack still stands | whenever a foe moves | MP, on the spread |
| **The Hunt** | three on one foe | a volley at the first Mark, then a staircase of kills | early, falling with each kill | regular: each kill | both: HP in the first moments, MP on reach |
| **The Toll** | one is struck, two profit | follows how much the foes attack | all on one unit | few | HP: the anchor's |
| **Barrage** | two serve one caster | in salvos from one unit | a cliff when the mana or the caster stops | each salvo | MP, more than any other |
| **Blood Pact** | one pays, one drinks, one gives | high and steady | swings and rights itself | many, medium | HP, in place of MP |

**Four of the nine are a status's road.** A status is the sign a player can read on floor one, and play showed it carries a run. What keeps the four from being one build in four colours is that each status is set off by a different clock:

| Road | Its status | The clock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bonfire | Burn | the party's feeding: it dies unless it's kept up |
| Cold Snap | Chill | its own count: it builds until it breaks |
| Thousand Cuts | Bleed | the party's hits: opened once, cashed by every hit after |
| The Storm | Shock | the foes' actions: everything they do costs them |

**Pairs on opposite sides of the space:**
- **Landslide and Thousand Cuts:** one huge blow, or many tiny ones.
- **Bonfire and Cold Snap:** a fire that dies unless it's fed, or a frost that builds until it breaks.
- **The Hunt and the Storm:** go and kill the back row, or make its own work kill it.
- **Barrage and Blood Pact:** mana poured out, or life.
- **The Toll** stands alone: it's the only one that waits to be hit.

## The builds

### Landslide

**Identity.** A party that loads one blow. Long stretches of small numbers go by behind stone bars. A wind-up bar fills on the hammer while Might piles onto it and its target is opened up. Then one number lands far bigger than anything else in the fight, and an elite loses a large share of its HP. The stone is rebuilt, and it happens again. It's waiting for the blow to come round.

- **Threads:**
  - the heavy action: slow swings, long wind-ups, long cooldowns;
  - loading it: Might on the slowest hitter (it's spent an action at a time, so it lasts where actions are rare), crits and Crit damage, Sunder laid on the target before the blow lands;
  - Defense, max HP and the Shield as damage stats, so the body is the weapon;
  - stone on the whole party, as the time the blow needs;
  - aim: at the healthiest foe, the most armored, the stunned.
- **Routes:** Geomancer, Knight, Hoplite · Geomancer, Paladin, Bard · no earth: Knight, Hoplite, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** a hammer whose actions come less often and land bigger; an anvil that wears the stone and draws the blows; a loader that gives the hammer what the party has to give (stone, Might).
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One unit whose heavy thing already out-sizes everything round it: rocks that crit from behind a Shield, a double swing, a bash from max HP. The sign is the number, and that Defense or HP on a piece makes it bigger: here the careful pick and the build's pick are the same piece. The step is the first piece that says so on its card, heavier and rarer.
  2. *The build.* A loading crew: Might aimed at the hammer, the target opened first, stone on everyone, the blow aimed at something worth it. Elites lose a large share at a time, and a boss takes a handful of blows.
  3. *The far end.* The stone is a weapon as well as cover: the Shield pool and its breaking are damage. The blow lands twice.
- **Stays alive:** stone on everyone: Shields, Guard and an Aegis across three heavy bodies. Fights run long, so the stone has to be rebuilt, and that's where its MP goes.
- **Packs and bosses:** bosses and armored foes are its best fights: a huge hit loses little to Defense. A pack is its worst: a blow spent on a wolf is a long wait wasted. Its pack game is the shove that hits every foe and the aim that saves the blow for the biggest. It fears dodgers, and anything that strips Shields.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** heavier and rarer, for swings and for abilities; Might for one unit, not three; damage from Defense, max HP and the Shield pool; a Shield's breaking as a blow. Every Landslide piece promises a blow on its card: the ones that promised stone alone were worn as plain defence, by everyone.
  - **Formations:** hammer, anvil, loader; formations that trade the party's speed for size.
  - **Upgrades:** slower and heavier versions; versions that aim the blow.
- **Asks of the game:** none. Its miss was the loot's signposting, not the kits.

### Thousand Cuts

**Identity.** A wound opens on a foe: a crit, a barb, a thorn. Then the party pours hits into it, tiny numbers, several a tick, and every one pays again in red beside its own white. The wound closes a little with each hit, until someone opens it wider. It's waiting for the next wound, and it never stops hitting.

- **Threads:**
  - Bleed: opened wide in one stroke, cashed by every hit after, closing as it pays;
  - the opener and the pourers: a few hits that open (crits, barbs, thorns) and many that cash;
  - speed: fast weapons, Haste, Attack speed;
  - extra strikes: Double strike, multi-hit abilities;
  - staying alive by hitting: life drunk, a Shield built, an ally mended, hit by hit;
  - Evasion, and the dodge as one more trigger;
  - Sunder under it all, its answer to Defense.
- **Routes:** Vampire, Assassin, Monk · behind briars: Bramble knight, Assassin, Ranger · Valkyrie, Ranger, Vampire.
- **Seats it likes:** an opener whose crits come more often; two blades that act more often and hit smaller.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit that opens wounds (four base kits do): the party's hits on that foe print twice. Early units mostly swing, so the step is anything that makes more hits: the faster of a class's two weapon kinds, a second strike. It's worth taking on any unit.
  2. *The build.* An opener and two who pour, a pacer's Haste, and units kept standing by their own hits. Armored foes stop being walls: Bleed goes past Defense, and the hits only have to land.
  3. *The far end.* The count: every hit landed this fight makes the next worth more, and extra strikes chain.
- **Stays alive:** by hitting. Each hit drinks, builds a Shield or mends an ally. The dodge alone didn't hold in play.
- **Packs and bosses:** a lone boss suits it: every hit on one foe, the wound never closing. In a pack its swings scatter where Bleed wants them together; its pack game is the Mark, multi-hits that fan out, and wounds opened on every foe at once. It fears foes that harden under many hits (the Ice Golem's crust), dodgers, and the Wisps' Shock, which fast units pay for most.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Bleed from more than crits; hits, however they come; a drink or a Shield per hit on Mail and Leather; a count of hits that grows into something.
  - **Formations:** opener and blades; formations that make the whole party faster at a cost.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that add hits, or open a wound on every foe.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### Bonfire

**Identity.** A fire that dies if it isn't fed. Burn falls away fast, so the number on the hottest foe is the party's feeding, live: it leaps when two casts land together, and sags when a feeder is stunned or between spells. Everyone feeds it: casts, swings that carry fire, the keeper's own blows. While it's high the foe melts, and the blow that reads the fire lands for the fight's biggest number. It's waiting for the fire to roar, and working to keep it there.

- **Threads:**
  - Burn as upkeep: it drains fast, so its height is how hard the party is feeding it right now;
  - two kinds of feeder: swings that carry fire hold it steady, casts make it leap;
  - the pile: every feeder aims at the hottest foe;
  - a blow that reads the fire and leaves it, and that waits for a fire worth reading;
  - tempo for the feeders: Haste, Cooldown, Cast speed, MP;
  - a keeper who holds the early fight, keeps the feeders casting and burns what it holds.
- **Routes:** Knight, Pyromancer, Monk · Paladin, Hexer, Pyromancer · no mage: Paladin, Monk, Hexer.
- **Seats it likes:** a keeper that holds the early danger; a stoker that acts more often; a torch whose fire reaches every foe that's already burning.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One unit that leaves Burn: three base kits do, and a version or a piece puts it on a swing. The fire stands as high as it's fed, and it gets through Defense where hits don't. The step is a second feeder, and because the fire dies so fast the step shows at once: the number stands twice as high. It sits on a weapon or a version, so it takes nothing from the party's safety.
  2. *The build.* Three feeders on one pile, some steady and some in bursts, a keeper who feeds too, and a reader that waits for the height. Elites and bosses burn for more than the party hits for.
  3. *The far end.* Heat: the party feeds faster the longer the fight runs, and the fire holds through what used to put it out.
- **Stays alive:** it leans on a keeper, a tank who holds the early fight and feeds the fire. It has nothing else of its own. A feeder stopped is the fire sagging, so the keeper guards the tempo as well as the HP.
- **Packs and bosses:** a lone boss is its best fight: one pile, all fight. A pack is its worst: every kill throws a fire away. Its pack game is fire laid thin on the whole field. It fears fights too short to get a fire up, and anything that stops its casters (a Silence, a Stun on the wrong unit).
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** fire on swings, for many kinds; a way for a keeper to feed (fire on its slow swing, or on what strikes it); a choice between a fire that lasts and one that flares; tempo where casters wear it; fire that passes on when its foe falls, as something the build finds.
  - **Formations:** keeper, stoker and torch, all three serving the fire.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that move fire from casts to swings, or make one cast a flare; a reader that waits for the fire to be high.
- **Asks of the game:** nothing of Burn's behaviour. Its rhythm is Burn's drain, a number (`STATUS.burn`): fast enough that an unfed fire is gone in a few ticks. That's the balance pass's, and it holds for every use of Burn.

### Cold Snap

**Identity.** Frost creeps up the foes and they visibly slow: each swing comes later than the last. Then a SHATTER: a burst, a Stun, and the party's heaviest hit lands on the frozen foe. Then the next one. It's waiting for the next foe to reach the cap, and the colder the field, the less the party is hit.

- **Threads:**
  - Chill to its cap and the shatter; anyone's Chill counts;
  - the slow on the way up: a colder field hits less often;
  - many small applications: swings that carry Chill, multi-hits;
  - the pile or the spread: one foe shattered again and again, or a pack reaching the cap together;
  - the opening a shatter makes: a stunned, sundered foe, and the heavy blow saved for it;
  - cold that attackers carry away from whoever they strike.
- **Routes:** Frostweaver, Hexer, Ranger · a Knight in frost: Knight, Frostweaver, Monk · no Frostweaver: Hexer, Ranger, Geomancer.
- **Seats it likes:** two frost seats that act often and carry Chill; a breaker that hits a frozen foe hardest.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One Chill source (three base kits have one): the foes slow as the counter climbs, and one shatters. The step is a second source. Every stack also slows a foe, so it's never a pick against the party's safety.
  2. *The build.* Sources enough that the same foe shatters again in the same fight, and a breaker for each one. The second shatter is the line between the sign and the build. It comes from the Chill counts, which *Core set: classes* already makes the balance pass's first job, and from what the build finds.
  3. *The far end.* A shatter that seeds the next, and a field that never thaws.
- **Stays alive:** the cold: a chilled field acts less, and every shatter is a Stun. On the test parties that alone was too little at today's sizes.
- **Packs and bosses:** spread over a pack, the cold slows everything and the shatters come together, like a string of firecrackers. Piled on a boss, it's a slow all fight and a burst on a beat; a boss shrugs off the Stuns after the first few, not the slow or the burst. It fears fights too short to reach the cap.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Chill on swings, for the fast kinds; Chill from being struck; payoffs on a frozen foe; a shatter that leaves cold behind or sends it to the next foe, as something the build finds.
  - **Formations:** the frost seats and the breaker; formations where one seat's hits set up another's.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that aim at the foe nearest its shatter, or spread the cold wide.
- **Asks of the game:** nothing of Chill's behaviour. The Chill counts, as above.

### The Storm

**Identity.** Everything the foes do costs them. The party's strikes spread a charge across the whole field: an arc to the foe beside, a chain down the line. Then the foes move, and each one that swings, casts, heals or calls for help is hurt for it. A wolf pack fries in its own frenzy. A healer behind a shield wall pays for every heal. A heavy blow comes down on the most charged. It's waiting for them to move.

- **Threads:**
  - Shock: paid whenever its holder acts, whatever the action, so the busiest foes pay most;
  - spread: strikes that arc, chain or land on three, so the whole field is charged, back row included, with no need to reach it;
  - quick, light strikes that keep the charge topped up, since half of it goes each time it pays;
  - a storm on the whole party's swings for a while;
  - a blow that reads the charge on its target and leaves it.
- **Routes:** Hoplite, Stormcaller, Valkyrie · Knight, Stormcaller, Monk · no Stormcaller: Valkyrie, Hexer, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** two squalls whose strikes spread; a rod that holds the field's attention.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit whose strikes spread and shock: fast foes take damage every time they swing, and the foe behind the front line is charged without being aimed at. The step is charge from a second unit.
  2. *The build.* The whole field charged all fight, the storm on everyone's swings, and a blow that reads the charge. Packs burn themselves down, and the back row pays for its own work.
  3. *The far end.* A storm that doesn't lift, and a blow that reads the charge on everything charged.
- **Stays alive:** it has nothing of its own. Shock punishes a foe for acting; it doesn't stop it, and in a pack every foe is alive and acting until late. Today it leans on a tank. What helps is that it lands hardest on whatever is acting most, which is what's hurting the party most.
- **Packs and bosses:** fast packs are its best fights, and groups with a busy back row (healers, cursers, summoners) its next best. A lone, slow boss is its worst: the charge pays on the boss's schedule, and its boss game is the blow that reads the charge. It fears Bomb Bugs (every blast at once) and Lava Slimes (an even field splits every slime).
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Shock on swings, for the fast kinds; a blow that reads the charge, for more classes than the Valkyrie; spread that comes with something else on the piece, since a slot whose whole promise was one more foe was never taken; something for a boss that rarely acts.
  - **Formations:** squalls and a rod; formations that pay for how many foes one action touches.
  - **Upgrades:** one foe or every foe; a longer storm or a harder one.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### The Hunt

**Identity.** The pack takes them one at a time. A foe is Marked, and everything the party has lands on it: swings, arrows, the bolt that was going to go anywhere. It falls, the Mark moves, and it happens again. Each kill pays for the next: a blow ready again, a volley. The foes' numbers drop like a staircase. It's waiting for the next kill.

- **Threads:**
  - focus: the Mark, which takes every pick the party makes at random, and aim at the lowest HP;
  - the first strike: the first Mark and its volley at the first tick, and each class's own opener (a first swing that lands bigger, a first hit that always crits, a strike from hiding);
  - reach: Seek past a Taunting front;
  - the kill as a trigger: a reset, a volley, the Mark moving;
  - executes: damage that grows as the foe's HP falls, so a boss is one long staircase;
  - crits and Crit damage on a few chosen hits;
  - Silence on the back row's casters.
- **Routes:** a judge who calls the target: Paladin, Assassin, Stormcaller · no Assassin: Hoplite, Valkyrie, Ranger · Assassin, Ranger, Valkyrie.
- **Seats it likes:** a marker whose pick sets the target; a chaser with reach; a warden that holds what's left.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit that picks its prey (the lowest, the back row, past a guard): the dangerous foe dies first, or never acts. The step is a kill or a first strike that pays something seen on the very next hit.
  2. *The build.* A Mark the whole party follows, hunters whose abilities aim where the Mark sits, and a kill that pays. Mixed groups fall in a drumbeat that speeds up.
  3. *The far end.* An opening that clears the weak, and executes that carry the boss.
- **Stays alive:** kill order: the most dangerous foe dies first, and the dead don't hit. Against a lone boss kill order buys nothing, and there it has nothing of its own.
- **Packs and bosses:** mixed groups with a dangerous back row are its best fights. A lone boss is its worst until the executes come: one step, no staircase. It fears Ghouls raised behind the hunt, and a Taunting front if it has no reach.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** the Mark from more hands than the Paladin's; kill rewards sized to be seen (a whole action, not a few ticks of one); executes; openers in each class's own terms, with hiding left to the Assassin.
  - **Formations:** marker, chaser and warden, all three serving the kill.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that change what an ability aims at, or make it ready again on a kill: the kind that carried a run.
- **Asks of the game:** Marked takes the party's random picks (approved).

### The Toll

**Identity.** They hurt themselves. Foes crowd onto one unit, and every blow they land comes back: Thorns flash, the attacker leaves bleeding, charged or slowed, and it pays again when it next moves. The swarm that rushes the wall dies on it. It's waiting to be hit. The danger is all on the anchor, and the rest of the party is almost untouched.

- **Threads:**
  - the draw: Taunt and Aggro;
  - Thorns and retaliation: being struck strikes back;
  - what an attacker carries away (Bleed, Shock, Chill, Weaken): each ally adds its own toll to the blows on the anchor;
  - a toll that follows the size of the blow, so a boss's few heavy swings pay as a swarm's many do;
  - the anchor's keeper: Guard, Regen, heals, Weaken on what hits it;
  - the dodge as the other anchor: a dancer who slips the blow and answers it.
- **Routes:** Bramble knight, Cleric, Stormcaller · Knight, Hexer, Hoplite · the dancer: Monk, Valkyrie, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** an anchor that draws and returns; a warden that keeps it standing; a free seat that finishes what the anchor started.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* On floor one, from the kit: a Bramble knight, or a Knight that Taunts in thorns. Packs die on it. There's nothing to find, which is why it was in most parties.
  2. *The build.* The other two join the toll: blows on the anchor set off what they've laid (the attacker bleeds, sparks, slows, weakens), and the anchor has a keeper. Swarms die on the wall while the third seat does something else.
  3. *The far end.* The toll follows the blow: retaliation sized by the hit taken, and growing with the beating. A boss's slow swings feed it.
- **Stays alive:** it is the tank. Its risk is the one play showed: when the anchor falls, the rest fall behind it. So the keeper is part of the build, not an extra.
- **Packs and bosses:** swarms and fast foes are its best fights. A boss or a pair of elites that barely swing is where it died in play; its far end is written for that, and untested. It gets nothing from foes that don't strike (Bone Priests, Witches), which is the Storm's ground, or from Area, which ignores the draw.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Taunt on gear, for more anchors; what an attacker carries away, for the allies to give; retaliation sized by the blow, and Thorns that grow with the beating; a dodge that protects as well as strikes back.
  - **Formations:** the anchor seat, with two seats that add to its toll.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that change who the anchor is and what it returns.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### Barrage

**Identity.** One caster, and the same great spell again and again. Its name scrolls up the log, twice running. Its MP bar is refilled by the other two, and the spell is ready before its cooldown should be done. The party's basic attacks are an afterthought. It's waiting for the spell to come back round. When the mana or the caster stops, so does the party.

- **Threads:**
  - one ability worth repeating: the soloist's;
  - cooldowns: the Cooldown stat, Haste, an ability made ready at once;
  - the cast twice;
  - MP that flows to the soloist (Clarity, Refrain, Siphon, life for mana);
  - Cast speed, and a wind-up that can be lost;
  - costs that change;
  - mana as a damage stat.
- **Routes:** Knight, Bard, Pyromancer · Frostweaver, Bard, Stormcaller · no Bard: Cleric, Vampire, Geomancer.
- **Seats it likes:** a soloist whose abilities come faster; a conductor who gives it tempo; a well that gives it mana.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A caster with one spell worth repeating, and anyone who gives mana. The sign is the caster not swinging its staff. Every caster party feels the shortage, so the step (mana in fights, however it comes) is one it wants anyway.
  2. *The build.* A conductor: the spell twice, or ready again; Cooldown and Cast speed on the soloist. The spell lands several times in a fight where it used to land once.
  3. *The far end.* A cast that pays for the next, and mana as damage.
- **Stays alive:** it has nothing of its own. The soloist draws little, and today the party leans on a tank, who is the well where it can be (the Knight's Taunt and Clarity are one ability). Its danger is a cliff, not a slope.
- **Packs and bosses:** whatever the soloist's spell is; choosing the soloist is choosing that. It fears Silence (the Wraiths, the Silent One), Leeches, and a fight longer than the mana.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** links sized to be seen: a whole cast's mana, a whole cooldown, not a tenth of either; Cooldown and Cast speed where casters wear them; an answer to Silence; something of their own for the two who serve, so they're more than batteries.
  - **Formations:** soloist, conductor, well.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that make an ability ready again on a cast or a kill, or pass mana on.
- **Asks of the game:** none in the kits. Clarity's and Refrain's mana have to be big enough to change what the caster does: the balance pass's.

### Blood Pact

**Identity.** Life spent and bought back. The party's HP bars swing. A unit pays a chunk of its life for a blow, or rides low on purpose, and drinks it back off the foes; the healers work twice as fast while someone is at the brink; a Shield or an Aegis sits over whoever is lowest. The party fights at half and ends full. It's waiting to be low enough.

- **Threads:**
  - Lifesteal, and drinking more the lower it is;
  - HP as a price, in place of MP;
  - below half as power: for the one who's low, and for the allies who answer it;
  - nets: a Shield or an Aegis over a low unit (a price paid isn't damage, so it doesn't spend the Shield);
  - life passed on: a heal given as a trigger;
  - max HP as a damage stat;
  - the last drink: ending the fight full.
- **Routes:** Vampire, Valkyrie, Cleric · Vampire, Bard, Hoplite · no Vampire: Assassin, Paladin, Monk.
- **Seats it likes:** a bleeder that pays HP for power; a drinker that feeds on hits; a donor that passes life on.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A drinker: the Vampire, or Lifesteal on anything that hits hard. A unit that heals itself by hitting, and potions saved. It's taken as sustain, which is right: sustain is the build's floor.
  2. *The build.* The price: abilities and pieces paid in HP that buy what mana can't, power below half for the payer and from its allies, and a net over whoever is low. Fights where the mana never runs out.
  3. *The far end.* Life as the party's one currency: strongest at the brink, and full when the fight ends.
- **Stays alive:** drink and nets. The lower it is the more it drinks; the healers quicken at the brink; a Shield or an Aegis takes the blow that would finish a low unit. It's the riskiest way to live in the set, on purpose.
- **Packs and bosses:** long fights against things it can drink from. It fears Bats (a dodged hit drinks nothing), the Witch's curse (a weakened hit drinks less), Rot, and burst that catches it low.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** prices in HP that buy more than their mana would, repaid inside the same fight; power below half that doesn't leave a unit low when the fight ends; nets; a last drink.
  - **Formations:** bleeder, drinker, donor; formations where life moves between seats.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that pay life or pass it.
- **Asks of the game:** none in the kits. Only the Vampire's base kit pays HP; the build widens through Lifesteal's kinds and versions other kits already hold, and the Berserker, when it's added, is its natural second home.

## Engines

**An engine is a found synergy, designed in advance for a player to find:** a few pieces that turn out to work together into something specific, on one road or more. A happy accident, made on purpose, and there should be many of them.

**What they're for:**
- **A build pays before it's complete.** An engine works with a small number of pieces, so a build doesn't only come online when all of its pieces are there.
- **Builds cross.** An engine that spans more than one road gives a party on one of them a reason to reach into the others, so a player isn't picking a build and then doing only that.
- **The web has many interesting points along the way,** on its roads and at its doors.

**What they aren't:**
- **Not a concept the player is taught.** An engine is a design pattern. Nothing in the game names one, and no card says what a piece combines with.
- **Not builds.** An engine is too narrow and too specific to carry routes and rungs.

**The one found in play: the shield detonation.** In the director's play, one piece made a Shield deal damage, by its original size, when it broke. Another effect brought a broken Shield back at about half its size. The party was laying Shields on its units from several sources. Together, most of the party's damage came from Shields detonating. Nothing else that party was doing belonged to any build in the set: it was a shield party that hadn't fully come online, and those pieces twisted it into one specific gimmick. It was one of the strongest and clearest rhythms the director met in play. It sets the size and the feel for the rest, and none of them is a variation of it.

**What's ruled about them:**
- **An engine sits on one road or more.** Some belong to a single road; some span two, three or more. Several can play on the same mechanic without touching another build.
- **They're designed as a game designer picks them, for being worth finding, not from a formula.** Two frames were tried and are too narrow: that every engine must cross two mechanics, which reads as pattern matching, and that every engine gives a verb to a moment that does nothing today.
- **No engine rests on a number the player can't see,** such as a foe's MP.
- **A status's own behaviour stays as it is.** An engine is one of the places a build can make a status do more: fire that passes on, a shatter that leaves cold behind.

**What each one below was picked for.** These are the design's own tests, not rulings:
- **It changes what a fight looks like.** Something the party has plenty of turns into something else, or an event starts causing itself again. A bigger number isn't enough.
- **Each part is worth holding without the other.** That's what makes the meeting an accident: the parts are taken for their own sake, by parties that aren't looking for the engine.
- **Mostly one piece meeting something a party already does.** The director's was two pieces meeting a party that was already laying Shields. A run sees a fraction of the loot, so an engine made of two rare pieces would almost never be found. The other part is a kit's passive, a status several classes lay, a small effect several kinds carry, or a formation.
- **It runs without waiting for a second cooldown.** The parts ride swings, passives, statuses, kills and the fight's start. The only chains that closed in play rode a swing or a reset.
- **It shows the first time it fires,** in what the screen already has: stacks, bars, wind-ups, kills, crits, dodges, casts.
- **Its parts turn up in more than one engine,** so a run holding one part has more than one partner to meet (*Parts that recur*, below).

Each engine says what combines, what the player sees, and which roads it sits on. The names are the design's handles and never reach the game. There are thirty-two designed here, beside the one found.

### A road's missing game

Engines that give a road the fight it's worst at, or a moment it never had in play.

- **The avalanche.** *Combines:* a piece that carries what a killing blow had left over into another foe, and any blow far bigger than what it lands on. *The player sees:* the hammer comes down on a wolf, the wolf is gone, and the rest of the number lands on the next wolf, then the next: one swing, a falling row of numbers. Aimed at the weakest, it runs furthest. *On:* Landslide (its pack game), the Hunt (an execute that spills over).
- **The fuse.** *Combines:* a piece that passes a burning foe's fire to another foe when it falls, and a party that kills the weakest first, or a pack of small foes. *The player sees:* the smallest foe goes up, and its whole fire jumps to the next, which starts already roaring. Each kill comes sooner than the last, and what reaches the big foe at the back is the whole pack's fire. *On:* Bonfire (its pack game), the Hunt.
- **The whole field alight.** *Combines:* something that feeds every foe already burning (the Pyromancer's Wildfire, a torch's seat), and anything that lights every foe at once (an ember on each as the fight starts, a Fireball, the Hexer's Plague). *The player sees:* the fight opens with every foe smouldering, and from then on each cast lifts every fire together. Apart, the ember dies in a few ticks and the feeding reaches one foe. *On:* Bonfire (its pack game).
- **The firecracker string.** *Combines:* a piece that sends a shattered foe's cold on to the others, or leaves some of it where it was, and a field already cold. *The player sees:* one foe bursts and the burst runs down the line, each one a Stun. On a lone foe the second shatter comes in half the time of the first. *On:* Cold Snap. It's the second shatter that play never saw.
- **The goad.** *Combines:* an effect that makes foes swing at once (when its wearer Taunts, or on the foe its blow lands on), and anything that makes a foe's own action cost it: a charge it holds, thorns on what it strikes, frost or a wound it picks up there. *The player sees:* the anchor shouts, every foe swings out of turn, and sparks and thorns fly off each one as it does. The anchor's bar dips, and the pack's drops further. Against a slow boss, the party sets the pace the boss pays at. The blow taken is real, and that's its price. *On:* the Storm and the Toll: the boss game both lack.
- **The resonance.** *Combines:* a piece that brings an ability back sooner for each foe it just hit, and a spell that hits many (Chain lightning, Blizzard, Fireball, the Hoplite's shove). *The player sees:* against six wolves the same spell's name scrolls again and again, and it slows as the pack thins. Mana is what stops it. *On:* Barrage (its pack game), the Storm, and the wide casts of Bonfire and Cold Snap.
- **The open tap.** *Combines:* a piece that makes its wearer's abilities cost nothing while it holds Clarity, and anyone who gives Clarity on a short clock (the Knight, the Frostweaver, the Vampire). *The player sees:* the Clarity icon goes up and the blue bar stops falling while the spells keep coming. The well's gift stops being a refill and becomes a window. *On:* Barrage.
- **The heckler.** *Combines:* a weapon whose swings stun a foe that's winding up, and many swings pointed at the caster (a fast kind, Haste, the Mark). *The player sees:* the Lich's bar starts to fill, three swings land, and it stops. Nobody waited for it: the swings were going there anyway. A boss shrugs it off after the first few. *On:* Thousand Cuts and the Hunt, as their answer to a great cast.

### Ways to live that are found

Engines that keep a party standing by what its road already does. None of them is a wall, and each stops when the road's own clock stops.

- **The warmth.** *Combines:* a piece that mends the party while a foe burns high, and a fire kept up. It reads the fire's height and leaves it. *The player sees:* while the fire stands tall, green numbers tick on the party; when a feeder is stunned and the fire sags, they stop. Feeding the fire is staying alive. *On:* Bonfire.
- **The cage.** *Combines:* a piece that gives a Shield to whoever a shocked foe strikes, as the foe pays its Shock, and a charged field. *The player sees:* a wolf bites, sparks leap off the wolf, and the bitten ally's Shield bar grows. The faster the pack attacks, the thicker the Shields. A slow boss gives it little. *On:* the Storm. It's also one more source of Shields for the detonation.
- **Mana as armour.** *Combines:* a piece that takes the damage its wearer suffers out of its MP while it has any, and the party's mana-givers (Clarity from the Knight, the Frostweaver or the Vampire; the Bard's Refrain). *The player sees:* the caster's blue bar takes the blow and the red one doesn't. Every note of the Bard's that reaches it is a heal. When the blue runs out, the caster stops casting and starts dying in the same moment: a cliff, which is Barrage's danger already. *On:* Barrage. It's Blood Pact turned round: mana spent as life.
- **The vanishing blade.** *Combines:* a piece that leaves its wearer Hidden after a killing blow, and a reason to strike from hiding (the Assassin's ambusher versions), or none. *The player sees:* a kill, and the killer is gone. It comes back with a crit, kills, and is gone again. In a pack it's never there to be hit. Against a lone boss there are no kills and no hiding. *On:* the Hunt, and Thousand Cuts' opener.
- **The dancer's shell.** *Combines:* a Taunt on a unit that dodges (the Monk's dancer version, a Taunt from gear), a dodge that builds a Shield, and a dodge that strikes back. *The player sees:* MISS, the Shield bar ticks up, a blow answers. Then one gets through, and the Shield takes it. The dodges that came before pay for the hit that lands, which a chance to dodge never did alone. *On:* the Toll (its other anchor), Thousand Cuts.
- **The opening freeze.** *Combines:* cold on every foe as the fight starts (a small effect at the start, a wide spell carried in ready, a first cast that lands twice), and frost on the party's swings. *The player sees:* the foes' first swings come late, and the first shatter lands before most of them have acted. The cold protects from the first tick, not from the middle of the fight. *On:* Cold Snap.
- **The red stone.** *Combines:* a piece that turns healing past full into a Shield, and a unit that's often healed while full (a drinker with a fast weapon, whoever the Bard's Refrain lands on, a Cleric with nothing to mend). *The player sees:* once a unit is full, its green numbers turn into a grey bar. A party with no healer finds it's building stone by hitting. *On:* Thousand Cuts, Blood Pact. The stone feeds the detonation.
- **The favourite.** *Combines:* something that keeps one unit the lowest in the party by share (half its HP locked away, a payer that rides low, a fight begun at half), and everything the party aims at the ally with the lowest share: the Knight's Guard, the Hoplite's Aegis, the Geomancer's Shield, Regrowth, Cure, Choosing. *The player sees:* every Guard, Aegis, Regen and Shield the party casts lands on the same unit, all fight. It stands at a third of its HP under four icons. *On:* Blood Pact (the net), the Toll (an anchor made by aim, not by body), Landslide (the stone).

### Two roads in one party

Engines that are a reason to reach from one road into another.

- **The capacitor.** *Combines:* anything that holds a foe still (a shatter, a Tremor, a bash), and Shock on the party's swings. It needs no piece: Shock is paid when its holder acts, and a held foe doesn't act. *The player sees:* the charge on the held foe climbs with nothing draining it. The heavy blow that reads it (the Valkyrie's Skyfall) lands for the fight's biggest number. Then the foe wakes, moves, and pays the lot. *On:* the Storm and Cold Snap.
- **The breaker's wait.** *Combines:* a heavy blow that waits for a stunned foe (a version of Smite, Rockfall or Skyfall), and shatters. *The player sees:* the wind-up bar fills and holds. A shatter. The blow falls on a frozen, sundered foe. The party makes the moment it waits for, which it never could with a foe's wind-up. *On:* Cold Snap (its breaker), Landslide.
- **The reckoning.** *Combines:* a link from one unit's beating to another's blow, where each hit the first takes adds to the second's next swing (a formation's two seats, or a piece worn by a unit that does both); a draw on the one that's struck; and a slow swing, so the count has time to climb. *The player sees:* a counter on the hammer climbs with every bite the anchor takes. Then the swing lands for all of it. The Toll's damage arrives in Landslide's rhythm. *On:* the Toll and Landslide.
- **The frozen gate.** *Combines:* frost on whatever strikes its wearer, and a draw. *The player sees:* the wolves on the anchor slow bite by bite, burst, and stand stunned. The anchor is hit less the longer the fight runs. With the firecracker string's piece, one burst sets off the rest. *On:* the Toll and Cold Snap.
- **The draught.** *Combines:* a piece that brings its wearer's abilities round sooner while a foe burns high, and a caster whose casts feed that fire. *The player sees:* the hotter the fire, the faster the spell names scroll, which feeds the fire. A Stun on the feeder costs twice: the fire sags, and the casting slows with it. *On:* Bonfire and Barrage.
- **The opening curse.** *Combines:* two different afflictions laid on every foe as the fight starts (an ember, a frost: each a small thing alone), and something that pays for breadth (the Hexer's Malison, the Paladin's Judgement). *The player sees:* every foe begins smouldering and frosted, and the first curse lands on all of them for more than anything else the party has. *On:* any two status roads. It's what a mixed party finds first.
- **The lead's colour.** *Combines:* a formation whose other two seats carry on their swings whatever the lead's swing carries, and one unit whose swing carries a status (the Hexer's frost and the Monk's Sunder are there from floor one; fire, shock or a wound is one piece or one version). *The player sees:* three weapons laying the same status. The counter climbs three times as fast, and what one unit could only start, the party finishes: the second shatter, the fire that stays up. *On:* every status road. It's the formation about one thing that play found, for any colour.
- **The live wire.** *Combines:* a piece that makes extra strikes carry what the swing carries, and a unit with extra strikes (the Stormcaller's Arc, the Monk's and the Vampire's Double strike) whose swing carries anything. *The player sees:* every swing lays its status on two foes. A fire or a frost spreads without a spell. *On:* the Storm (spread that comes with something else), Thousand Cuts, and whichever status the swing carries.
- **The pulse.** *Combines:* a piece that makes its wearer's next swing come at once each time an ally heals it, and many small heals (the Bard's Refrain, Regen's ticks, Cure on its quick clock). *The player sees:* every green number on the unit is followed by a blow. The healer sets the hitter's pace. *On:* Blood Pact (life passed on), Thousand Cuts.

### One unit's own

Engines a player builds in one unit's slots, where play showed building happens.

- **The one swing.** *Combines:* shapes that each make a unit's swing rarer and heavier (the Hoplite's Phalanx, a piece, a seat), and Might. *The player sees:* a unit that swings three times in a fight, each for a large share of an elite. Might is spent by acting, so on this unit a few stacks last the whole fight, and a ballad meant for three is worth most here. *On:* Landslide.
- **The stonecutter.** *Combines:* the Geomancer's Bedrock (it crits far more often while its Shield holds), and a Shield built by its own hits. *The player sees:* every rock that lands thickens the stone, and while the stone stands the rocks crit. It no longer needs someone to keep the foes off it. *On:* Landslide, without a protector.
- **First blood.** *Combines:* a first swing that comes at once, power handed out at the fight's start that the first action spends, and the heaviest swing in the party. *The player sees:* at the first tick, one number, and the most dangerous foe is gone before it acts. Then an ordinary fight, one foe short. All of it is within act I's reach. *On:* the Hunt (its first strike), Landslide.
- **The emptied quiver.** *Combines:* a crit that brings the next swing at once (the Ranger's Quick draw), and crits made near certain for a while (on a bleeding foe, a stunned one, one that hasn't acted yet). *The player sees:* three, four, five arrows in one tick, then a pause. The burst lasts as long as the crits do. *On:* Thousand Cuts, the Hunt.
- **The hollow.** *Combines:* a piece that locks away half its wearer's HP, power that switches on below half (the Vampire's Bloodlust, and what Blood Pact finds), and a Shield, which the lock doesn't touch. *The player sees:* an HP bar that never rises past the middle, a below-half icon that never goes out, and a Shield bar doing the living. *On:* Blood Pact, Landslide (stone as health). Its wearer is the favourite too.

### Exotics

Engines that belong to no road, for the chaos.

- **The borrowed gift.** *Combines:* a piece that gives another ally whatever its wearer gives itself, and a kit with something it gives itself on a short clock. *The player sees:* something different for each class. The Assassin vanishes, and the most hurt ally vanishes with it. The Vampire's Frenzy lands on the hammer too. The Monk's Flow quickens the slowest. *On:* whichever road the gift belongs to.
- **The carrier.** *Combines:* a piece that passes a stack of each affliction its wearer holds on to whatever it hits, and a way to be afflicted: the act's own foes (a hound's fire, a spirit's frost, a wisp's spark, a snake's venom, the Witch's curse), or a piece that sets its wearer alight for power. *The player sees:* the Monk is bitten by a hellhound and catches fire, and from then on everything it touches burns. In the Ice Pass the same unit spreads frost. A Cleanse puts it out. *On:* no road. It turns the region into the build.

## The doors

Where a run drifts from one road to its neighbour, through something both want.
- **Any status road into another, through a second colour on a swing.** A fire party picks up frost. The Hexer's Malison, which pays for each different affliction a foe holds, is what makes a mixed party worth being.
- **Thousand Cuts into the Hunt, through the opener's crits.** The crit that opens a wound finishes the weakest. Play found these two as one.
- **Thousand Cuts into Blood Pact, through the drink.** A party that lives on Lifesteal starts paying with it.
- **The Toll into Thousand Cuts, through briars.** Foes leave the anchor already bleeding.
- **The Toll into Cold Snap, through frost on the anchor.** What strikes it slows.
- **The Toll into Landslide, through Defense and stone.**
- **The Storm into Cold Snap, through a held foe:** a frozen foe keeps its charge until it moves.
- **The Storm into the Hunt, through the back row:** taxed, or reached.
- **Landslide into Cold Snap, through the heavy blow on a frozen foe.**
- **Landslide into Barrage, through the conductor:** the blow twice.
- **Landslide into the Hunt, through the first strike:** Might at the start on the slowest hitter.
- **Barrage into Bonfire, the Storm or Cold Snap, through the soloist:** the spell worth repeating is the one that feeds the fire, spreads the charge or lays the frost.
- **Blood Pact into Barrage, through life for mana.**
- **Blood Pact into Landslide, through max HP in the blow.**

## Open problem: staying alive without a tank

**Not solved, and no longer unknown.** A measurement on hand-built parties says where the problem sits, and it isn't mostly in the builds. Below: what's known, the ways it could be solved with what each would change in the game, and the design's pick. None of it is decided.

**What play showed:**
- **A wall was needed in every run.** Every run in the sample that reached act II had a Knight, Paladin or Bramble knight seated. From the director's chair the wall comes up at once, nothing else is whole before the first fights that need it, and the wall gets the gear first: a party with another way to live has either taken a wall by the time that way comes online, or lost.
- **When the tank falls, the rest follow.** The first to fall was the unit drawing the most in 31 of the 49 fights where anyone fell, and the others, near full, fell after it.
- **Debuffs don't do it at their sizes today.** Weaken and Slow on every foe, all fight, take about three fifths off what the foes deal (`STATUS.weaken`, `STATUS.slow`), reached late and paid for in MP.

**What the measurement adds.** The design's 27 routes, each as a run would hold it at eight points from floor one to act II's boss, against the rogue's own fights, with every knob raised in steps of 25 until the parties break. Thirteen routes have a wall (a Knight, Bramble knight or Paladin), five have a heavy body and no wall (a Hoplite or Geomancer), and nine have neither. The parties are built by the calibration's own rule (`tools/rogue-parties.ts`: abilities known, gear and legendaries by act, in Neutral), three builds of each and three seeds a fight, about 15,000 fights a step (`npx tsx steps/builds/tankless.ts`, the step's scratch; `--variant` plays each of the ways below that has a number). It's this branch's game, not the one the director played, and it ranks; it doesn't balance.

- **At today's knobs nothing separates them.** Every group wins nearly every fight. A party without a wall pays a little more: 22% of its HP a won fight in the middle of act I against a walled party's 18%, and 14% against 12% in act II.
- **The wall's edge is in act I, and mostly in its fights and elites.** The knob, above today's, at which a group stops winning three fights in four:

  | | A wall | A heavy body, no wall | Neither |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Act I's fights and elites | +117 | +133 | +90 |
  | Act I's boss | +76 | +65 | +65 |
  | Act II's fights and elites | past +200 | past +200 | past +200 |
  | Act II's bosses | +109 | +112 | +136 |

  In act I's fights and elites a wall is worth about 27 points of knob: foes with a quarter more HP that hit a tenth harder, or about one of act I's early floors (`knobs[0].early`). At act I's boss it's worth 11. In act II's fights it's worth nothing. At act II's bosses, which are races, it costs about as much as it was worth.
- **It's a body more than a draw.** With foes' picks ignoring Aggro, a walled party gives up 10 points and still leads by 19: that's the wall's HP, Defense and Shield being in the party at all. A Hoplite or a Geomancer, who never Taunt, do better in act I than a wall.
- **It isn't the frail lines, and it isn't kits arriving one at a time.** A point of HP and Defense on the frailest classes is worth 12 points to a party without a wall, and whole kits from floor one are worth 22. Both lift a walled party as much, so the gap stays.
- **It's in particular fights:** the wolf packs (many small bites, where Defense does the most) and the archers behind a Shieldman (blows that delete a light body). At +100, a few rooms into act I, the walled routes win 65% of Night Wolves and 75% of Bandit Camp, and the routes with neither win 31% and 40%. Against snakes, bandits and the boss the groups are close.
- **Without a wall, a party wins with someone down.** At the same knob it does so half again to twice as often: 32% of its act I wins at +75, against 18%. A knockout carries, so each of those wins leaves two to fight the next room.
- **Somebody is always being hit, and without a wall the hits spread.** Foes pick by draw (`RULES.partyDraw`). The most-struck member takes about half the party's damage without a wall and about two thirds with one. The first to fall is most often a Stormcaller or a Pyromancer, then the Knight.
- **Late, the parties of robes still fall.** In act II the routes without a wall hold with the walled ones, and better at the bosses (71% of them won at +150, against 49%), but not evenly. The routes built on fighters win 63% to 98% of those bosses. The routes of casters win under half: Frostweaver, Hexer, Ranger 44%; Bonfire's and Barrage's first routes with the wall swapped for a third light unit, 33% and 25%; Frostweaver, Bard, Stormcaller none.

**What that says.** There's a band of difficulty, about 27 points wide, in which act I kills a party without a wall and spares one with. The game the director played sat in that band or above it: 17 of 23 runs ended in act I. Today's knobs sit under it, which is why nothing separates: a party without a wall stops winning 19 fights in 20 about 45 points up, a walled one about 60. So the wall is required whenever act I is tuned into the band, and what decides that is the difficulty pass, which aims harder than today. The builds don't decide it.

**The director's three questions, as far as the numbers go:**
- **Do classes need to be able to live without a tank by default?** By act II they can, the parties of robes apart. In act I they're about a floor behind.
- **Can other ways of staying alive come online as quickly as a tank does?** Not by handing them out sooner. A wall's way is its line, there before it knows anything, and whole kits help it as much as anyone.
- **Is the start of a run simply too hard?** For a party without a wall it was, and that's the whole of the constraint.

**What isn't the answer** (the director's rulings):
- **A wall in every build's first route.** Every run would start the same, which is what the pass is meant to correct.
- **A front seat that lets more classes be the frontline.** It keeps the constraint that every party needs a frontline fighter, and that constraint is the uninteresting part.

**The ways it could be solved.** The points are of knob, for a party with neither a wall nor a heavy body, in act I's fights and elites, where it breaks at +90 today.

| The way | What it changes in the game | What the numbers say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Act I is sized for a party without a wall** | No rule. A line of direction for the balance pass: act I's regular fights are set against parties without a wall, and the pass's hardness goes to bosses, elites and the later acts. The calibration's act I rosters change with it: seven of the twelve field a wall. | The band is 27 points wide, and bosses don't favour a wall. A walled start gets a comfortable act I and pays for it at act II's bosses: a safe start against a strong finish. |
| **2. The fallen stand up after a won fight** | What carries: a knockout ends with its fight, at little HP. The phoenix feather, the campfire's raise and the pieces that do this today change their job. | Not measured: it's a rule of the run. It's aimed at the 32% against 18%. |
| **3. A hurt unit draws less** | The Aggro rule: foes still pick by draw, and a unit's draw falls as its HP does. The player never sees it, and it reaches every fight. | +10 points, and wins with someone down fall from 32% to 18%. A walled party gains 6. |
| **4. Foes strike the healthiest** | The Aggro rule, replaced for foes' swings: each goes where the largest share of HP is left. Aggro on gear and seats stops meaning anything outside Taunt. | +20 points, and wins with someone down fall to 10%. A walled party gains 14: its wall still takes most of the hits, because each one costs it least. |
| **5. Sturdier frail lines** | Five class lines: no class under HP 3 and Defense 2. | +12 points, and as much for a walled party. |
| **6. Whole kits from floor one** | What a unit knows at the start. The intent leaves this unjudged until the game is balanced. | +22 points, and as much for a walled party. |
| **7. A second road out of town** | Content. Act I is one region, and its hard fights are the ones a wall answers. Two or three first regions that ask different things, each shown with its boss before the draft, as acts II and III are. | Not measured. It's the only way here that makes starts differ, where the others make one start survivable. |
| **8. A way to live in every party from floor one** | The start: a choice of first formation in Neutral's place, each a way to live that isn't a front line. | Not measured. It's how formations arrive in a run, which the intent puts outside this pass. |
| **9. The engines** | The loot: each road's own way to live, as something found (*Engines*, Ways to live that are found). | They arrive in act II, where the gap has already closed, except for the parties of robes, which three of them are for. |

One more was tried and dropped: a knob for each pool in place of one for each fight leaves the gap at 27.

**The design's pick: the first two, and the engines for the rest.**
- **Size act I for a party without a wall** (1). The constraint is a tuning stance, and the cheapest way to lift it is to take the other stance on purpose. It asks nothing of the rules, the kits or the foes. It turns the wall from a requirement into a trade the numbers already hold: safer early, slower at the bosses. It's a challenge to `docs/rules.md`, "the coming difficulty pass aims harder than today": harder at the bosses and elites, not in act I's regular fights.
- **Let the fallen stand after a won fight** (2). A party without a wall wins with someone down. While a knockout carries, one such win is the run; when it doesn't, it's HP to mend. *Elements, not builds* already asks that a knockout needn't be carried.
- **Leave each road's own way to its threads and its engines** (9), from act II, where there's something to build with. That's the interesting part of staying alive, and act I isn't where it can live.
- **Held back: how foes choose** (3 and 4). It works, and it's the strongest thing here against falls. It also helps a walled party nearly as much, so it doesn't change who needs a wall, and it rewrites a standing rule that every fight runs on. If runs still end on one fall after 1 and 2, a hurt unit drawing less (3) is the next thing to reach for.
- **Not picked:** sturdier lines and whole kits lift everyone alike (5, 6); a first formation waits on a question outside this pass (8).
- **For the director to weigh apart from the rest: a second road out of town** (7). It costs the most, and it's the only one that answers the worry as it was put: that every run starts the same.

**Where the builds stand on it:**
- **Revolve around a tank by nature:** the Toll (its anchor), Landslide (stone on heavy bodies).
- **Have something of their own that isn't a tank:** Thousand Cuts and Blood Pact (living by what they hit and drink), Cold Snap (the cold slows and stuns, too little alone today), the Hunt (kill order, and nothing against a lone boss).
- **Lean on a tank, with a way of their own only as something found:** Bonfire (the warmth), the Storm (the cage), Barrage (mana as armour). Until it's found, these are the parties of robes that fall late.

## Elements, not builds

Things the twelve treated as builds, or as a build's whole identity, that are better as things any party can have.

- **Falls and returns: the net.** Nobody chooses who falls, so nothing is built on it. A revive, and whatever softens a fall, serves every build. What the net has to do: the returned stay up (each of the five revives inside a fight in the sample was followed by a second fall), the survivors get cover in the moment after, and a knockout needn't be carried into the next fight. It's sized as protection. No piece wants a fall.
- **The first move.** The fight's start is the one trigger nothing gates. It's the Hunt's first Mark, and a thread several others use: stone at the start, cold at the start, a Taunt at the start, a first cast free.
- **Spread.** Reaching more foes is each build's pack game, not a build. The Storm is the one road whose nature is spread.
- **Stopping a cast.** A Stun or a Silence timed to a wind-up is an answer to a boss's great cast, as *Core set: enemies* asks, and the act's boss is shown from the start. The versions that wait for a wind-up are that answer. A wind-up was caught five times in 150 fights, so no build waits for one.
- **Foes that do less.** Weaken, Slow and Silence make a party's fights cheaper. At their sizes today they don't keep one alive.

**What a formation has to be** for a party to pivot to it, from what was and wasn't fought in:
- **About one thing.** All three seats serve what the build does, and no seat is a tax.
- **A promise in plain words,** big enough to see: a stat, a status by name, who it lands on.
- **Seats many classes can fill.** A seat that reads who sits in it says so on the seat.
- **One for each phase.** Every build has a common formation a run could start in, a rare that reads who sits where, and a legendary that changes how the party fights.

A build still plays in Neutral, and no build needs one formation. How formations arrive in a run is outside this pass.

## Checks on the whole set

- **The rhythms and shapes spread across the space:** rare and huge (Landslide), spikes in a dense stream (Thousand Cuts), a fire that leaps and sags (Bonfire), bursts on a beat (Cold Snap), following the foes' actions (the Storm), a volley and then a staircase (the Hunt), following the foes' attacks (the Toll), in salvos from one unit (Barrage), see-sawing (Blood Pact).
- **Four roads are a status's, each on its own clock,** and five aren't a status's at all. A status the party stacks read as a build in play; it isn't what a build has to be.
- **Every build's sign is in a base kit.** At least one class shows each build's direction with a passive or one ability, before any piece, and most builds have two or three. It's a check on this set, not a rule for what a build is: a sign can as well come from a piece or a formation.
- **Every build has a route with a starter** (the Knight, Cleric or Ranger).
- **Every build has a pack game and a boss game,** and says which is worse.
- **Builds that share a mechanic feel nothing alike.**
  - Being hit: the Toll's return, Blood Pact's brink.
  - Big numbers: Landslide's loaded blow, the Hunt's chosen strike, Barrage's repeated spell.
  - The foes' own doing: the Toll's attackers, the Storm's every action.

**Set aside:**
- **Stranglehold:** a build on debuffs. They can't keep a party alive at today's sizes, and the director doubts they'd neuter a foe team at any number. It could come back if the balance pass makes debuffs big enough to stand on.
- **Phoenix:** a build on falls. Nobody chooses who falls; revives are the net.
- **Ambush alone:** an opening with no tail. On the test parties it has the most distinct rhythm of the twelve, which is why the first strike is kept in the Hunt.
- **Harvest alone:** spread as a build. It's each build's pack game, and the Storm's nature.
- **Lockdown:** waiting for the foe's wind-up. Stopping a cast is an answer, not a build.
- **Overflow's relay:** mana passed round three casters. Its links can't be made big enough to see without being a build's whole power.
- **The chorus:** the party's own buffs as the thing stacked. Today a buff helps every build about equally, which is what a piece shouldn't do.
- **The gambler:** crits on everything. The Hunt's and Landslide's crits cover it, and it may come back as exotics.
