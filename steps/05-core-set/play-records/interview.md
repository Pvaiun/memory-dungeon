## The reaction (the designer's)

What the interview overturns in `design-read.md`:

- **The bench.** Graded "working as meant" because the swaps are there; the player calls swapping a Hail Mary to refill mana. The feature's premise is several viable trios, and that premise isn't met, so the bench is a mana hospital under duress.
- **Aggro.** The read treated formations as draw tables as if the draw worked. The draw rule (Oct 26) gives a unit at most three times an ordinary one, so a maxed wall beside two casters draws about 65%, and one gear step moves that by about three points. The seats that "draw far more" are the dead version of Taunt. Either Taunt is the targeting mechanic and Aggro leaves gear and seats, or the draw gets steep enough to be a plan.
- **Rarity as specificity.** The read called it working because the player takes fit over the word. It works as a choice and fails as a feeling: choosing between stats and an effect you can't make happen was the worst moment.
- **Upgrades.** The favourite mechanic, but half the picks were to cut mana cost and narrow a class to one ability: part of the joy is a symptom of the mana economy.

Where the records and the player disagree: casters cast and hit when fielded (Frost lance twice a fight; Burn the party's engine whenever a Pyromancer was in; the Stormcaller the highest damage a tick of any class), and per fight they were knocked out about one time in five against the Knight's one in three. But half the caster deaths came before tick 15, before a first cast, in the fights that ended runs. The perception was formed by a few total, early failures, and it's fair: a caster's failure mode is total and early, and nothing softens it.

What's new, that no log could show:

- **Rhythm isn't a player axis.** The builds were designed on where damage and danger fall and on turns; the player never once thought about speed. The player's axes are who gets hit, how much they take, mana, healing, and triggers they can stack toward. The builds need re-describing on those axes.
- **Every "when" needs a "how".** Kill triggers worked because Seek let the player aim them; death, stun and shatter triggers read as bait because nothing held outside the fight makes them happen. A rule for the whole piece set, and a cause for the build report's 195 items that never move a result.
- **One mandatory mechanic collapses the strategy space.** Once Taunt was required, the Clarity-versus-Taunt upgrade stopped being a choice, the draft shrank to four classes, the formations to three. If Taunt is mandatory it should be free, with the choices moved elsewhere; if not, something else must answer the first ten ticks.
- **The enemies read; the party has no answers.** Shieldman and archers, evasive ghosts, splitting slimes were noticed, and nothing answered them: the bench holds no second viable trio and gear is synergy passives. Adaptation has to live in cheap, reliable, situational things: targeting rules like Seek, formations that say "against many" or "against one", supplies.
- **Mana shapes everything else.** Made scarce on purpose in 5c and 5h; the player's answer was fewer abilities, cheaper upgrades, mana regen over anything. A one-ability specialist is legible, but it isn't a kit.

The proposals, reordered by the interview: targeting (Taunt or draw); the condition rule for pieces and a cull of what fails it; rarity must not read as weaker; mana and how many abilities a party is meant to run; legibility (damage a tick on a weapon's card, a debuff's worth as a number, the draw share); caster survival as a design question; two-handers. Then the leader, the boss as a removal, the phoenix and the shop.

The good moments the player named share one shape: an advantage made reliable before the fight, or a trigger the odds were stacked toward. The bad moments share the opposite: an outcome the player was asked to want and given no way to cause. The design pass this asks for moves everything from the second shape into the first. The director's detailed notes are to be folded in when they arrive.

## The notes (`notes.md`), and what they change

- **Legibility goes to the top, next to targeting.** Half the notes are numbers the player couldn't see (Weaken, Might, a Vanguard shield, an item's amount, "every so often", damage a tick, the aggro share, a boss's damage type, Armor against Magic resist). Several mechanics filed as dead are partly unseen; the player can't value what the card doesn't say.
- **The rarity rule is contradicted five ways** (no stats on cool gear, no vertical increase, flat legendaries, gear a trigger not an upgrade, a two-legendary Vampire the lowest damage dealer). The Oct 29 rule, rarity as specificity with fewer stats, was approved by the designer and rejected by the player. The clearest reversal in the playtest: every piece carries stats, and rarity adds rather than replaces.
- **The fill drifted from the director's own preference.** Spite for Thorns, Cuts for Sunder, Fervor, "every so often": bespoke words where a known status would do, against the standing rule of a few numerical systems. A sibling to the condition rule: every effect names a status the player already knows.
- **The bench's grade falls again.** The best run recruited nobody until the end of act II; the records agree (run 11, the one act I win at full knobs, made zero swaps). The only swap that worked was tank for tank.
- **Classes as mechanic themes is a direction.** Thornheart transforming a class, Stonewall feeding the Geomancer, a bleed upgrade matching a bleed build: the liked moments are shared mechanics meeting across classes. Write the kits as mechanics (shield, thorns, bleed, mark), not fantasy roles, so synergies read before loot arrives.
- **New, and backed by the records:** early elites are free loot (the early ramp applies to elites: the twin ogres and the war camp on floor 2 at two thirds of their HP, costing nothing, for a legendary and a formation); casters are a binary by design (a few big slow casts in a thirty-tick fight, nuke or die); offhand kinds shared across unlike classes produce misfit loot (a shield on a Ranger, a bell on a Monk); the two damage types aren't a player axis yet (no class Magic resist, few magic foes, the preview silent on type).
