# Learnings from play: the builds and the game

A draft, for the agent that designs the next builds. It collates the director's read of about 30 sessions (`feedback.md`) with what the data and a few checks say. It's analysis, not proposals. Open: the director's answers to the questions at the end, and the play-data analysis from the other agent, to be folded in.

## About the game, before any build

1. **Survivability is the scarce resource, and only one shape buys it.** A Taunting tank and a healer (or a bruiser party) read as mandatory. Squishies die in three or four hits. Everything a build asks of a squishy unit over a long fight (Overflow's engine, Cold Snap's stacking, Thousand Cuts' blur, Ambush's tail) fails because the unit is dead first. The Toll happens by default and Landslide works because both ride the Aggro and Defense systems. *Check:* in the simulation at heat 0, the hand-built build parties win nearly every fight and someone falls in about 3% of them; with the knob raised 120 points, falls happen in 38% of fights and a fall loses the fight about half the time (bosses four in five). The fights the director played were harder than the heat 0 the test harness measures: which heat and version they played is the first open question.
2. **Evasion is not a defence as a probability, only as a near-certainty.** Below the cap it's "40% fewer hits" against a unit that dies to three or four, so it swings between fine and dead; only the Monk near the cap can live on it, and one or two misses kill it. *Data:* the Evasion cap is 75 and is a placeholder "meant to sit far out of reach"; gear gives 20 (medium) or 35 (a legendary's high); the Monk starts at 35, the Assassin at 20. The design never meant the cap to be reached; play found it as the only place Evasion works. A timed 100% dodge was raised and set aside as "Taunt with another name".
3. **Builds that chain don't come online.** A hit that begets a hit, a kill that feeds the next, a cast that feeds the next, a fall that feeds the next: all of them stalled, for the same reasons. Cooldowns are long against fights of about 50 ticks (a medium cooldown is 30, so a lock fires once or twice); cooldowns carry between fights and don't reset (to keep); targeting of basic attacks and most abilities is random, so stacks and Marks spread instead of piling; the party is outnumbered; and the unit meant to run the engine is the one that dies. The game as tuned is attrition, and "builds that aren't grindy are hard to pull off". The director noticed this as a trend across Thousand Cuts, Bonfire, Cold Snap, the Hunt, Ambush, Overflow, Lockdown and Phoenix.
4. **What worked was a visible status you stack and a core system you invest in.** Landslide (Shield, Aggro, Defense), the Toll (Thorns, Taunt), Bonfire as "collect the orange things" (Burn). Each piece was good alone, so the build paid before it was complete, and the thread was visible on the field as a counter or a bar. The rhythm layered on top (the chain, the pass, the beat) is what didn't show. The philosophy said a build is never one keyword turned up; in play the keyword was the only part that read.
5. **Trigger payoffs are too small to perceive.** A few Might, a first cast free, a tenth off a cost, Clarity once a fight, a cooldown a few ticks closer: next to Chill, Burn and Thorns dealing "hundreds and hundreds", these don't register, and some are never seen to fire. *Data:* Might is 6% a stack, cap 15, and spends a stack on every action, so "a few" (about three stacks) is three actions at +18%. A tenth off a cost is about 30 MP of a 1,000 to 3,800 pool. Overflow's rung 3 pieces (a free cast per ally, free casts on Clarity) read as the first usable level.
6. **Preconditions the enemies rarely supply are worth nothing.** Silence immunity (two of 36 enemies Silence: the Wraith and the Silent One; none Stuns); payoffs on a disabled foe (the party's own Stuns last 8 ticks, Silence 16, on medium cooldowns); first-X effects in fights that run long past the first X.
7. **A single unit's four slots synergize more easily than three units.** The strongest thing seen was one Assassin in a full Bleed-and-crit set with Ambush pieces: a gear set, not a party build. Within one unit a player can see and assemble a synergy; across three units, with random targeting and a tank that dies first, the web didn't form.
8. **Death and HP are too costly to spend.** A knockout carries into the next fight and can't be potioned, so Phoenix's risk dwarfs its payoff; HP is what the game drains, so paying it was never considered, and Blood Pact's pieces were used as sustain. There's no agency over who falls or when: the highest-Aggro unit does, slowly, and its fall leaves survivors who are near full and who then collapse. *Check:* when someone falls in the simulation, it's the highest-Aggro unit about half the time, and the other two average about three quarters of their HP.
9. **Opportunity cost in a slot beats spread.** A piece that reaches one or two more foes lost to one that did more to the foes already hit, unless the slot was empty. Area was valued as power, not as a build.
10. **Readability decided which builds existed in the player's mind.** Burn and Chill were understood as builds because their status is visible and colour-coded; the Hunt and Harvest were never recognized as builds; Lockdown's pieces read as "what is this design"; "lighter kit" and several formations were cryptic. Cards say what a piece does and never what it's for; in play that left only the visible-status builds legible.
11. **Formations read when they were about the front line.** Aggro, Defense and protecting the squishies were understood and used (Heavy line, Shieldwall, The mountain, Thorn hedge, Blood oath). Engine formations weren't: Wellspring felt like a debuff, The choir's no-regen a risk not worth taking, Relay never started, Gaol added nothing visible, The ambuscade was plainly worse.
12. **Class weakness shaped the builds more than their pieces did.** The Bard "a dead unit", the Pyromancer and Stormcaller dying in most fights, the Frostweaver weak: routes through them didn't get played.

## Build by build

| Build | In play | What worked | What didn't, and why | Learning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landslide | The best: effective and interesting | Shield into big hits and crits; every piece good alone; many routes; formations about Aggro and Defense served it | Nothing named | A build on a core system, whose pieces each pay alone, gets built and recognized |
| Thousand Cuts | Never as a party; once as one Assassin's gear set | Bleed, crit, multi-hit pieces valued on the Assassin, Ranger, Monk | Evasion isn't survival below the cap; the Bard dead, the casters die, the Ranger wanted elsewhere; crits inconsistent without guarantees; attrition punishes non-grindy play | An evasion party can't live in this action economy; single-unit synergy is what the slots support |
| Bonfire | Worked as "the burn build"; the rhythm didn't | Burn as a stackable investment across gear, versions, formations; fits a tank party since tanks have Burn | Burn is upkeep-limited (no cap; 15% of stacks drop a pulse), hard to hold on one foe, kills slowly; so the whole board burns and nothing passes on | A status you can collect reads as a build even when the designed rhythm is absent |
| Cold Snap | A handful of shatters in 30 games | Clearly signposted | Twelve stacks, thawing one per 14 ticks, from random-target applications; only Area Chill piles; long cooldowns; squishy, as no tank or healer Chills | A payoff at a cap needs focused application or fast stacking; it also needs a tank who contributes |
| The Hunt | Never recognized as a build | Mark pieces made any party a little more consistent | Few Mark sources (four pieces, two formations, Condemn), on long cooldowns, marking the wrong foe; kill payoffs (Might) too small; no burst to follow in an attrition game | Chaining kills needs a consistent, cheap focus tool and payoffs on the scale of the statuses |
| Harvest | Never recognized as a build | Area pieces valued as raw power | Spread lost to focus on the slot's opportunity cost; not frequent or synergistic enough to read as a plan | Spread has to be a plan the player can see, not a bonus they pass over |
| Ambush | Once, as the Assassin set | The pieces that do reset cooldowns worked | No reset otherwise; first-X effects run out; a party that functions while building it is the tank party, and picking for Ambush loses the run; formations cryptic or worse | A build has to be viable on the way; an opening with no tail isn't |
| Overflow | Never, even a little | Pieces read as nice economy actions | Effects too small to perceive; engine seats are squishy and dead first; cooldowns too long to see a link twice; formations felt like debuffs | An engine build needs a visible, sizeable link and an engine that survives the fight |
| Lockdown | The worst | Nothing | The window is rare (8 ticks, medium cooldowns, no enemy Stuns to exploit) and nothing coordinates the pile-in; Gaol's seats read as nothing | A payoff behind a rare, short window that the player can't make happen isn't a build |
| The Toll | The strongest and most consistent; the default | Everything: being tanky and Taunting is already necessary, so damage from being hit comes free | Dominant and uncountered; it's one of the only viable parties | The default party already is the Toll; its dominance is the survivability economy, not its pieces |
| Blood Pact | Worse than bad, as a pact; good as sustain | Lifesteal, swings from max HP, Blood oath, taken for healing | Paying HP is refused because HP is what the game drains; few triggers reward it | HP can't be a currency while it's the scarce resource |
| Phoenix | Heavily signposted; never worth the risk | Nothing | A knockout carries into the run; revives rare and slow; the tank falls first and the survivors are full, so fall payoffs (heals, a few Might) do nothing; no agency over who dies | Death can't be a turn while it's a run loss; fall payoffs sized to the statuses' hundreds would be the floor |

## Checks made against the data

- Enemy control: Silence on two enemies (Wraith, Silent One); no enemy Stun. Silence immunity and Waking bell are worth little by design.
- Party locks in the base kits: only Unmake waits for a cast; Shield bash hits a random foe, Pressure point the strongest, Garrote a random non-Taunter, Tremor a random foe. The waiting ones are versions (Measured blow, Choke, Frozen moment) and Gaol's seat. Stun 8 ticks, Silence 16; cooldown words 14, 22, 30, 40, 56 (revives); regular fights about 50 ticks, bosses about 76.
- Sources: Marked on 4 pieces, 2 formations and Condemn; Hidden on 4 pieces and Vanish; Stun on 3 abilities, 4 pieces, Gaol; Weaken on 2 abilities, 3 pieces, Thorn hedge; Chill on 5 abilities and 11 pieces; Burn on 5 and 10; Taunt on 2 abilities, 6 pieces, 4 formations; Shield on 17 pieces; Guard 10; Haste 12; Might 10.
- Might: 6% a stack, cap 15, spent one stack an action. Burn: 55 a stack every 2 ticks, no cap, 15% of stacks drop a pulse. Chill: cap 12, thaws one per 14 ticks, shatters for 250 a stack and a Stun. Evasion: cap 75 (placeholder), medium 20, high 35.
- The first-fall measurement (the build parties on their own act's fights, one seed): at heat 0, won 100%, someone fell in 3%; knob +60: won 94%, fell 17%; knob +120: won 79%, fell 38%, the first to fall the highest-Aggro unit 48% of the time, the other two at 74% of their HP, the fight lost after a fall 54% (bosses 82%). The script is in the session's scratch, not the repo.

## Open questions for the director

1. Which version and which heat were the 30 sessions on, and where did runs usually end? The harness at heat 0 wins nearly everything with hand-built parties.
2. What kills the squishies: the number of foes, the tank falling first, Area attacks, or the healer running dry? Is the healer for the fight or for carrying HP between fights?
3. The Assassin set: which pieces, and was the rest of that party a tank and a healer?
4. Landslide: did the big blow show as an event, or was it the stone keeping everyone alive while crits came? Which pieces are remembered?
5. Bonfire: is "collect the colour" an acceptable identity for a build, or does the rhythm have to show?
6. Cold Snap: was the Frostweaver fielded with Chill on the others, or benched? Did a shatter feel big when it came?
7. Is a build allowed to exist only as a visible status counter? Would in-game naming of builds be acceptable, against the no-signposting rule?
8. Which formations were picked most, and did any ever make you rethink a party?
9. Did any version choices shape a build? Which are remembered?
10. Phoenix: would a chosen martyr (a seat that draws far more with HP down) count as agency, or is a planned death unacceptable while a knockout carries into the run?
