# The colours

How the web is sorted: five colours as its poles, on the model of Magic's colour pie. This page is the frame. The colours themselves aren't designed yet: that's the wheel, the current step (`steps/the-wheel/`). Magic is the model for the structure only, never for the content.

## The director's rulings

The director's standing direction on this page's subject. Direction is guidance, not law: challenge any of it with evidence. Until a line is changed here, it stands, and it wins over the rest of this page and over older lines elsewhere: where they differ, fix the other page. When the director changes a ruling, rewrite its line; don't add history.

- **Five colours are the web's poles,** in place of the builds. A colour is a temperament, not an element and not a role. Every colour has its own ways to sustain and to kill, and a party of one colour can be built. A colour's exclusions come from its opposites.
- **A colour is four things:** what it never does, the mechanics it prefers, the ways to win it leans to, and its style: its own way of doing whatever it does, so a mechanic two colours share behaves differently in each.
- **Four ways to win, none exclusive,** and none a mirror or an opposite of another. A colour leans toward some and owns none; a party draws on several. Each is wide enough to be done in many ways, and the comparisons that matter are inside a way (how this colour does it, against how that one does), not between the ways.
- **A colour's profile is stars on the four ways, and a style in each way it leans to.** Every colour has a peak; where two colours share one, their style tells them apart. There's no equal budget: a colour can be wide, or narrow and spiky and weaker overall, so some pairs come easily and some are exotic.
- **A class is a commander:** one colour or several, read by the colours its mechanics carry. It comes into the design only as far as the party needs it.
- **The builds don't carry over,** not as a test of the colours and not as a target. A build's shape, a deck a party ends as, is the model; the nine's spread and fun are unproven.
- **Later, not now:** whether colours are shown to the player, and what stops a party taking the best of every colour (the mana question).

## The layers

| Layer | In Magic | Here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanics | keywords, card types | the threads, the statuses, the stats, the gear kinds |
| Poles | the five colours | the five colours |
| Commanders | the commander | the classes, one colour or several |
| Ways to win | aggro, control, tempo… | burst, build up, debuff or disable, sustain |
| Decks | archetypes, and all the rest | what a party ends as; none is designed in advance as a target |

Everything a party is made of is a colour's card: the classes' kits and versions, the formations, the gear kinds, the gear and its effects. What has no colour is the exotic.

## The ways to win

Each is a wide space of its own, done in many ways. None is paired with, mirrors or opposes another.

- **Burst:** damage that lands earlier than it otherwise would, in any size and number of hits.
- **Build up:** the party growing more dangerous as the fight goes on, in itself or in what it lays on the foes.
- **Debuff or disable:** turning the fight's terms against the foes, in what they can do to the party and in what the party can do to them.
- **Sustain:** not dying, and keeping the fight going: harm kept off, harm mended, and the fuel to go on.

## A colour

- **What it never does,** traced to its opposites.
- **The mechanics it prefers,** some its own, some shared and done its way.
- **Its profile:** one to four stars on each way to win, never none, since every colour can do each its own way, however weakly. Every colour has a peak and a hole, however wide it is: a colour with no hole pairs with anything and never needs a partner, which is a colour good at everything by another road.
- **Its temperament is the connective tissue between its mechanics:** a pattern, not something born from any one mechanic. Most mechanics belong to more than one colour, read through each one's temperament and set among different company, so colours aren't sorted by mechanics. A temperament still has to be carried by gameplay, or it's a theme painted on.
- **Its style,** inside each way it leans to. One way to write it: a few questions any way can be asked (how big and how often, how wide, when, at what price), answered differently by each colour that does it.

**Symmetry is a pull to resist.** Five colours invite giving each the same shape, and four ways invite mirroring. Neither is wanted.

## What checks a wheel

On paper, before anything is built:
- **Coverage:** no colour has a hole so deep it can't sustain or kill, and each can field a party alone.
- **Shape:** every colour has a peak and a hole; a shared peak is told apart by style; no two colours have the same profile.
- **Pairs:** what two colours cover together, and what they lack; some pairs come easily and some are exotic.
- **Opposites:** each colour's exclusions can be traced to its opposites.
- **Identity:** each colour reads as one thing, by the director, on its card.

Whether the parties that come out are fun is judged later: the director on samples, then simulation and the playtest.

## The order of work

One layer at a time, each checked against what exists without changing it. Nothing in the game changes before the classes.

1. **The wheel:** the five colours, each on one card (what it never does, the mechanics it prefers, its profile and style, what it can't answer, named as the challenges it loses to), and the checks above.
2. **The pie:** the threads against the colours (primary, secondary, never) and how each colour's style reads a shared thread. Then the existing effects and pieces tagged by colour: how much survives, which colours are thin, what has no colour.
3. **The classes,** read through the pie: which colours each one's mechanics carry.
4. **Authored gaps resumes** (`steps/authored-gaps/`), making loot into the pie's holes.
5. **The run and the numbers:** the mana question, whether colours are shown, recruit offers (outside the start's draft, recruits mostly come one at a time), and simulation that the pairs are viable.
