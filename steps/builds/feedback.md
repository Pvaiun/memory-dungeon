# The director's read of the twelve builds

Their subjective memory of about 30 sessions of play, Nov 9, condensed per build; their words quoted where the wording matters. The learnings drawn from it are in `learnings.md`.

**Landslide.** "Probably the most effective build and the most interesting." The fantasy (a lot of Shield, leveraged into big hits and crits) worked. Why: every piece was useful on its own, because Shield is useful on its own; the Geomancer was central but not necessary, since Shields are on enough classes and upgrades; no formation said Shield, but many said Aggro, Defense and protection, so there were many ways to build it. "It plays straight into our aggro and defense systems." Building it "felt very creative", and it won.

**Thousand Cuts.** Never came together as described. Its pieces (Bleed, crit, Evasion, multi-hit) were rated highly on the Assassin, Ranger and Monk, and the strongest thing seen in 30 sessions was an Assassin in a full set of this gear plus some Ambush gear, doing massive damage through Bleed and crits: "an ultra synergistic gear set on a single unit", not a build. Why it didn't converge: the Bard was "mostly a dead unit"; the Ranger suited other builds better (elemental, crit); the Pyromancer and Stormcaller "auto died in most fights"; and the game doesn't support an evasion team. A tank and a healer, or a bruiser team, feel mandatory. Evasion isn't a survivability strategy below the 75% cap: only the Monk near the cap can do it, and one or two missed dodges is death; at 40% it isn't consistent enough when three or four hits kill a squishy, and the healer can't be evasive. A 100% Evasion on a timer would be "taunt with another name". Crits aren't consistent without a guaranteed-crit piece or version, so the fight has to run long. Cooldowns not resetting (keep it) and being outnumbered make non-grindy builds hard. Maybe with four or five party members.

**Bonfire.** Read as "the burn build": collect everything orange through gear, upgrades and formations. It worked and felt clever to assemble. The rhythm didn't happen: Burn caps or soft-caps low from application speed, doesn't kill fast, and is hard to keep on one foe, so the whole board burns and ticks down, "a high damage gambit on a fast kill based on your pyromancer's survivability". Even maxed on one foe it doesn't die fast enough to pass anything on. Burn as a concept, an itemization path and a stackable investment worked; the chaining bonfire didn't. It helped that tanky units have Burn, so it fit the tank-and-healer party.

**Cold Snap.** Clearly signposted, like fire. A handful of shatters in 30 games. Cooldowns are long and the targeting of abilities and basic attacks is random, so only Area Chill stacks the same foe; it loops a little on bosses. Not a viable team plan: it needs luck with the right items, is still underpowered, and is squishy, since healers and tanks have no Chill (a problem of the tank-and-healer meta, not the build).

**The Hunt.** Never came together. Not enough Mark sources: a Mark piece now and then, sometimes helpful, often marking the wrong foe. Payoffs underwhelming: Might degrades every attack, so it never stacks across kills. Lots of little triggers meant to chain, but no consistent Mark off a long cooldown, no guarantee of the squishiest, and even when it fired there was no burst to follow, "because it's kind of an attrition game". Mark ended up a status you find on gear that makes the party a bit more consistent at its other build. Possibly not built into deeply enough.

**Harvest.** The same as the Hunt: Area effects were valued and powerful but not frequent, widespread or synergistic enough to read as a build. "I didn't know that The Hunt or Harvest were builds I was trying to go for": pieces with cool effects. A signposting problem or a content problem? Taking a spread piece meant not taking something else, and spreading damage onto one or two more foes didn't beat doing more to the targets already hit, so they were taken only for an empty slot.

**Ambush.** Worked once, with the Assassin above (the same party as the Cuts case), and felt like a strong unit, not a build. The volley is neutered by no cooldown reset outside specific pieces (which did work). Unclear effects: "lighter kit" meant nothing. First-X effects are hard to value in long fights: the first hit does double, then the enemies aren't dead. It needs a party that functions while it's being built, and that party is the tanky one; picking for Ambush loses the run before it comes online. Formations especially bad: cryptic, or clearly worse (The ambuscade: everyone's first action twice "doesn't kill anything, and then now you're just weaker for the rest of the fight"). Not that it can't work; this implementation wasn't viable.

**Overflow.** Its pieces came up often and read as good economy actions one by one; it read as a build only through the formations. It never worked even a little. First, the effects weren't tuned to the game: a first cast free barely matters, a tenth cheaper is meaningless, Silence immunity when almost no enemy silences, Clarity once a fight. "It felt like the rung 3 ones were the base level of what could be actually usable." Second, it was hard to tell when it was working. The choir's no-regen is a risk you won't take; Wellspring "felt like a debuff" (Attack down for sustain you can't perceive, and a tenth off a cost "almost insultingly" small); Relay reads well on paper, but the casters are dead before the engine starts, and with long inconsistent cooldowns one cast helps another once and the fight is over. It relied on squishy units living long for small value over time. A trend: "a hit begets a hit begets a hit" styles may not fit the game, or we haven't cracked how to do them.

**Lockdown.** "The single worst build." The condition (a disabled foe) is far too rare: disables are on cooldown, last six or seven ticks, and a basic attack is unlikely even to land in the window; nothing in the game coordinates the pile-in. The pieces read as "what is this design?" No formation spoke to it understandably. Gaol: most Stuns and Silences already wait for a wind-up, or some do; Aggro on a seat didn't help; the turnkey's ticks closer from one or two locks a fight is no build.

**The Toll.** "The strongest, most consistent build in the game. It almost felt like it's hard not to make this build." Almost always outnumbered, its units are the ones that work, tanky and Taunt are necessary, so it's what you do by default and you have to push to do anything else. "Survivability right now is the most precious resource in the whole game", so a build that does damage while surviving just happens. Not that its stats are too widespread: the strategy is dominant, has no counter, loses to nothing, and is one of the only viable ones.

**Blood Pact.** "Worse than bad." The Vampire healing itself is a unit, not a build. Lifesteal, swings from max HP and Blood oath were good, on tanky builds, as sustain: the game drains HP and these give it back, never because HP was spent. Anything asking HP in place of MP was "anathema to success", never used, and few triggers would have rewarded it; "it felt like a lot of ways to heal." Partly the survivability issue.

**Phoenix.** Heavily signposted. Dying is the worst thing in the game: a knockout carries past the fight, past potions, so a revive is needed; Raise went off once or twice, and the payoff is nowhere near the risk; sometimes the fight ends before the revive, "a victory but a dead party member, and then a dead run". Revives are inconsistent and on few kit elements. The fundamental flaw: you need Aggro, Taunt and heals to survive, so the default party is tank, damage, healer; "the first unit to die in 90% of fights is your tank", with the others near full because of Taunt; the fall buffs the two "goobers" who then get nuked; the heal and reset effects do nothing because the survivors are already full; the revive is too slow to capitalize. The payoffs (a couple of Might) are nowhere near "the hundreds and hundreds of damage" of Chill, Burn or Thorns. A full squishy party can't work, and any party has a highest-Aggro unit that dies first. "You have no agency in it."

**Across the twelve.** The pass is to collate learnings for another agent; suggestions are welcome but not the goal. The twelve aren't to be preserved and repaired as such: some may go, new ones may come. The rhythm principles still guide.
