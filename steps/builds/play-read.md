# Builds: what 23 real runs say

A designer's read of the director's own runs (23 saved runs, 150 fights, Oct 31 to Nov 4, engine 0.17 and 0.18), against the twelve builds in `docs/gear-design/builds.md`. It asks one question: did the builds happen, and where they didn't, why not. It is not a balance read; the knobs, win rates and the act II elites are out of scope except where they explain a build.

**Method.** Every run's record was replayed at the commit it was played on, fight by fight, with the log on. All 150 fights replayed to the same outcome and the same HP, so what follows is the fights as they were. Three things were read: what was offered and taken (gear, formations, training, recruits, tagged with the designer's build data), what each party wore and sat in at each fight, and where each fight's damage came from (basic attacks, abilities, each status). The director's own impressions are the other half of this picture and win where they differ.

## The short version

- **Two builds happened and carried runs: Bonfire and the Bleed hunt** (Thousand Cuts and the Hunt, woven together). Each was assembled by the player across a run, each reached rung 2 or 3, and together they account for the two deepest runs. Burn and Bleed were the two biggest sources of party damage in the whole sample.
- **The Toll happened constantly, at rung 1, from kits alone** (the Bramble knight, the Knight's Hold the line, Spinebark pole, Ring of steel), and died exactly where the document says it fears to: bosses, Liches, slow single hitters.
- **Phoenix was the most taken and the least rewarding.** Its pieces were everywhere (insurance), its formations were fought in more than any other, and its turn in the party's favour almost never came: a fall in this game is nearly always the start of the end.
- **Landslide, Cold Snap, Lockdown, Harvest, Overflow and Ambush did not happen.** Their pieces were taken (Landslide's a lot), but never on the unit, with the partner, in the seat, that the build needs. Cold Snap was tried four times and produced twelve shatters in 150 fights.
- **Three structural reasons explain more than any single piece does:** units fought with one ability known (264 of 450 unit-fights), act I parties fought with under two of four slots filled, and 71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral. A build needs a kit, a few pieces and a seat; in act I, where 17 of 23 runs ended, the game gives one of the three.
- **The player reads cards for their party and takes defence.** Taunt, Shields, Thorns, revives and lifesteal were taken at the highest rates; the openers, the sweeps, the mana engines and the stone blows at the lowest. Every run ended with the tank falling first.

## What shaped every build

These cut across the twelve and set what any one build could become.

### Kits arrive one ability at a time, and mostly stay that way
At fight time, 264 of 450 unit-fights were fought by a unit knowing one ability, 152 knowing two, 34 knowing three. Training went to learning (63 picks) and versions (74) over power steps (35), which is the right instinct, but a unit with one ability is a basic attacker: the Cleric dealt 98% of its damage with its mace, the Hexer 86%, the Bard 85%, the Valkyrie 79%, the Paladin 76%. The builds are written as kits plus loot; the parties as played were sticks plus loot. A Frostweaver that starts with Stillness, a Cleric with Raise, a Valkyrie with Valhalla, a Paladin with Sanctuary each fought three or four fights contributing nothing of its identity, and in two runs the Frostweaver was benched or sent home for it.

### Act I gives one piece a unit, and runs end in act I
In act I, parties fought with the weapon slot filled 99% of the time, armor 28%, offhand 24%, amulet 16%. By act II it is three slots of four. Seventeen of 23 runs ended in act I (eleven before the boss, six at it). Rung 2 of any build asks for two or three pieces on one unit plus a seat or a version; the game supplies that in act II, after most runs are over. The builds that happened early happened through the kit (the Toll) or through one basic-attack rider plus a formation (Bonfire).

### Formations were the part that didn't work
- 71 of the 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral; every act II fight was in a formation.
- Ten formations owned at a run's end were never fought in (First light four times; Thorn hedge, Reaping line, Hearth, Watchtower, Heavy line, Arrowhead, Blood oath, The wheel once each).
- The formations fought in most were the plain ones: Pyre of heroes (11 fights), Shieldwall (9), Vigil (9), Wellspring (7), Ring of steel (6), The undying (6), Heavy line (6). The build-defining rares and legendaries were taken rarely and fought in once each: Hunting party, The pack, The vise, Relay, Hoarfrost line (3), Hammer and anvil (3), Blood oath (2), Spur (3).
- Formation offers by build: Overflow's were taken at 16%, Thousand Cuts' at 29%, Blood Pact's 31%, Cold Snap's 33%; Harvest's at 73%, Phoenix's 65%, the Hunt's 64%, the Toll's 59%.
- Two runs show the player toggling: 26 formation changes in a ten-fight run, 16 in an eleven-fight run, cycling through everything owned before a fight and often landing back where they started.
- Seats were filled by whoever was in the party, not by role: Hammer and anvil with a Cleric in the Hammer seat and an Assassin, then a Pyromancer, as the Mason; Hoarfrost line with a Knight and a Bramble knight as the two Frost seats; The pack with the Cleric as the Lead; Spur with a Bramble knight as the pacer and a Geomancer as a blade; The vise on a party with no Stun or Silence.

The director's intent was that a new formation makes you rethink the party. In these runs the party was rethought for other reasons (who is alive, who has gear, who the captive was), and the formation was fitted to the party afterwards, by the seats' stat words, or left at Neutral.

### The roster churns, so builds don't accumulate
Long runs swapped members 12 to 19 times. Eleven of thirteen captives and strangers were taken. Legendaries followed the churn: of 54 legendary and mythic pieces taken, 11 were never worn in a fight and about a dozen more were worn in one or two (Volley bow twice taken, never worn; Still hand, Bell of the hour, Vestments of return twice, Earthmother totem once, Windrunner's garb, Thousandth cut once, Cinderheart staff, Idol of the hungry dark). The wearer was on the bench, or the piece needed a trigger the party never had.

### The player takes defence
Build-tag weight of pieces taken against offered, over all runs: Cold Snap 52% (small sample, mostly Frostwalker's furs and Icebreaker), Thousand Cuts 49%, the Toll 46%, Phoenix 46%, Overflow 43%, the Hunt 42%, Lockdown 42%, Harvest 39%, Bonfire 39%, Blood Pact 38%, Landslide 38%, Ambush 31%. Among legendaries at the far end, Phoenix's were offered 26 times and taken 9, far more than any other build (Blood Pact 17 offered, 5 taken; Bonfire 3 offered, 1 taken). The Knight ended 19 of its 65 fights knocked out and the Bramble knight 8 of 29; the tank falls first in nearly every lost fight, so taking Taunt, Shields and revives is a rational answer to what the player keeps watching.

### What the player watched was statuses
In 26 of 150 fights more than half the party's damage came from statuses laid on foes, and in the Bonfire and Bleed runs it was 50% to 73% fight after fight. Burn (437k), Bleed (397k) and Thorns (157k) were the three biggest damage sources in the whole sample; Shock (80k) and Chill (25k) far behind. On screen that damage belongs to no unit, which is what makes those builds watchable, and may be why the Hexer, whose Malison deepens everyone else's statuses, was the lowest-share class (12%) and was benched or died in 7 of 19 fights.

## Build by build

**Bonfire: happened, rung 3, the deepest run.** The Ranger-led run of Nov 3 16:46 reached act III on Paladin, Hexer and Pyromancer in Hearth: Keeper, Stoker and Torch. The threads were all there and the player chose them one by one: Hex of flames (the Hexer's swings add Burn), Zeal (the Paladin's swings add Burn to a debuffed foe), Kindling (the Pyromancer's swings add Burn), Slow burn, Flashfire, Pyre of judgement, Hexweave mail, Crown of embers at the end. Burn was over half the party's damage in every elite and boss fight but Bomb Nest (37%, with Shock making up the rest); peak Burn climbed from 27 to 59 stacks across the run; the Torch seat did the passing-on. It is the clearest case of a build read, chosen and working. Two things didn't happen: the fire passing from a dying foe (Wick was offered once and skipped, Ember heart once in a shop and not bought), and the party's own heat (Kindled robes offered twice, never bought). It died to Honour Guard in act III with Burn still at 64% of its damage. A second run (Oct 31 23:45, Knight, Pyromancer, Ranger in Shieldwall) reached act II floor 11 on Kindling, Fireball and Pyre with Burn at 28% to 58% of its damage, without the player chasing it.

**Thousand Cuts and the Hunt: happened together, as one Bleed build, rung 3.** The Ranger-led run of Nov 3 02:20: Assassin with Thousandth cut (Cuts climbing to 17), Venom vial, Gambler's coin, Opening wound, Coup de grâce and Night's edge; Ranger with Deep barbs, Pack leader's coat and a Briar totem; Knight in Heavy line's Holder seat. Bleed was 46% to 64% of the party's damage in act I's elites and boss, 28% to 48% in act II, and kills came as a staircase (the Packmaster fell at ticks 24, 28, 31, 39, 54). Coup de grâce was up to 83% of the Assassin's damage in act II: an upgrade carrying a build. The two builds are one here because their pieces are: Gambler's coin feeds Lacerate, Lacerate feeds Venom vial, Night's edge and Pack leader's coat pay on the kill. The run died when the Assassin was benched for a bare Hexer against seven hellhounds, with the Knight carried in knocked out for a tick-one Valhalla. The Hunt's focus thread (Marked) showed up in about twenty fights across six runs (Marking knife in three runs, Huntmaster's quiver, Gravemark twice, Hunting party, The pack) and steered basic attacks; it never steered the party, because abilities keep their own targets.

**The Toll: happened at rung 1 in five runs, from the kit; rung 2 once; never rung 3.** Thorns was the Bramble knight's biggest damage source (44% of its own over 29 fights, 67% to 89% against wolf packs). The Knight's Hold the line turned a Knight into an anchor (77% of its damage from Thorns against Night Wolves) with no gear at all. Spinebark pole, Briar totem, Portcullis, Urchin shell and Ring of steel's Beacon were taken and used. Rung 2, what attackers carry away, happened once: the Cleric-led run of Nov 2 17:54 (Bramble knight with Briar totem beside a Vampire) ran Bleed at 29% to 53% of its damage from its third fight to its eleventh and died to the Lich Lair, two foes who barely swing, which is the Toll's written fear. Every Toll run died at a boss or a two-foe elite. Weaken on the attackers (Thorn hedge) was taken twice and never fought in; Thorns that grow (Thornheart plate) and the Bramble knight's mythic never appeared. The dodge that pays (Duelist's leathers) was worn in two fights, on a Monk and a Valkyrie, neither of whom drew the blows.

**Phoenix: attempted most, functioned as insurance, never as a turn.** Pyre of heroes, Vigil and The undying were fought in 26 fights across seven runs; Lamp of vigil, Martyr's locket, Last bastion, Bones of the returned, Thrall's chalice, Mantle of the fallen, Challenger's plate and Phoenix ember were all worn. What happened: a party member fell in 49 of 150 fights, and 23 of those were lost; eleven revives happened in the whole sample, six of them the after-the-fight stands of Lamp of vigil and The undying, and each of the five in-fight revives was followed by the revived unit falling again before the fight ended. The martyr's fall did fire Pyre of heroes once (the Chief's Return, won) and The undying three times (the others healed to full, the Paladin back after the fight), which is the one place the build worked as written. Against it: Last bastion on the Martyr seat held the Knight at one HP in two of its three fights there, so the seat's fall never came while it could have mattered, two Phoenix pieces cancelling each other. The return with everything ready (Einherjar, Bones of the returned) never happened in a fight. The document's own warning, that the second rung is thin, is borne out: a fall is almost never a turn, because the fight is already lost.

**Blood Pact: attempted twice, functioned on one unit, never as a party.** Blood oath with a Vampire Bleeder, Blood covenant and Bloodsworn mail (Oct 31 13:28): the Vampire took 8,459 and healed 8,449 in the fight it lost, alone, after the Cleric fell at tick 11 and the Assassin at 21. Life passed on (Shared feast, the Donor seat) had nobody to pass to. The Wellspring Vampire of Nov 2 17:54 with Savage drain was the best Vampire seen (31% to 44% of its party's damage) and that run's Cleric wore Phylactery and Censer mace: the pact's donor pieces work as healer pieces. HP as a cost showed up once (Blood vial, one fight).

**Landslide: pieces taken in quantity, the build never assembled.** Earthmother totem was worn for nine fights by a Ranger in a party with one Shield source; Worldbreaker for six fights by a Knight who was knocked out in three of them; Cornerstone maul one fight, Heartsplitter three, Icebreaker three, Rubble plate five, Spiked bulwark two, War horn one, Stone totem never, Millstone offered four times and never taken. Hammer and anvil was fought three times with a Cleric as the Hammer. The Geomancer fought 20 fights and never sat in a Hammer seat or swung a Millstone or a Cornerstone maul; its big blows were Rockfall (13% to 58% of its own damage) and the crits that came while its Shield held and stopped when it broke. No fight in the sample showed the rhythm (a few huge blows); the biggest single hits were Coup de grâce, Backstab and Skewer. One build nobody planned did happen: Rubble plate on a Knight with Stonewall and Earthmother turned the Shield breaking into 50% of the party's damage in one act II fight, which is the stone becoming the blow, on the wrong class.

**Cold Snap: attempted four times, never functioned.** Twelve shatters in 150 fights, at most three in one fight, nine fights with any. The Frostweaver fought 16 fights in three runs: Chill reached its cap in most of them and shattered about once, then thawed. Frost arrow and Cold hex beside it didn't change that. Hoarfrost line's two Frost seats went to a Knight and a Bramble knight; Chill peaked at 2 to 8 and never shattered in those three fights. Frostwalker's furs (seven fights) and Winter's chain (one) need shatters to do anything. Rime knuckles, Heart of winter, Frostbite bow and Primer of frost were each offered once and skipped. The Frostweaver did 61% of its damage with its weapon, was dismissed from one roster and benched in two others, and the Ranger-led run of Oct 31 14:27 lost its War Camp fight with Chill at its cap and the Frostweaver swinging its rod. The build's rhythm, bursts on a beat you can count, was never on screen.

**Lockdown: pieces taken, never a build.** Stuns on foes came one or two a fight from Shield bash, Tremor and Pressure point; Silence once a fight from Unmake or Garrote; a wind-up was caught five times in the sample. Chains of light was worn four fights by a Paladin whose Smite rarely fired (Absolve first in the order, and in several fights no ability fired at all). The vise was fought once, by a party with no Stun or Silence. Idol of binding was offered five times and never taken; Opportunist's charm four times, taken once, never worn; Still hand taken, never worn; Gaol never offered. Measured blow and Frozen moment, the director's wait-for-the-wind-up versions, were each trained once, and the unit was benched or the fight ended. Nothing in a card reads as "I will hold them, then hit them" to a player who has no lock yet.

**Harvest: rung 1 happened by accident, the rest never.** The Stormcaller dealt 49% to 61% of its party's damage against packs (Nov 1 17:35), most of it through Thunderstorm, and Oathkeeper on a Knight turned a Taunt into a sweep (the sweep was 27% to 79% of the Knight's damage, but the Knight's share of the party's was 3% to 10%). Sweeping blade on a Bramble knight, Chain wand on a Bard, Cataclysm staff on a Cleric whose third ability was Raise: the spread pieces landed on units with nothing to spread. Stormweave was offered four times, Last page twice, Storm pike once; none taken. Reaping line taken once, never fought in. The field never fell together.

**Overflow: least attempted, its formations refused most.** Wellspring was fought seven times, with the two cheapest casters in the game as its Spenders, chosen because its card plainly says "regains mana", which every run needed: 86 tonics were drunk against 62 potions, the Monk ended most fights under 150 MP, the Frostweaver and Stormcaller at zero. Relay was fought once (a Paladin with Absolve as the Well); The choir never offered; Horn of the keep skipped for a recruit; Annotated grimoire skipped; Bell of the hour taken and never worn; Archmage's mantle worn three fights on a Bard. Nobody ever cast all fight, because nobody had the abilities to cast.

**Ambush: taken least, never a build.** First light was owned five times and fought in once. The ambuscade was offered once and the player took First light over it. Knives drawn, Quietus (twice), Drawn steel (twice), Horn of the charge never, Volley bow taken twice and never worn, Ready stance worn two fights on a Bramble knight, Shadow cloak worn once. The Assassin's Vanish fired one to three times a fight in the early runs and then the slot order moved on. Nothing in the fight's text marks the opening as a moment, so there is nothing for the player to build toward.

## Not seen at all

Formations never fought in by anyone: Thorn hedge, Gaol, Reaping line, Long march, Knives drawn, The ambuscade, The choir, The forge, The hurricane, The mountain, The storm front (the last five were never offered either). Mythics never offered: Heartwood mail, Ember palms, Metronome, Eye of the tempest, Glacier's heart, Mountain's crown, Stormspear, Halo of the saint, The last wall, The witch-blade. Pieces never offered that a build's far end rests on: Thornheart plate, Sunhammer, Wildmagic wand, Pike wall, Horn of the charge, Static mail, Treatise on the field. Pieces offered and never taken that a build's second rung rests on: Idol of binding (five offers), Millstone (four), Stormweave (four), Kindled robes (four), Wick, Ember heart, Rime knuckles, Heart of winter, Signal lantern (two), Tracker's coat (two), Last page (two).

## Samples worth watching

Each is a saved run and a fight, replayable from the record.
- **Bonfire at full heat:** Nov 3 16:46, the Giant's Wrath (act II boss): Burn 65% of the party's damage, peak 59 stacks, Hearth.
- **Bonfire losing anyway:** the same run, Honour Guard (act III elite): Burn 64%, four soldiers, the martyr falls at tick 18.
- **The Bleed hunt's staircase:** Nov 3 02:20, the Packmaster: Bleed 64%, kills at 24, 28, 31, 39, 54; then Imp Kennel and the Miners in act II, Coup de grâce at 69% to 83% of the Assassin's damage.
- **The Toll working and then meeting its fear:** Nov 2 17:54, Night Wolves (Thorns and Bleed 53%, seven kills in 21 ticks) and then Lich Lair.
- **Cold Snap not shattering:** Oct 31 21:02, Wolf Pack and the Packmaster: Chill at its cap all fight, one shatter each.
- **Phoenix pieces cancelling:** Nov 3 02:20, Ogre Bridge and Twin Ogres: the Martyr in Last bastion held at one HP, the seat's flare never fires.
- **Landslide's pieces on the wrong class:** Nov 4 14:51, Bone Wall: Worldbreaker and Rubble plate on a Knight, Rubble plate's break doing half the party's damage.
- **Formation toggling:** Nov 2 14:23, from act I floor 8 to act II floor 2: 26 changes among four formations before six fights.

## What this suggests for the pass

Questions for the director's intent, not designs.
- **Should a build be reachable from one unit's kit plus one piece, in act I?** The two builds that happened early did it that way (Hearth plus Kindling; Thicket plus Briar). The six that didn't all need two or three pieces to meet on one unit, which act I doesn't supply.
- **Should the kit be what carries a build, with loot steering it?** Units fought with one ability known in 59% of unit-fights. If the builds are meant to be read in act I, either abilities arrive sooner or the builds are written for basic attacks and one ability.
- **Formations are chosen for the party at hand, not the other way round.** They were fitted by stat words after the party was set, or left at Neutral. If a formation is to make you rethink your party, its card has to make a promise the party can't refuse, and the party has to be stable enough to build toward it.
- **Falls are losses.** Phoenix's premise (a fall as a turn) needs falls that aren't the end; in this sample they were. Either the martyr's fall has to come early and cheaply, or the build is about standing back up between fights.
- **The player takes insurance over threads.** Taunt, Shields, Thorns, revives and lifesteal were taken most; the openers, sweeps, stone blows and mana engines least. The builds that reward defence (the Toll, Phoenix) are the ones the loot keeps offering; the ones that need the player to commit to offence (Ambush, Landslide, Overflow) have nothing that pays before they're complete.
- **Statuses are the watchable damage.** Burn, Bleed and Thorns are what the player saw win fights, and the builds that happened are the ones built on them. Cold Snap's Chill and Lockdown's windows don't yet show up the same way, and Harvest's spread and Ambush's opening don't show up at all.
