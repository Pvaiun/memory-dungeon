# The wheel, version C: the director's intent

What the director wants from this work. Where it's wrong, they correct it.

- **Two ways were tried and set aside.** A complete list of moments in a fight couldn't be judged: a fight is the part of this game where the player has no say, and the fun is in the choices between fights, building toward a combination or one spiky thing. Narrow problems to answer at length asked for volume, against a blank page, with no way to see how the answers would add up to a wheel.
- **Quick judgements of kinship, in the director's words.** Three choosable things at a time: which two belong together, which doesn't, and a few words for why. The distinctions that keep coming back, in the director's own vocabulary, are where the colours are looked for.
- **What a player would choose, not what a fight does.** Every card is something a player could pick, and the question is what kind of player reaches for it.
- **Coverage from the cards, not from memory.** The agents make the cards span everything the rules allow, so nothing depends on what the director thinks of on the day.
- **Agnostic of the classes and the builds,** and of the director's earlier answers, which were written against questions about the builds. That's why this workspace holds none of them.
- **The director may design the wheel from their own words** at the end, with the agents drafting apart and challenging it.
- **The creative output has to come from the director.** The cards only draw it out: what the director says two cards want, and the cards they add of their own. A deck they can't feel anything about has failed, however complete it is.
- **A pilot first,** for quality: the cards tuned on the director's reactions before the deck is written.
