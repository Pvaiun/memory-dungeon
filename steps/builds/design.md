# Design: the builds

The design stage of the builds pass, at the director's gate for the second time. The intent is `intent.md`; the evidence is `state-of-play.md`. It's written the way `docs/gear-design/philosophy.md` asks for a builds stage: each build as a way to play, with its rhythm, threads, routes, rungs and needs, and the web between them. No pieces, and no numbers but the measurements under *Staying alive*. Each build also says what the intent asks of it: how a player finds it at each phase of a run, how it stays alive, and what it asks of the kits.

Nothing here is built. Approved, it replaces `docs/gear-design/builds.md`, and the loot's spine is written from it.

## For the director

### Your question: yes

*Do we need to design the nodes between the builds, as destinations of their own?* Yes, and it's the biggest change in this version.

- **Everything that worked in play was a tight interlock, never a trend:** your shield party (two parts and a supply), the one Assassin whose kill reset fed its execute, the one formation whose three seats served one fire. Nothing that was "more of the build" was ever the exciting part.
- **A build has to be broad,** to have routes and rungs, and breadth is what makes it read as a mechanic and everything that touches it. The specific has to live somewhere else.
- **So the design has two layers now.** The **builds** are the roads: eight of them, broad, easy to see. The **engines** are where a run arrives: nineteen small builds, each two or three parts that lock into a loop, each crossing two mechanics, each reached from two builds or more. Your shield party is the first of them and the model for the rest.
- **A build's far end is no longer a bigger version of itself.** It's the engines it can turn into. The doors between builds are gone as a list: every door now has a destination in it.
- **One caution.** The old document left these undesigned on purpose, so they'd be found. Designed ones can still be found: no card names its partner, each part is worth wearing alone, and parts overlap so that pieces still meet in ways nobody wrote down.

### Your rulings, applied

- **Marked takes the party's random picks:** approved. The Hunt is now led by it.
- **Malison pays for breadth:** approved. It seeds an engine (the Row).
- **Burn and Chill keep their base.** Both changes are withdrawn. What a build wants from a status beyond its definition comes from a class, a version, a piece or a seat. I've put that line in `docs/rules.md`, as yours.

### Decisions

1. **The engines.** The idea, and the nineteen as a first set: which read like your shield party, and which don't.
2. **Eight builds,** with the Storm cut and Stranglehold moved down.
3. **Bonfire on upkeep.** It leans on Burn dying fast, which is a number (`STATUS.burn`) and holds for every use of Burn.
4. **A wall first.** Every build's first route has one, and standing without one is somewhere a build goes later in a run (*Staying alive*).

### Least sure

- **Bonfire.** Upkeep is a rhythm only if the fire visibly leaps and sags. If it settles to a level, it's a status again, and its engines are all it has.
- **Two statuses still have a build each** (Burn, Chill). They're the two that pile on one foe, so each gives the whole party one thing to work on. It's the cheap way in you described, and I haven't found better.
- **The Hunt without hiding** may be thin for a class whose kit is hiding. The Assassin's loop is an engine (the Cull), so it keeps a home either way.
- **Blood Pact** still asks the player to spend what the game drains.
- **Three engines:** the Row (it's a ramp, not a loop), the Drought (the foes' MP is hidden, and it may flatten bosses whose challenge is one great cast), Twice read (it may be a route through two kits and no more).

## The two layers

**A build** is a road: a broad way to play, with a shape, a rhythm, several routes and two rungs. Its sign shows in act I and it's built in act II.

**An engine** is a small build: two or three parts that lock into a loop, so that something the party already does starts paying in a new place. It has no routes or rungs. It's where a run arrives, and what the run is remembered for.

**What makes an engine:**
- **It crosses mechanics:** an event of one (a Shield breaking, a shatter, a kill, a dodge, a heal) wired to an effect of another.
- **It loops:** something re-arms it, so it happens many times a fight.
- **Each part is worth wearing alone.** Together they change where the party's damage, or its safety, comes from.
- **Its supply is common:** many classes can pour in what it runs on.
- **It sits between builds:** two or more of them reach it.
- **It's seen:** one new number on screen, and soon the biggest.

**What that asks of the loot:**
- **A build's own steps are the common things:** commons, uncommons and the common formations. **Engines are made of the specific ones:** rares, legendaries, versions, seats and mythics, each worth wearing alone in the build it comes from. The spine is written backwards from the engines.
- **Every part comes from more than one place** (a piece in more than one kind, a version, a seat), since a run sees under half the legendaries.
- **No part names its partner.** A card says what the part does.
- **Parts overlap on purpose,** so one engine's part is half of something nobody wrote down. Exotics stay.

## The frame

**What a build is doesn't change** (`docs/gear-design/philosophy.md`): a way to play you can watch, with a rhythm, woven from several threads, reached by more than one route, in rungs.

**What a run gives a build:**
- **Act I:** three units knowing an ability or two each, a weapon each and a piece or two more by the boss, mostly in Neutral. A build can show its direction here. It can't be assembled.
- **Act II:** most slots filled, a version or two a unit, formations, a mythic. This is where a build is built, and where an engine's first part turns up.
- **Act III:** whole kits and legendaries. Where an engine closes.

**Left unjudged, as the intent asks:** kits arriving one ability at a time, and cooldowns carrying. The set is written to hold either way.

**What every build answers:**
- **Through a run:** the sign and the build, each saying what the player sees, what the step is and what it pays if the run goes no further; then where it goes, which is its engines.
- **Stays alive:** its plan for who is being hit.
- **Packs and bosses:** it has a game for both, and says which is its worse fight. Three of the twelve were written to fear a lone boss, and bosses and pairs of elites are where most of the sample's runs ended.
- **Asks of the game:** kit or rule changes.

**What every build asks of the loot,** so it isn't repeated eight times:
- **A first step that's seen the first time it fires.** In play a few Might, a tenth off a cost and a cooldown a few ticks closer were never seen to happen.
- **A first step that doesn't cost the front.** In act I a careful party takes defence first, and it's right to.
- **A formation about one thing:** three seats that all serve what the build does.

The eight sit in pairs on opposite sides of the space:
- **Landslide and Thousand Cuts:** one huge blow, or many tiny ones.
- **Bonfire and Cold Snap:** a fire that dies unless it's fed, or a frost that builds until it breaks.
- **The Hunt and the Toll:** go to them, one at a time, or let them all come.
- **Barrage and Blood Pact:** mana poured out, or life.

## The builds

### Landslide

**Identity.** A party that loads one blow. Long stretches of small numbers go by behind stone bars. A wind-up bar fills on the hammer while Might piles onto it and its target is opened up. Then one number lands far bigger than anything else in the fight, and an elite loses a large share of its HP. The stone is rebuilt, and it happens again. It's waiting for the blow to come round.

- **Threads:**
  - the heavy action: slow swings, long wind-ups, long cooldowns;
  - loading it: Might on the slowest hitter (it's spent an action at a time, so it lasts where actions are rare), crits and Crit damage, Sunder laid on the target before the blow lands;
  - Defense, max HP and the Shield as damage stats, so the body is the weapon;
  - stone on the whole party, as the time the blow needs;
  - aim: at the healthiest foe, the most armored, the stunned.
- **Routes:** Geomancer, Knight, Hoplite · Geomancer, Paladin, Bard · no earth: Knight, Hoplite, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** a hammer whose actions come less often and land bigger; an anvil that wears the stone and draws the blows; a loader that gives the hammer what the party has to give (stone, Might).
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One unit whose heavy thing already out-sizes everything round it: rocks that crit from behind a Shield, a double swing, a bash from max HP. The sign is the number, and that Defense or HP on a piece makes it bigger: here the careful pick and the build's pick are the same piece. The step is the first piece that says so on its card, heavier and rarer.
  2. *The build.* A loading crew: Might aimed at the hammer, the target opened first, stone on everyone, the blow aimed at something worth it. Elites lose a large share at a time, and a boss takes a handful of blows.
- **Where it goes:** Rubble, the Quake, the Mirror, the Drumbeat, the blood hammer, the brimming cup.
- **Stays alive:** stone on everyone. Shields, Guard and an Aegis across three heavy bodies. Fights run long, so the stone has to be rebuilt, and that's where its MP goes.
- **Packs and bosses:** bosses and armored foes are its best fights: a huge hit loses little to Defense. A pack is its worst: a blow spent on a wolf is a long wait wasted. Its pack game is the shove that hits every foe and the aim that saves the blow for the biggest. It fears dodgers, and anything that strips Shields.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** heavier and rarer, for swings and for abilities; Might for one unit, not three; damage from Defense and max HP. Every Landslide piece promises a blow on its card: the ones that promised stone alone were worn as plain defence, by everyone.
  - **Formations:** hammer, anvil, loader; formations that trade the party's speed for size.
  - **Upgrades:** slower and heavier versions; versions that aim the blow.
- **Asks of the game:** none. Its miss was the loot's signposting, not the kits.

### Thousand Cuts

**Identity.** A blur. Tiny numbers, several a tick, and every one leaves something behind. A wound opens on a foe and each hit after cashes it in, red beside white. The party is hard to pin down: a MISS, a Shield bar rebuilt by every hit, HP drunk back as fast as it's lost. It isn't waiting for anything; it never stops.

- **Threads:**
  - speed: fast weapons, Haste, Attack speed;
  - extra strikes: Double strike, multi-hit abilities;
  - what a swing carries: Bleed is its own (opened wide by a crit, a barb or a thorn, and cashed by every hit after), and a storm on the party's swings makes it Shock;
  - riders that count every hit, extra strikes included;
  - staying alive by hitting: life drunk, a Shield built, an ally mended, hit by hit;
  - Evasion, and the dodge as one more trigger;
  - Sunder under it all, its answer to Defense.
- **Routes:** wounders behind briars: Bramble knight, Assassin, Ranger · no wall: Valkyrie, Vampire, Monk · a pacer and two blurs: Bard, Monk, Stormcaller.
- **Seats it likes:** a pacer that speeds the others; two blades that act more often and hit smaller.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* Early units mostly swing, so this build starts by itself: a fast weapon, and a passive that pays on every hit (a wound, a Sunder, a Shield, a mend, a spark). The step is the faster of a class's two weapon kinds and one rider, and it's worth taking on any unit.
  2. *The build.* A wounder and a blur together, a pacer, and a front kept standing by its own hits or by briars. Bleed opens wide and is cashed fast, and armored foes stop being walls.
- **Where it goes:** the Cull, the briar patch, the Capacitor, the Dance, Bellows, the Dynamo, the Drought, the Row, the brimming cup.
- **Stays alive:** behind briars first, since foes that strike a Bramble knight arrive already bleeding. Later by hitting: each hit drinks, shields or mends, and its front is whoever does that best. The dodge alone didn't hold in play.
- **Packs and bosses:** a lone boss suits it: every hit on one foe, the wounds never closing. In a pack its swings scatter where Bleed wants them together; its pack game is multi-hits that fan out and riders that pay wherever they land. It fears heavy Defense before the Sunder builds, foes that harden under many hits (the Ice Golem's crust), and the Wisps' Shock, which fast units pay for most.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** riders that count every hit; Bleed from more than crits; a drink or a Shield per hit on Mail and Leather; a count of hits that grows into something.
  - **Formations:** pacer and blades; formations that make the whole party faster at a cost.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that add hits or spread them.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### Bonfire

**Identity.** A fire that dies if it isn't fed. Burn falls away fast, so the number on the hottest foe is the party's feeding, live: it leaps when two casts land together, and sags when a feeder is stunned or between spells. Everyone feeds it: casts, swings that carry fire, the keeper's own blows. While it's high the foe melts, and the blow that reads the fire lands for the fight's biggest number. It's waiting for the fire to roar, and working to keep it there.

- **Threads:**
  - Burn as upkeep: it drains fast, so its height is how hard the party is feeding it right now;
  - two kinds of feeder: swings that carry fire hold it steady, casts make it leap;
  - the pile: every feeder aims at the hottest foe;
  - a blow that reads the fire and leaves it, and that waits for a fire worth reading;
  - tempo for the feeders: Haste, Cooldown, Cast speed, MP;
  - a keeper who holds the early fight, keeps the feeders casting and burns what it holds.
- **Routes:** Knight, Pyromancer, Monk · Paladin, Hexer, Pyromancer · no mage: Paladin, Monk, Hexer.
- **Seats it likes:** a keeper that holds the early danger; a stoker that acts more often; a torch whose fire reaches every foe that's already burning.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One unit that leaves Burn: three base kits do, and a version or a piece puts it on a swing. The fire stands as high as it's fed, and it gets through Defense where hits don't. The step is a second feeder, and because the fire dies so fast the step shows at once: the number stands twice as high. It sits on a weapon or a version, so it takes nothing from the front.
  2. *The build.* Three feeders on one pile, some steady and some in bursts, behind a keeper who feeds too, and a reader that waits for the height. Elites and bosses burn for more than the party hits for.
- **Where it goes:** Tinderbox, Bellows, Twice read, the Row.
- **Stays alive:** a keeper. A wall holds the early fight, and because it feeds the fire the seat isn't lost to the build. A feeder stopped is the fire sagging, so the keeper guards the tempo as well as the HP. It has no plan without one.
- **Packs and bosses:** a lone boss is its best fight: one pile, all fight. A pack is its worst: every kill throws a fire away. Its pack game is fire laid thin on the whole field, until it finds Tinderbox. It fears fights too short to get a fire up, and anything that stops its casters (a Silence, a Stun on the wrong unit).
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** fire on swings, for many kinds; a way for a wall to feed (fire on its slow swing, or on what strikes it); a choice between a fire that lasts and one that flares; tempo where casters wear it.
  - **Formations:** keeper, stoker and torch, all three serving the fire.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that move fire from casts to swings, or make one cast a flare; a reader that waits for the fire to be high.
- **Asks of the game:** nothing of Burn's behaviour. Its rhythm is Burn's drain, which is a number (`STATUS.burn`): fast enough that an unfed fire is gone in a few ticks, so its height shows who's feeding. That's the balance pass's, and it holds for every use of Burn.

### Cold Snap

**Identity.** Frost creeps up the foes and they visibly slow: each swing comes later than the last. Then a SHATTER: a burst, a Stun, and the party's heaviest hit lands on the frozen foe. Then the next one. It's waiting for the next foe to reach the cap, and the colder the field, the less the party is hit.

- **Threads:**
  - Chill to its cap and the shatter; anyone's Chill counts;
  - the slow on the way up: a colder field hits less often;
  - many small applications: swings that carry Chill, multi-hits;
  - the pile or the spread: one foe shattered again and again, or a pack reaching the cap together;
  - the opening a shatter makes: a stunned, sundered foe, and the heavy blow saved for it;
  - cold that attackers carry away from a front.
- **Routes:** a front in frost: Knight, Frostweaver, Monk · Frostweaver, Hexer, Ranger · no Frostweaver: Hexer, Ranger, Geomancer.
- **Seats it likes:** two frost seats that act often and carry Chill; a breaker that hits a frozen foe hardest.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* One Chill source (three base kits have one): the foes slow as the counter climbs, and one shatters. The step is a second source. Every stack also slows a foe, so it's never a pick against the party's safety.
  2. *The build.* Sources enough that the same foe shatters again in the same fight, and a breaker for each one. The second shatter is the line between the sign and the build. It comes from the Chill counts, which *Core set: classes* already makes the balance pass's first job, and from seeds the build finds: a shatter that leaves cold behind or sends it to the next foe, as a version, a piece or a seat.
- **Where it goes:** Snowball, the frozen gate, the Capacitor, the Quake, Rubble, Twice read, the Row.
- **Stays alive:** a front first, and the best one wears frost, so what strikes it slows. The cold is its second layer: a chilled field acts less, and every shatter is a Stun.
- **Packs and bosses:** spread over a pack, the cold slows everything and the shatters come together, like a string of firecrackers. Piled on a boss, it's a slow all fight and a burst on a beat; a boss shrugs off the Stuns after the first few, not the slow or the burst. It fears fights too short to reach the cap.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Chill on swings, for the fast kinds; Chill from being struck, for Plate and shields; payoffs on a frozen foe; a shatter that seeds the next.
  - **Formations:** the frost seats and the breaker; formations where one seat's hits set up another's.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that aim at the foe nearest its shatter, or spread the cold wide.
- **Asks of the game:** nothing of Chill's behaviour. The Chill counts, as above.

### The Hunt

**Identity.** The pack takes them one at a time. A foe is Marked, and everything the party has lands on it: swings, arrows, the bolt that was going to go anywhere. It falls, the Mark moves, and it happens again. Each kill pays for the next: a blow ready again, a volley. The foes' numbers drop like a staircase. It's waiting for the next kill.

- **Threads:**
  - focus: the Mark, which takes every pick the party makes at random, and aim at the lowest HP;
  - the first strike: the first Mark and its volley at the first tick, and each class's own opener (a first swing that lands bigger, a first hit that always crits, a strike from hiding);
  - reach: Seek past a Taunting front;
  - the kill as a trigger: a reset, a volley, the Mark moving;
  - executes: damage that grows as the foe's HP falls, so a boss is one long staircase;
  - crits and Crit damage on a few chosen hits;
  - Silence on the back row's casters.
- **Routes:** a judge who calls the target and holds the front: Paladin, Assassin, Stormcaller · no Assassin: Hoplite, Valkyrie, Ranger · no wall: Assassin, Ranger, Valkyrie.
- **Seats it likes:** a marker whose pick sets the target; a chaser with reach; a warden that holds the front.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A unit that picks its prey (the lowest, the back row, past a guard): the dangerous foe dies first, or never acts. The step is a kill or a first strike that pays something seen on the very next hit.
  2. *The build.* A Mark the whole party follows, hunters whose abilities aim where the Mark sits, and a kill that pays. Mixed groups fall in a drumbeat that speeds up.
- **Where it goes:** the Cull, the Drumbeat, Tinderbox, the Row.
- **Stays alive:** a warden who holds the front, and kill order: the most dangerous foe dies first and the dead don't hit. Against a lone boss kill order buys nothing, and the warden is the plan.
- **Packs and bosses:** mixed groups with a dangerous back row are its best fights. A lone boss is its worst until the executes come: one step, no staircase. It fears Ghouls raised behind the hunt, and a Taunting front if it has no reach.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** the Mark from more hands than the Paladin's; kill rewards sized to be seen (a whole action, not a few ticks of one); executes; openers in each class's own terms.
  - **Formations:** marker, chaser and warden, all three serving the kill.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that change what an ability aims at, or make it ready again on a kill: the kind that carried a run.
- **Asks of the game:** Marked takes the party's random picks (approved).

### The Toll

**Identity.** They hurt themselves. Foes crowd onto one unit, and every blow they land comes back: Thorns flash, the attacker leaves bleeding, charged or slowed, and it pays again when it next moves. The swarm that rushes the wall dies on it. It's waiting to be hit. The danger is all on the anchor, and the rest of the party is almost untouched.

- **Threads:**
  - the draw: Taunt and Aggro;
  - Thorns and retaliation: being struck strikes back;
  - what an attacker carries away (Bleed, Shock, Chill, Weaken): each ally adds its own toll to the blows on the anchor;
  - the anchor's keeper: Guard, Regen, heals, Weaken on what hits it;
  - the dodge as the other anchor: a dancer who slips the blow and answers it.
- **Routes:** Bramble knight, Cleric, Stormcaller · Knight, Hexer, Hoplite · the dancer: Monk, Valkyrie, Bard.
- **Seats it likes:** an anchor that draws and returns; a warden that keeps it standing; a free seat that finishes what the anchor started.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* On floor one, from the kit: a Bramble knight, or a Knight that Taunts in thorns. Packs die on it. There's nothing to find, which is why it's in most parties; the question it puts is what the wall wears next.
  2. *The build.* The other two join the toll: blows on the anchor set off what they've laid (the attacker bleeds, sparks, slows, weakens), and the anchor has a keeper. Swarms die on the wall while the third seat does something else.
- **Where it goes:** the Mirror, the briar patch, the frozen gate, Rubble, the Capacitor, the Dance.
- **Stays alive:** it is the wall. Its risk is the one play showed: when the anchor falls, the rest fall behind it. So the keeper is part of the build, not an extra.
- **Packs and bosses:** swarms and fast foes are its best fights. A boss or a pair of elites that barely swing is where it died in play, and the Mirror is written for that. It gets nothing from foes that don't strike (Bone Priests, Witches), or from Area, which ignores the draw.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** Taunt on gear, for more anchors; what an attacker carries away, for the allies to give; Thorns that grow with the beating.
  - **Formations:** the anchor seat, with two seats that add to its toll.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that change who the anchor is and what it returns.
- **Asks of the game:** none.

### Barrage

**Identity.** One caster, and the same great spell again and again. Its name scrolls up the log, twice running. Its MP bar is refilled by the other two, and the spell is ready before its cooldown should be done. The party's basic attacks are an afterthought. It's waiting for the spell to come back round. When the mana or the caster stops, so does the party.

- **Threads:**
  - one ability worth repeating: the soloist's;
  - cooldowns: the Cooldown stat, Haste, an ability made ready at once;
  - the cast twice;
  - MP that flows to the soloist (Clarity, Refrain, Siphon, life for mana);
  - Cast speed, and a wind-up that can be lost;
  - costs that change.
- **Routes:** Knight, Bard, Pyromancer · Frostweaver, Bard, Stormcaller · no Bard: Cleric, Vampire, Geomancer.
- **Seats it likes:** a soloist whose abilities come faster; a conductor who gives it tempo; a well that gives it mana.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A caster with one spell worth repeating, and anyone who gives mana. The sign is the caster not swinging its staff. Every caster party feels the shortage, so the step (mana in fights, however it comes) is one it wants anyway.
  2. *The build.* A conductor: the spell twice, or ready again; Cooldown and Cast speed on the soloist. The spell lands several times in a fight where it used to land once.
- **Where it goes:** Snowball, the Dynamo, the Drought, Twice read, Radiance.
- **Stays alive:** a keeper, who is the well where it can be: the Knight's Taunt and Clarity are one ability, and the Frostweaver is a sturdy well. The soloist draws little. Its danger is a cliff, not a slope.
- **Packs and bosses:** whatever the soloist's spell is; choosing the soloist is choosing that. It fears Silence (the Wraiths, the Silent One), Leeches, and a fight longer than the mana.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** links sized to be seen: a whole cast's mana, a whole cooldown, not a tenth of either; Cooldown and Cast speed where casters wear them; an answer to Silence; something of their own for the two who serve, so they're more than batteries.
  - **Formations:** soloist, conductor, well.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that make an ability ready again on a cast or a kill, or pass mana on.
- **Asks of the game:** none in the kits. Clarity's and Refrain's mana have to be big enough to change what the caster does: the balance pass's.

### Blood Pact

**Identity.** Life spent and bought back. The party's HP bars swing. A unit pays a chunk of its life for a blow, or rides low on purpose, and drinks it back off the foes; the healers work twice as fast while someone is at the brink; a Shield or an Aegis sits over whoever is lowest. The party fights at half and ends full. It's waiting to be low enough.

- **Threads:**
  - Lifesteal, and drinking more the lower it is;
  - HP as a price, in place of MP;
  - below half as power: for the one who's low, and for the allies who answer it;
  - nets: a Shield or an Aegis over a low unit (a price paid isn't damage, so it doesn't spend the Shield);
  - life passed on: a heal given as a trigger;
  - max HP as a damage stat;
  - the last drink: ending the fight full.
- **Routes:** Vampire, Bramble knight, Valkyrie · Vampire, Valkyrie, Cleric · no Vampire: Assassin, Paladin, Monk.
- **Seats it likes:** a bleeder that pays HP for power; a drinker that feeds on hits; a donor that passes life on.
- **Through a run:**
  1. *The sign.* A drinker: the Vampire, or Lifesteal on anything that hits hard. A unit that heals itself by hitting, and potions saved. It's taken as sustain, which is right: sustain is the build's floor.
  2. *The build.* The price: abilities and pieces paid in HP that buy what mana can't, power below half for the payer and from its allies, and a net over whoever is low. Fights where the mana never runs out.
- **Where it goes:** the blood hammer, the brimming cup, Radiance, Rubble, the Mirror.
- **Stays alive:** drink and nets. The lower it is the more it drinks; the healers quicken at the brink; a Shield or an Aegis takes the blow that would finish a low unit. It's the riskiest plan in the set, on purpose.
- **Packs and bosses:** long fights against things it can drink from. It fears Bats (a dodged hit drinks nothing), the Witch's curse (a weakened hit drinks less), Rot, and burst that catches it low.
- **Needs:**
  - **Pieces:** prices in HP that buy more than their mana would, repaid inside the same fight; power below half that doesn't leave a unit low when the fight ends; nets; a last drink.
  - **Formations:** bleeder, drinker, donor; formations where life moves between seats.
  - **Upgrades:** versions that pay life or pass it.
- **Asks of the game:** none in the kits. Only the Vampire's base kit pays HP; the build widens through Lifesteal's kinds and versions other kits already hold, and the Berserker, when it's added, is its natural second home.

## The engines

A first set of nineteen. Each one's line of builds says who reaches it. Where a kit already holds a part, as a base ability or a version, the entry says so: engines are made of versions and seats as much as pieces.

### Stone

**Rubble.** *Landslide, the Toll, Blood Pact, Cold Snap.* Shield bars burst with a number on whatever broke them, and are back a moment later, smaller. The director's shield party.
- **Loop:** a Shield breaks, and deals damage by the size it was laid at; it returns smaller; it breaks again.
- **Parts:** a break that deals damage by the Shield's size · a broken Shield that returns · Shields from anywhere (five kits lay them).

**The Quake.** *Landslide, Cold Snap.* Rocks that stun, and a rock that lands far bigger on the stunned.
- **Loop:** behind its Shield the hitter crits; the crit Stuns; the next blow lands far bigger on a stunned foe, and crits again.
- **Parts:** crits that Stun · a blow that's bigger on a stunned foe · any other Stun to start it, and a shatter's is the best. The Geomancer's versions hold the first two.

**The Mirror.** *The Toll, Landslide, Blood Pact.* The one great blow, stopped and sent back. The Toll's answer to the bosses that barely swing.
- **Loop:** a boss winds up; Defense, a Shield or an Aegis stops most of the blow; what was stopped returns to the attacker; the cover is rebuilt for the next.
- **Parts:** retaliation sized by what was stopped · cover that's up when the blow lands (an Aegis aimed at the anchor is the best, and the Hoplite's kit holds it) · a Taunt that lasts through the wind-up.

### Fire

**Tinderbox.** *Bonfire, the Hunt.* A burning foe that falls goes up, and every other foe takes its fire at once. A pack burns down in a chain.
- **Loop:** fire laid thin on the whole field; a death bursts its Burn onto the rest; the next death comes sooner.
- **Parts:** a burning foe's death that bursts onto the others · fire on the whole field first · kills aimed at the hottest foe.
- This is the passing the old Bonfire promised, as a blast that lands now and not a fire to be kept. The build finds it; Burn doesn't do it.

**Bellows.** *Bonfire, Thousand Cuts.* While a foe burns high the party swings faster, and faster swings carry more fire.
- **Loop:** the fire crosses a line; the party's swings quicken; swings that carry fire feed it higher. A feeder stopped drops the fire under the line, and the speed goes with it.
- **Parts:** speed for the party while a foe holds a high fire · fire on swings · a keeper.

**Twice read.** *Bonfire, Barrage, Cold Snap.* The blow that reads a status and leaves it, cast twice at the same height.
- **Loop:** the party builds the fire, the frost or the charge; the reader lands; the status stays, so the reader lands again.
- **Parts:** a blow that reads a status and leaves it (three kits hold one) · the cast twice, or ready again (the Bard's kit) · the status held at its height for the moment between.

### Frost and lightning

**Snowball.** *Cold Snap, Barrage.* The shatter re-arms the spell that caused it.
- **Loop:** the soloist's frost spell lays Chill on the field; a foe reaches the cap; the shatter brings the spell round again.
- **Parts:** a shatter that brings the caster's abilities nearer · a spell that lays Chill on every foe · mana for it.

**The frozen gate.** *The Toll, Cold Snap.* Foes freeze on the wall they're hitting, and each one that shatters rebuilds it.
- **Loop:** foes strike the front and carry Chill away; they slow, then shatter and are stunned; each shatter lays a Shield on the lowest ally, which is the front.
- **Parts:** Chill on whatever strikes the wearer · a shatter that shields (the Frostweaver's kit holds it as a version) · a front that draws.

**The Capacitor.** *Thousand Cuts, Cold Snap, the Toll.* A foe that can't act can't discharge. Shock piles on a slowed or frozen foe to its cap, and its first action pays the lot.
- **Loop:** Shock from the party's swings; the foe slowed, frozen or stunned; the charge climbs untouched; it acts, and one huge bolt lands.
- **Parts:** Shock on many swings · something that holds a foe still (Chill is this already) · a payoff when a Stun ends on a charged foe, which reads the Shock and leaves it.

**The Dynamo.** *Barrage, Thousand Cuts.* A spread spell that pays for itself by how many it touches. Barrage's pack game.
- **Loop:** the soloist's spell strikes the field; mana and time come back for each foe touched; it's cast again, and the field sinks together.
- **Parts:** mana back for each foe touched (the Stormcaller's kit holds it as a version) · a cooldown that shortens for each foe touched · a conductor.

### The anchor

**The briar patch.** *The Toll, Thousand Cuts.* The attacker's own blow opens its wound and cashes it.
- **Loop:** a foe strikes the anchor; Thorns answer and leave it bleeding; the Thorns' return counts as a hit on a bleeding foe, so the Bleed pays; the next blow does it again.
- **Parts:** retaliation that leaves Bleed (the Bramble knight's passive) · retaliation that counts as a hit for what a hit sets off · Bleed from the allies on whatever is striking the anchor.

**The Dance.** *The Toll, Thousand Cuts.* Every miss is answered, and the answer rebuilds the dancer's cover.
- **Loop:** a foe swings and misses; the dancer strikes back; the strike-back counts as a swing, so it drinks, shields and carries riders; the one blow that lands is soaked.
- **Parts:** a dodge that strikes back · a strike-back that counts as a swing · a Taunt on a dodger (the Monk's kit holds it as a version).

### The kill

**The Cull.** *The Hunt, Thousand Cuts.* One unit, one loop: crit, wound, execute, again. It carried a run to act II.
- **Loop:** its first hit on a foe crits; the crit opens a wound; the execute finishes the wounded; the kill makes the execute ready; the next foe.
- **Parts:** a first hit on each foe that always crits · an execute that's ready again on a kill · crits that read the wound. The Assassin's versions hold all three, and other hunters want them too.

**The Drumbeat.** *The Hunt, Landslide.* The Mark moves on a kill, and every new Mark calls a volley.
- **Loop:** a Mark; every ally's next swing lands on it at once; a kill; the Mark moves; a volley.
- **Parts:** a Mark that moves when its foe falls · a Mark that calls a volley (the Paladin's does) · swings worth volleying: a hammer's slow swing gets a free one each time, and a swing that carries a rider lays three at once.

### Mana and life

**The Drought.** *Barrage, Thousand Cuts.* The foes' casters run dry, and the party's caster drinks what they lose.
- **Loop:** hits that take MP; a foe's great cast fizzles for want of it; the MP taken goes to the ally with the least; more casts.
- **Parts:** Siphon on many small hits · MP taken handed to the ally who needs it · a cast that takes MP from a casting foe (the Hexer's kit holds it as a version).

**The brimming cup.** *Blood Pact, Landslide, Thousand Cuts.* Life drunk past full becomes stone.
- **Loop:** a drinker at full HP; what it drinks over the top becomes a Shield; the Shield is read (crits while it holds, damage from its size) or stands over the next price; it drinks again.
- **Parts:** healing past full that becomes a Shield · something that reads a Shield · a drink big enough to spill, and one huge hit drinks hugely.

**The blood hammer.** *Blood Pact, Landslide.* The blow is loaded with the hitter's own life, and drinks it back.
- **Loop:** a price paid in HP; the next blow lands harder by what was paid; Lifesteal on one huge hit heals in one gulp; it pays again.
- **Parts:** a price in HP that loads the next blow · Lifesteal on the hammer · max HP as the blow's stat (two kits hit from it).

**Radiance.** *Blood Pact, Barrage.* Every heal given is struck back out, and the healer works twice as fast at the brink.
- **Loop:** an ally rides low; the healer mends it; the healer's next swing lands as hard as the heal; near the brink its cooldowns halve, and so do the gaps between blows.
- **Parts:** a heal repeated as damage (the Cleric's kit holds it as a version) · heals that quicken at the brink (the Cleric's passive) · someone who rides low on purpose.

### Mixing

**The Row.** *Bonfire, Cold Snap, Thousand Cuts, the Hunt.* A party that mixes its colours: every different affliction on a foe makes each hit on it bigger.
- **Loop:** none; it's a ramp. Kinds are laid, and a Mark, a Weaken and a Chill count as much as Burn does; the Hexer's curse and a judge's hits grow with each kind.
- **Parts:** the curse that reads the row (Malison, as approved) · hits that count kinds · one more kind from a swing or a fight's start.
- What's left of Stranglehold, at the size it can be proven at.

## Staying alive

The intent's question: does every build need a tank? **Every build needs a plan for who is being hit. Late in a run the wall is one plan of several. In act I it's the only one that's whole in time.**

| Plan | What it is | Builds | Whole on floor one? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A wall** | one unit draws the blows and soaks them | the Toll, Bonfire, Barrage, Cold Snap | yes: a Taunt and a body |
| **Stone on everyone** | Shields, Guard and Aegis across heavy bodies | Landslide | yes |
| **By hitting** | each hit shields, drinks or mends | Thousand Cuts, Blood Pact | only where it's a passive (the Valkyrie's Shield, the Vampire's drink) |
| **Kill order** | the dangerous die first | the Hunt | no |
| **Denial** | the foes slowed, weakened, silenced | no build stands on it | no: it builds over a fight |

**The director's read, from play:** the wall comes up at once, nothing else is whole before the first fights that need it, and the wall gets the gear first. A party with another way to live has either taken a wall by the time that way comes online, or lost. So act I picks walls, whatever the other plans are worth later.

**What the design does with that:**
- **Every build's first route has a wall,** and says what the wall wears for it. Standing without one is somewhere a build goes in act II or III, when its own plan is whole and the wall can sit at camp.
- **Two things can make a plan whole on floor one without a wall.** A passive (the Valkyrie's and the Vampire's are). And a formation taken at the start of a run, which is the director's separate change: it's the one lever that could give a first party a plan that isn't a Taunt.

**What I can measure.** Today's hand-built parties for the twelve (`npx tsx tools/build-test.ts --quick --no-ablate`): whole kits, every fight from full HP, two seeds, today's knobs. Nearly every fight is won at those knobs, so the read is HP lost per won fight, on act II's fights at rung 2. The pieces are the old ones, so it's a pointer, not a verdict.

| Party | Its plan | HP lost per won fight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knight, Hexer, Hoplite | a wall | 6% |
| Knight, Pyromancer, Hexer | a wall | 7% |
| Bramble knight, Cleric, Stormcaller | a wall | 8% |
| Valkyrie, Ranger, Vampire | by hitting | 6% |
| Vampire, Monk, Cleric | by hitting, and a healer | 9% |
| Valkyrie, Ranger, Assassin | kill order | 11% |
| Frostweaver, Hexer, Ranger | denial | 28% |
| Stormcaller, Hexer, Pyromancer | none | 30% |
| Bard, Frostweaver, Hexer | none | 37% |

Across all thirty-six routes: with a Knight or a Bramble knight, 3% to 29%, the middle one 11%; without, 4% to 40%, the middle one 14%.

- **Once a party is built, a real plan with no wall costs no more HP than a wall.** What costs three to five times as much is three light bodies with no plan.
- **Half-built, the gap is wider.** The same parties at rung 1 on act II's fights: the walls 7% to 17%, by hitting 14% and 19%, kill order 37%. That's the director's read in numbers: on the way, the wall is ahead.
- **Denial doesn't hold, and the reason is its size.** Weaken and Slow on every foe, all fight, take about three fifths off what the foes deal (`STATUS.weaken`, `STATUS.slow`). That's the ceiling, reached late and paid for in MP. A wall takes about as much off on floor one for one Taunt, and puts what's left on the body built to take it. So no build stands on denial: Stranglehold is an engine now, and Cold Snap keeps a front.

**For the balance pass:**
- **Price the other plans against the wall,** with hand-built parties that have none, at acts II and III.
- **If debuffs are to carry a party,** they have to be bigger than they are, and they're the foes' debuffs too (the Witch's curse is Weaken and Slow on the whole party).
- **The wall is cheap by rule.** A unit built to draw takes 85–90% of the foes' picks (`docs/rules.md`, Aggro by doubling), and under one kind of damage the prototype's report found walls stronger still. If at the harder knobs the difficulty pass is aiming for nothing lives without one, the draw rule is the thing to look at.

**Walls fail suddenly, the other plans gradually.** In play the anchor fell first in most fights where anyone fell, and the others, near full, fell after it. That's the wall's own danger, and the reason a keeper is written into the Toll.

## Elements, not builds

Things the twelve treated as builds, or as a build's whole identity, that are better as things any party can have.

- **Falls and returns: the net.** Nobody chooses who falls, so nothing is built on it. A revive, and whatever softens a fall, serves every build. What the net has to do: the returned stay up (each of the five revives inside a fight in the sample was followed by a second fall), the survivors get cover in the moment after, and a knockout needn't be carried into the next fight. It's sized as protection. No piece wants a fall.
- **The first move.** The fight's start is the one trigger nothing gates. It's the Hunt's first Mark, and a thread several others use: stone at the start, cold at the start, a Taunt at the start, a first cast free.
- **Spread.** Reaching more foes is each build's pack game, and three engines (Tinderbox, the Dynamo, Snowball), not a build.
- **Stopping a cast.** A Stun or a Silence timed to a wind-up is an answer to a boss's great cast, as *Core set: enemies* asks, and the act's boss is shown from the start. The versions that wait for a wind-up are that answer. A wind-up was caught five times in 150 fights, so no build waits for one.
- **Foes that do less.** Weaken, Slow and Silence make a party's fights cheaper. At their sizes today they don't keep one alive.
- **Shock and Bleed.** They land wherever hits land, so they're what swings carry and what attackers leave with, and parts of engines. Burn and Chill pile on one foe, so each gives the whole party one thing to work on, and each keeps a build as the cheap way in.

### The wall, dressed for each build

Every build's first route has a wall, and what that wall wears is one of a run's real choices, between builds and not between sizes. Thorns (the Toll) are what it wears for free. It can wear fire and be Bonfire's keeper, or frost and front Cold Snap. It can be Landslide's anvil, Barrage's well or the Hunt's warden. So a party with a wall isn't the Toll by default, and the Toll's second rung has rivals.

**What a formation has to be** for a party to pivot to it, from what was and wasn't fought in:
- **About one thing.** All three seats serve what the build does, and no seat is a tax.
- **A promise in plain words,** big enough to see: a stat, a status by name, who it lands on.
- **Seats many classes can fill.** A seat that reads who sits in it says so on the seat.
- **Builds and engines both.** Every build has a common formation a run could start in (its front, and its simplest promise) and a rare that reads who sits where. A legendary formation is an engine's part, or a whole engine in three seats.

A build still plays in Neutral, and no build needs one formation. How formations arrive in a run is outside this pass.

## Checks on the whole set

- **The rhythms and shapes spread across the space:** rare and huge (Landslide), a dense even stream (Thousand Cuts), a fire that leaps and sags (Bonfire), bursts on a beat (Cold Snap), a volley and then a staircase (the Hunt), following the foes' attacks (the Toll), in salvos from one unit (Barrage), see-sawing (Blood Pact).
- **Every build's sign is in a base kit.** At least one class shows each build's direction with a passive or one ability, before any piece, and most builds have two or three. It's a check on this set, not a rule for what a build is: a sign can as well come from a piece or a formation.
- **Every build has a route with a starter** (the Knight, Cleric or Ranger), and its first route has a wall.
- **Every build has a pack game and a boss game,** and says which is worse.
- **Every class is in three builds or more,** and every identity serves a build or an engine.
- **Every engine crosses two mechanics and is reached from two builds or more.** Every build reaches four engines or more.
- **The builds are less mechanic-driven than they were, not cured of it.** Two are a status's (Bonfire, Cold Snap), one is stone's, one is life's. The engines are where the mechanics cross, and that's where I'd expect the game's best moments to come from.

**Set aside:**
- **The Storm:** a build on Shock. It listed a status. A build about the foes' own tempo, or about the party's speed gathering like weather, are the two hooks I looked at; neither is more than Thousand Cuts with a difference.
- **Stranglehold:** a build on debuffs. Its denial can't keep a party alive at today's sizes. It comes back if the balance pass makes debuffs big enough to stand on.
- **Phoenix:** a build on falls. Nobody chooses who falls; revives are the net.
- **Ambush alone:** an opening with no tail. On the test parties it has the most distinct rhythm of the twelve, which is why the first strike is kept in the Hunt and not dropped.
- **Harvest alone:** spread as a build. It's each build's pack game, and three engines.
- **Lockdown:** waiting for the foe's wind-up. Stopping a cast is an answer, not a build.
- **Overflow's relay:** mana passed round three casters. Its links can't be made big enough to see without being a build's whole power.
- **The chorus:** the party's own buffs as the thing stacked. Today a buff helps every build about equally, which is what a piece shouldn't do.
- **The gambler:** crits on everything. The Hunt's and Landslide's crits cover it, and it may come back as exotics.
