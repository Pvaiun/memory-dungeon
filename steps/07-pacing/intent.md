# Step 7 intent: the Knight and the Cleric, then a pacing and value pass

From the director's two messages in the session of Oct 25, 2026, after reading the campaign report's follow-up probes (the Cleric is its free Mercy heal; a Knight with MP to spare locks bosses out of their big moves; most trials ask for a handicap). Not an interview: the items are their words, lightly cleaned, tagged with how firmly each was held. Correct anything here and the design follows.

## Part 1: three changes to test, and keep if they work

- [firm] **The Cleric.** "Right now, they're a reviving, cleansing, long-term regen heal bot. So they get everything for free. They can do everything." Remove the free heal; "give them more mana sinks, and make them more of an active healer."
  - Combine Regen and the dispel into one single-target ability, "that it prefers targets with debuffs, but then make it still fire if someone doesn't have one, giving regen. That way it'll only be held if there's literally no valid target."
  - "That frees up an ability to be a single target heal, which we differentiate from the Valkyrie's by the Cleric's being smaller and quicker."
  - "Give the Cleric the can't be silenced passive from the Bard."
- [firm] **The Bard** gets a new passive: "its basic attacks grant a little bit of mana to a random ally, since the Bard was one of the worst classes anyways."
- [firm] **Stun and Silence.** "The issue is that we're confusing stun and silence. Silence is a thing that's supposed to stop people from doing stuff, like stop a spellcaster or stop a big attack from happening, where stun is supposed to be like a disable." Stun "doesn't interrupt casting, it just pauses it", and lasts longer than 2 ticks, "so the Knight actually has a thing that can kind of temporarily knock someone out of the fight, but not lock them down or interrupt them."
- [firm] **No healing or MP between fights.** "I'm certain that we shouldn't." MP "is always either going to run out on a character or it's not. There's no moment where it matters other than it will run out or not." Their answer: a **campfire**, once per run, "in the middle of each run, or wherever you deem appropriate per run, but only one", with a choice: "an X percent heal to your whole party or an X percent mana heal." It "should give a real choice between itemization for effectiveness and for mana, and also hopefully make other team comps viable if you don't want to bring a healer."
- [idea] Later, with five abilities of which three are brought, "you can choose if you want expensive ones or some cheap ones."
- [firm] Test, simulate as much as needed, use helpers: "if those tests are successful, then that's great and I would apply those changes."

## Part 2: a big pass on pacing and value

"I'm pretty much certain that we're not going to be in a healthy place even if we fix those problems up above." Four problems, which "doesn't mean there's [four] separate solutions":

1. [firm] **Action economy.** "In most fights, unless they really drag, units are not using their basic attack, or very rarely, because their cooldowns relative to the duration of combat are so short, which means that there isn't really a neutral stage for things to play out, like status effects or the various stats. All that matters is the effects of your abilities triggering over and over and over. I think that's causing some of that really lock and key swinginess." And "allies are acting like 2 to 3 times before every single time a single enemy acts." "The cooldown versus game speed versus action economy on each side needs major changes. The abilities should not be the only thing that your units are using."
2. [firm] **Relative value of actions.** "Not in terms of percentages of wins, but in terms of, in any given fight, what percentage came from what source." "The Ranger's poison does like 2 damage per tick, but the attack that applies it does 14 or 12. So your poison would have to tick 6 times to even just equal the damage that doing the application gives, which means that poison basically becomes irrelevant." "Most of the status effects, except for rarely burn, basically are useless", partly because "if there's no dead turns or people are just basic attacking, then there's no turns for these things to tick". "When there are basic attacks, or especially crits, which seem to be really frequent, they can just do so much damage that it doesn't matter what the rest of your unit did." "We need to make sure that we're not just tuning win rates, but the actual relative efficacy between kits and between the units' different actions."
   - [firm, method] "I don't think you need to do a bunch of simulations to arrive at the right thing. I think that's the second step. The first step is literally just comparing, eyeballing it. It's super obvious that poison doing 2 damage and the attack that applies it doing 12 or 14 is wrong."
3. [firm] **Pacing.** "There's only three speeds for everything", so "you really often have a lot of stuff happening at the exact same tick": all three basic attacks on one tick, the Knight's three abilities ready at once and chained, "all of your units will cast an ability all at once, which will immediately kill one of the enemies before they can do anything", poison applied and the target killed the same tick. "We don't need to have things be in categories." Cooldowns "should probably be a lot longer", "more varied. Definitely within a unit, they can never all be the same. You should probably never even have two that are the same." "We need to try to generally avoid things happening on the same tick. That way the fight actually has the chance to play out and things have a chance to react to each other."
4. [firm] **Mobs and bosses.** "Often you'll literally just insta-kill, or very nearly, by just spamming all your abilities on the mobs. And then when you get to the boss it's like 20 times the health of the monsters." "The bosses are basically the only unit you're actually fighting, because the rest are so squishy and so useless. The bandits at the start do 2 damage to you, and they rarely get more than 1 or 2 hits in." "Even the bosses, it's either you find some gimmick to stun it forever, or it's a long attrition war. There's no spikiness. They do so little damage." Tune "the enemies relative to the bosses better, and make sure the enemies are actually efficacious and really doing things to the party."
   - [guess, theirs] "Most of the losses are probably mana runs out, fight stalls, slowly chip away, dead."

"A lot to do here, but I think we need a big pass."
