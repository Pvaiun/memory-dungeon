# The wheel: the director's intent

What the director wants from this step, from the conversation of Nov 23. Where it's wrong, they correct it.

- **Gameplay first, then the colours, then the fantasy.** The game's fantasy is classic JRPG and generic, and that's fine: the colours are found in the gameplay, and a fantasy tailored to them comes after.
- **The agent's observation and the director's rambling, together.** The agent brings the whole of what the game's rules allow, so nothing depends on what the director remembers on the day; the director reacts to it and rambles on it.
- **Agnostic of the classes and the builds.** Both are tailored to synergies and combinations already made, and would bring them back in.
- **The mechanics and how they meet,** not the fight's text alone. The log is something a player looks at afterwards, and some of what happens isn't clear in it.
- **Scope is the agent's call.** The director has the time and will read hundreds of things if that's what the work needs.
- **The best way of working is in the brief,** several agents included where that's better.
- **Problems first, patterns after,** which is how the director designs best: from material in front of them, never a blank page.
- **Triads, in place of both earlier ways** (version C). The list of fight moments couldn't be judged, because a fight is where the player has no say and the fun is in the choices; the narrow problems asked for volume with no way to see the wheel in it. Three choosable things at a time, which two belong together and why in a few words, puts the director's own distinctions at the centre and leaves the coverage to the cards.
- **No earlier answers.** The director's answers from authored gaps were written against questions about the builds, and stay out.
