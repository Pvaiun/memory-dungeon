# Defining the web: the director's intent

What the director wants from this step, in their words where the wording matters. Nothing here is a ruling on the design.

- **The question:** what sits at the edges of the web: the builds, as now, or something else. The builds "felt too limiting".
- **Sorting by pure mechanics went generic.** The model before this one (how damage is dealt, how it's mitigated, how a fight plays) was "more correct, more objective, but it makes an abstraction of any sort of emotional or thematic experience the player might have". Too many things that don't work together land on the same spot, and a payoff for Shield or Evasion wants that one thing stacked, so it stops sorting. They liked the four lines better (`four-lines.md`) for their "thematic families that I could understand", though "less correct and more arbitrary". Preferring the less correct one told them the strategy was wrong.
- **What excites a player is the words:** "A player isn't going to get excited because a fight ran long. A player is going to get excited because a fight ran long while they were doing something": shocking the whole team, burning it, killing it with Thorns.
- **The model is Magic's colours and its deck styles, as two things.** A colour holds themes, the kinds of effect it's often seen with. A deck wins by a style (aggro, control, go wide). Each colour suits each style more or less, a deck is rarely one colour, and two colours' themes converge on one way to win, so two decks can win the same way by different means.
- **"I think the builds are our colors. But the problem is that the way we had described the build was mixing the color with the winning strategy."** So "our builds are too narrow". Burn and Chill show it: two poles that are "a single win condition tied to a theme, rather than a set of themes that the player can use to build win conditions".
- **The work, as they see it:** design the ways to win ("we've kind of already done that in a lot of ways"), then which families of themes suit which, and where they overlap.
- **Maybe not a web:** in Magic any colours mix, and the player finds the right way to win for theirs. They don't know that this game is "complex enough to support that every color matches everything".
- **Classes** are bundles far more controlled than a card. A class can stand for one or a few families, so the combos can be curated from a smaller set than every mechanic in the game.
- **Asked of the agent:** challenge it if it's the wrong way to work, otherwise try to apply it. A back and forth.
