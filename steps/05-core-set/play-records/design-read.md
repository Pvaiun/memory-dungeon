# The director's recorded runs as a playtest (Nov 3, 2026)

The same seventeen runs as `read.md`, read again for the design: what the player reached for, what they ignored, what the builds, loot, formations and classes did once they were in someone's hands. Counts here are of what the player did, not of what the numbers should be; the evidence is in `tables.md`.

## What's connecting

- **Preparation is the game, and the player prepares.** Every boss party arrived near full, with the wall seated first, the formation chosen and the supplies spent beforehand. Abilities were reordered 27 times and left out of a fight four times to save MP. Nothing offered at spoils was ever skipped (141 offers). The hands-off fight hasn't made the player passive; it has moved the play to the map, the camp and the party screen, where the design wants it.
- **The map's rooms have real pull, in the right order.** With a choice of rooms, the player took an elite 9 times of 11, a campfire 10 of 12, an event 27 of 44, a fight 14 of 55, a shop 7 of 22. An elite is a legendary, a formation and sixty gold; a fight is two commons and ten gold. The elite is the run's engine and the player knows it.
- **The roster and the bench.** Recruits are taken whenever offered (eight at campfires, eight captives, five from events); rosters grow to five. 68 swaps: two thirds to rest a hurt member, one third tactical, and the tactical ones cluster before bosses (ten of thirteen boss swaps were of healthy members). The bench is a hospital and a lineup, both as 5h meant.
- **The fights have a texture you can read.** Bleed is on a foe in seven fights of ten; Burn and Bleed together are a quarter of the party's damage; Thorns, Shield, Taunt and Guard are on the party in half of all fights. A player can watch a fight and name what is happening.
- **Versions are the training choice.** 53 versions chosen against 48 new abilities and 19 power steps, the two versions nearly evenly. They show up in the logs (Coup de grâce, Hold the line, Skewer, Static field, Dawn muster raising the Knight at tick 1). The upgrade system gives a choice that is taken as a choice; the power step reads as a tax.
- **Classes whose identity shows:** the Vampire (drafted four of six offers; half its damage from Blood drain and Frenzy, 35,000 self-healing), the Bramble knight (thorns are 42% of its damage; Bramble lash is the most-cast ability in the game), the Assassin (Vanish, Coup de grâce, the one build the player assembled on purpose), the Ranger (arrows are 57% of its damage), the Stormcaller when it's present (the highest damage a tick of any class; Static field).

## What's not connecting

- **The caster half of the roster.** The Pyromancer was taken once in ten offers, the Hexer once in seven, the Bard and the Frostweaver once in six. Casters reach the party as free recruits and get benched or die. The draft is read as "who survives", and a 2,000-HP body isn't drafted into a game where a fight can open with one blow that removes a member. The consequence is upstream of balance: half the builds (Bonfire, Harvest, Overflow, Cold Snap) can't begin, because their trio is never picked. What a caster buys for its glass isn't visible at the draft.
- **The Bard and the Hexer are passengers in play too.** The Hexer's abilities deal nothing, it died in three of seven fights, and Weaken's worth (on foes in 24 fights) is invisible to the player. The Bard sang twice a fight in four fights. 5e's shape problem (a buff on one unit in a party of three) is confirmed by someone not reading a balance sheet.
- **The leader isn't a leader.** Benched in ten of seventeen runs, once on floor 1 for a captive. Nothing in the slice makes the leader special: no seat only it can hold, no perk, no cost to sitting it out. In the full game the leader is the thing you own; in the slice "a leader and two companions" is a draft of three.
- **The boss as a removal.** The boss's threat is one blow that takes a member off the field, under no control in fights and draw-based targeting. The player sees the move and its size in the preview, arrives prepared, and the preparation can't answer it: the question becomes whether the draw finds the wall and whether Guard is up at that tick. The design question is what kind of threat a boss should be in a hands-off game: sustained pressure a team out-heals or out-races, or a blow that is announced and answered by the wall's job automatically, or a seat that takes the next big hit. As it stands the boss fight is the one place the game feels like dice.
- **The phoenix has no moment.** Bought four times, used never. The campfire's free share stands fallen members up, the Valkyrie and the Cleric raise in fights, and a wipe ends the run before a revive could matter. Either it becomes an in-fight return (the Phoenix build's "returns that don't wait on a reviver") or it goes.
- **REST is not a choice.** Ten rests, six of them on the floor before the boss; TRAIN wins every other time. The free share plus potions carry the HP, so the campfire's own choice is "train, unless the boss is next".
- **The shop is a training hall with a junk buyer.** 1,320 gold on training, 1,160 on everything else, eight pieces bought in seventeen runs, 24 pieces sold. Gear isn't worth gold to this player; a formation (100) and a phoenix (60) sometimes are.
- **Formations are used as draw tables.** Half of all fights were at Neutral (formations arrive mid-act). The ones used most say who draws: Pyre of heroes 11 fights (for its Martyr seat, mostly with no reviver in the party), Shieldwall 7, Heavy line 6, Ring of steel 5. The formations with a story (Relay, The wheel, Hoarfrost line, Blood oath, Spur, Arrowhead, The pack) were tried once or three times and dropped; Hoarfrost line was held by a party with no Chill. Before a fight the player flips through every owned formation, up to twelve changes in a row, because the screen doesn't say what each would do for this trio.
- **The offhand slot stays empty.** The Knight fought 33 of 56 fights with no offhand, the Hoplite 11 of 14 (a shield class with no shield), the Valkyrie 14 of 16, the Hexer 7 of 7. Offhands are the most offered slot (139 of 413 pieces), but each kind fits two or three classes and what's offered rarely fits who's fighting; 39 were taken and few worn. The two-hander rule hardly bites because the offhand is empty anyway; where it does, the Ranger put down its bow for a knife and a totem (a dagger in 23 fights, the bow in 12).
- **Events are free value, so they beat fights.** The options never taken: hire the mercenaries (0 of 3), pay the hospital, donate to the pilgrims, the wagon's kit, the smith's reforge. Anything that costs gold loses to training. The gambles are taken every time (the Gambler, the smith's "try", the armoury's "deeper", which led into an elite that ended a run on act II's second floor). The one real dilemma, A Rival Company's trade of a member, was taken once and declined once. Most events are rest stops with a prize.

## The builds, the loot and rarity

- **One build assembles by itself: the Toll.** A wall that draws everything, with thorns: Spiked bulwark and Ring of steel (run 5), Oathkeeper, Fortify and Shieldwall (run 11, which won act I), a Bramble knight under Wellspring with the Cleric and Vampire (run 15). The player built it three times without naming it, because the draw formations, the Knight and the thorn pieces are everywhere, and because survival is what they draft for. The Hunt was half-built twice (Gravemark, Wild hunt totem) and abandoned. Thousand Cuts was built once, deliberately, on the Assassin (Thousandth cut, Night's edge, Gambler's coin, Heavy line's Bruiser) and it carried that run into act II. Phoenix pieces were found often (Funeral bell, Bones of the returned, Last bastion, Lamp of vigil) and worn as stat pieces. Landslide's Cornerstone maul was passed over three times. Bonfire, Harvest, Cold Snap, Overflow, Lockdown, Blood Pact and Ambush were never started.
- **Builds are found, not chosen.** The path to a build is: find a legendary, read its card, see whether the party happens to fit. The player saw 42 distinct legendaries in seventeen runs and a build's first piece often, its third almost never; the same few recur (Blood covenant in four runs, Wild hunt totem in four, Oathkeeper, Cornerstone maul, Last bastion and Rallying horn in three). A build needs a way to be aimed at: a lean given at the draft or the first elite, or the next piece of a build held being likelier than a stranger's.
- **Loot choices are real, and the deliberation is "who can wear this now".** Legendaries were taken 27 of the 37 times one was on offer and passed ten times for a plain piece that fit the fighting trio. Rarity as specificity works as intended: the player reads the card and takes fit over the word. But the frequency undercuts the find. A legendary sat in a quarter of all offers, and commons, uncommons and rares were offered in equal numbers (114, 112, 115, with 72 legendaries): "legendary" is a kind of piece, not an event. A fifth of what was taken was never worn.
- **Mythics read as the prize.** Taken in three boons of four, over a formation and a recruit.
- **Slots fill slowly.** Non-weapon slots were 2.5 of 9 filled by mid-act I, 4.8 at its boss, 7 in act II. Act I is played half-dressed, which may be right for a first act; the offhand's emptiness is the part that isn't.
- **Upgrades matter as choices; whether they decide fights, seventeen runs can't say.** They are visible, chosen with interest, and the two versions of an ability are both taken.

## What I'd change, as proposals

1. **Give the draft a reason to take glass.** A caster needs something visible at the draft that a wall can't offer (reach, the first kill, a kill the preview promises), or a protection the party gives by seat. Or the draft offers roles, not single classes.
2. **Give the leader a job.** A seat only it can hold, a perk the run keeps, or the one member who can't sit out.
3. **Decide what a boss is in a hands-off game.** Pressure the team answers as a team, or a blow the wall answers automatically; not a removal the draw decides.
4. **Formations: a compare view, and the draw as the first line.** Say who draws on every card, since that is what the player uses them for, and let the story be the second line.
5. **Let the offhand fill.** Widen who wears each kind, or bias the offer to what the fighting trio can hold. Then decide what a two-hander buys for giving the slot up.
6. **Fold REST into the share** and make the campfire's choice something else (a swap of a known ability, the stranger's lesson, a seat).
7. **The phoenix becomes an in-fight return or goes.**
8. **The shop sells training, buys junk, and stocks gear for the roster it sees.**
9. **Give builds a way to be aimed at**, from the draft or the first elite.
10. **A few events that cost something the player has** (a member, a formation, a piece) for something they want; the free ones can stay as rest stops.
11. **Make the Bard's and the Hexer's worth visible on the party card** ("foes hit 30% less"), or change their shape.

The next pass this asks for is design, not tuning: which of these to pursue is the director's call.
