# The builds: state of play

Where the twelve builds of `docs/gear-design/builds.md` stand after real play, from two sources read together. It's the state of things the builds pass starts from: analysis, not proposals.

**The fights.** The director's 23 saved runs (150 fights, Oct 31 to Nov 4, engine 0.17 and 0.18, heat 0), each replayed fight by fight at the commit it was played on, with the log on. All 150 replayed to the same outcome and HP, so this is the fights as they were. What was offered and taken (tagged with the designer's build data), what each party wore and sat in, and where every fight's damage came from. This source leads: where the two differ, the fights are what happened.

**The director's read.** About 30 sessions as the director remembers them, collated by another agent with a few checks against the content. It's here as the lens: why a piece was taken, what a fight looked like from the chair, which names meant nothing. Where it says what the fights can't see (how a card reads, what a bar looks like), it stands alone. Where it says what happened, it's checked.

Each point below gives what the fights say, then the director's read, then where they meet or differ. The differences are gathered at the end for the director to rule on.

## The short version

- **Two builds happened and carried runs: Bonfire, and Thousand Cuts and the Hunt woven into one Bleed build.** Both were assembled across a run, reached rung 2 or 3, and account for the two deepest runs. Burn and Bleed were the two biggest sources of party damage in the sample. The director remembers Bonfire as "the burn build" and the Bleed build as "one Assassin's gear set"; the fights agree on both, and add that the Bleed set was a build by the document's own measure.
- **The Toll happened constantly at rung 1, from kits alone,** and died where the document says it fears to. The director calls it the default and dominant; the fights say dominant in regular fights, dead at every boss and two-foe elite it met.
- **Phoenix was the most taken and the least rewarding.** Insurance, not a turn. A fall was the start of the end in nearly every fight it happened in, and two Phoenix pieces cancelled each other.
- **Landslide, Cold Snap, Lockdown, Harvest, Overflow and Ambush did not happen as builds.** The director rates Landslide the best build and the fights don't show it: its pieces were taken and worn in quantity, its party (a Shielded, Taunting front) was the party that lived, and its blow never came. The other five, both sources agree, never formed.
- **The game beneath the builds explains more than any piece.** Units fought with one ability known in 264 of 450 unit-fights and entered 194 of them with every ability on cooldown; act I parties fought with under two of four slots filled and 17 of 23 runs ended in act I; 71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral. The director's read names the same thing from the chair: fights too short and cooldowns too long for anything that chains, and the unit meant to run an engine dead first.
- **What the player saw win was a visible status stacked by the whole party.** Burn, Bleed and Thorns, each the biggest damage source in the fights that reached deepest, each worth taking piece by piece, each on screen as a counter. The rhythm the builds were designed for (the pass, the beat, the volley, the window) sat on top of that and didn't show.

## The game beneath the builds

### Kits arrive one ability at a time, and cooldowns carry
**The fights.** At fight time, 264 of 450 unit-fights were fought by a unit knowing one ability, 152 two, 34 three. On top of that, 194 of 450 began with every known ability on ten or more ticks of cooldown, 402 with something on cooldown, and in 44 the unit used no ability at all. The Haunted Hall loss (Oct 31 23:45) began with the Knight's three abilities at 29, 33 and 37 ticks; it fell at tick 14 having only swung. Training went to learning (63 picks) and versions (74) over power steps (35), which is the right instinct, but a one-ability unit is a basic attacker: the Cleric dealt 98% of its damage with its mace, the Hexer 86%, the Bard 85%, the Valkyrie 79%, the Paladin 76%. A Frostweaver that starts with Stillness, a Cleric with Raise, a Valkyrie with Valhalla, a Paladin with Sanctuary each fought three or four fights contributing nothing of its identity.

**The director's read.** "Builds that chain don't come online": cooldowns are long against fights of about 50 ticks (a medium cooldown is 30, so a lock fires once or twice), cooldowns carry between fights and don't reset, and the game as tuned is attrition.

**Where they meet.** Fully. The fights add the size of it: not a lock firing once, but two in five units entering a fight with nothing ready and one in ten never casting. The builds are written as kits plus loot; the parties as played were sticks plus loot.

### Act I gives one piece a unit, and runs end in act I
**The fights.** In act I, the weapon slot was filled 99% of the time, armor 28%, offhand 24%, amulet 16%. By act II it's three slots of four. Seventeen of 23 runs ended in act I (eleven before the boss, six at it); five in act II, one in act III. Rung 2 of any build asks for two or three pieces on one unit plus a seat or a version; the game supplies that in act II.

**The director's read.** Doesn't name this, but its learning for Ambush ("a build has to be viable on the way") and for Landslide ("every piece good alone, so the build paid before it was complete") is the same fact from the chair.

### Survivability, and who falls
**The fights.** A tank was in the party of every run that reached act II (Knight three times, Paladin, Bramble knight); a healer was not: two of the five deepest runs had no healer seated (Knight, Pyromancer, Ranger; Assassin, Knight, Ranger). Where a Cleric sat, it healed little (one to four Cures a fight) and swung a mace. Someone fell in 49 of 150 fights and 23 of those were lost; the first to fall was the highest-Aggro unit in 31 of the 49 (the Knight 17 times), a squishy in the rest, mostly against archers, Liches and Ghosts, or when the Taunt had lapsed. The Knight ended 19 of its 65 fights knocked out, the Bramble knight 8 of 29, the Cleric 11 of 44, the Pyromancer 4 of 28.

**The director's read.** "Survivability is the scarce resource, and only one shape buys it": a Taunting tank and a healer, or a bruiser party; squishies die in three or four hits; the engine unit is dead first. Its check: the hand-built build parties at heat 0 lose almost nothing and fall in 3% of fights; with the knob raised 120 they fall in 38% and lose half the fights they fall in. It asks which heat and version the director played.

**Where they meet.** The tank is mandatory in both. The healer isn't in the fights. The director's own play sits where their check put the raised knob: falls in a third of fights, half of them lost. The open question is answered: all 23 saved runs were heat 0, 17 on engine 0.17 and 6 on 0.18, at the knobs recorded fight by fight (act I regulars from −57 to +150, elites −7 to +60, bosses +47 to +50; act II regulars +31 to +280, elites +91 to +241). The act II elite knobs are the balance pass's business, but they are where four of the six runs that left act I died.

### Chains, and what piles
**The fights.** Two chains did come online: the Hearth's Torch passing Burn to every burning foe while three units' swings fed it (peak Burn climbing from 27 to 59 stacks over a run), and Coup de grâce resetting on each kill behind Opening wound and Night's edge (up to 83% of the Assassin's damage). Neither needed random targeting to cooperate: Firebolt, Pyre and Stoke aim at the foe with the most Burn, Frost lance at the most Chill, so Burn and Chill piled on one foe in every fight that had them. What spread was Bleed (Barbed arrow and Lacerate land where the hit lands) and Marks, and the Bleed build won anyway because Bleed on every foe plus a lowest-HP execute is the Hunt's staircase.

**The director's read.** Random targeting of basic attacks and most abilities makes stacks and Marks spread instead of pile; the party is outnumbered; the engine unit dies.

**Where they differ.** "Instead of pile" is true of Bleed, Marks and Shock, not of Burn and Chill, whose abilities already focus. Cold Snap's failure is not spread; it reaches the cap and shatters once (next section). Otherwise the two agree, and the fights name the two exceptions, which are the two builds that worked.

### What worked was a visible status
**The fights.** In 26 of 150 fights more than half the party's damage came from statuses laid on foes; in the Bonfire and Bleed runs it was half to three quarters, fight after fight. Burn (437k), Bleed (397k) and Thorns (157k) were the three biggest damage sources in the sample; Shock (80k) and Chill (25k) far behind. On screen that damage belongs to no unit, which is what made those builds watchable, and may be why the Hexer, whose Malison deepens everyone else's statuses, had the lowest damage share of any class (12%) and was benched or fell in 7 of 19 fights.

**The director's read.** "What worked was a visible status you stack and a core system you invest in": Landslide (Shield, Aggro, Defense), the Toll (Thorns, Taunt), Bonfire as "collect the orange things". Each piece good alone, the thread visible as a counter or a bar. The rhythm layered on top didn't show. Burn and Chill were understood as builds because their status is colour-coded; the Hunt and Harvest were never recognized; Lockdown's pieces read as "what is this design". Trigger payoffs (a few Might, a first cast free, a tenth off a cost) are too small to perceive next to statuses dealing hundreds.

**Where they meet.** Fully on the statuses. One difference on recognition: the Hunt was taken even if it wasn't named. Marking knife was carried in three runs, Gravemark in two, Huntmaster's quiver, Pack leader's coat, Bounty charm three times, Hunting party and The pack once each: the player kept choosing the Hunt's pieces for the Ranger and the Assassin, and the deepest Bleed run was the Hunt by the document's own rhythm. It formed without a name.

### Formations
**The fights.** 71 of 122 act I fights were fought in Neutral; every act II fight in a formation. Ten formations owned at a run's end were never fought in (First light four times; Thorn hedge, Reaping line, Hearth, Watchtower, Heavy line, Arrowhead, Blood oath, The wheel once each). The ones fought in most are the plain ones: Pyre of heroes (11 fights), Shieldwall (9), Vigil (9), Wellspring (7), Ring of steel (6), The undying (6), Heavy line (6). The build-defining rares and legendaries were fought in once each: Hunting party, The pack, The vise, Relay; Hoarfrost line and Hammer and anvil three times; Blood oath twice. Overflow's formation offers were taken at 16%, Thousand Cuts' at 29%, Blood Pact's 31%, Cold Snap's 33%; Harvest's 73%, Phoenix's 65%, the Hunt's 64%, the Toll's 59%. Seats were filled by whoever was in the party: Hammer and anvil with a Cleric in the Hammer seat and an Assassin, then a Pyromancer, as the Mason; Hoarfrost line with a Knight and a Bramble knight as the two Frost seats; The pack with the Cleric as the Lead; Spur with a Bramble knight as the pacer; The vise on a party with no Stun or Silence. Two runs cycled through every owned formation before each fight: 26 changes before six fights, 16 before eleven.

**The director's read.** Formations read when they were about the front line (Heavy line, Shieldwall, The mountain, Thorn hedge, Blood oath); engine formations didn't: Wellspring felt like a debuff, The choir's no-regen a risk, Relay never started, Gaol added nothing visible, The ambuscade plainly worse; "lighter kit" and several cards were cryptic.

**Where they meet and differ.** They agree on which kind read. Two of the director's examples aren't in the saved runs: The mountain was never offered, and Thorn hedge was taken twice and never fought in. Wellspring, which "felt like a debuff", was the one engine formation fought in at all (seven fights), chosen for its plain promise of mana; the feeling and the use are both real. The director's intent that a new formation makes you rethink the party didn't happen in either source: the party was rethought for other reasons, and the formation fitted to it afterwards by its stat words, or left at Neutral.

### One unit's slots, and the roster
**The fights.** Long runs swapped members 12 to 19 times; eleven of thirteen captives and strangers were taken. Of 54 legendary and mythic pieces taken, 11 were never worn in a fight and about a dozen were worn in one or two, because the wearer was benched (Still hand, Volley bow twice, Vestments of return twice) or the trigger never came. The one three-unit synergy that formed, Hearth with three Burn-on-swing units, needed a formation whose three seats are all for the same thing and three riders that each pay alone.

**The director's read.** "A single unit's four slots synergize more easily than three units": the strongest thing seen was one Assassin in a full Bleed-and-crit set. Within one unit a player can see and assemble a synergy; across three, with random targeting and a tank that dies first, the web didn't form.

**Where they meet.** Fully, and the fights add the one counter-example and what it cost.

### What the player takes, and why
**The fights.** Build-tag weight taken against offered: Cold Snap 52% (a small sample, mostly Frostwalker's furs and Icebreaker), Thousand Cuts 49%, the Toll 46%, Phoenix 46%, Overflow 43%, the Hunt 42%, Lockdown 42%, Harvest 39%, Bonfire 39%, Blood Pact 38%, Landslide 38%, Ambush 31%. Among far-end legendaries, Phoenix's were offered 26 times and taken 9; Blood Pact's 17 and 5; Bonfire's 3 and 1. 86 tonics were drunk against 62 potions and one phoenix feather in 150 fights. Blood vial was worn one fight, Blood covenant two.

**The director's read.** Death and HP are too costly to spend: a knockout carries into the next fight, so Phoenix's risk dwarfs its payoff; HP is what the game drains, so paying it was never considered and Blood Pact's pieces were used as sustain. A piece that reaches one more foe lost to one that did more to the foes already hit, unless the slot was empty. Preconditions the enemies rarely supply are worth nothing.

**Where they meet and differ.** The fights show the same hand: defence first, spread and openers last, the one feather unused. One correction to the director's check: in the version played, four enemies Stun (the Shieldman's and Mummy's Shield slam, the Ogre's and Troll's Flatten), and the Shieldman and Ogre are in a third of act I's fights; only Silence is as rare as the check says (the Wraith and the Silent One). Lockdown's problem stands either way: payoffs on a disabled foe need the party's own locks, which came once or twice a fight.

### Evasion, and class weakness
**The fights.** No evasion party lived long enough to be read: Skirmish line was fought once and lost, Duelist's leathers was worn in two fights by units that drew no blows, Windrunner's garb in one (Tempo climbed to 11, then the Monk fell). On the classes: the Frostweaver was dismissed from one roster and benched in two others, and did 61% of its damage with its weapon; the Bard was seated in five fights across two runs; the Pyromancer ended 4 of 28 fights knocked out, the fewest of any caster, and carried two of the five deepest runs; the Stormcaller had the highest damage share of any class (39%) in its 11 fights and was knocked out in 3.

**The director's read.** Evasion isn't a defence below the cap, only near it, and its check found the cap a placeholder meant to be out of reach. The Bard "a dead unit", the Pyromancer and Stormcaller dying in most fights, the Frostweaver weak; routes through them didn't get played.

**Where they differ.** The Frostweaver, agreed. The Pyromancer did not die in most saved fights; it was the sample's best caster. The Bard can't be judged from five fights. Evasion can't be judged from the fights at all, so the director's read stands there.

## Build by build

### Bonfire
**The fights.** Happened, rung 3, the deepest run. Nov 3 16:46 reached act III on Paladin, Hexer and Pyromancer in Hearth with Hex of flames, Zeal, Kindling, Slow burn, Flashfire, Pyre of judgement, Hexweave mail and, at the end, Crown of embers. Burn was over half the party's damage in every elite and boss fight but Bomb Nest (37%, Shock the rest); peak Burn climbed from 27 to 59 stacks across the run; the Torch seat did the passing-on. The fire passing from a dying foe never appeared: Wick was offered once and skipped, Ember heart once in a shop and not bought, Kindled robes twice and not bought. A second run (Oct 31 23:45, Knight, Pyromancer, Ranger in Shieldwall) reached act II floor 11 on Kindling, Fireball and Pyre with Burn at 28% to 58% of its damage, without chasing it.
**The director's read.** Worked as "the burn build"; the rhythm didn't. Burn is upkeep-limited and hard to hold on one foe, kills slowly, so the whole board burns and nothing passes on. Learning: a status you can collect reads as a build even when the designed rhythm is absent.
**Where they meet and differ.** They agree it was the build that read and the one the player built on purpose. The fights disagree that Burn is hard to hold on one foe: the Pyromancer's casts aim at the hottest foe and peak Burn reached 43 to 59 on bosses, and the Torch seat spread it from there. "Each kill comes sooner than the last" did not show; kills in the Bonfire fights were evenly spaced. What's missing is the kill-passing rung, which was offered three times and never taken.

### Thousand Cuts and the Hunt
**The fights.** Happened together, as one Bleed build, rung 3. Nov 3 02:20: Assassin with Thousandth cut (Cuts climbing to 17), Venom vial, Gambler's coin, Opening wound, Coup de grâce and Night's edge; Ranger with Deep barbs, Pack leader's coat and a Briar totem; Knight in Heavy line's Holder seat. Bleed was 46% to 64% of the party's damage in act I's elites and boss, 28% to 48% in act II, and kills came as a staircase (the Packmaster fell at ticks 24, 28, 31, 39, 54). The run died when the Assassin was benched for a bare Hexer against seven hellhounds, with the Knight carried in knocked out for a tick-one Valhalla. The Hunt's focus (Marked) showed up in about twenty fights across six runs and steered basic attacks; abilities kept their own targets. Flurry wraps was worn ten fights across two runs on a Monk and a Vampire, Hummingbird charm seven.
**The director's read.** Thousand Cuts never as a party, once as one Assassin's gear set; Evasion isn't survival; the Bard dead, the casters die, the Ranger wanted elsewhere; crits inconsistent without guarantees. The Hunt never recognized as a build; Mark pieces made any party a little more consistent; few Mark sources, on long cooldowns, marking the wrong foe; kill payoffs too small; no burst to follow.
**Where they meet and differ.** The Assassin set is the same thing seen from both chairs. The fights call it a build: it had a rhythm (the staircase), several threads (crits, Bleed, the kill reset, the hidden opener), a formation seat and a route through two classes, and it carried a run. The director didn't see the Hunt in it, and the document wouldn't help them: it files the kill-reset and the execute under the Hunt and the Cuts counter under Thousand Cuts, and the player found them as one thing. "Kill payoffs too small" is half right: Bounty charm's two Might didn't register; Coup de grâce's reset and Pack leader's coat's volley were most of the damage.

### The Toll
**The fights.** Rung 1 in five runs, from the kit; rung 2 once; never rung 3. Thorns was the Bramble knight's biggest damage source (44% of its own over 29 fights; 67% to 89% against wolf packs). The Knight's Hold the line made a Knight an anchor with no gear (77% of its damage from Thorns against Night Wolves). Spinebark pole, Briar totem, Portcullis, Urchin shell and Ring of steel's Beacon were taken and used. Rung 2 happened once: Nov 2 17:54 (Bramble knight with Briar totem beside a Vampire) ran Bleed at 29% to 53% of its damage from its third fight to its eleventh, then died to the Lich Lair, two foes who barely swing. Every Toll run died at a boss or a two-foe elite. Thorn hedge was taken twice and never fought in; Thornheart plate and the Bramble knight's mythic were never offered; the dodge that pays was worn twice by units that drew nothing.
**The director's read.** The strongest and most consistent; the default. Being tanky and Taunting is already necessary, so damage from being hit comes free. Dominant and uncountered. Learning: the default party already is the Toll; its dominance is the survivability economy, not its pieces.
**Where they meet and differ.** They agree it's the default and why. "Uncountered" isn't what the fights show: it was countered by every fight that doesn't swing much, which is exactly the document's list of fears, and that's where every Toll run ended. The director's learning stands: it is the economy, not the pieces. Rungs 2 and 3 were barely reached, so whether the pieces add anything is untested.

### Phoenix
**The fights.** Attempted most, functioned as insurance, never as a turn. Pyre of heroes, Vigil and The undying were fought in 26 fights across seven runs; Lamp of vigil, Martyr's locket, Last bastion, Bones of the returned, Thrall's chalice, Mantle of the fallen, Challenger's plate and Phoenix ember were all worn. Someone fell in 49 fights and 23 of those were lost. Eleven revives happened, six of them the after-the-fight stands of Lamp of vigil and The undying; each of the five in-fight revives was followed by the revived unit falling again before the fight ended. The martyr's fall fired Pyre of heroes once (the Chief's Return, won) and The undying three times (the others healed to full, the Paladin back after the fight), which is the one place the build worked as written. Last bastion on the Martyr seat held the Knight at one HP in two of its three fights there, so the seat's flare never came while it could have mattered: two Phoenix pieces cancelling each other. The return with everything ready never happened in a fight. One phoenix feather was used in 23 runs.
**The director's read.** Heavily signposted; never worth the risk. A knockout carries into the run; revives rare and slow; the tank falls first and the survivors are near full, so fall payoffs (heals, a few Might) do nothing; no agency over who dies. Learning: death can't be a turn while it's a run loss.
**Where they meet.** Fully. The fights put numbers on "the survivors are near full" (the director's check: the other two at about three quarters of their HP when one falls) and add the cancelling pieces. The document's own warning that the second rung is thin is borne out by both.

### Blood Pact
**The fights.** Attempted twice, functioned on one unit, never as a party. Blood oath with a Vampire Bleeder, Blood covenant and Bloodsworn mail (Oct 31 13:28): the Vampire took 8,459 and healed 8,449 in the fight it lost, alone, after the Cleric fell at tick 11 and the Assassin at 21; Shared feast had nobody to feed. The Wellspring Vampire of Nov 2 17:54 with Savage drain was the best Vampire seen (31% to 44% of its party's damage), and that run's Cleric wore Phylactery and Censer mace: the donor pieces work as healer pieces. HP as a cost showed up in one fight.
**The director's read.** Worse than bad as a pact; good as sustain. Paying HP is refused because HP is what the game drains. Learning: HP can't be a currency while it's the scarce resource.
**Where they meet.** Fully.

### Landslide
**The fights.** Pieces taken in quantity, the build never assembled. Earthmother totem was worn nine fights by a Ranger in a party with one Shield source; Worldbreaker six fights by a Knight knocked out in three of them; Cornerstone maul one, Heartsplitter three, Icebreaker three, Rubble plate five, Spiked bulwark two, War horn one, Stone totem never; Millstone offered four times and never taken. Hammer and anvil was fought three times with a Cleric as the Hammer. The Geomancer fought 20 fights and never sat in a Hammer seat or swung a Millstone or a Cornerstone maul; its big blows were Rockfall (13% to 58% of its own damage) and the crits that came while its Shield held and stopped when it broke (60% of its party's damage in one Snake Pit, five to eight crits a fight). No fight showed a few huge blows; the biggest single hits in the sample were Coup de grâce, Backstab and Skewer. The runs that carried the stone kits ended at the Ogre Chief twice, the Packmaster, and the Lich Lair; none passed act II. One build nobody planned did happen: Rubble plate on a Knight with Stonewall and Earthmother turned the Shield breaking into half the party's damage in one act II fight, which is the stone becoming the blow, on the wrong class.
**The director's read.** The best: effective and interesting. Shield into big hits and crits; every piece good alone; many routes; formations about Aggro and Defense served it. Nothing named as not working. Learning: a build on a core system, whose pieces each pay alone, gets built and recognized.
**Where they differ.** This is the largest disagreement. What the director built and enjoyed is in the fights: Shields, Defense and Aggro on a front that lives, and a Geomancer critting behind its own stone. That is Landslide's first rung, "steadier boss fights", and it's also the Toll's party and the party that survives at all, so it was effective for the reason the director gives the Toll. The build's own blow (the hammer seat, the slow swing made huge, the stone spent as damage) was never seen, and the stone parties died at the bosses the build is written to love. Both can be true: the system was recognized and invested in, the build wasn't reached. Whether "interesting" survives that is the director's call; it's the one thing here the fights can't weigh.

### Cold Snap
**The fights.** Attempted four times, never functioned. Twelve shatters in 150 fights, at most three in one, nine fights with any. The Frostweaver fought 16 fights in three runs: Chill reached its cap in most of them, shattered about once, and thawed. Hoarfrost line's two Frost seats went to a Knight and a Bramble knight; Chill peaked at 2 to 8 and never shattered in those three fights. Frostwalker's furs (seven fights) and Winter's chain (one) need shatters to do anything. Rime knuckles, Heart of winter, Frostbite bow and Primer of frost were each offered once and skipped. The Frostweaver did 61% of its damage with its weapon.
**The director's read.** A handful of shatters in 30 games; clearly signposted. Twelve stacks, thawing one per 14 ticks, from random-target applications; only Area Chill piles; long cooldowns; squishy, as no tank or healer Chills. Learning: a payoff at a cap needs focused application or fast stacking, and a tank who contributes.
**Where they meet and differ.** The count agrees. The cause differs in one part: Frost lance and Frost arrow aim at the foe with the most Chill, so the stacks did pile and the cap was reached in most Frostweaver fights; what didn't happen was the second shatter, because the cap spends the stacks and the applications come once a cooldown. The director's "fast stacking" is the right lever; "focused application" is already there. The rest (no tank or healer Chills; the Frost seats fell to slow Plate units) the fights confirm. Its rhythm, bursts on a beat you can count, was never on screen in either source.

### Lockdown
**The fights.** Pieces taken, never a build. Stuns on foes came one or two a fight from Shield bash, Tremor and Pressure point; Silence once a fight from Unmake or Garrote; a wind-up was caught five times in 150 fights. Chains of light was worn four fights by a Paladin whose Smite rarely fired. The vise was fought once, by a party with no Stun or Silence. Idol of binding was offered five times and never taken; Opportunist's charm four times, taken once, never worn; Still hand taken, never worn; Gaol never offered. Measured blow and Frozen moment, the wait-for-the-wind-up versions, were each trained once; the unit was then benched or the run ended.
**The director's read.** The worst. The window is rare (8 ticks, medium cooldowns, no enemy Stuns to exploit) and nothing coordinates the pile-in; Gaol's seats read as nothing. Learning: a payoff behind a rare, short window the player can't make happen isn't a build.
**Where they meet and differ.** They agree on the verdict and the cause. One correction to the check: enemies did Stun in this version (four of them, two common in act I), so foes' own locks could in principle be exploited; nothing in the loot does so, and the party's own locks were as rare as the director says.

### Harvest
**The fights.** Rung 1 by accident, the rest never. The Stormcaller dealt 49% to 61% of its party's damage against packs (Nov 1 17:35), most of it through Thunderstorm. Oathkeeper on a Knight turned a Taunt into a sweep that was 27% to 79% of the Knight's damage, but the Knight's share of the party's was 3% to 10%. Sweeping blade on a Bramble knight, Chain wand on a Bard, Cataclysm staff on a Cleric whose third ability was Raise: the spread pieces landed on units with nothing to spread. Stormweave was offered four times, Last page twice, Storm pike once; none taken. Reaping line taken once, never fought in.
**The director's read.** Never recognized as a build; Area pieces valued as raw power; spread lost to focus on the slot's opportunity cost. Learning: spread has to be a plan the player can see, not a bonus they pass over.
**Where they meet.** Fully.

### Overflow
**The fights.** Least attempted; its formations refused most. Wellspring was fought seven times, with the two cheapest casters in the game as its Spenders, chosen because its card plainly says "regains mana", which every run needed: 86 tonics were drunk, the Monk ended most fights under 150 MP, the Frostweaver and Stormcaller at zero. Relay was fought once; The choir never offered; Horn of the keep skipped for a recruit; Annotated grimoire skipped; Bell of the hour taken and never worn; Archmage's mantle worn three fights on a Bard. Nobody cast all fight, because nobody had the abilities to cast.
**The director's read.** Never, even a little. Effects too small to perceive; engine seats are squishy and dead first; cooldowns too long to see a link twice; formations felt like debuffs. Its check: a tenth off a cost is about 30 MP of a 1,000 to 3,800 pool; a few Might is three actions at a few percent. Learning: an engine build needs a visible, sizeable link and an engine that survives the fight.
**Where they meet.** Fully, and the fights add that the mana problem the build is for was felt in every run.

### Ambush
**The fights.** Taken least, never a build. First light was owned five times and fought in once. The ambuscade was offered once and First light taken over it. Knives drawn, Quietus (twice) and Drawn steel (twice) skipped; Horn of the charge never offered; Volley bow taken twice and never worn; Ready stance worn two fights on a Bramble knight; Shadow cloak worn once. The Assassin's Vanish fired one to three times a fight in the early runs. Nothing in the fight's text marks the opening as a moment.
**The director's read.** Once, as the Assassin set. The pieces that reset cooldowns worked; no reset otherwise; first-X effects run out; a party that functions while building it is the tank party, and picking for Ambush loses the run; formations cryptic or worse. ## Where the two sources differ

For the director to rule on. Each is a place where the read from the chair and the fights say different things; the fights are what happened, the read is what it felt like, and a build has to satisfy both.
1. **Landslide: the best build, or never reached?** The director rates it first; the fights show its first rung (a Shielded front that lives) and never its blow, and its parties died at the bosses it's written to love.
2. **The Toll: uncountered, or countered by every boss?** Dominant in regular fights in both sources; dead at every boss and two-foe elite in the fights.
3. **The Hunt: never recognized, or built without a name?** Its pieces were taken run after run and its staircase carried the second-deepest run, inside what the director calls the Assassin's gear set.
4. **Burn and Chill: spread or piled?** The director's read says random targeting spreads stacks; the fights show Burn and Chill piling to high stacks on one foe because their abilities aim there. Cold Snap's problem is the second shatter, not the first.
5. **The Pyromancer: dies in most fights, or the best caster seen?** Knocked out in 4 of 28 fights and the carry of two deep runs.
6. **A healer: mandatory, or not?** Two of the five deepest runs seated none.
7. **Enemy Stuns: none, or four?** The played version had four stunning enemies, two of them common in act I. This changes nothing about Lockdown's verdict and something about which payoffs could exist.

## Questions for the intent

Questions, not designs; the director's answers are the intent the pass starts from.
- **Should a build be reachable from one unit's kit plus one piece, in act I?** The two that happened early did it that way (Hearth plus a Burn rider; Thicket plus Briar). The six that didn't all need two or three pieces to meet on one unit, which act I doesn't supply.
- **Should the kit carry the build, with loot steering it, and should kits arrive whole sooner?** Units fought with one ability known in 59% of unit-fights and with everything on cooldown in 43%. Either abilities arrive sooner and cooldowns don't carry so far, or the builds are written for basic attacks and one ability.
- **Is a fall allowed to be anything but the end?** Phoenix's premise needs falls that aren't losses; in the fights they were. Either the martyr's fall comes early and cheaply, or the build is about standing back up between fights.
- **Can HP be a currency while it's the scarce resource?** The director's read says no, the fights agree; Blood Pact as written depends on yes.
- **Formations: chosen for the party, or the party for the formation?** In both sources they were fitted to the party afterwards by stat words, or left at Neutral. If a formation is to make you rethink your party, its promise has to be one the party can't refuse, and the party has to hold still long enough to build toward it.
- **Which rhythm, if any, has to be on screen?** The statuses were the watchable damage, and the builds built on them happened. The pass, the beat, the volley and the window didn't show, in either source. Is the rhythm space of `builds.md` still the frame, or is "a status the whole party stacks, plus one turn" the frame the game can show?
- **Is Landslide's identity the stone front (which was played and liked) or the stone blow (which never came)?** The answer decides whether it's a build apart from the Toll.
