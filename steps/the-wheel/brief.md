# The wheel: the brief

A design step, with the director: the five colours that sort the web. Nothing is built in it and nothing in the game changes. The frame is `docs/colours.md`; its rulings bind this step.

## What it's for

Five colours, each on one card (what it never does, the mechanics it prefers, its profile and style, what it can't answer), that pass the checks in `docs/colours.md`, What checks a wheel. The director picks or merges one wheel. The pie, the classes and the loot come after, in later steps.

## The danger

The colours coming out generic: temperaments that could sit unchanged in any fantasy game, or a reskin of Magic's five. That's what an agent writes when it authors the core of a colour itself: the middle of everything it has read. The project has seen this before (`steps/authored-gaps/brief.md`, What the game needs): the agent holds a structure well and authors generic things inside it; the director authors unique things easily and finds coherence across a set hard. So the split here is the same: **the director seeds the colours, the agent holds the frame and the checks.**

## How to work

1. **Intent first.** Ask the director for two things and record them in `intent.md`, in their words where the wording matters, before proposing anything:
   - **The question the five colours answer.** Five colours are five answers to one question, which is what makes them conflict and keeps each specific. It should be a question only this game asks.
   - **Their phrases:** fragments, images, moments they want to see in a fight, things they'd hate to see. Unsorted, more than five, contradictions welcome.
2. **Candidate temperaments, unnamed.** More than five, each a short card in plain words, labelled by letter. Each says what it values and what it can't stand, as an answer to the question; which of the director's phrases it grew from; and how it shows in this game's own rules. The director marks the ones that are alive.
3. **Two or three wheels** assembled from what the director marked, each colour on its full card, with the checks run and shown. The director picks or merges one.
4. **Names last,** with the director, once the wheel is picked. A name chosen early pulls the colour toward everything else that shares it.

If the director wants a wider first pass, several agents can each draw candidates from one source on their own, and the director marks across all of them: one agent asked for variety tends to give variations of its first idea.

## What fails a colour

- It could sit unchanged in another fantasy game.
- It matches one of Magic's colours one to one.
- It's an element, or a role.
- It can't say what it does with the rules this game has and others don't: preparation as the whole of the player's part, the order abilities are slotted in, wind-ups and cooldowns, HP and MP carried from fight to fight, who draws the foes' blows, the enrage.
- Its profile mirrors another colour's, or the wheel gives every colour the same shape.

## What to read

The list is closed.

- **Every session:** `docs/primer.md`; this brief; `docs/colours.md`; `intent.md` once it exists.
- **For the mechanics a colour prefers:** the threads in `steps/authored-gaps/web.md`, The threads, and a status's entry in `docs/core-set/vocabulary.md` when a card leans on it.
- **For what a colour can't answer:** the challenges in `docs/core-set/enemies.md`, Challenges.
- **Only when a claim rests on how a fight runs, and only that section:** `docs/core-set/combat.md`.
- **Leave alone:** the classes (`docs/core-set/classes.md`, and the primer's class table as a source); the builds and engines (`docs/gear-design/`); the rest of `steps/authored-gaps/`; the code. The classes come after the pie, and the builds don't carry over.

## Rules for the cards

- **No examples in anything written for an agent,** and none of the director's illustrations written down as design: an agent copies what it's shown.
- **Plain words.** A card says what it means in its first sentence, and is short.
- **Don't predict how the director will answer.** Their rulings are what's needed from them.
- **Symmetry is a pull to resist** (`docs/colours.md`, A colour).
