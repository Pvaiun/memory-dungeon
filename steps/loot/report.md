# The loot pass: gate 1

For the director. The spine (`spine.md`) is the lead's design for the loot the nine builds need: its rules, one line an item, each engine's link and the web's checks. Nothing is built, and no number is set. A fresh reader read the draft against the builds; what it found is applied, and what's yours to rule is below.

## Decisions

**1. How loot is offered, not only what there is.** The spine's count of the web found the thing you asked it to look for.
- **A road's first step is met.** A party on a road is offered one of its first-step pieces in a little over half of act I's for Bonfire, Cold Snap and the Storm, and two in three or more for the other six. With the versions and common formations that also carry a first step, every road reaches three in four or better.
- **An engine's keystone isn't.** A party on an engine's road is offered a keystone piece it can wear in about a fifth of act I's, a third of runs by the end of act II, and under half of full runs. That's with nearly every keystone in two to eight places.
- **Why.** An offer rolls a slot, then a class, then one of its kinds, then one piece of the two to five in that kind at that rarity. No stock gets a given piece in front of a run often, and more places help little.
- **What is met.** A version is in about three of every eight trainings its class takes. Ten engines have a keystone in a version, and those are met by most runs that field the class.
- **`loot.md` says the designer's tags are "used to aim drops". The offer roll doesn't use them.**
- **The choice:**
  - **Leave it.** Engines stay rare finds, and the ten with a version carry the idea.
  - **Aim one offer of each fight's three** at what the party is already doing, by the designer's tags. On the script's estimate that takes a keystone from a fifth of act I's to three in ten, and from under half of full runs to six in ten. It takes every road's first-step piece to over nine in ten in act I.
- **I'd aim it.** It's the rogue mode's rule, not this pass's, so it would be a line in the build's brief. The fill doesn't wait on it.

- **More uncommons,** because they carry nine roads' first steps and the engines' common halves.
- **More rares, fewer legendaries,** because of decision 4.
- **It's a sixth more to write and tune.** If that's too much, the place to cut is the uncommons, and the status roads' first steps pay for it.

**3. The same effect in more than one place.** `loot.md` says that where two items would read the same, one changes. builds.md asks for fire on swings "for many kinds", and for every keystone "from more than one place: a piece in more than one kind, a version, a seat". Both can't hold everywhere. The spine reads it this way, and needs your word on it:
- **A plain rider sits in more than one kind** when no unit could hold both. The plainest case: the fist weapons have three (Shock, Burn, Chill) that differ in one word.
- **A keystone's second place is a different form of it** wherever one exists. The fuse's four places each send the fire somewhere else.
- **Where no second form exists, a piece and a seat carry the same rule.** Second dawn and Daybreak; Twin-knot and Twins.

**4. An engine's keystone is a rare; its grown form is the legendary.** About two dozen of today's legendary effects move down a rarity: the cold a shatter throws, the kill that calls the volley, the Shield that breaks as a blow. builds.md says a keystone has to be met while its other half is close at hand, and a fight's spoils roll a rare more than four times as often as a legendary. The far ends keep the legendaries.

**5. Three rules the spine bends.** Each is one or two items, and a line to cut if you'd rather not.
- **A status on a swing is an uncommon,** and so are two swing shapes (Millstone, Hummingbird charm). Today all of these are rares. builds.md makes them the first steps.
- **"All fight" means renewed as it runs out.** Challenger's plate and one legendary seat Taunt all fight, as two of today's seats do. The status keeps its length, and its length matters less.
- **The last drink.** The Red Feast heals its wearer up to half when a fight is won. builds.md asks for it; `docs/rules.md` says no build lasts more fights.

**6. Three smaller rulings.**
- **Malison counts the five afflictions** (Burn, Chill, Shock, Bleed, Sunder), the ones the old Malison deepened. The Hexer's own kit reaches three; the other two have to be found. Counting every debuff would let its own Weaken and a Mark stand in for them.
- **Gear never adds a second Mark.** Marked now steers abilities too, so two Marks split the party. Every marker piece lays one "while no foe is Marked" or moves one at a fall. The Paladin's Condemn can still put a second one up; one Mark a party would be a change to the vocabulary, and it's yours.
- **The mirror reads two ways in builds.md:** a share of the blow as it was struck, or exactly what the wall's cover stopped. Only the second makes better stone send more back. The spine has a piece of each.

**7. Where builds.md or the core set doesn't hold.** None of these stopped the design.
- **"Every keystone comes from more than one place" isn't enough for it to be met.** Decision 1.
- **Two engines have one place.** The emptied quiver's keystone is Quick draw, one version on the Ranger, as builds.md wrote it. The carrier's is one legendary.
- **The Storm is the road a run is shown least.** Its sign is in half of starting parties, and no version gives a first step. The spine answers it from the loot. A version on a third class would do more, and it's yours to ask for.
- **The opening, again fights the amulet.** The amulet holds four of the start-of-fight uncommons it wants to fire again, and a unit wears one. Its first taste is on Leather.
- **The red stone is in the game today.** builds.md's table says nothing does it. The Red Feast and Phylactery both turn healing past full into a Shield.
- **The Hunt's Mark is short.** Marked lasts under half the time its foe takes to fall (`STATUS.marked`). How long it lasts is the balance pass's.
- **`classes.md` names a Hexer version, Culmination, that isn't in the content.** Enfeeble's second version is Expose.

## What the spine holds

- **The rules:** the stock by kind and rarity with the why of each kind, six counters (Cuts, Heat, Spite, Charge, Crescendo, Cascade; Tempo, Surge and Fervor go, and Heat now means speed), the rules the pieces bend, and twenty-two lines of code support.
- **The lines:** 242 uncommon, rare and legendary pieces, 36 formations, 10 changed versions, 32 kept versions the web needs to see, and 16 mythics. A road's first step and an engine's keystone say what the player sees.
- **Commons and uncommons by kind,** in one table, with what each kind's stats lean to on each road.
- **Each engine's link,** in plain words: what one part makes and what the other reads.
- **The web,** checked for rungs, routes, doors, exotics, and how often a run is offered a first step or a keystone.

**What changed from the brief:** nothing in its order or its scope. The spine adds a small script (`web-check.ts`) that reads its tags and writes its tables, so the counts can't drift from the lines.

**What the fresh read changed.** It found pieces that resized a status, hits that went past Defense, Marks that would have scattered the party, mythics that repeated a piece, four keystones that didn't do their engine's mechanism, and several pieces only one wearer could use. Those are fixed. It also found the tensions in decisions 3, 5 and 6, which the draft had stated too quietly.

## What the numbers say, and how sure

- **The counts are exact:** they're read from the lines.
- **The offer estimates are rough.** They use the real offer roll and its weights, and an estimate of a run's shape (six regular fights, an elite and a chest an act; a roster of four, five, then six). Read them for which threads are thin, not to a decimal.
- **Thin by that count:** the three status roads' first steps as pieces; the Storm overall; five engines with two places and no version (the reckoning, the avalanche, the resonance, mana as armour, the shatter bell); and the borrowed gift, whose two places are both legendary.
- **Nothing here says any of it is fun or strong.** That's the build's test parties and your play.

## Least sure

- **Whether 92 uncommons can be told apart,** and whether riders as uncommons make the four status roads feel like one. Tinder charm and The standard press on the same doubt.
- **Rares that used to be legendaries.** If they come to feel like the legendaries did, the far ends have less room above them.
- **The wide hits.** Five rares fan a swing out and carry what it carries. Their promise is still more foes, which play never took.
- **Heat as speed.** Speed feeds every status road at once, and three roads build it.
- **The Toll is over-served.** It sits on the most doors, so it has the most second rungs, and builds.md calls it the road that least needs them.
- **Seven formations are a rule for the party with three plain seats** (six of them legendary). Play fought in formations whose seats had roles.
- **How few versions change.** Ten of 128. The earlier versions were split toward identities the nine roads use; it may also mean they were read too kindly.
