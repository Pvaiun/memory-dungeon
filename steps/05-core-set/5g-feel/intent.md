## How this step reads the intent

- **The loop is the method:** a feel read (an agent reading rogue fights tick by tick) after every round of changes, with short simulations beside it; the read's findings drive the next round. Rogue first; the campaign only where a rogue finding points there.
- **Numbers, not behaviour.** Fix a problem by the amount applied, the cost, the cooldown, the duration, never by a new rule that changes what a thing does (a cap that binds on one hit, a target rule that excludes the caster). A behaviour change that could read as esoteric goes to the director first, one question at a time, as it comes up. 5f's Burn cap is such a sledgehammer and comes out.
- **The enemy side of 5f's intent** (buffs, taunts, multi-hits on more foes) is in scope and may be built without asking, as a secondary objective after the fights read right.
- **Win rates and class win-rate spreads are not the measure** in this step; subjective combat experience is. The knobs get re-tuned at the end, as before.
- The three standing rules hold: a status scales by its stacks, MP costs by hand, Aggro by draw.

## Round 9: the director's play notes (Oct 28, 2026)

After eight rounds and about ten rogue runs of their own, the director's words, verbatim. "I think we may have reached the limit of this current iteration of the reader. It's worth doing one more pass just for the sake of it, but I have some changes I want to enact, and here they are."

> **POTIONS AND TONICS**
> 1. The system is a huge improvement. The simulated player isn't very good at using them, but if you use them intelligently, it really does add sustain and dynamism to the run.
> 2. The way the inventory works now is something I would change. Right now you have a flat inventory of 5 of any of these potions or tonics, which means that you can not be allowed to pick up a tonic because you have 5 potions, for example. This leads to weird moments where these economies are merged, health and mana, when they shouldn't be. I want this to be changed to, you have 2 slots, health tonics and mana potions. Each can hold a maximum of 2. And at the end of every fight, rather than being given one of each, you're given a choice to pick between one of the 2 that you want to take.
> 3. Having played a lot, I believe even with those changes above, it's still going to be too generous, and the mana economy across the board needs to be made more challenging. Again, the simulated player doesn't play intelligently. If you try to have a clarity unit on your team at all times, you basically have infinite mana right now. Don't nerf clarity necessarily. It's just the cost of everything need to be higher, and regen needs to be tweaked, especially because I'm going to suggest we raise cooldowns.
> 4. Also, right now, a potion or a tonic is just used on, I guess, whichever ally is lowest. The player needs to have the ability to select which person they're using this on. The feature doesn't work without that.
>
> **ACTION ECONOMY**
> 1. some really good findings and some regressions. First off, the speed of everything is just way too high. The way that it played before was a little bit sluggish. Like, I was recommending raising the attack speeds a little bit, maybe lowering the cooldowns a little bit based on what it was in main. But we've gone way, way, way too far. We need to go 80% back to where it was in terms of attack speeds and cooldowns. I do like the greater variance. It's a big improvement between the different cooldowns. And I do like the greater variance in attack speeds, another big improvement. They're just tuned too far apart from the original pacing.
> 2. However, like I said, variance is strong, and we need to go even further on that. For example, certain abilities do feel good with the really short cooldowns. The might buff on the bard feels great. The frost weaver feels good because they rely a lot on their shatter for their damage. I agree with your number tuning. The stacks are too low that they give. But like, by and large, I would not simply shift everything to be way slower. Things that are geared specifically around wanting to be repeated, notably applying stacks, so mostly the three elemental mages, those are things that I would be inclined to keep short or as close to we can as they are now. Within reason, you know the math and the ratios. Both on weapons and on cooldowns, an increase in variance is better than a decrease at this point.
> 3. The cooldown thing is egregious in certain cases, like the knight is useless because the cooldown for their ability that taunts and gives clarity is shorter than the time taunt takes to fizzle. So what you end up having is a knight that is basically permanently giving everyone maximum mana and then just dying because they are the target of the enemy 100% of the hits. So unless you have like a very specific like knight cleric team, which is an invincible team, it doesn't work. And there's a lot of status effects that you reapply before they run out. That's okay for stacking stuff like might and things we apply to enemies, but for effects we apply to ourselves, you know, the cooldowns need to be longer. And again, that's what I'm saying. We need to go way closer to what we were doing before.
> 4. Similarly, basically the same thing as point one, but I like the variance in the basic attacks, but they're too long. On a lot of characters, their basic attacks and cooldown for their cheapest thing ends up being like very much similar, which means that it can feel like your unit is basically just waiting around to just cast their spells and spam them. There's no regular flow to the fight. Like I said, I'd go way, way closer back to what we were doing before.
> 5. Cast times are better now. Again, they mesh poorly with some of the crazy cooldowns, because sometimes you have a cast time of 8 on an ability, which means that your next ability, which had a cooldown of 12, is ready 4 ticks after your cast, and then if you have 2 12 cost abilities, you're just cycling back and forth endlessly between them. But like, that is not a problem of the cast time, it's a problem of the cooldown times.
> 6. You proposed in your number changes to make silence less powerful. I think you need to do the quick calculation between the fire giant and the cooldown of The silence ability to just make sure you can't stun lock them, but I don't want silence to become irrelevant again once the cooldown is longer.
> 7. Another good tell for what we want to avoid in terms of feel is that we want to avoid allies two or three allies often casting a spell on the same tick. And we want to avoid a unit, not using its ready basic attack for a long moment because it's stalling out by casting multiple abilities back to back. Both of these happen often now.
> 8. You'll see a lot of mention of scaling and spamming and stuff later down. That's just a small subset of the types of things we need to do. Really what we're trying to do is give each ability a identity. When you're tuning this cooldown and ability cost and effectiveness stuff, like if you understand the identity clearly of the ability, then go ahead and do it. But I would prefer you ask me with, like, you know, a short list of abilities about how I want them to feel, than just like sort of invent something if you're not sure.
>
> **ENEMY ACTIONS**
> 1. Just making a note that I think the enemy actions, obviously they're going to need to be tuned in reaction to the players, but I think the enemies are acting more close to what is appropriate than the players are. So like, if we're putting the players back to 80% of what it was before, I would maybe put the enemy ability cooldowns to 50% or something like that, because in the old build, you would maybe see one attack a fight, like one ability, one crush from a giant. We need to make sure that we're still seeing like two or three, because it feels really good right now. use your trust in the numbers more than mine after you've done the testing.
>
> **CARRYOVER AND HEAD STARTS**
> 1. Okay, so cooldown carryover works really well. It adds a pace to the game that's way more interesting than what we had before. And I think the The next version we try is just removing the head start. The combination of the head start and the carryover makes a lot of stuff ready immediately, and I just don't think it's going to be a problem anymore. It's a cool mechanic, and I might try to find a way to bring it back, so don't delete it. Just set, you know, all the head starts to zero. It'd be amazing if in the settings I was able to change the head start ticks for the first, second, and third slot. Default them to zero, but then I could tweak it and see what feels best. But I'm pretty sure zero is going to work.
> 2. The carryover feels especially good with long cooldown abilities that are situational, things like raise. Like, you can kind of sit with them in your slot, and the order matters because your unit will cast that last. So you sometimes will swap the order to put it earlier in order to make use of it at the start of the next fight. It's a cool system.
> 3. However, I wouldn't reset cooldowns between acts. If it's full carryover and there's no agency, it's totally arbitrary to reset them, and it feels like it's just sort of stunting the units at the start of the fight, especially when we imagine a world where cooldowns are longer.
> 4. Also, another thing that contributes maybe to why the cooldown should be longer is I'm almost certain the cooldown reduction stat doesn't do anything. I got cooldown reduction gear on my unit, and from what I could tell, looking at the ticks, it does nothing. It doesn't shorten it. It doesn't do anything at all.
> 4. Also, there's a bug with the cleric's cooldown ability. They're passive, where as soon as they reach the threshold for getting the passive to trigger, it never disappears once their threshold goes up, meaning that they have half cooldowns for the rest of the fight. With the cooldown and attack speed rebalancing, you're going to have to do a pass on the damage of the basic attacks, on the damage of abilities, on the mana costs, on the regen speed, on the HP of enemies. Like, the balancing is going to be totally out of whack when we do this. The general difficulty now is quite good, except I would say that weak monsters basically just don't do anything. Like, it's just like bosses and elites can kill you. Everything else is pretty much trash. They have a lot of HP, which is fine, but they just do zero, like they'll do like 200 damage to a unit with 5,000 health. Like, it's, they're just completely useless.

> 1. I agree with your proposed changes to make crits worse. They're too strong right now. They're also really prevalent. > 2. I think your scaling on weapons, like for just basic attacks, I mean, is too high and the base damage is too low. Like, high attack units like the Hunter and like the Cleric with high magic, they are just like DPS gods with their basic attacks. I might be wrong, but I have the impression that what's happening is it's just a base number that is then scaled. > 1. So we have guard and we have ward, which are sort of opposites of each other. And then we have might and weaken that are opposites of each other. Like, not just for Sunder, even if, because even if Sunder is flat damage, I still believe that we need armor to matter, so that fast hits versus slow hits, elemental application versus non-elemental application, like, mitigation needs to be something that is part of the damage formula that is meaningful.
